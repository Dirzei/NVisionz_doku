Word recognition with a closed vocabulary – background

How a recording becomes a word: word limits from energy, features of fixed length, training with variants, rejection threshold and word spotting in a running stream (Wortgrenzen, Wortmerkmal, Augmentierung, KwsTraining, KwsModell, Horcher).

What this is about

The task is not dictation but the decision “which of a few dozen known words was spoken?” – for instance for voice commands of one’s own applications, without cloud and without a third-party model. This is a classification problem with a fixed number of classes and can be solved with a small network if three things hold: the recording must be cut to the word (otherwise the network learns the silence and the delay after the start signal), the input width must be fixed (a word lasts 300 ms one time, 700 ms the next), and the network must be trained with more examples than a recording session provides.

Word limits from the energy

The recording is cut into frames of 10 ms without overlap, and the energy in decibels is determined per frame:

Li=10log10⁡(1M∑k=0M−1xiM+k2+10−20)L_i = 10\log_{10}\!\Big(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{k=0}^{M-1} x_{iM+k}^2 + 10^{-20}\Big)

The noise floor BB is the 0.2 quantile of these values – not the minimum (a single quiet frame would be a poor yardstick) and not the mean (which contains the word). Above it lie two thresholds, B+12B+12 dB (on) and B+6B+6 dB (off). Two, because a word starts loud and fades out quietly; with a single one you either lose the fade-out or pick up breath and room noise – the same hysteresis as in a Schmitt trigger. The search looks for the first and the last frame above the on-threshold and extends outwards from there as long as the off-threshold holds. Inner dips, such as the closure before the p in “stop”, lie in between and are kept.

Features of fixed length

The MFCC chain is applied to the cut-out (MFCC page): TT windows of 13 coefficients. Three steps follow:

  1. Mean subtraction per coefficient over all windows of the word. Microphone, room and level colour the whole recording equally; in the logarithm of the spectrum that is a shift, and after the DCT it still is. Speaking louder changes only a constant summand, and that is subtracted.
  2. Derivatives, first and second (Delta): 13 become 39 values per window.
  3. Pooling to RR sections: section rr is the mean of the windows from ⌊rT/R⌋\lfloor rT/R\rfloor to ⌊(r+1)T/R⌋−1\lfloor (r+1)T/R\rfloor - 1.

The division is gap-free and non-overlapping as long as T≥RT \ge R; if the word is shorter, the respective next window is repeated – better a stretched copy than a gap. With R=8R = 8 this gives 8⋅39=3128\cdot 39 = 312 input values. Pooling costs time resolution but makes the features insensitive to speaking rate: the same word said fast and slowly gives similar values.

Variants, network and training

A self-recorded dataset has a few hundred recordings, the network on 312 inputs tens of thousands of weights: it could memorise the recordings. Every recording of the training part therefore gets variants that a human would still hear as the same word:

  • Time stretching by a factor τ∈[0.85;1.15]\tau \in [0.85;\,1.15], linear between the samples (the pitch goes along – a slightly different speaker at a slightly different tempo),
  • white noise at a distance of 15 to 30 dB below the RMS of the signal.

Loudness does not help: the mean subtraction removes it completely, a louder copy has the same features. Neither does a shift in time, which the cut-out undoes.

The network has one hidden layer with 64 ReLU units and dropout 0.30, linear class outputs and a softmax cross-entropy loss:

pc=ezc∑c'ezc',loss=−ln⁡pctruep_c = \frac{e^{z_c}}{\sum_{c'} e^{z_{c'}}}, \qquad \text{loss} = -\ln p_{c_{\text{true}}}

Training uses AdamW (learning rate 10−310^{-3}, decoupled weight decay 10−410^{-4}), batch size 32, at most 400 epochs, stopping after 40 epochs without improvement of at least 10−410^{-4}. The z-normalisation of the inputs is determined from the core only.

Rejection threshold

The network has no class “none of these”; softmax always distributes probability over the known words. Against coughs and slamming doors only a threshold on the largest probability helps: if it lies below θ=0.6\theta = 0.6, the result is “no hit”. Besides the word, Treffer returns the confidence and the second most probable word.

Word spotting in a running stream

A blind sliding window (every 50 ms the last half second into the network) computes even in silence, never hits the word limits correctly and reports every word a dozen times. Horcher instead uses the same energy consideration to decide when a word is present, and computes only then – so the network sees cut-outs like those in training. A stream differs from a file in two ways: the floor cannot be a quantile over the whole, it is tracked (in silence it moves slowly towards the measured energy, during speech it stays put), and the end of the word is a state transition with a hold time – the word ends when it has been quiet for 200 ms. It must not be shorter: the closure before a p or t lasts up to 100 ms and is completely silent.

Decisions in the framework

Decision in the framework: Cutting comes before everything else, and the floor is a quantile, not a minimum. A recording without at least 15 dB swing between the loudest frame and the floor counts as silent (Wortgrenzen.KEINES); it yields no cut-out, and Wortmerkmal.vonAufnahme then returns all zeros instead of features of silence.
Decision in the framework: Variants only for the core. A variant in the test part would be a recording whose original the network has seen – the measured rate would rise without the model having become better. For the same reason the normalisation is determined from the core only, and stopping is decided on a validation part of its own (20 % of the rest), not on the test part (25 % of the whole). Default: variantenJeAufnahme = 4, seed 20260823.
Decision in the framework: Model and feature setting belong together. Anyone who computes the features differently than in training does not get recognisable nonsense but good-looking probabilities for the wrong word. KwsModell therefore stores network, normalisation, word list, rejection threshold and every number of the feature chain in one .ennet file, and Horcher rejects a source whose sampling rate differs from the model’s with an exception.

How it is checked

KwsSelfTest (45 checks, about 7 s) pins down the properties one is meant to rely on:

  • Word limits hit start and end of a generated word within the permitted deviation, the cut-out contains the whole word, silence yields no word, an inner closure does not split.
  • Pooling: correct length, gap-free (deviation below 10−910^{-9}), order preserved; a constant sequence stays constant (below 10−1210^{-12}).
  • Mean subtraction makes the features mean-free (below 10−1210^{-12}). The features are insensitive to loudness (below 10−910^{-9}) and to a shift in time (below 10−610^{-6}); without mean subtraction loudness does come through (distance above 1).
  • Dataset: nothing is lost in the split, every word is present in both parts, no recording is in both.
  • Training on an artificial dataset: recognition rate at least 90 %; with swapped labels below 35 % (the network then learns nothing, so the setup is not blind to errors).
  • Save and load: word list and feature setting survive, the probabilities are equal value by value and sum to 1 (deviation below 10−1210^{-12}).
  • Horcher finds six words in a sequence of six and recognises at least five of them correctly.

Limits

  • Word recognition has been accepted on an artificial voice. Acceptance with a real voice needs a recorded dataset; rates from the self-test say nothing about real speakers.
  • Closed vocabulary: an unknown word is assigned to the most similar known one, provided it clears the rejection threshold.
  • The energy consideration does not distinguish speech from other sound; coughs and slamming doors produce hits that only the threshold catches.
  • Pooling to eight sections resolves speaking rate only within limits; the stretching in the variants changes the pitch at the same time.
  • Wortmerkmal holds the buffers of the feature chain and is not usable concurrently; one instance per thread.
  • When recording, name the input explicitly (not the system default) and write nothing to the console during recording: an output can hang for 150 to 200 ms while the input buffer keeps running.
