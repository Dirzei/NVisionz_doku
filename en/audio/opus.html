Opus decoder – background

How Ogg Opus becomes PCM again: packets and frame sizes, the range decoder, SILK, CELT, hybrid and mode changes with redundancy, concealment of lost packets (PLC, DTX, FEC), the Ogg header with pre-skip, gain, start offset and channel families, seeking and the radio stream (OpusDekoder, Bereichsdekoder, SilkDekoder, SilkPlc, CeltDekoder, OpusKopf, OpusMehrstrom, OpusSource, OpusStrom).

What it is about

Opus (RFC 6716) combines two codecs in one stream. SILK is a speech codec based on linear prediction: a filter describes the timbre of the vocal tract, a long-term predictor the pitch, and only the remainder is transmitted. CELT is a transform codec: the spectrum of an MDCT is split into bands, the energy of each band is transmitted precisely, the shape inside the band coarsely as a lattice point on a sphere. A packet takes one of three paths: SILK only (up to wideband, 8/12/16 kHz internally), CELT only (up to fullband) or hybrid – SILK below 8 kHz, CELT above. Output is always at 48 kHz.

The decoder is written entirely within the framework, without a third-party library. It follows the reference of RFC 6716 with the corrections of RFC 8251 and the behaviour of libopus 1.5.2 (floating-point build, without the optional neural extensions DRED and Deep PLC); it is measured against libopus. The container is Ogg (RFC 7845), with the multichannel and ambisonics families of RFC 8486.

Packets and frame sizes

The first byte of a packet (TOC) carries the configuration cc (5 bits), a stereo bit and the code (2 bits). The configuration fixes path, bandwidth and frame duration:

ConfigurationPathFrame duration
0–11SILK, NB/MB/WB10, 20, 40, 60 ms
12–15Hybrid, SWB/FB10, 20 ms
16–31CELT, NB/WB/SWB/FB2.5, 5, 10, 20 ms

Code 0 is one frame, code 1 two frames of equal size, code 2 two frames with a length for the first (one or two bytes, ℓ=4b1+b0\ell = 4b_1 + b_0 from b0≥252b_0 \ge 252), code 3 up to 48 frames with padding and optionally individual lengths; a packet may last at most 120 ms. Anything that violates the rules of RFC 6716 3.4 is rejected with a reason – for example code 1 with an odd remaining length or padding that reaches beyond the packet. The self-delimiting form of appendix B (multistream: all packets but the last carry their length) is read by the same class.

The range decoder

Both codecs read their symbols from one shared arithmetic decoder (RFC 6716 4.1). Its state is a range rr and a value vv, both 32 bits. For a symbol with cumulative frequencies fl<fh≤ftf_l < f_h \le f_t

q=⌊rft⌋,fs=ft−min⁡(⌊vq⌋+1,ft),q = \left\lfloor \frac{r}{f_t} \right\rfloor,\qquad f_s = f_t - \min\!\left(\left\lfloor \frac{v}{q} \right\rfloor + 1,\ f_t\right),

and the symbol sought is the one with fl≤fs<fhf_l \le f_s < f_h. Then

v←v−q(ft−fh),r←{q(fh−fl)fl>0r−q(ft−fh)fl=0v \leftarrow v - q\,(f_t - f_h),\qquad r \leftarrow \begin{cases} q\,(f_h - f_l) & f_l > 0\\ r - q\,(f_t - f_h) & f_l = 0\end{cases}

The remainder of the division goes to the first symbol so that the encoder wastes no range. When rr drops below 2232^{23}, one byte is shifted in. Raw bits (bits, for example CELT's fine energy) come instead from the end of the frame, backwards; both readers meet somewhere in the middle, and tell reports in eighth bits how much has been used – CELT's remaining allocation depends on it.

SILK

A SILK frame is 10 or 20 ms long and divided into 5 ms subframes. Read are the frame type and gains, the line spectral frequencies (NLSF) via a two-stage vector quantiser, the pitch lag per subframe with a contour table, five LTP coefficients per subframe and finally the excitation as pulses in shells of 16 values. Reconstruction is

e[n]→x[n]=e[n]+∑k=−22bkx[n−T+k]→y[n]=gx[n]+∑j=1dajy[n−j],e[n] \;\to\; x[n] = e[n] + \sum_{k=-2}^{2} b_k\, x[n - T + k] \;\to\; y[n] = g\,x[n] + \sum_{j=1}^{d} a_j\, y[n-j],

first long-term, then short-term prediction with the LPC coefficients aja_j of order d=10d = 10 (NB/MB) or 16 (WB). Before conversion the NLSF are spread so that the filter is stable (stabilisieren, with the clamping of silk_LIMIT even for swapped bounds); if the inverse prediction gain is still too small, the bandwidth is widened in up to 16 steps. Computed in integers as in the reference, hence bit-exact with libopus. Finally Wandler resamples from 8/12/16 kHz to 48 kHz.

SILK transmits stereo as mid and side with a prediction of the side from the mid; in addition each frame may carry an LBRR copy (low bit-rate redundancy) of the previous frame – the basis of FEC, see below.

CELT

CELT transmits the energy of each band bb and, separately, its shape. The coarse energy is a prediction across time and frequency with a Laplace-coded residual (decay from a table, laplace), the fine energy comes from raw bits. The shape is a lattice point

y∈ZN,∑i|yi|=K,xb=Eby‖y‖,\mathbf y \in \mathbb Z^{N},\qquad \sum_{i} |y_i| = K,\qquad \mathbf x_b = \sqrt{E_b}\,\frac{\mathbf y}{\lVert \mathbf y \rVert},

i.e. pyramid vector quantisation: KK pulses on NN lines, the index enumerated via the number V(N,K)V(N,K) of such points. How many pulses each band receives, the decoder recomputes itself from the remaining bits (CeltZuteilung) – the allocation is not transmitted, only its deviations. Added to this are spreading (a rotation so that few pulses do not sound tonal), time-frequency switching per band, stereo as mid/side with an angle, avoidance of holes after transients (anti-collapse, noise in short blocks left empty) and finally the IMDCT with 2.5 ms overlap, a comb filter on the pitch (postfilter, cross-faded over 2.5 ms) and de-emphasis y[n]=x[n]+0.85y[n−1]y[n] = x[n] + 0.85\,y[n-1].

libopus computes CELT in float; the pulse search and the rotation follow bit-exact integer formulas (bitexactCos, bitexactLog2tan), otherwise the allocation would depend on rounding. The decoder also computes CELT in float and in the same order: energies, shape vectors (normalised as the reciprocal of the root times the gain), inverse MDCT via an FFT of length N/4N/4 with mixed radix 5⋅3⋅4⋅4⋅25\cdot 3\cdot 4\cdot 4\cdot 2 and with the tables of libopus (window, twiddles, bit reversal), comb filter and de-emphasis. Against the C build of libopus 1.5.2 the output is therefore bit-exact. The window computed by ourselves, sin⁡(π2sin2⁡(⋅))\sin\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\sin^2(\cdot)\bigr), misses the table after rounding to float at 5 of 120 entries.

Hybrid and mode changes

A hybrid frame first reads SILK (up to 8 kHz), then CELT from band 17 in the same range decoder, and adds both. The changes between paths are harder: CELT's IMDCT needs half of the previous window, which SILK does not leave behind. The encoder therefore puts a redundancy frame of 5 ms CELT into the SILK packet at the transition (flag and length at the end of the SILK part):

  • SILK → CELT (to_celt): the redundancy belongs to the end of the SILK frame; its last 2.5 ms are cross-faded into the following CELT frame with the square of the CELT window.
  • CELT → SILK (celt_to_silk): the redundancy is decoded before the SILK frame and replaces its first 2.5 ms, cross-faded into the rest.

For CELT → SILK, libopus 1.5 applies a condition that RFC 6716 does not have: the copy is used only if the previous frame really used CELT,

use⟺redundancy∧celt_to_silk∧(previous mode≠SILK∨previous redundancy).\text{use} \iff \text{redundancy} \wedge \text{celt\_to\_silk} \wedge \bigl(\text{previous mode} \ne \text{SILK} \vee \text{previous redundancy}\bigr).

If the CELT packets before the change were lost and concealed as SILK, the CELT state would still be at the last CELT frame seen; a copy from it would be an audible jump (measured: 18 dB from libopus in the transition frame, bit-exact with the condition).

A change without redundancy is cross-faded, as in libopus, with a concealed continuation of the old path.

Concealing lost packets

A packet is missing when an Ogg page is corrupt (CRC), when the granule position shows a gap or when a packet cannot be parsed. Packets of length 0 or 1 are no loss but DTX (the encoder stays silent during pauses) – both are concealed alike, with the frame duration of the previous packet.

SILK (SilkPlc) repeats the last pitch period through the last LPC filter, attenuates the LTP gain per frame, widens the LPC bandwidth and mixes in comfort noise; at the next real frame the gain is matched (naht). Integer arithmetic as in the reference, hence bit-exact.

CELT conceals with the pitch: the period TT is searched in the last 1024 values, an LPC filter of order 24 is derived from their autocorrelation, and the excitation is continued periodically. The autocorrelation is conditioned before the Levinson recursion,

R0←1.0001R0,Ri←Ri(1−(0.008i)2),i=1..24,R_0 \leftarrow 1.0001\,R_0,\qquad R_i \leftarrow R_i\bigl(1 - (0.008\,i)^2\bigr),\quad i = 1..24,

a noise floor of −40 dB and a Gaussian lag window. The recursion stops as soon as the prediction error is ε≤10−3R0\varepsilon \le 10^{-3} R_0 (30 dB gain). The excitation e=A(z)xe = A(z)\,x is continued from the last two periods with

δ=E1E2,E1=∑ne[n]2 (last period),E2=∑ne[n−T]2 (before),\delta = \sqrt{\frac{E_1}{E_2}},\qquad E_1 = \sum_{n} e[n]^2 \text{ (last period)},\quad E_2 = \sum_{n} e[n-T]^2 \text{ (before)},

attenuated per period, filtered through 1/A(z)1/A(z) and guarded against energy growth. After 100 ms of loss, for hybrid, and when pitch concealment is not suitable, the decoder instead fills the bands with noise at the decaying last energy.

This stage is computed in float and in the same summation order as libopus, and so is everything that goes into the buffer it reads from. If the prediction error of the Levinson recursion lies just at its threshold 10−3R010^{-3} R_0, the last digit of the buffer decides whether one more step is computed. With the autocorrelation in double the concealment was about 50 dB from libopus, with the buffer in double 27 dB at the threshold; with the whole synthesis in float it is bit-exact. Part of this is that the comb filter does not cross-fade when the old and new sets are equal: the cross-fade would give the same value only up to a rounding.

FEC: if packet kk is missing and packet k+1k+1 is present, the latter's SILK part often contains the LBRR copy of kk. OpusSource therefore looks one packet ahead and decodes the missing frame from the copy (like opus_decode with decode_fec = 1); without a copy it conceals.

Ogg Opus: header, timeline, channels

The first packet (OpusHead) gives the channel count, the pre-skip, the original sample rate (informational only), the output gain GG in Q7.8 dB and the channel family; the second (OpusTags) the tags as in Vorbis. The gain is applied as a factor:

g=10G/(20⋅256)=10G/5120.g = 10^{G/(20\cdot 256)} = 10^{G/5120}.

The granule position of a page counts the 48 kHz samples up to the end of its last packet, including the pre-skip. Length and start time follow from it (RFC 7845 4.5):

t0=g1−∑packets of page 1np,L=glast−pre-skip−t0.t_0 = g_1 - \sum_{\text{packets of page 1}} n_p,\qquad L = g_{\text{last}} - \text{pre-skip} - t_0 .

A stream that begins in the middle of a recording has t0>0t_0 > 0; the samples stay, the timeline begins at t0t_0. If t0<0t_0 < 0, the first −t0-t_0 samples are dropped. If the first audio page is also the last, its granule may be smaller than the samples – that is then end trimming, and t0=0t_0 = 0.

The channel family determines how the streams of a packet (coupled stereo pairs and single channels) map onto channels and what the channels mean:

2ambisonics, (n+1)2+2j(n+1)^2 + 2j channels (ACN, SN3D), jj = non-diegetic stereoL=W/2+Y/2,R=W/2−Y/2L = W/2 + Y/2,\ R = W/2 - Y/2

Seeking bisects over the pages to a page at least 80 ms (3840 samples) before the target; the decoder is reset and decodes up to the target without output. Since SILK and CELT have settled after 80 ms but are not bit-exact with reading straight through, the position is sample-exact and the sound after the pre-roll equal up to the settling residue.

Ogg Opus in the radio stream

Internet radio sends Opus as continuous Ogg (audio/ogg, audio/opus). Radiostrom looks at the first 300 bytes; if OpusHead is there, OpusStrom reads the stream without length and without seeking. A station strings titles together as a chain of logical streams: a new beginning page with a different serial number brings new header packets, hence a new decoder; the title from OpusTags goes to the title listener if the station sends no ICY titles. If the channel count changes, it is matched (mono doubled, stereo averaged) so that playback need not be reopened. The sample rate is always 48 kHz for Opus. Recording uses the ENCODED path (decoded sound into our own MP3 encoder).

Framework conventions

Framework convention: Output is at most stereo. Multichannel (family 1) is downmixed in Vorbis order, ambisonics (2, 3) via the omnidirectional part WW and the lateral part YY, family 255 and reserved families with equal weights. For probes, OpusMehrstrom also hands out the channels before downmixing.
Framework convention: Corrupt pages and gaps in the granule position are counted (fehlseiten, luecken), the missing samples concealed or restored via FEC; the timeline stays that of the original. The search for the length when opening does not count.
Framework convention: A packet that cannot be parsed counts as lost and is concealed instead of stopping playback; a wrong header (version, channel count, family, mapping) is rejected with a reason.

How it is checked

  • Opusprobe (82 checks): range decoder against our own encoder (20,000 symbols), frame sizes of all 32 configurations, broken packets and headers, thirteen test files from libopus 1.5.2 via ffmpeg (SILK NB/MB/WB, hybrid, CELT 2.5 to 20 ms, clicks, CBR, 5.1, long stereo, LBRR) against fingerprints of the libopus decoding: SILK bit-exact, CELT and hybrid at most 0.22 of the tolerance (ffmpeg builds libopus with other code paths; against the C build bit-exact); pre-skip and gain, tags, seeking, 240 corrupt files and 4000 random packets (only IOException).
  • Opusstromprobe (16): every test file as a stream equal to the file, chains of two titles, channel matching, 150 corrupt streams.
  • Opusnachtragprobe (32): start time with rewritten granules (+96,000, −2000, single page as end page), families 2 and 255 before downmixing against libopus, family 3 from a file rewritten by the probe (ffmpeg does not encode family 3) with S=DXS = DX to 1.5⋅10−81.5\cdot 10^{-8}; DTX (SILK bit-exact, fullband 0.09 of the tolerance); losses against libopus with the same packets left out (SILK bit-exact with and without FEC, hybrid and CELT with pitch concealment bit-exact, limit 120 dB); a corrupt page in the file, counted exactly once; the mode change CELT → SILK on a test file we encoded ourselves (libopus with forced mode, frames 20 to 29 CELT) with and without missing CELT packets.
  • Radioprobe, Boeswilligprobe: an Opus station of two titles on the test server, corrupt Opus files without a crash.
  • 29 counter-probes aa31-*, all red.

Limits

  • Decoder only; an Opus encoder is not built.
  • The neural extensions of libopus 1.5 (DRED, Deep PLC, LACE/NoLACE) are not built; they are opt-in there as well, and streams without them are decoded as by the standard build.
  • CELT is bit-exact against the C build of libopus 1.5.2 without vector instructions, concealment included. A libopus compiled with vector instructions or fused multiply-add (as in ffmpeg) sums differently; against it a distance in the last digit remains, and at the Levinson threshold its concealment may decide differently. exp and cos come from the Java runtime, in libopus from the C library; measured equal, but not guaranteed. The probes therefore require 120 dB for the pitch concealment, not equality.
  • Output at most stereo (see conventions).
  • No seeking and no length in the radio stream.
