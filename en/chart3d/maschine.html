The drawing engine – background

How triangles in space become an image, without OpenGL and without a third-party library: camera and projection, clipping at the near plane, rasterisation with a depth buffer, shading, colour by value, edge emphasis and fog, supersampling. Pseudocode following the source, conventions, checks, limits.

What this is about

A surface, a point cloud or a bar chart in space consists, for the engine, of triangles (plus polylines and points). An image is made by projecting every triangle onto the screen and filling it pixel by pixel – where several triangles cover the same pixel only the nearest counts. This is the classic Z-buffer method. The engine computes it entirely in Java (ZBufferRenderer, Framebuffer, Scene3D, Camera, Mat4, Vec3) and returns a BufferedImage; it therefore runs without a graphics card and in a native image.

Camera and projection

The Camera orbits a target z→\vec{z} (default: the centre of the chart box, (0.5|0.5|0.5)(0.5\,|\,0.5\,|\,0.5)). Its position is given by three numbers: azimuth θ\theta about the vertical axis, polar angle φ\varphi from the vertical axis, and distance ρ\rho:

e→=z→+ρ(sin⁡φcos⁡θ,sin⁡φsin⁡θ,cos⁡φ)\vec{e} = \vec{z} + \rho\,\bigl(\sin\varphi\cos\theta,\; \sin\varphi\sin\theta,\; \cos\varphi\bigr)

The view matrix Mat4.lookAt(e, z, (0,0,1)) rotates and shifts the world so that the eye is at the origin and the viewing direction f→\vec{f} points along the negative zz axis: its rows are s→=f→×up→\vec{s} = \vec{f}\times\vec{up}, u→=s→×f→\vec{u} = \vec{s}\times\vec{f} and −f→-\vec{f}, the translation is −s→⋅e→-\vec{s}\cdot\vec{e}, −u→⋅e→-\vec{u}\cdot\vec{e}, f→⋅e→\vec{f}\cdot\vec{e}. If the view is parallel to the vertical axis, (0,0,1)(0,0,1) or, last, (1,0,0)(1,0,0) is used instead.

Perspective (opening angle α=\alpha =fovY, default 30°, aspect ratio aa, near plane nn, far plane ff):

P=ca0000c0000f+nn−f2fnn−f00−10,c=1tan⁡(α/2)P = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{c}{a} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & c & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{f+n}{n-f} & \frac{2fn}{n-f} \\ 0 & 0 & -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad c = \frac{1}{\tan(\alpha/2)}

The fourth component of the result is w=−zeyew = -z_{\text{eye}}, the distance in front of the eye. Only the division by ww makes distant things small. Parallel projection (setOrthographic) omits that division; the half height is chosen so that the display at the target is the same size as the perspective one: h=ρtan⁡(α/2)h = \rho\tan(\alpha/2). The scale therefore does not jump when switching. Near and far plane follow the distance: n=max⁡(10−4,0.01ρ)n = \max(10^{-4}, 0.01\rho), f=100ρf = 100\rho. If the user pans (pan), the image is shifted by 2pan⁡2\,\mathrm{pan} in normalised image coordinates after the projection.

Worked example. The chart starts with θ=−55∘\theta = -55^\circ, φ=65∘\varphi = 65^\circ, ρ=3.6\rho = 3.6. With sin⁡65∘=0.9063\sin 65^\circ = 0.9063, cos⁡65∘=0.4226\cos 65^\circ = 0.4226, cos⁡(−55∘)=0.5736\cos(-55^\circ) = 0.5736, sin⁡(−55∘)=−0.8192\sin(-55^\circ) = -0.8192 the eye is at x=0.5+3.6⋅0.9063⋅0.5736=2.371x = 0.5 + 3.6\cdot0.9063\cdot0.5736 = 2.371, y=0.5−3.6⋅0.9063⋅0.8192=−2.173y = 0.5 - 3.6\cdot0.9063\cdot0.8192 = -2.173, z=0.5+3.6⋅0.4226=2.021z = 0.5 + 3.6\cdot0.4226 = 2.021. Near plane n=0.036n = 0.036, far plane f=360f = 360, and at the target the image shows a half height of 3.6tan⁡15∘=0.9653.6\tan 15^\circ = 0.965 units – for a box of edge length 1 plenty of room for the axis labels.

Convention in the framework: The vertical axis is zz (the predecessor library put yy up). The polar angle is clamped to [10−3,π−10−3][10^{-3},\,\pi - 10^{-3}] so that the view never runs exactly along the vertical axis; the distance stays positive (at least 10−610^{-6}).

Clipping at the near plane

A triangle that pierces the camera plane has corners with w≤0w \le 0; dividing by ww there flips the image. Every triangle is therefore clipped at w=nw = n before the division, by Sutherland-Hodgman against exactly one plane: corners with w>nw > n stay, every edge that crosses the plane yields an intersection with t=(n−wi)/(wj−wi)t = (n - w_i)/(w_j - w_i), interpolated linearly in all four components. One triangle becomes a triangle or a quadrilateral (which then continues as two triangles).

clipNear(input[3 corners, each x,y,z,w]; near) → output:
    m ← 0
    for i ← 0 to 2:
        j ← (i+1) mod 3
        in_i ← w[i] > near;  in_j ← w[j] > near
        if in_i: output[m] ← corner[i];  m ← m+1
        if in_i ≠ in_j:
            t ← (near − w[i]) / (w[j] − w[i])
            output[m] ← corner[i] + t·(corner[j] − corner[i]);  m ← m+1
    return m                                           0, 3 or 4 corners

Source: envisionz-framework-chart3d/src/envisionz/chart3d/render/ZBufferRenderer.java, clipNear. The corners are then brought to pixel coordinates: sx=(x/w⋅0.5+0.5)Ws_x = (x/w \cdot 0.5 + 0.5)\,W, sy=(0.5−y/w⋅0.5)Hs_y = (0.5 - y/w \cdot 0.5)\,H, sz=z/ws_z = z/w.

Rasterisation with a depth buffer

For every triangle with pixel-space corners P0,P1,P2P_0, P_1, P_2 and every pixel PP in its bounding rectangle the barycentric coordinates w0,w1,w2w_0, w_1, w_2 are computed from area ratios; the pixel lies inside the triangle if all three are non-negative. Sampling is at the pixel centre (x+12,y+12x + \tfrac12,\ y + \tfrac12). The depth of the point is z=w0z0+w1z1+w2z2z = w_0 z_0 + w_1 z_1 + w_2 z_2; the point is written only if it is nearer than the value in the depth buffer.

w0=(x1−px)(y2−py)−(x2−px)(y1−py)A,w1=(x2−px)(y0−py)−(x0−px)(y2−py)A,w2=1−w0−w1w_0 = \frac{(x_1 - p_x)(y_2 - p_y) - (x_2 - p_x)(y_1 - p_y)}{A}, \quad w_1 = \frac{(x_2 - p_x)(y_0 - p_y) - (x_0 - p_x)(y_2 - p_y)}{A}, \quad w_2 = 1 - w_0 - w_1

with twice the triangle area A=(x1−x0)(y2−y0)−(x2−x0)(y1−y0)A = (x_1 - x_0)(y_2 - y_0) - (x_2 - x_0)(y_1 - y_0); if |A|<10−12|A| < 10^{-12} the triangle is degenerate and skipped.

Parallelisation. The image is divided into tiles of 64 by 64 pixels. Each triangle is sorted into the tiles touched by its bounding rectangle; the tiles run in parallel (IntStream.parallel). Because each tile writes only its own image section, no two tasks ever write the same pixel and no locks are needed.

Translucent surfaces need an order; a depth buffer alone is not enough. Hence two passes: first all opaque triangles, in any order. Then the translucent ones, sorted back to front, tested against the depth buffer but without writing it, and blended over the background with opacity α\alpha: c=αcnew+(1−α)coldc = \alpha\,c_{\text{new}} + (1-\alpha)\,c_{\text{old}}. The relation translucent/opaque is thus exact; only translucent surfaces among themselves are ordered by centroid.

rasterize(triangle t, tile):
    A ← twice the area;  if |A| < 1e−12: return
    for each pixel (x, y) in bounding rectangle ∩ tile:
        p ← (x+½, y+½);  w0, w1, w2 ← barycentric coordinates of p
        if any w < 0: continue
        z ← w0·z0 + w1·z1 + w2·z2
        if z ≥ depth[x,y]: continue
        tone ← if Gouraud: w0·s0 + w1·s1 + w2·s2  else s            shading
        colour ← if corner values: table[ ⌊clamp((v − lo)/(hi − lo)) · 2047⌋ ]   v = w0·v0 + w1·v1 + w2·v2
                 else triangle colour;   colour ← colour · tone
        if opaque: depth[x,y] ← z;  image[x,y] ← colour;  id[x,y] ← pick id
        else:      image[x,y] ← over(colour, image[x,y], α)           depth unchanged

Source: envisionz-framework-chart3d/src/envisionz/chart3d/render/ZBufferRenderer.java, rasterize, fillRange, fillTriangles. Back faces of closed bodies are dropped when projecting (sign of the image area); with an active section plane this is skipped, because the plane opens every closed body.

Convention in the framework: The depth is the image depth z/wz/w, linear in the image – that is correct for the comparison. Values and shading tones are likewise blended linearly in the image over the barycentric coordinates, without perspective correction. For small triangles, as grid surfaces deliver, the error is small; for a very large, strongly oblique triangle it would become visible.

Shading and colour by value

Lighting is not done with a light source but with a substitute lighting by the inclination against the vertical axis: with the unit normal n→\vec{n} and the ambient light κ\kappa (ambient, default 0.35) the brightness factor is

s=κ+(1−κ)(0.35+0.65|nz|)s = \kappa + (1 - \kappa)\,\bigl(0.35 + 0.65\,|n_z|\bigr)

A horizontal surface (|nz|=1|n_z| = 1) appears in full colour, a vertical one (nz=0n_z = 0) with 0.35+0.65⋅0.35=0.57750.35 + 0.65\cdot0.35 = 0.5775 of the brightness; at |nz|=0.5|n_z| = 0.5, s=0.35+0.65⋅0.675=0.789s = 0.35 + 0.65 \cdot 0.675 = 0.789. With κ≥1\kappa \ge 1 everything stays unchanged.

Flat: each triangle gets one factor from its face normal. Gouraud (setGouraud) computes the factor per corner from an averaged corner normal and blends it across the image: for each corner the face normals of all triangles at the same location are averaged, but only those enclosing at most the crease angle with its own normal (default 40°, cos⁡\cos as the threshold) – so real edges stay sharp and curved surfaces smooth. "The same location" means: coordinates rounded to 10−610^{-6} of the largest extent of the scene, sorted, grouped and, within a group, compared exactly once more so that a hash collision does not merge two different places.

Colour by value. If the scene carries corner values, the colour is not set per corner but looked up per pixel from the blended value in a table of 2048 entries built from the ColorScale (value →t∈[0,1]→\to t\in[0,1]\to colour). With a banded scale the band boundary therefore lies exactly on the pixel, even in the middle of a triangle; setIsoLines draws a line where the band number changes.

Edges, fog, supersampling

After rasterisation one pass over the whole image runs if fog (setDepthCue) or edge emphasis (setEdges) is set. Both use the depth buffer.

Fog. With the smallest and largest depth present, zmin⁡,zmax⁡z_{\min}, z_{\max}, every colour is blended towards the background: t=z−zmin⁡zmax⁡−zmin⁡⋅dt = \dfrac{z - z_{\min}}{z_{\max} - z_{\min}}\cdot d with strength d∈[0,1]d \in [0,1], c'=c+t(cbackground−c)c' = c + t\,(c_{\text{background}} - c).

Edge emphasis. What is measured is not the depth difference to the neighbour – on every inclined surface that is large and would trace the triangle mesh – but the second derivative of the depth: m=max⁡(|2z−zl−zr|,|2z−zo−zu|)m = \max\bigl(|2z - z_{l} - z_{r}|,\ |2z - z_{o} - z_{u}|\bigr). On a plane surface the middle point lies exactly between its neighbours, m=0m = 0, however steep the surface. Only at a crease or a silhouette is mm large; an empty neighbour always counts as a silhouette. If mm exceeds the threshold σ⋅(zmax⁡−zmin⁡)\sigma\cdot(z_{\max} - z_{\min}) (default σ=0.004\sigma = 0.004), the colour is blended towards the edge colour with weight min⁡(1,m/(4σ(zmax⁡−zmin⁡)))⋅\min(1, m/(4\sigma(z_{\max}-z_{\min})))\cdot strength.

Supersampling. At factor kk (1 to 4, default 2) the computation uses kk times the width and height and at the end k×kk \times k points are averaged into one, per colour channel with integer division. The pick query itemAt reads the centre of the corresponding group.

Source: envisionz-framework-chart3d/src/envisionz/chart3d/render/ZBufferRenderer.java (nachbearbeiten, kantenmass, buildLut, eckschatten, shadeFactor), Framebuffer.java (downsample), scene/Scene3D.java (eckennormalen).

How it is checked

The checks do not compare with an earlier run but with closed-form target values.

  • Chart3dprobe (225 checks): determinant and orthogonality of the view matrix (tolerance 10−910^{-9}), view point on the negative zz axis, edge of the perspective cone exactly at ±1\pm 1 (tolerance 10−1210^{-12}), near and far plane at ∓1\mp 1, view point at the image centre for perspective and parallel projection, clamping of the polar angle, visibility for a known viewing direction.
  • render.Rasterprobe (26 checks): the anchors are horizontal surfaces seen vertically from above – there the shading factor is exactly 1, and every target colour can be read from the colour scale. Checked are colouring by value including a change of colour table, isolines at band boundaries, fog (full, half, clamped), edge emphasis, the clip at the near plane, outlines and polylines by value. In addition 24 counter-tests, each with a deliberate corruption, all red.
  • The pure painting – shading tones, text layout, Swing cards – is left to the viewing programs (Chart3DPruefung, Chart3DProof) and to inspection by eye.

Limits

  • No real light: the tone depends only on the inclination against the vertical axis, not on a light direction; there are no shadows and no reflections.
  • Translucent surfaces among themselves are ordered only by centroid; interpenetrating translucent surfaces may be blended wrongly.
  • Values, tones and colours are blended without perspective correction (see above).
  • Full fog gives fefeff instead of ffffff at the farthest point, because the conversion to integers cuts off the last bit of the depth (noted in the raster check, no finding).
  • The cost grows with the number of triangles and with k2k^2 of the supersampling; the tiles use all processor cores but no graphics card.
