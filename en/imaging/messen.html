Period and perforation

One method for two questions: how far apart the holes of a stamp are, and how many pixels a millimetre has on a ruler. Both are the fundamental period of a brightness profile.

The profile

Brightness is sampled along a line in the image, bilinearly between pixels and averaged across the line over a width. That gives a sequence p0,…,pn−1p_0, \dots, p_{n-1}. Uneven light puts a gradient underneath; mean and linear trend are therefore removed by least squares:

qi=pi−a−bi,(a,b)=arg⁡min⁡∑i(pi−a−bi)2.q_i = p_i - a - b\,i, \qquad (a, b) = \arg\min \sum_i \big(p_i - a - b\,i\big)^2.

If qq no longer varies, there is no period, and the result is not a number.

Normalised autocorrelation

For every shift kk between kmin⁡k_{\min} and kmax⁡k_{\max}

r(k)=∑iqiqi+k∑iqi2∑iqi+k2r(k) = \frac{\sum_i q_i\,q_{i+k}}{\sqrt{\sum_i q_i^2 \,\sum_i q_{i+k}^2}}

is computed over the overlapping values. The highest peak is sought. The quality of the result is rr at the period: 1 means strictly periodic.

Sub-harmonics. The highest peak often lies at a multiple of the fundamental period. On a ruler the long strokes every 5 mm are stronger than the short ones in between. Therefore, for m=8,7,…,2m = 8, 7, \dots, 2, the method checks whether a peak with at least 60 percent of the maximum lies near k*/mk^*/m; the first such peak wins.

Fine position

A parabola through the three values around the peak gives the position between two integer shifts:

k^=k+r(k−1)−r(k+1)2(r(k−1)−2r(k)+r(k+1)).\hat k = k + \frac{r(k-1) - r(k+1)}{2\,\big(r(k-1) - 2\,r(k) + r(k+1)\big)}.

For a square wave (row of holes, sequence of strokes) the autocorrelation is triangular, however, and the parabola misses: for a period of 7.3 it gave 7.21. The fine position is therefore measured at a high multiple, at the jj-th peak, and divided by jj:

T=k^jj.T = \frac{\hat k_j}{j}.

The error of the parabola stays about the same, but division by jj shrinks it by that factor. The largest jj whose peak still reaches 60 percent of the quality is taken.

Perforation

The perforation gauge of a stamp is the number of holes per 20 mm. With the hole period TT in pixels and the scale ss in pixels per millimetre

z=20T/s.z = \frac{20}{T / s}.

Catalogues give the gauge in quarters: round⁡(4z)/4\operatorname{round}(4z)/4, written as “13¾” and spoken as “13 and three quarters”.

Limits

  • So far someone has to give the scale; taking it from a ruler or graph paper in the same image is open.
  • The line is given. Finding the edge of the stamp itself and measuring each side separately is open.
  • The profile must be at least two largest periods long, otherwise it is rejected.
