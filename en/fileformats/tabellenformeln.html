Excel workbooks and their formulas – background

What has to be computed when reading an .xlsx: column names as bijective base 26, two date systems with a day that never existed, formula precedence, shifting shared formulas, tinting theme colours and comparing the recomputed value with the stored one.

What this is about: numbers meant differently from how they read

A workbook is a ZIP with XML parts. Almost nothing in it is stored as the sheet shows it: a date is a day count whose meaning depends on a cell format and a workbook switch; a column is called AB and means the 28th; a formula is often stored only in the first cell of a range and applies shifted to the others. The reader XlsxQuelle gives values, the workbook Mappe adds appearance and formulas, and the Auswerter recomputes the formulas – like Excel, not like school. The rules below are those at which the result silently shifts.

Column names: bijective base 26

Columns are named A to Z, then AA to ZZ, then AAA … There is no digit zero: Z is followed by AA, not BA. With letter values ci∈{1,…,26}c_i \in \{1, \ldots, 26\} (A = 1) the number is

n=∑i=0k−1ci26k−1−i,back: c=(n−1)mod26+1,n←⌊n−126⌋.n = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} c_i \, 26^{\,k-1-i}, \qquad \text{back: } c = (n-1) \bmod 26 + 1,\ \ n \leftarrow \left\lfloor \frac{n-1}{26} \right\rfloor .

A = 1, Z = 26, AA = 27, AZ = 52, BA = 53, ZZ = 702, AAA = 703, and the last column of a workbook is XFD = 16,384. Converting back with nmod26n \bmod 26 turns 26 into “@”. A cell reference beyond XFD exists in no workbook; without a limit the sum overflowed for long letter sequences, and a single r="ZZZZZZ1" made the reader create 321 million empty cells. Everything beyond XFD is therefore rejected.

Two date systems and 29 February 1900

A date is the number of days since an epoch, the fraction the time of day. Which epoch applies is given by the workbook attribute date1904:

systemday 0day 1peculiarity
1900 (default)“0 January 1900”1 January 1900day 60 is 29 February 1900, which never existed
19041 January 19042 January 1904none; offset by 1462 days from 1900

Lotus 1-2-3 treated 1900 as a leap year, and Excel adopted this so that sheets kept the same numbers. For the 1900 system, therefore

date(t)={1899-12-31+t1≤t≤59no datet=601899-12-31+(t−1)t≥61date1904(t)=1904-01-01+t.\text{date}(t) = \begin{cases} 1899\text{-}12\text{-}31 + t & 1 \le t \le 59 \\ \text{no date} & t = 60 \\ 1899\text{-}12\text{-}31 + (t-1) & t \ge 61 \end{cases} \qquad \text{date}_{1904}(t) = 1904\text{-}01\text{-}01 + t .

Examples: 59 is 28 February 1900, 61 is 1 March 1900, 45,000 is 15 March 2023; in a 1904 workbook 1462 is 2 January 1908 and 43,830 is 1 January 2024. Day 60 is not interpreted but left as a number – better undetermined than wrong. A reader that ignores date1904 delivers every date of such a workbook four years and one day too early, without any sign.

The date functions of the formulas compute the same systems backwards and forwards: DATE(1900,2,28) is 59, DATE(1900,3,1) is 61, DATE(2024,1,1) is 45,292 – and 43,830 in a 1904 workbook; YEAR(0) is 1904 there.

Weekdays, months and year fractions

Since 2026-10-25 the evaluator also computes WEEKDAY, EDATE, EOMONTH, DATEDIF, NETWORKDAYS, WORKDAY, WEEKNUM, ISOWEEKNUM, YEARFRAC, DAYS and DAYS360, in both date systems; before, they gave #NAME?. Every date argument is truncated to whole days; negative or beyond 31-12-9999 it is #NUM!, text without a number #VALUE!.

Weekday. It is computed on the serial number, not the calendar: day 1 of the 1900 system is a Sunday in Excel, day 0 of the 1904 system a Friday. With w=(t'−1)mod7w = (t' - 1) \bmod 7 (0 = Sunday) and t'=tt' = t or t'=t+1462t' = t + 1462

WEEKDAY(t,a)={w+1a=1,17((w−1)mod7)+1a=2,11(w−1)mod7a=3((w−(a−10))mod7)+1a=12…16\text{WEEKDAY}(t, a) = \begin{cases} w + 1 & a = 1, 17 \\ ((w - 1) \bmod 7) + 1 & a = 2, 11 \\ (w - 1) \bmod 7 & a = 3 \\ ((w - (a - 10)) \bmod 7) + 1 & a = 12 \ldots 16 \end{cases}

any other type is #NUM!. WEEKDAY(DATE(2008,2,14)) is 5, with type 2 it is 4, with type 3 it is 3.

Months. EDATE moves by the truncated number of months and clamps at the month end (31-1-2011 plus one month is 28-2-2011); EOMONTH takes the month end. DATEDIF counts whole months

$$ M = 12\,(j_2 - j_1) + (m_2 - m_1) - [\,d_2 < d_1\,] , $$

hence "Y" =⌊M/12⌋= \lfloor M/12 \rfloor, "YM" =Mmod12= M \bmod 12; "D" is the distance in days, "MD" =d2−d1= d_2 - d_1, if negative plus the length of the month before the end – as in Excel this can stay negative (31-1 to 1-3-2011 gives −2). "YD" moves the start into the year of the end (or the one before, if it would otherwise lie after it) and counts the days. Start after end is #NUM!.

Working days. NETWORKDAYS counts the days Monday to Friday between start and end inclusive, without the given holidays (each at most once); if the end lies before the start, the number becomes negative. WORKDAY moves that many working days forward or back and skips weekends and holidays; with 0 it stays at the start, even on a Saturday. The examples of the Excel help: from 1-10-2012 to 1-3-2013 there are 110 working days, with three holidays 107; the 151st working day after 1-10-2008 is 30-4-2009, with three holidays 5-5-2009.

Weeks. WEEKNUM counts from the week containing 1 January; the week starts by type on Sunday (1, 17), Monday (2, 11) or Tuesday to Saturday (12 to 16):

WEEKNUM=⌊n−1+v7⌋+1,v=(w1.1.−b)mod7,\text{WEEKNUM} = \left\lfloor \frac{n - 1 + v}{7} \right\rfloor + 1 , \qquad v = (w_{1.1.} - b) \bmod 7 ,

with the day of the year nn, the weekday of 1 January w1.1.w_{1.1.} and the first weekday bb. Type 21 and ISOWEEKNUM give the ISO 8601 week (week 1 contains the first Thursday): 3-1-2021 is in week 53.

Year fraction and the 360-day year. DAYS360 computes

360(j2−j1)+30(m2−m1)+(d2−d1)360\,(j_2 - j_1) + 30\,(m_2 - m_1) + (d_2 - d_1)

with adjusted days: in the US method a start on the 31st or on the last day of February becomes the 30th, an end on the 31st becomes the 30th if the start is (then) a 30th – otherwise it stays and counts like the 1st of the next month; in the European method (third argument true) every 31st becomes the 30th. YEARFRAC divides by basis: 0 like US DAYS360, with the extra rule that an end on the last day of February becomes the 30th if the start is one too, divided by 360; 2 and 3 the days by 360 or 365; 4 European by 360; 1 the days by the year length – within the same year 365 or 366, within one year 366 if a 29 February lies in between, over several years the mean length of the calendar years touched. From 1-1 to 30-7-2012 this gives 0.580 555 6 (basis 0), 0.576 502 7 (basis 1) and 0.578 082 2 (basis 3), as in the Excel help.

All expected values of the probe are also recomputed with LibreOffice Calc; over 426 formulas in both systems all agree except at two places where LibreOffice differs from Excel: Excel's DAYS truncates the time of day (LibreOffice computes with fractions), and WEEKNUM with type 14 or 16 already starts week 1 on 31 December of some years in LibreOffice.

Formula precedence

Excel binds, from strong to weak: range :, sign, percent %, power ^, multiplication and division, addition and subtraction, concatenation &, comparisons. At two points this differs from school mathematics, and both are measured:

−22=(−2)2=4,232 is computed as (23)2=64 (left-associative).-2^2 = (-2)^2 = 4, \qquad 2^{3^2} \text{ is computed as } (2^3)^2 = 64 \text{ (left-associative)} .

Likewise 1+2⋅3=71 + 2 \cdot 3 = 7, 10%*2 =0.2= 0.2, "a"&1+1 == “a2” (compute first, then concatenate), and 1<2=TRUE is TRUE (comparisons left-associative). Setting the two deviations “right” gives different numbers than the workbook.

Comparing different types

Excel orders values of different kinds by a fixed rank: number < text < logical; text is compared case-insensitively. TRUE>1 is therefore true, and so is "a">99. An empty cell counts as 0 or “” in a comparison, depending on what it is compared with.

Shifting shared formulas

If a formula is the same in many cells, the workbook stores it once (t="shared") and gives the other cells only an identifier. For a cell at offset (Δs,Δz)(\Delta s, \Delta z) from the origin cell every reference is shifted unless fixed with $:

(s,z)↦(s+Δs,z+Δz),$s or $z stay.(s, z) \mapsto (s + \Delta s,\ z + \Delta z), \qquad \$s \text{ or } \$z \text{ stay} .

If a reference falls off the sheet (column below 1 or beyond XFD, row below 1 or beyond 1,048,576), it becomes #REF!, as in Excel. A word that looks like a reference but lies beyond XFD is a name and stays.

Theme colours and tint

A cell colour can refer to the workbook theme and carry a tint t∈[−1,1]t \in [-1, 1]. Excel computes it in the integer HLS space of Windows with 240 steps: the lightness LL becomes

$$ L' = \begin{cases} L\,(1 + t) & t < 0 \\ L\,(1 - t) + 240\,t & t \ge 0 \end{cases} $$

and is then truncated, not rounded. With floating point and 255 steps, seven of ten colours of the colour picker were off by one. Theme colours 0 to 3 are crossed: 0 is “light 1”, 1 is “dark 1”.

Comparison with the stored value

Every formula cell carries the value the writing program last computed. The comparison puts the recomputed value aa next to the stored one bb and calls them equal if

$$ |a - b| \le 10^{-12} \cdot \max(|a|, |b|) \quad \text{or} \quad |a - b| < 10^{-300} , $$

that is relatively, with an absolute bound only for the case that both are zero. Text must match character for character, logicals must be equal, errors by their code; an empty cell and an empty string count as equal. The bound lies above the rounding Excel does anyway when saving to 15 significant digits, and below any difference that reveals a different computation. Numbers are also brought to 15 significant digits when concatenated.

What follows

  • Assumptions. The workbook was saved by Excel or by a program that computes like Excel; cell formats correctly state what is a date.
  • A date is a property of the format, not of the number: without a cell format 45,000 stays a number.
  • The date1904 switch applies to the whole workbook; reader and evaluator both follow it.
  • What the evaluator cannot compute (other workbooks, 3-D references, structured references, data tables, unknown functions) it reports with a reason instead of inventing a number.

How it is tested

  • Xlsxprobe against a workbook from openpyxl and generated workbooks; Mappenprobe, Formelzerlegerprobe, Auswerterprobe against a workbook computed by LibreOffice (98 formulas, one known difference at the leap day).
  • xlsx.formel.TheorieFDprobe tests the round trip of column names for all 16,384 columns and the limit, both date systems in the reader and in the formulas, and precedence; since 2026-10-25 also the eleven date functions with 67 formulas and 19 error paths in both systems.
  • xlsx.formel.TheorieG2probe computes every built-in function with its normal and error path after the Excel documentation (429 checks), plus names, a second sheet, array arithmetic, R1C1 references and broken workbook parts. Corrected on the way: an error text as criterion (COUNTIF(B,"#N/A")) matches the error cell, TEXT(5,"0.") gives 5., and -{1,"a"} gives #VALUE! at the text position.

Limits

  • Five points at which LibreOffice computes differently from Excel are tested by hand with a caveat (leap day, TRUE&"", TRUE>1, exponent notation, OFFSET with negative height).
  • The time of day is rounded to whole seconds.
  • Cell references beyond XFD are rejected, not truncated.
  • Functions the evaluator does not know yield #NAME? and are reported as not computed in the comparison. TEXT knows number formats only, no date format.
  • Before 1 March 1900, WEEKDAY, NETWORKDAYS, WORKDAY and WEEKNUM count the weekday by Excel's serial number (with 29 February 1900), ISOWEEKNUM, EDATE, DATEDIF and YEARFRAC by the real calendar; day 60 counts there as 1 March 1900. Holidays given as text yield #VALUE!, even if the text is a date; dates given as text are not interpreted.
  • A shared-string reference beyond the table appears in the reader as ?N – no text is invented. Since 2026-10-25 each sheet carries a finding with the number of affected cells and the first of them; before, this happened silently.
