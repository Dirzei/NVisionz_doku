CAD exchange: STL, STEP and IGES as a boundary representation – background

How three very different files turn into the same view: bodies, shells, faces, loops, edges and vertices with their geometry, all lengths in metres. What is computed on the way, namely units, axis placements and transformations, and what deliberately is not: no geometry kernel, no intersection, no meshing.

What it is about

A part usually leaves a design program in one of three formats. STL is a loose triangle mesh without units and without connectivity; STEP (ISO 10303-21 with the schemas AP203, AP214 and AP242) and IGES (version 5.3) describe the part as a boundary representation: a body is defined by its hull, the hull by faces, each face by a carrier (plane, cylinder, B-spline …) and by loops of edges that bound it. The readers bring all three into the same view (CadModell), without third-party libraries, read-only. Only what belongs to reading is computed.

The common view

The view separates two questions. Topology says what bounds what: a body has shells, a shell faces, a face loops, a loop oriented edges, an edge two vertices. Geometry says where it lies: the carrier of a face, the curve of an edge, the point of a vertex. Every edge and every vertex exists exactly once; loops refer to them with a direction. Only this makes it possible to check whether a hull is closed: for a body without holes and handles Euler's formula holds,

V−E+F=2,V - E + F = 2 ,(1)

and every edge occurs in exactly two loops, once forwards and once backwards. The probes check both on the generated cubes from STEP and IGES; both files give the same counts and the same vertices in metres.

All lengths and points are in metres, angles in radians; the file's factor stays available. Parameter-space curves, i.e. curves in the parameter space of a carrier, stay in the file's units: a parameter is not a length.

STL: from triangles to a mesh

An STL file stores each vertex as often as triangles meet there. The reader welds them exactly: equal coordinates give the same point; there is no tolerance, because it would merge points the writer meant to keep apart. The single exception is −0=+0-0 = +0: both are the same number but have different bit patterns, and a comparison of the bits would create the same vertex twice. Binary or text is decided, as in the table reader, by the size 84+50n84 + 50\,n, not by the word solid.

The text form is read strictly by its grammar (facet normal, outer loop, three vertex, endloop, endfacet); a deviation is rejected with its line number. Several solid blocks become groups. Degenerate triangles (two equal vertices) are counted and reported, not removed.

STEP: the exchange file of part 21

A STEP file is a list of numbered instances #n = TYPE(parameters); that form a graph through references #m. The parser knows all value kinds of the standard: numbers, strings with the escapes \X\, \X2\, \X4\ and '', references, enumerations .T., lists, typed values, binaries, $ (unset) and * (derived), and complex instances (A(...) B(...)) whose parts are found by type. Lists may be nested at most 64 levels deep; deeper counts as an attack, not as a part.

Units: SI with prefix and converted units

The length unit belongs to the context of the representation (GLOBAL_UNIT_ASSIGNED_CONTEXT). It is either an SI unit with prefix – SI_UNIT(.MILLI.,.METRE.) gives 10−310^{-3} – or a converted unit relating a factor to another unit:

f=c⋅fref,e.g. inch: f=25.4⋅10−3m.f = c \cdot f_{\text{ref}} , \qquad \text{e.g. inch: } f = 25.4 \cdot 10^{-3}\ \text{m}.(2)

The reference may itself be converted; after eight levels the reader stops. Angles work the same way with the radian as base unit; a cone with half angle 30 in a file with degrees as angle unit has π/6\pi/6.

Axis placements

Plane, cylinder, cone, sphere and torus lie in an AXIS2_PLACEMENT_3D of origin, axis zz and reference direction vv. Both directions are optional and need not be perpendicular. The standard (ISO 10303-42, build_axes) forms a right-handed basis from them:

z^=z‖z‖,x^=v−(v⋅z^)z^‖v−(v⋅z^)z^‖,y^=z^×x^.\hat z = \frac{z}{\lVert z\rVert}, \qquad \hat x = \frac{v - (v\cdot\hat z)\,\hat z}{\lVert v - (v\cdot\hat z)\,\hat z\rVert}, \qquad \hat y = \hat z \times \hat x .(3)

Without zz, (0,0,1)(0,0,1) applies; without vv or with vv parallel to zz, the standard takes (1,0,0)(1,0,0), and if that is parallel too, (0,0,1)(0,0,1). A direction of length zero is rejected. IGES places its surfaces (190 to 198) by point, axis and reference direction in the same way and takes the same path.

STEP: topology and orientation

A body is a MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP (with cavities: BREP_WITH_VOIDS), a surface model a SHELL_BASED_SURFACE_MODEL. Below follow CLOSED_SHELL/OPEN_SHELL, ADVANCED_FACE, FACE_OUTER_BOUND/FACE_BOUND, EDGE_LOOP or VERTEX_LOOP, ORIENTED_EDGE, EDGE_CURVE and VERTEX_POINT. An orientation appears in four places: on the shell, on the face (same_sense), on the bound and on the oriented edge. An ORIENTED_CLOSED_SHELL reverses the whole shell when its flag is false – a cavity is exactly such a reversed shell. Orientations are combined, not overwritten: reversed twice is forward again.

Face names come from the face itself or, if it is empty, from a STYLED_ITEM pointing at it. What the reader does not know – a polygon loop, an offset surface, a faceted body – is reported with instance number and type in nichtUnterstuetzt() and inserted as Unbekannt, not dropped.

B-splines: knots from multiplicities

STEP does not write a knot vector out but as distinct values with their multiplicities. For a curve of degree pp with nn control points,

∑imi=n+p+1\sum_i m_i = n + p + 1(4)

must hold, otherwise knots and control points do not fit; the reader rejects instead of guessing. The degree must be at least 1 and at most n−1n-1, the knots must increase and rational weights must be positive. A rational surface appears in STEP as a complex instance with the weights in a separate part; IGES (type 128) writes the weights before the control points, both with the first index running fastest.

IGES: fixed format and Hollerith strings

An IGES file has lines of 80 columns; column 73 names the section (start, global, directory, parameter, terminate), columns 74 to 80 the sequence number. Each entity has two directory lines of nine fields of eight columns and a parameter record whose lines point back to their directory line in columns 66 to 72. The first two global fields define the delimiters; strings are Hollerith strings nHtext with their length in front. A string may contain the delimiter and extend over the line end; it is therefore read by its length, never up to the next delimiter.

The unit is a flag in global field 14 (1 inch, 2 millimetre, 4 foot, 6 metre, 10 centimetre …); with flag 3, the name in field 15 decides. A name the reader does not know gives the factor 1 and a finding. The scale from field 13 is reported, not applied.

IGES: transformations

Every entity may refer to a transformation matrix (type 124), and that one to another. A 124 is x'=Rx+Tx' = R\,x + T; chaining with the parent (R2,T2)(R_2, T_2) gives

R=R2R1,T=R2T1+T2.R = R_2 R_1, \qquad T = R_2 T_1 + T_2 .(5)

Points are mapped with RR and TT, directions with RR only. The translation TT is a length and is converted to metres, the rotation is not. After eight chained matrices the reader stops. Parameter-space curves stay in parameter space; they are neither transformed nor converted.

IGES: two routes to a boundary representation

IGES knows the boundary representation as body 186 with shells 514, faces 510, loops 508, edge lists 504 and vertex lists 502 – edges and vertices are addressed there by list and index. The older and more common route is trimmed surfaces (144): a carrier with one outer and any number of inner boundaries, each a curve on the surface (142) with model-space and parameter-space curve. They are read as faces without shared edges and collected into a surface model; a 144 without outer boundary is bounded by the natural boundary of its carrier. If both routes are in one file, the body counts and the trimmed surfaces are reported.

How it is tested

  • Stlnetzprobe: binary with solid in the header, text against binary, several blocks, welding with −0-0, rejections with line number, malicious variations.
  • Stepbrepprobe: part 21 with all value kinds, cube by (1), units in five forms, cylinder with B-spline mantle and seam, all surface kinds, cavity, rejections and findings, malicious variations.
  • Igesbrepprobe: fixed format with own delimiters and an overlong Hollerith string, cube as with STEP, units per flag, trimmed surfaces with chained transformations, free shells, rejections and malicious variations.
  • The test files are written by two writers of our own under test/, built from the standards independently of the readers.

Limits

  • No geometry kernel: surfaces are not evaluated, intersected, meshed or checked for watertightness. The view describes what is in the file.
  • STEP: assemblies, product structure, colours other than the name, tolerances and faceted or CSG bodies are reported, not read. Only the first representation determines the unit of the view; if others differ, that is a finding.
  • IGES: the compressed and the binary format, conics (104), ruled surfaces (118), tabulated cylinders (122) and offset surfaces are reported, not read. The scale (global field 13) is not applied.
  • STL: no unit; the factor is 1.
