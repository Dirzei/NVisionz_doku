FITS – background

Blocks of 2880 bytes, scaling via BSCALE and BZERO, the Rice method for compressed tiles and world coordinates with the gnomonic projection.

What this is about: measured values with a place in the sky

FITS stores arrays of measured values with a readable header of 80-character cards. The rules date from the time of magnetic tapes and have only been extended since. Three things make reading more demanding than the simple structure suggests: the numbers are not stored as they are meant (scaling), large images are compressed tile by tile, and every pixel has a place through the world coordinates.

Blocks of 2880 bytes

Header and data are each padded to a multiple of 2880=36⋅802880 = 36 \cdot 80 bytes (36 cards per block; 2880 is also divisible by 8, 16, 32 and 64 bits). A data part of dd bytes occupies

d'=2880⌈d2880⌉ bytes.d' = 2880 \left\lceil \frac{d}{2880} \right\rceil \text{ bytes.}(1)

Three values of 16 bits therefore occupy a whole block; the next unit begins after it.

BSCALE and BZERO

The physical value is w=BZERO⁡+BSCALE⁡⋅rw = \mathrm{BZERO} + \mathrm{BSCALE} \cdot r with the raw value rr. FITS knows no unsigned integers; the usual detour is BZERO⁡=215\mathrm{BZERO} = 2^{15} with BITPIX 16:

w=r+32768,r∈[−32768,32767]⇒w∈[0,65535]w = r + 32768, \quad r \in [-32768, 32767] \;\Rightarrow\; w \in [0, 65535](2)

Without the scaling the bright half of an image would become negative.

Compressed tiles: Rice

Compressed images lie tile by tile in a binary table. The Rice method writes the first value uncompressed and then differences to the predecessor, computed in the width of the pixel. Each difference dd is folded so that small magnitudes become small numbers:

k={2d,d≥0−2d−1,d<0d={k/2,k even−(k+1)/2,k oddk = \begin{cases} 2d, & d \ge 0 \\ -2d - 1, & d \lt 0 \end{cases} \qquad d = \begin{cases} k/2, & k \text{ even} \\ -(k+1)/2, & k \text{ odd} \end{cases}(3)

Per block (usually 32 values) a split parameter ss is chosen and written as s+1s+1 in 3, 4 or 5 bits (1, 2, 4 bytes per pixel). A value kk then stands as ⌊k/2s⌋\lfloor k/2^s \rfloor zeros, a one and the lower ss bits. Two special cases: s+1=0s+1 = 0 means "all differences zero" (a flat area, no further bit), the highest value means "values stand unsplit in 8, 16 or 32 bits". The length of a word is

ℓ(k)=⌊k2s⌋+1+s,\ell(k) = \left\lfloor \frac{k}{2^s} \right\rfloor + 1 + s,(4)

minimal at 2s≈kln⁡22^s \approx \bar k \ln 2 – the same relation as for Golomb-Rice in the audio encoder.

World coordinates

The pixel numbers are one-based in the file. The intermediate coordinates (in the unit of CUNIT, usually degrees) are

xi=∑jMij(pj−CRPIXj⁡)x_i = \sum_j M_{ij}\,(p_j - \mathrm{CRPIX}_j)(5)

with the matrix MM from one of three mutually exclusive notations: M=CD⁡M = \mathrm{CD}; or Mij=CDELTi⁡PCij⁡M_{ij} = \mathrm{CDELT}_i\,\mathrm{PC}_{ij}; or, old, a rotation by CROTA2:

M=(Δ1cos⁡ρ−Δ2sin⁡ρΔ1sin⁡ρΔ2cos⁡ρ)M = \begin{pmatrix} \Delta_1 \cos\rho & -\Delta_2 \sin\rho \\ \Delta_1 \sin\rho & \Delta_2 \cos\rho \end{pmatrix}(6)

For linear axes the world value is CRVALi⁡+xi\mathrm{CRVAL}_i + x_i; all three notations must deliver the same for the same mapping.

Gnomonic projection (TAN). From (x,y)(x, y) follow the native spherical coordinates φ=arctan⁡2(x,−y)\varphi = \arctan2(x, -y), θ=arctan⁡(180°/(πr))\theta = \arctan\big(180°/(\pi r)\big) with r=x2+y2r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} in degrees; then a spherical rotation turns the pole onto the reference point (α0,δ0)(\alpha_0, \delta_0). Together this gives the well-known tangent plane: with ξ=x\xi = x, η=y\eta = y in radians

α=α0+arctan⁡2(ξ,cos⁡δ0−ηsin⁡δ0),δ=arctan⁡2(sin⁡δ0+ηcos⁡δ0,ξ2+(cos⁡δ0−ηsin⁡δ0)2)\alpha = \alpha_0 + \arctan2\!\big(\xi,\ \cos\delta_0 - \eta\sin\delta_0\big), \qquad \delta = \arctan2\!\Big(\sin\delta_0 + \eta\cos\delta_0,\ \sqrt{\xi^2 + (\cos\delta_0 - \eta\sin\delta_0)^2}\Big)(7)

Plate carrée (CAR). Here the intermediate coordinates already are the native spherical coordinates, φ=x\varphi = x, θ=y\theta = y, and the reference point does not lie at the native pole but at (φ0,θ0)=(0,0)(\varphi_0, \theta_0) = (0, 0). CAR too is a rotation of the sphere, only with a different pole: from φp\varphi_p (LONPOLE, default 0 for δ0≥θ0\delta_0 \ge \theta_0, else 180°) follows the latitude of the celestial pole

δp=arctan⁡2(sin⁡θ0,cos⁡θ0cos⁡(φp−φ0))±arccos⁡sin⁡δ01−cos2⁡θ0sin2⁡(φp−φ0),\delta_p = \arctan2\big(\sin\theta_0,\ \cos\theta_0\cos(\varphi_p - \varphi_0)\big) \pm \arccos\frac{\sin\delta_0}{\sqrt{1 - \cos^2\theta_0\,\sin^2(\varphi_p - \varphi_0)}} ,(8)

of the solutions with |δp|≤90°|\delta_p| \le 90° the one closer to LATPOLE (default +90°), and its longitude

αp=α0−arctan⁡2(sin⁡(φp−φ0)cos⁡θ0cos⁡δ0,sin⁡θ0−sin⁡δpsin⁡δ0cos⁡δpcos⁡δ0),\alpha_p = \alpha_0 - \arctan2\!\Big(\frac{\sin(\varphi_p - \varphi_0)\cos\theta_0}{\cos\delta_0},\ \frac{\sin\theta_0 - \sin\delta_p\sin\delta_0}{\cos\delta_p\cos\delta_0}\Big) ,

for δp=±90°\delta_p = \pm 90° simply αp=α0+φp−φ0−180°\alpha_p = \alpha_0 + \varphi_p - \varphi_0 - 180° or α0−φp+φ0\alpha_0 - \varphi_p + \varphi_0. Then the same rotation as for TAN and SIN applies:

δ=arcsin⁡(sin⁡θsin⁡δp+cos⁡θcos⁡δpcos⁡(φ−φp)),α=αp+arctan⁡2(−cos⁡θsin⁡(φ−φp),sin⁡θcos⁡δp−cos⁡θsin⁡δpcos⁡(φ−φp)).\delta = \arcsin\big(\sin\theta\sin\delta_p + \cos\theta\cos\delta_p\cos(\varphi - \varphi_p)\big), \quad \alpha = \alpha_p + \arctan2\big({-\cos\theta\sin(\varphi - \varphi_p)},\ \sin\theta\cos\delta_p - \cos\theta\sin\delta_p\cos(\varphi - \varphi_p)\big) .(9)

For δ0=0\delta_0 = 0, δp=90°\delta_p = 90°, αp=α0−180°\alpha_p = \alpha_0 - 180°, and the whole reduces to the addition α=α0+x\alpha = \alpha_0 + x, δ=y\delta = y. Until 2026-10-25 exactly this addition was computed for every δ0\delta_0; at δ0=40°\delta_0 = 40° a point eight degrees in longitude and twelve in latitude from the reference point was thereby off by 0.84° in longitude and 0.40° in latitude. If the equation has no solution (for instance LONPOLE 180° at δ0=40°\delta_0 = 40°), there is no coordinate but NaN.

What follows

  • A raw value without its scaling is not a measurement. The reader delivers the physical value and reports the scaling.
  • The Rice method is lossless for integers; compressed floating-point images are quantized before compression and are then only exact up to the quantization.
  • An error of one whole pixel (zero- instead of one-based) cannot be seen in a celestial coordinate; only the reference point CRPIX⁡↦CRVAL⁡\mathrm{CRPIX} \mapsto \mathrm{CRVAL} shows it.

How it is checked

fits.TheorieFCprobe writes FITS files with the test writer, recomputes the scaling, compresses values with its own Rice encoder according to the description (all three widths, all special cases) and compares the world coordinates with the closed formula of the tangent plane at 100 points; CAR is checked at seven headers with δ0\delta_0 from −50° to 75°, with LONPOLE and LATPOLE, against astropy (wcslib). The foreign anchors from astropy stand in Fitsprobe.

Limits

  • The projections TAN, SIN and CAR are computed; others are named, the linear axes are computed anyway.
  • For CAR with δ0=±90°\delta_0 = \pm 90° (reference point at the celestial pole) αp=α0\alpha_p = \alpha_0 is taken; this case is not checked against a foreign library.
  • Distortion terms (SIP, TPV) are not applied.
