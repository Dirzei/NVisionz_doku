Methods compared – what was tried on which case

Which methods were run side by side on which test case, with which result, and why one method is of little use on a case while another does better.

What this is about

For almost every sub-step the solver has several methods: gradients, limiters, fluxes, boundary forms, preconditioners, turbulence models. New methods are added as options and change nothing without their switch. Whether an option is better is only decided by a case on which it is run next to the default. This page summarises these comparisons. The details are on the page of each case in the section “Methods tried”. For cases without a page of their own they are below.

A verdict holds for the case on which it was measured: “unsuitable” means that on this case the method does not converge or lies further from the reference than the alternative. It does not mean that the method is wrong. Every method on this page has a probe that checks it against a known solution (see Methods and test cases).

Shock on a turbulent boundary layer at Mach 5 (Schülein)

A shock generator at 10 degrees deflects the flow in a channel at Mach 5. The shock meets the turbulent boundary layer of the base plate and separates it. Wall pressure and skin friction were measured. Separation begins at S = 334 ± 1 mm and reattachment is at R = 345 ± 1 mm, so the bubble is 11 mm long. The computation uses Spalart-Allmaras and Newton second order, on meshes of our own shock generator and on the mesh of the experiment (hexahedra with prisms at the tip of the generator).

Conclusions. From a first spacing of 0.17 mm the result is mesh-independent. Ahead of the shock cf lies about 14 % below the measurement, and the wall pressure behind it about 2.5 % below, equal to 1 to 2 % on all meshes. The too-short bubble is a property of the model. The quadratic constitutive relation (QCR) does not change it, because it hardly shifts the stresses in the plane shear layer of the boundary layer. SST and EARSM stalled in first order on the clustered mesh (residual fixed at 0.61 of the reference from step 49). The cause has not yet been investigated.

Why the mesh of the experiment does not converge. In the final state half of the square sum of the residual sits in three cells at the tip of the generator. There prisms meet hexahedra, the volume ratio of two neighbours is 826, and the deviation from orthogonality is 60.7 degrees. With the Venkatakrishnan limiter and K = 1 the solution oscillates there. First order converges on the same mesh in 125 steps. Which limiter helps at this location is currently being measured: Venkatakrishnan frozen, R3 after Nishikawa, Venkatakrishnan with K = 5, with the threshold after Wang, and at the nodes (MLP), 300 steps each. The results will follow.

Taylor-Couette in the rotating frame

Annular gap with a 10-degree sector. Inside, the wall rotates with the frame; outside, the casing is at rest. Second order with Green-Gauss and Newton. Measured is the error of the absolute circumferential velocity against the exact solution (RMS, relative to Ω r0), each converged to 1e-7 to 1e-8:

Mesh (circumferential × radial)no limiterVenkatakrishnan, K = 1
8 × 326.9e-49.9e-4
16 × 326.1e-49.3e-4
16 × 641.7e-42.4e-4

Verdict. The wall, the co-rotating no-slip wall, Coriolis and centrifugal force are right. The limiter costs about 50 % accuracy and needs more than 4000 steps to 1e-7. Without the limiter, 352 steps to 1e-6 suffice. A first comparison with unconverged runs had shown a tenfold error with the limiter. It measured the distance to convergence, not the limiter.

Why. The relative velocity w = c − ω × r contains the rigid-body rotation, which grows linearly with position in the duct. Every limiter that checks extrema against the neighbours cuts it at the edges of the pitch, and that becomes entropy. Here the absolute velocity is constant and is not cut. On Rotor 37 the option changes the efficiency only in the fourth digit (0.8562 against 0.8560). It is correct, but it is not the lever for the deficit there.

Why. The flow separates in the diffuser. Laminar, the separation oscillates and blocks the throat sometimes more and sometimes less. The turbulent viscosity keeps it steady. Whether the laminar computation would become equally steady beyond 0.05 s has not been computed.

Sod shock tube

One-dimensional, density against the exact solution (L1 error), 100 cells, computed side by side in one run:

MethodL1 errorVerdict
HLLC5.6e-3suitable
central after Kennedy and Gruber with pressure sensor (-fluss:zentral)5.9e-3, no new extremasuitable

Verdict. The central flux is built for large eddy simulation. At the shock its pressure sensor raises the damping, and there it is only 5 % behind HLLC. On the Taylor-Green vortex it damps the kinetic energy by 1.1 % too much, the Roe-type flux with low-Mach scaling by 12.3 %. That is why the central flux is the default for large eddy simulation and HLLC the default for shock computations.

Limits

  • Every verdict holds for the case and the setting on which it was measured. A method called “unsuitable” here may be the better one on another mesh. That applies, for example, to least squares, which is accurate on tetrahedra without offset at the wall.
  • The tables are transcribed by hand from the runs and are not re-measured when the solver changes. What a case computes today is shown on its page.
  • The run times come from different days and machines and are only comparable within one table.
