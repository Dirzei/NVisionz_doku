Boundary conditions – background

The mathematics behind the classes in envisionz.cfd.rand: characteristics and Riemann invariants, far field, inlet from total quantities, outlet with pressure, non-reflecting outlet after Rudy and Strickwerda, inviscid, no-slip and heat-flux walls, inflow with velocity or mass flux, translational and rotational periodic boundaries, sliding interface, mixing plane and radial equilibrium – with derivations, assumptions, checks and limits.

What this is about

A finite-volume solver needs the same flux at every boundary face as at an interior face: two states left and right and a flux scheme in between. The right state is supplied by the boundary condition as a ghost state Wg\mathbf{W}_g – from the state of the boundary cell Wi\mathbf{W}_i (primitive: ρ,u,v,w,p\rho, u, v, w, p), the outward normal n\mathbf{n}, the location and the time. Every class implements Randbedingung.geist for this. Whether the ghost counts as a face value (gradients at the boundary) or as a mirrored cell is stated by geistIstFlaechenwert.

The art lies in prescribing at the boundary exactly as much as the equations allow – not more (the problem would be over-determined and reflect), not less (it would be under-determined and drift). How much that is, the characteristics tell.

Characteristics and Riemann invariants

In the normal direction and in primitive variables the Euler equations read ∂tW+A∂nW=0\partial_t \mathbf{W} + A \, \partial_n \mathbf{W} = 0 with

A=(vnρ00vn1/ρ0γpvn),eigenvalues vn−c,vn,vn+cA = \begin{pmatrix} v_n & \rho & 0 \\ 0 & v_n & 1/\rho \\ 0 & \gamma p & v_n \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \text{eigenvalues } v_n - c, \ v_n, \ v_n + c

(plus twice vnv_n for the tangential velocity). Along the eigenvalues quantities travel unchanged: the entropy s=p/ργs = p/\rho^\gamma and the tangential velocity with vnv_n, and in a homentropic field the Riemann invariants

R±=vn±2cγ−1R^\pm = v_n \pm \frac{2 c}{\gamma - 1}

with vn±cv_n \pm c. Derivation: ∇R±⋅A=(vn±c)∇R±+k∇s\nabla R^\pm \cdot A = (v_n \pm c) \nabla R^\pm + k \nabla s – the remainder is a multiple of the entropy gradient and vanishes at constant entropy. With the outward normal, quantities with positive eigenvalue leave the domain (taken from inside), those with negative eigenvalue enter (prescribed). Hence the count:

Boundaryprescribedfrom inside
subsonic inlet (−c<vn<0-c < v_n < 0)4 (entropy, 2 tangents, R−R^-)1 (R+R^+)
subsonic outlet (0<vn<c0 < v_n < c)14

Throughout, γ\gamma stands for κ\kappa of the gas model, the exponent of the speed of sound – γ\gamma for an ideal gas.

Far field

Fernfeld knows a fixed state W∞\mathbf{W}_\infty. In supersonic flow all characteristics run one way. The decision uses the normal Mach numbers of both sides: if the far field enters supersonically normal to the boundary (vn,∞≤−c∞v_{n,\infty} \le -c_\infty) or the inside already does (vn,i≤−civ_{n,i} \le -c_i), the ghost is W∞\mathbf{W}_\infty; if the inside leaves supersonically (vn,i≥civ_{n,i} \ge c_i), it is the boundary cell. Deciding by the inside state alone is not enough: during start-up the inside is often still subsonic while a supersonic stream arrives from outside – then R+R^+ would come from inside although this characteristic also comes from outside. If the two sides contradict each other (inside leaving supersonically, outside entering supersonically: two streams run against each other with a compression region between them), the boundary state from the invariants below decides: vn≤−cv_n \le -c gives W∞\mathbf{W}_\infty, vn≥cv_n \ge c the boundary cell, in between the subsonic calculation applies. In subsonic flow the outgoing invariant comes from inside, the incoming one from outside,

R+=vn,i+2ciγ−1,R−=vn,∞−2c∞γ−1,vn=R++R−2,c=γ−14(R+−R−),R^+ = v_{n,i} + \frac{2 c_i}{\gamma - 1}, \qquad R^- = v_{n,\infty} - \frac{2 c_\infty}{\gamma - 1}, \qquad v_n = \frac{R^+ + R^-}{2}, \qquad c = \frac{\gamma - 1}{4} (R^+ - R^-),

and entropy and tangential velocity from the side the flow comes from. From cc and ss follow

ρ=(c2γs)1/(γ−1),p=ρc2γ.\rho = \left( \frac{c^2}{\gamma s} \right)^{1/(\gamma - 1)}, \qquad p = \frac{\rho c^2}{\gamma} .

Smooth transition. The choice “entropy from inside or outside” jumps at vn=0v_n = 0; at a far-field face grazed by the flow it then switches from step to step and stalls the Newton method. Therefore it is blended with ξ=vn/(0.1c)\xi = v_n / (0.1 \, c):

θ(ξ)=12+ξ(34−ξ24)for |ξ|<1,θ=0 below,1 above,\theta(\xi) = \frac12 + \xi \left( \frac34 - \frac{\xi^2}{4} \right) \ \text{for } |\xi| < 1, \qquad \theta = 0 \text{ below}, \ 1 \text{ above},

s=(1−θ)s∞+θsis = (1 - \theta) s_\infty + \theta s_i, likewise the tangent. θ\theta is continuously differentiable (θ'(±1)=0\theta'(\pm 1) = 0) and outside a band of 10 % of the speed of sound it is the hard choice.

In a rotating frame W∞\mathbf{W}_\infty refers to the absolute frame; ω×r\omega \times \mathbf{r} is subtracted from the far-field velocity before forming the invariants.

Inlet from total quantities

Einlass prescribes total pressure p0p_0, total temperature T0T_0 and the direction (without direction normal inward, −n-\mathbf{n}); the static pressure comes from inside, limited to p≤p0p \le p_0. Isentropically

M2=2γ−1((p0p)(γ−1)/γ−1),T=T01+γ−12M2,ρ=pRT,|v|=MγRT.M^2 = \frac{2}{\gamma - 1} \left( \left( \frac{p_0}{p} \right)^{(\gamma - 1)/\gamma} - 1 \right), \qquad T = \frac{T_0}{1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2}, \qquad \rho = \frac{p}{R T}, \qquad |\mathbf{v}| = M \sqrt{\gamma R T} .

Derivation. Energy: cpT0=cpT+|v|2/2c_p T_0 = c_p T + |\mathbf{v}|^2/2 with cp=γR/(γ−1)c_p = \gamma R/(\gamma - 1) gives the temperature; isentropically p0/p=(T0/T)γ/(γ−1)p_0/p = (T_0/T)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)} gives M2M^2. If the pressure inside exceeds p0p_0, M2M^2 would be negative; the limit turns this into rest (v=0\mathbf{v} = 0, ρ=p0/(RT0)\rho = p_0/(R T_0)).

What follows. Four quantities (total pressure, total temperature, two angles) are prescribed, one (the pressure, equivalently R+R^+) comes from inside – exactly the count of the subsonic inlet. The mass flow adjusts itself; this is why compressor maps are computed with total quantities at the inlet.

Supersonic inflow. If the flow enters supersonically normal to the boundary, no characteristic leaves any more and all five quantities must come from outside. Total pressure and total temperature then do not fix the state on their own – the Mach number is missing. The inlet knows the Mach number MM of the prescribed flow (in the case file that of the free stream) and uses it isentropically

T=T01+γ−12M2,p=p0(1+γ−12M2)−γ/(γ−1),v=MγRTe.T = \frac{T_0}{1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2}, \qquad p = p_0 \left(1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2\right)^{-\gamma/(\gamma - 1)}, \qquad \mathbf{v} = M \sqrt{\gamma R T}\, \mathbf{e} .

The prescription decides, as with the supersonic inlet of m-ACHIEVE, which receives total quantities and Mach number: supersonic applies when it is supersonic normal to the boundary (M(−e⋅n)≥1M\,(-\mathbf{e} \cdot \mathbf{n}) \ge 1); for an oblique supersonic prescription (M>1M > 1, Mn<1M_n < 1) also when the inside enters supersonically normal to the boundary. A subsonic prescription stays subsonic even if the inside is briefly supersonic during start-up. If the Mach number is missing and the inside enters supersonically normal to the boundary, the run stops with a message instead of silently forming a subsonic state with the pressure from inside.

Outlet with pressure

Auslass prescribes the static pressure pbp_b. Entropy, tangential velocity and the outgoing invariant come from inside:

ρb=ρi(pbpi)1/γ,cb=γpbρb,vn,b=vn,i+2γ−1(ci−cb).\rho_b = \rho_i \left( \frac{p_b}{p_i} \right)^{1/\gamma}, \qquad c_b = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma p_b}{\rho_b}}, \qquad v_{n,b} = v_{n,i} + \frac{2}{\gamma - 1} (c_i - c_b) .

Replacing only the pressure would be simpler – then only the pressure jumps at the boundary, density and velocity do not, and the boundary reflects more. In supersonic outflow everything comes from inside. With backflow (vn,i<0v_{n,i} < 0) the normal velocity of the ghost is set to zero: the outlet pumps nothing in; the tangent remains.

Non-reflecting outlet (Rudy-Strickwerda)

A fixed back pressure reflects every outgoing pressure wave. Rudy and Strickwerda (1980) replace the prescription by a relaxation of the incoming wave towards the back pressure. With the amplitude of the incoming characteristic L1=(vn−c)(∂np−ρc∂nvn)\mathcal{L}_1 = (v_n - c) (\partial_n p - \rho c \, \partial_n v_n) the condition reads

L1=K(p−paus⁡),K=σc(1−M2)L,\mathcal{L}_1 = K (p - p_\mathrm{aus}), \qquad K = \frac{\sigma c (1 - M^2)}{L},

with the domain length LL in the flow direction and σ≈0.25\sigma \approx 0.25.

Derivation of the discrete form. Acoustically the outgoing invariant comes from inside: dp+ρcdvn=0dp + \rho c \, dv_n = 0, so vn,b−vn,i=−(pb−pi)/(ρc)v_{n,b} - v_{n,i} = -(p_b - p_i)/(\rho c). One-sided over the distance dd from the cell centre to the face, ∂np−ρc∂nvn≈2(pb−pi)/d\partial_n p - \rho c \, \partial_n v_n \approx 2 (p_b - p_i)/d, and with vn−c=−c(1−M)v_n - c = -c (1 - M)

−c(1−M)2(pb−pi)d=σc(1−M2)L(pb−paus⁡)⇒pb=pi+βpaus⁡1+β,β=σ(1+M)d2L.-c (1 - M) \frac{2 (p_b - p_i)}{d} = \frac{\sigma c (1 - M^2)}{L} (p_b - p_\mathrm{aus}) \quad \Rightarrow \quad p_b = \frac{p_i + \beta p_\mathrm{aus}}{1 + \beta}, \qquad \beta = \frac{\sigma (1 + M) d}{2 L} .

From pbp_b the ghost follows as for the outlet (entropy and R+R^+ from inside). What follows: for uniform flow pb=paus⁡p_b = p_\mathrm{aus} exactly; σ=0\sigma = 0 lets the pressure drift freely, σ→∞\sigma \to \infty returns the fixed pressure. The distance dd per boundary face must be known; the solver sets it when binding (abstaende); without it the condition reports an error instead of silently computing something wrong.

Walls

Inviscid (Randbedingung.WAND): the normal velocity is mirrored, vg=vi−2vn,in\mathbf{v}_g = \mathbf{v}_i - 2 v_{n,i} \mathbf{n}, density and pressure come from inside. On average vn=0v_n = 0; the flux through the face then contains only the pressure.

No-slip (HaftendeWand): the ghost mirrors about the wall velocity and the wall temperature,

vg=2vw−vi,Tg=max⁡(2Tw−Ti,0.1Tw),pg=pi,ρg=piRTg,\mathbf{v}_g = 2 \mathbf{v}_w - \mathbf{v}_i, \qquad T_g = \max(2 T_w - T_i, \ 0.1 \, T_w), \qquad p_g = p_i, \qquad \rho_g = \frac{p_i}{R T_g},

so that the mean of cell and ghost at the face carries the wall values. The clamp at 0.1Tw0.1 \, T_w acts only if the cell is more than twice as hot as the wall – then the mirrored temperature would be negative. Adiabatic (TwT_w open) gives ρg=ρi\rho_g = \rho_i; zero heat flux comes from the gradient. With a prescribed heat flux (mitWaermestrom) the ghost stays adiabatic and the solver sets the heat flux of the face itself. If the wall is at rest in a rotating frame (casing), vw=−ω×r\mathbf{v}_w = -\omega \times \mathbf{r}.

Assumptions. Mirroring assumes that the cell centre is close to the wall and the profile in between is linear; it is second order as long as the boundary layer is resolved. With wall functions the shear stress is taken from the law of the wall, not from this gradient.

Inflow with velocity or mass flux

Zustrom is the inlet of pipe hydraulics: temperature TT and velocity v\mathbf{v} prescribed (or the mass flux density GG, then v=−(G/ρ)n\mathbf{v} = -(G/\rho) \mathbf{n}), the pressure comes from inside, and

ρ=piRT\rho = \frac{p_i}{R T}

with the gas constant of the inflow. Four quantities are prescribed and one (the pressure) comes from inside – subsonic. If a pressure is given and the inflow is supersonic (vn2≥γRTv_n^2 \ge \gamma R T), it is prescribed as well: all five quantities from outside. With mass flux, |v||\mathbf{v}| is then formed with the new density, so that ρ|v|=G\rho |\mathbf{v}| = G.

Periodic boundaries

Periodisch connects two boundaries A and B whose faces coincide under xB=RxA+t\mathbf{x}_B = R \, \mathbf{x}_A + \mathbf{t} – translated (R=IR = I, channel, unwrapped blade row) or rotated about an axis through the origin (sector of a turbomachine, t=0\mathbf{t} = 0). The ghost is the state of the partner cell with the velocity rotated into the own frame; the solver treats the face like an interior face, with the mapped partner centre for the gradients.

Derivation of conservation. The Euler flux is rotation-invariant: F(RW,Rn)=(m˙,RI,H˙)\mathbf{F}(R \mathbf{W}, R \mathbf{n}) = (\dot m, \ R \mathbf{I}, \ \dot H) with mass flow m˙\dot m, momentum flux I\mathbf{I} and enthalpy flow H˙\dot H of the unrotated pair. The flux at B is thus exactly the negative, rotated flux at A – mass and energy are conserved exactly, momentum in the rotated sense (angular momentum about the axis).

Assumptions. The meshes at A and B must match (face centre, size, opposite normal after the mapping); otherwise set-up stops at the first face without partner or with non-matching size or normal. A ghost requested before binding to the field or at a foreign location reports this as well. Turbulence quantities, mass fractions and transition quantities take the value of the partner cell at a periodic boundary, like the flow.

Mixing plane

Between a stationary and a rotating blade row neither the meshes nor the frames match. The Mischungsebene after Denton gives each side as ghost the area-weighted mean of the primitive variables of the cells on the other side – mixed out circumferentially, optionally per band of a spanwise coordinate (radial in 3D). If B runs in a frame moving with velocity U\mathbf{U} relative to A, the velocity is converted: wB=wA−U\mathbf{w}_B = \mathbf{w}_A - \mathbf{U}.

Flux-conserving. The mean of the primitive variables is not the mixed-out state; mass, momentum and energy would pass only up to a remainder of the order of the non-uniformity. Optionally B therefore takes over the sum of the fluxes leaving A, distributed by area share and converted into its own frame. For a frame velocity in the plane (U⋅n=0\mathbf{U} \cdot \mathbf{n} = 0) with w'=w+U\mathbf{w}' = \mathbf{w} + \mathbf{U}

m˙'=m˙,I'=I+Um˙,H˙'=H˙+U⋅I+|U|22m˙.\dot m' = \dot m, \qquad \mathbf{I}' = \mathbf{I} + \mathbf{U} \dot m, \qquad \dot H' = \dot H + \mathbf{U} \cdot \mathbf{I} + \frac{|\mathbf{U}|^2}{2} \dot m .

Sliding interface with frozen frame

A stator row and a rotor row of an unwrapped cascade: the meshes meet at a face, but the rotor row computes in a frame moving with U\mathbf{U} in the circumferential direction. Unlike the mixing plane nothing is averaged – each face has exactly one partner cell as on a periodic boundary (with zero offset for the frozen position), only its state is converted into the own frame:

wA=wB+U,wB=wA−U,ρ,punchanged.\mathbf{w}_A = \mathbf{w}_B + \mathbf{U}, \qquad \mathbf{w}_B = \mathbf{w}_A - \mathbf{U}, \qquad \rho,\ p \ \text{unchanged}.

In conserved variables this is a linear map, ρw'=ρw+ρU\rho\mathbf{w}' = \rho\mathbf{w} + \rho\mathbf{U}, ρE'=ρE+ρw⋅U+12ρ|U|2\rho E' = \rho E + \rho\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{U} + \tfrac12\rho|\mathbf{U}|^2; its derivative enters the block-ILU matrix exactly.

If U\mathbf{U} lies in the face (U⋅n=0\mathbf{U}\cdot\mathbf{n} = 0), Roe, HLLC and AUSM+-up are invariant under this Galilean shift: the flux at B is the negative flux at A converted into the frame of B. Mass, momentum and absolute energy thus pass over exactly, without a separate flux take-over.

The rotor-stator interfaces of m-ACHIEVE (boundary types 121 to 124) are read this way: 122 (stator, outlet) belongs to 123 (rotor, inlet), 121 to 124; the block header carries isl_mesh (1 = moving) and vtrans, related to the stagnation speed of sound, so U=(0,vtransa0,0)\mathbf{U} = (0,\ v_\text{trans}\, a_0,\ 0). Nodes of moving and resting blocks are not merged, so that the interface stays a boundary.

Radial equilibrium

Behind a blade row with swirl the static pressure is not uniform: the centripetal force of the circumferential motion requires

dpdr=ρcθ2r.\frac{d p}{d r} = \frac{\rho c_\theta^2}{r} .

RadialesGleichgewicht divides the outlet into radial bands with equal numbers of faces, forms per band the area-weighted mean of ρcθ2\rho c_\theta^2 from the boundary cells (in a rotating frame with c=w+ω×r\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{w} + \omega \times \mathbf{r}) and integrates with the trapezoidal rule over the band radii. The pressure then depends on one number: at the innermost band (NABE) or in the area mean (MITTEL) it equals the prescribed value. Every boundary face receives the pressure of its radius (linear between band radii, constant outside) and from it the ghost as for the outlet. With a throttle KK the prescribed pressure is p+Km˙2p + K \dot m^2 with the mass flow through the boundary – a characteristic that intersects the compressor map also where it becomes flat.

Check anchor. For a free vortex cθ=Kw/rc_\theta = K_w/r at constant density p(r)=ph+ρKw22(1rh2−1r2)p(r) = p_h + \frac{\rho K_w^2}{2} \left( \frac{1}{r_h^2} - \frac{1}{r^2} \right); the trapezoidal rule meets this with an error of second order in the band width.

Assumptions. Radial velocity and streamline curvature in the meridional plane are neglected (simple radial equilibrium). In the preconditioner Jacobian the profile stays frozen.

How it is checked

New probe envisionz.cfd.rand.TheorieC2probe (ghost states by hand, under one second): far field in supersonic and subsonic flow with the invariants (TD22-C2-R1, R2), inlet with recovered total quantities, without direction and above total pressure (R3), outlet with entropy, R+R^+, backflow and supersonic flow (R4), no-slip wall with mean values and clamp (R5), non-reflecting outlet with β\beta and without binding (R6). In addition the existing probes of complete runs: Eulerprobe, Navierprobe, Auslassprobe (reflection), Zustromprobe, Zustromgeistprobe, Periodenprobe, Mischungsprobe, Turbomaschinenprobe, Uebernahmeprobe. Seven new counter-probes (td22c2-*) corrupt the supersonic choice, inlet direction, isentrope, pressure limit, backflow protection, wall clamp and the binding check; all are noticed. The probe envisionz.cfd.loeser.Befundprobe checks supersonic inflow with subsonic and oblique prescriptions (TD33-E1 to E3), the supersonic choice by inside and far state with streams running against each other (F1), non-matching periodic faces (P1), the mixing plane with one band (M1) and the partner values of mass fractions and turbulence quantities at periodic boundaries (Q1–Q3). The recalculation docs/theorie/rechnung/cfd_raender.py checks eigenvalues and invariants, the far-field and inlet formulas, the derivation of β\beta, the rotation invariance of the flux, radial equilibrium and the flux conversion of the mixing plane.

Limits

  • The invariants are one-dimensional in the normal direction; obliquely leaving waves are partly reflected at the far field and the outlet.
  • The non-reflecting outlet depends on σ\sigma and LL: too small and the mean pressure drifts; too large and it reflects like a fixed one.
  • The mixing plane averages primitive variables; without flux take-over conservation is only approximate. It suppresses all circumferential interaction between the rows (wakes, potential effects).
  • The radial equilibrium is the simple one, without meridional curvature.
  • The sliding interface is frozen: the relative position of the meshes stays fixed (frozen rotor), and the interaction of the rows depends on that position. Time-accurate sliding with a growing offset follows. The meshes must match at the interface; no inlets or outlets are allowed in the moving frame.
  • Without a Mach number the inlet cannot form supersonic inflow; it reports it instead. With a subsonic prescription it stays a subsonic inlet even if the inside is supersonic. For supersonic inflow with a state of its own there is fest.
  • If two supersonic streams run against each other at the far field, the boundary only carries the approximate state from the invariants, not the exact solution of the Riemann problem with shocks.
