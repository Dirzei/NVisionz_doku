Initial field for nozzles from stream-tube theory – background

The computation behind -anfang:quasi1d: the area distribution taken from the mesh, on it the one-dimensional nozzle flow with choked throat and normal shock – for the ideal gas with the relations of gas dynamics, for real gases with the equation of state –, and the direction of the velocity between the walls. With derivations, checks and limits.

What it is about

A nozzle computation usually starts from a uniform field, from gas at rest or from a linear transition between inlet and outlet state. Until the flow with choked throat, supersonic region and shock has formed from that, a time-accurate computation spends a long start-up that has nothing to do with the flow sought. Stream-tube theory provides this flow in good approximation beforehand: it needs only the area distribution A(x)A(x), the stagnation state at the inlet and the back pressure.

Stromfadenanfang determines A(x)A(x) from the mesh, solves the one-dimensional problem and gives every cell the density, velocity and pressure of its cross-section. This is an initial field, not a solution: boundary layer, separation and everything two-dimensional is established by the computation afterwards.

Cross-section from the mesh

The extent of the mesh along xx is divided into NN bands of equal width bb (N=2number of cellsN = 2\sqrt{\text{number of cells}}, at least 20, at most 400). The volume of every cell is distributed uniformly over its extent [xa,xe][x_a, x_e] onto the bands it covers:

Ai=1b∑cVc|[xa,xe]c∩bandi|xe−xa.A_i = \frac{1}{b} \sum_c V_c \, \frac{|[x_a, x_e]_c \cap \text{band}_i|}{x_e - x_a}.

The sum ∑iAib\sum_i A_i\, b is then exactly the volume of the mesh. A=dV/dxA = \mathrm{d}V/\mathrm{d}x is the cross-section in every kind of mesh: in a planar mesh height times layer thickness, in the wedge about the axis the circular sector, in three dimensions the area itself. Only ratios are needed. The two outermost bands on each side take the cross-section of the third: inlet and outlet faces are not always perpendicular to xx, and a band that is only partly filled would feign a contraction.

Stream tube with an ideal gas

Along the nozzle the mass flow, the stagnation enthalpy and – except across the shock – the entropy are fixed. For the ideal gas with constant γ\gamma this gives the area–Mach relation

AA*=1M(2γ+1(1+γ−12M2))γ+12(γ−1),pp0=(1+γ−12M2)−γγ−1,\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{M} \left( \frac{2}{\gamma + 1} \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2 \right) \right)^{\frac{\gamma + 1}{2(\gamma - 1)}}, \qquad \frac{p}{p_0} = \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2 \right)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}},

with two branches per area ratio: subsonic and supersonic. The narrowest cross-section is the throat. Depending on the back pressure pap_a there are three cases:

Back pressureFlow
above the pressure of the subsonic branch at the exit with a choked throatsubsonic everywhere; A*A^* follows from the exit and lies below the throat
below that, but above the pressure behind a shock in the exitthroat choked, supersonic behind it up to the normal shock, then subsonic
lower still, or no back pressure (supersonic exit)supersonic up to the exit

Across the shock at Mach number M1M_1 the stagnation pressure falls to p02p_{02}, and because the mass flow stays, the critical cross-section grows:

M22=1+γ−12M12γM12−γ−12,A2*=A*p01p02.M_2^2 = \frac{1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M_1^2}{\gamma M_1^2 - \frac{\gamma - 1}{2}}, \qquad A^*_2 = A^* \, \frac{p_{01}}{p_{02}}.

Behind it the subsonic branch with A2*A^*_2 applies. The pressure at the exit falls the further downstream the shock stands; its position therefore follows from the back pressure by bisection between throat and exit. The stagnation temperature is preserved across the shock: T=T0/(1+γ−12M2)T = T_0 / (1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2), ρ=p/(RT)\rho = p / (R T), u=MγRTu = M \sqrt{\gamma R T}.

Worked example. Nozzle with A(x)=1+2.2(x−1.5)2A(x) = 1 + 2.2\,(x - 1.5)^2 on 0≤x≤30 \le x \le 3, γ=1.4\gamma = 1.4. A shock at x=2.1x = 2.1 has M1=2.0712M_1 = 2.0712, M2=0.5657M_2 = 0.5657 and p02/p01=0.6876p_{02}/p_{01} = 0.6876; at the exit pa/p0=0.677854p_a / p_0 = 0.677854 follows. From this back pressure the bisection finds the shock at 2.10000002.1000000.

Stream tube with a real gas

For a real gas there are no closed relations; the balances stay the same. Realgasstromfaden evaluates them with the equation of state:

Isentrope. At constant entropy dh=dp/ρ\mathrm{d}h = \mathrm{d}p / \rho. From the stagnation state (p0,T0)(p_0, T_0) downwards this is integrated with the trapezoidal rule over 400 geometrically spaced pressures; the temperature for (p,h)(p, h) comes from a Newton iteration on the equation of state. The velocity follows from the stagnation enthalpy, u=2(h0−h)u = \sqrt{2 (h_0 - h)}.

Choked throat. The mass flux G=ρuG = \rho u is largest along the isentrope at a pressure p*p^* – this is the critical state. For every cross-section GA=G*A*G A = G^* A^* holds, above p*p^* on the subsonic and below on the supersonic branch. Between two steps the blend is linear in G2G^2: near rest G2=2ρ2(h0−h)G^2 = 2 \rho^2 (h_0 - h) is linear in the pressure, while GG itself behaves like the square root.

Shock. The balances of mass, momentum and energy

ρ1u1=ρ2u2=m,p1+mu1=p2+mu2,h1+12u12=h2+12u22=h0\rho_1 u_1 = \rho_2 u_2 = m, \qquad p_1 + m u_1 = p_2 + m u_2, \qquad h_1 + \tfrac12 u_1^2 = h_2 + \tfrac12 u_2^2 = h_0

leave one unknown: for an assumed density ρ2\rho_2 the values u2u_2, p2p_2 and h2h_2 follow, and the equation of state must return the same density for (p2,h2)(p_2, h_2). The root is found by bisection, with a bracket around the estimate of the ideal gas – the second root ρ2=ρ1\rho_2 = \rho_1 is the state ahead of the shock. Behind it a second isentrope runs upwards to the stagnation enthalpy; the subsonic branch behind the shock lies on it.

Worked example. The same nozzle with nitrogen at 100 mbar and 300 K – an ideal gas there: the shock stands at 2.100032.10003, the pressure ratio is 4.8384.838 as from the relations. With carbon dioxide at 50 bar and 400 K and a back pressure of 0.68p00.68\,p_0 it stands at 2.1092.109; the mass and momentum balances across the shock are met to rounding.

Direction between the walls

The velocity of the stream-tube solution is the one along the axis: mass flow divided by cross-section and density. If it pointed in the xx direction everywhere, it would hit every inclined wall – in supersonic flow the gas there expands or compresses by the full wall angle in the first step. Therefore the direction follows the walls. For each of the four sides (yy lower and upper, zz lower and upper) the outermost mesh node of each band is taken – a point lying exactly on the wall. The points of one side form a polyline, its slope at a point is the chord through the two neighbours. Between the walls the blend is linear:

vu=su+y−yuyo−yu(so−su),u=u1D,\frac{v}{u} = s_u + \frac{y - y_u}{y_o - y_u} \, (s_o - s_u), \qquad u = u_{1D},

with the wall positions yu,yoy_u, y_o and their slopes su,sos_u, s_o; likewise for ww in zz. With u=u1Du = u_{1D} the field satisfies continuity – ρuA\rho u A is the same along the nozzle. If u1Du_{1D} is taken as the magnitude along the inclined direction instead, the mass flow through a cross-section drops towards the ends of a widely opened nozzle.

Application

-anfang:quasi1d in the case file. The back pressure is -pa: if the case has an outlet with back pressure or a far field behind the nozzle; it must lie below the stagnation pressure. Without such a boundary (supersonic exit) no shock stands in the nozzle. With supersonic inflow the supersonic branch applies from the start (ideal gas only). Not with a gas mixture and not in the rotating frame.

Effect. At a Venturi nozzle with a perfect gas (16 384 cells, time-accurate explicit) the flow from the linear initial field is still starting up after the time 0.02: the Mach number stays below 0.9, the mass flow in the throat is still rising. From the stream tube the throat is choked from the first step, and the mass flow in the throat is fixed to ±2 % after a short settling. Steady with the Newton method, a Laval nozzle with a shock converges from the stream tube in about 150 to 200 steps, from rest not within 600 steps.

How it is checked

The expectations of the check are computed by hand from the relations, not taken from a run: the shock is placed at a position, the back pressure follows from it, and the solution must find the position again. The cross-section is checked on a distorted channel against the trapezoidal sum of its height (to 10−1110^{-11}), the field against Mach number, pressure, wall direction, stagnation temperature and the mass flow per column. For the real gas the balances that every gas satisfies apply, and the limiting case of the ideal gas.

Limits

  • The main flow must run along xx; the cross-section is dV/dx\mathrm{d}V/\mathrm{d}x.
  • No boundary layer, no separation, no oblique shock. The shock position of the computation differs from the one-dimensional one – at the nozzle of the check (first order, 120 × 8 cells) 1.95 against 2.1.
  • Outflow blocks attached behind the exit count as cross-section; the free jet in them is not modelled.
  • Where the Newton method converges from the uniform inflow in a few steps anyway (nozzle without shock), the initial field saves nothing and may cost steps.
  • Real gas: if the expansion leaves the range in which the equation of state returns a state (two-phase region), the set-up stops with the pressure at which the isentrope ends. Near rest the mass flow is accurate to about 1 % (first step of the table).
