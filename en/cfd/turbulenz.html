Turbulence models – background

The mathematics behind SpalartAllmaras, Turbulenz, TurbulenzSst, TurbulenzKe, Transition, Grobstruktur and Wandabstand: eddy viscosity after Boussinesq, Spalart-Allmaras with SA-neg, k-ω after Wilcox and SST after Menter, k-ε after Chien, γ-Reθ transition after Langtry-Menter, exact wall distance, wall functions after Spalding and large-eddy simulation with Smagorinsky and WALE – with derivations of the constants from the law of the wall, assumptions and limits.

What this is about

A turbulent flow averaged in time (RANS) leaves the Reynolds stresses −ρui'uj'-\rho \overline{u_i' u_j'} in the momentum equations. All models here close them with the eddy viscosity after Boussinesq,

−ρui'uj'=μt(∂ui∂xj+∂uj∂xi−23∇⋅vδij)−23ρkδij,−ρcpuj'T'=cpμtPrt⁡∂T∂xj,-\rho \overline{u_i' u_j'} = \mu_t \left( \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x_i} - \frac{2}{3} \nabla \cdot \mathbf{v} \, \delta_{ij} \right) - \frac{2}{3} \rho k \, \delta_{ij}, \qquad -\rho c_p \overline{u_j' T'} = \frac{c_p \mu_t}{\mathrm{Pr}_t} \frac{\partial T}{\partial x_j} ,

that is μ→μ+μt\mu \to \mu + \mu_t in the viscous flux (viscous flow, without the part 23ρk\frac{2}{3}\rho k). The models differ in where μt\mu_t comes from: from one transported quantity (Spalart-Allmaras), from two (k-ω, k-ε) or, in large-eddy simulation, directly from the resolved velocity field.

The common touchstone is the logarithmic law of the wall. In the wall layer the shear stress is constant, τ=ρuτ2\tau = \rho u_\tau^2, and with the mixing length ℓ=κy\ell = \kappa y follow μt=ρκuτy\mu_t = \rho \kappa u_\tau y and

u+=1κln⁡y++B,u+=uuτ,y+=uτyν,κ=0.41,B=5.2.u^+ = \frac{1}{\kappa} \ln y^+ + B, \qquad u^+ = \frac{u}{u_\tau}, \quad y^+ = \frac{u_\tau y}{\nu}, \quad \kappa = 0.41, \quad B = 5.2 .

Every model must carry this solution; it implies relations between its constants, which the probes recompute.

Spalart-Allmaras

One transport equation for ν˜\tilde\nu, which away from the wall equals νt\nu_t (Spalart and Allmaras 1992/1994, version without trip term):

∂(ρν˜)∂t+∇⋅(ρν˜v)=cb1S˜ρν˜−cw1fwρ(ν˜d)2+1σ[∇⋅((μ+ρν˜)∇ν˜)+cb2ρ|∇ν˜|2].\frac{\partial (\rho \tilde\nu)}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \tilde\nu \mathbf{v}) = c_{b1} \tilde S \rho \tilde\nu - c_{w1} f_w \rho \left( \frac{\tilde\nu}{d} \right)^2 + \frac{1}{\sigma} \left[ \nabla \cdot \big( (\mu + \rho \tilde\nu) \nabla \tilde\nu \big) + c_{b2} \rho \, |\nabla \tilde\nu|^2 \right] .

The eddy viscosity is damped by fv1f_{v1} in the sublayer:

μt=ρν˜fv1,fv1=χ3χ3+cv13,χ=ν˜ν,S˜=Ω+ν˜κ2d2fv2,fv2=1−χ1+χfv1.\mu_t = \rho \tilde\nu f_{v1}, \qquad f_{v1} = \frac{\chi^3}{\chi^3 + c_{v1}^3}, \quad \chi = \frac{\tilde\nu}{\nu}, \qquad \tilde S = \Omega + \frac{\tilde\nu}{\kappa^2 d^2} f_{v2}, \quad f_{v2} = 1 - \frac{\chi}{1 + \chi f_{v1}} .

Ω\Omega is the vorticity magnitude, dd the wall distance. To keep S˜\tilde S from becoming negative (for \(f_{v2} < 0\)), for \(\bar S = \tilde\nu f_{v2}/(\kappa^2 d^2) < -c_{v2}\Omega\) the continuation after Allmaras, Johnson and Spalart (2012) applies,

S˜=Ω+Ω(cv22Ω+cv3S)(cv3−2cv2)Ω−S,cv2=0.7,cv3=0.9.\tilde S = \Omega + \frac{\Omega \left( c_{v2}^2 \Omega + c_{v3} \bar S \right)}{(c_{v3} - 2 c_{v2}) \Omega - \bar S}, \qquad c_{v2} = 0.7, \ c_{v3} = 0.9 .

Destruction carries fw(r)f_w(r) with r=min⁡(ν˜/(S˜κ2d2),10)r = \min\!\big(\tilde\nu/(\tilde S \kappa^2 d^2), 10\big):

g=r+cw2(r6−r),fw=g(1+cw36g6+cw36)1/6.g = r + c_{w2}(r^6 - r), \qquad f_w = g \left( \frac{1 + c_{w3}^6}{g^6 + c_{w3}^6} \right)^{1/6} .

Derivation of cw1c_{w1}. In the logarithmic layer χ\chi is large, so fv1→1f_{v1} \to 1, ν˜=νt=κuτy\tilde\nu = \nu_t = \kappa u_\tau y, Ω=∂u/∂y=uτ/(κy)\Omega = \partial u/\partial y = u_\tau/(\kappa y) and S˜≈Ω\tilde S \approx \Omega. Then r=κuτy/(Ωκ2y2)=1r = \kappa u_\tau y/(\Omega \kappa^2 y^2) = 1, g=1g = 1, fw=1f_w = 1. The terms per density: production cb1Ων˜=cb1uτ2c_{b1} \Omega \tilde\nu = c_{b1} u_\tau^2, destruction cw1κ2uτ2c_{w1}\kappa^2 u_\tau^2, diffusion 1σ[∂y(ν˜∂yν˜)+cb2(∂yν˜)2]=1+cb2σκ2uτ2\frac{1}{\sigma}[\partial_y(\tilde\nu \partial_y \tilde\nu) + c_{b2}(\partial_y \tilde\nu)^2] = \frac{1 + c_{b2}}{\sigma} \kappa^2 u_\tau^2. Their sum vanishes exactly for

cw1=cb1κ2+1+cb2σ≈3.239,cb1=0.1355,cb2=0.622,σ=23.c_{w1} = \frac{c_{b1}}{\kappa^2} + \frac{1 + c_{b2}}{\sigma} \approx 3.239, \qquad c_{b1} = 0.1355, \ c_{b2} = 0.622, \ \sigma = \tfrac{2}{3} .

SA-neg. A negative ν˜\tilde\nu arises numerically at boundary-layer edges. After Allmaras, Johnson and Spalart then μt=0\mu_t = 0, production cb1(1−ct3)Ωρν˜c_{b1}(1 - c_{t3})\Omega\rho\tilde\nu and destruction −cw1ρ(ν˜/d)2-c_{w1}\rho(\tilde\nu/d)^2 with ct3=1.2c_{t3} = 1.2 – both lift ν˜\tilde\nu toward zero –, and diffusion carries μ+ρν˜fn\mu + \rho\tilde\nu f_n, fn=(cn1+χ3)/(cn1−χ3)f_n = (c_{n1} + \chi^3)/(c_{n1} - \chi^3), cn1=16c_{n1} = 16, so that the coefficient stays positive.

What follows. Spalart-Allmaras is cheap (one equation) and robust for attached and mildly separated boundary layers in external aerodynamics. Because cw1c_{w1} follows from κ\kappa, it meets the law of the wall without a wall function when the first cell lies at y+≤1y^+ \le 1. Free shear layers and jets it reproduces worse than a two-equation model.

k-ω after Wilcox and SST after Menter

Two equations for the turbulent kinetic energy kk and the specific dissipation ω\omega:

∂(ρk)∂t+∇⋅(ρkv)=Pk−β*ρωk+∇⋅((μ+σkμt)∇k),\frac{\partial (\rho k)}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho k \mathbf{v}) = P_k - \beta^* \rho \omega k + \nabla \cdot \big( (\mu + \sigma_k \mu_t) \nabla k \big),

∂(ρω)∂t+∇⋅(ρωv)=γρμtPk−βρω2+∇⋅((μ+σωμt)∇ω)+2(1−F1)ρσω2ω∇k⋅∇ω.\frac{\partial (\rho \omega)}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \omega \mathbf{v}) = \frac{\gamma \rho}{\mu_t} P_k - \beta \rho \omega^2 + \nabla \cdot \big( (\mu + \sigma_\omega \mu_t) \nabla \omega \big) + 2 (1 - F_1) \frac{\rho \sigma_{\omega 2}}{\omega} \nabla k \cdot \nabla \omega .

Menter's SST blends the constants ϕ=F1ϕ1+(1−F1)ϕ2\phi = F_1 \phi_1 + (1 - F_1) \phi_2 between the inner set (Wilcox, σk1=0.85\sigma_{k1} = 0.85, σω1=0.5\sigma_{\omega 1} = 0.5, β1=0.075\beta_1 = 0.075) and the outer one (k-ε rewritten, σk2=1\sigma_{k2} = 1, σω2=0.856\sigma_{\omega 2} = 0.856, β2=0.0828\beta_2 = 0.0828); β*=0.09\beta^* = 0.09. The blending function

F1=tanh⁡(arg14⁡),arg1⁡=min⁡[max⁡(kβ*ωd,500νd2ω),4ρσω2kCDkω⁡d2],CDkω⁡=max⁡(2ρσω2ω∇k⋅∇ω,10−10)F_1 = \tanh(\arg_1^4), \quad \arg_1 = \min\!\left[ \max\!\left( \frac{\sqrt k}{\beta^* \omega d}, \frac{500 \nu}{d^2 \omega} \right), \frac{4 \rho \sigma_{\omega 2} k}{\mathrm{CD}_{k\omega} d^2} \right], \quad \mathrm{CD}_{k\omega} = \max\!\left( \frac{2 \rho \sigma_{\omega 2}}{\omega} \nabla k \cdot \nabla \omega, 10^{-10} \right)

is 1 at the wall and 0 at the boundary-layer edge: inside the accurate k-ω, outside the k-ε that is insensitive to the free stream. The eddy viscosity is limited by Bradshaw's assumption (shear equal to a1ρka_1 \rho k in the boundary layer):

μt=ρa1kmax⁡(a1ω,SF2),F2=tanh⁡(arg22⁡),arg2⁡=max⁡(2kβ*ωd,500νd2ω),a1=0.31.\mu_t = \frac{\rho a_1 k}{\max(a_1 \omega, S F_2)}, \qquad F_2 = \tanh(\arg_2^2), \quad \arg_2 = \max\!\left( \frac{2 \sqrt k}{\beta^* \omega d}, \frac{500 \nu}{d^2 \omega} \right), \quad a_1 = 0.31 .

Derivation of γ\gamma. In the law of the wall k=uτ2/β*k = u_\tau^2/\sqrt{\beta^*}, ω=uτ/(β*κy)\omega = u_\tau/(\sqrt{\beta^*}\kappa y), μt=ρk/ω\mu_t = \rho k/\omega, Pk=μt(∂u/∂y)2P_k = \mu_t (\partial u/\partial y)^2. The k equation is in equilibrium (Pk=β*ρωkP_k = \beta^*\rho\omega k); the ω equation demands γρ(∂u/∂y)2−βρω2+∂y(σωμt∂yω)=0\gamma \rho (\partial u/\partial y)^2 - \beta\rho\omega^2 + \partial_y(\sigma_\omega \mu_t \partial_y \omega) = 0. Substitution gives

γ=ββ*−σωκ2β*,\gamma = \frac{\beta}{\beta^*} - \frac{\sigma_\omega \kappa^2}{\sqrt{\beta^*}} ,

for the inner set 0.5530.553 (set to 5/95/9), for the outer 0.4400.440 (set to 0.440.44). The probe compares both.

Wall. At the wall k=0k = 0. For ω\omega the equation has the sublayer solution ω=6ν/(β1y2)\omega = 6\nu/(\beta_1 y^2) (substitution: νω''=36ν2/(β1y4)=β1ω2\nu\,\omega'' = 36\nu^2/(\beta_1 y^4) = \beta_1 \omega^2); because it becomes infinite at the wall, the model sets, after Menter, ten times the value at the distance of the first cell centre d1d_1 as boundary value: ωw=60ν/(β1d12)\omega_w = 60\nu/(\beta_1 d_1^2).

Wilcox (1988) is the same solver with F1=1F_1 = 1 everywhere, σk=σω=0.5\sigma_k = \sigma_\omega = 0.5 and without F2F_2 in the limiter: μt=ρa1k/max⁡(a1ω,S)\mu_t = \rho a_1 k/\max(a_1\omega, S).

What follows. SST is the workhorse for flows with adverse pressure gradient and separation; the limit on μt\mu_t keeps the shear stress small in regions of strong deceleration, where plain eddy-viscosity models overpredict it. Wilcox is sensitive to the free-stream value of ω\omega – exactly why SST blends to k-ε outside.

k-ε after Chien

For the dissipation ε\varepsilon Chien (1982) writes an equation for ε˜=ε−2νk/y2\tilde\varepsilon = \varepsilon - 2\nu k/y^2, which is zero at the wall:

D(ρk)Dt=Pk−ρε˜−2μky2+∇⋅((μ+μtσk)∇k),\frac{D(\rho k)}{Dt} = P_k - \rho\tilde\varepsilon - \frac{2\mu k}{y^2} + \nabla \cdot \left( \left( \mu + \frac{\mu_t}{\sigma_k} \right) \nabla k \right),

D(ρε˜)Dt=C1ε˜kPk−C2f2ρε˜2k−2με˜y2e−y+/2+∇⋅((μ+μtσε)∇ε˜),\frac{D(\rho\tilde\varepsilon)}{Dt} = C_1 \frac{\tilde\varepsilon}{k} P_k - C_2 f_2 \frac{\rho\tilde\varepsilon^2}{k} - \frac{2\mu\tilde\varepsilon}{y^2} e^{-y^+/2} + \nabla \cdot \left( \left( \mu + \frac{\mu_t}{\sigma_\varepsilon} \right) \nabla\tilde\varepsilon \right),

μt=Cμfμρk2ε˜,fμ=1−e−0.0115y+,f2=1−0.22e−(Ret⁡/6)2,Ret⁡=k2νε˜,\mu_t = C_\mu f_\mu \frac{\rho k^2}{\tilde\varepsilon}, \quad f_\mu = 1 - e^{-0.0115 y^+}, \quad f_2 = 1 - 0.22 \, e^{-(\mathrm{Re}_t/6)^2}, \quad \mathrm{Re}_t = \frac{k^2}{\nu\tilde\varepsilon},

with Cμ=0.09C_\mu = 0.09, C1=1.35C_1 = 1.35, C2=1.80C_2 = 1.80, σk=1\sigma_k = 1, σε=1.3\sigma_\varepsilon = 1.3. As above, the law of the wall demands C1=C2−κ2/(σεCμ)C_1 = C_2 - \kappa^2/(\sigma_\varepsilon \sqrt{C_\mu}), here 1.3691.369 against the set 1.351.35.

For time-accurate computations with dual time stepping the model keeps kk and ε˜\tilde\varepsilon of two earlier time levels for BDF2; the probe checks that at the transition level n−1n-1 becomes level n−2n-2.

What follows. k-ε is good for free shear layers and too optimistic under adverse pressure gradient (separation too late). Chien's wall damping, like the other models, needs a first cell at y+≈1y^+ \approx 1 – or the wall function.

γ-Reθ transition after Langtry-Menter

On top of SST the model adds two equations: the intermittency γ\gamma (0 laminar, 1 turbulent) and the transported transition Reynolds number Re⁡˜θt\tilde{\mathrm{Re}}_{\theta t}. The source of intermittency,

Pγ=Flength⁡ca1ρSγFonset⁡(1−ce1γ),Eγ=ca2ρΩγFturb⁡(ce2γ−1),P_\gamma = F_{\mathrm{length}} \, c_{a1} \rho S \sqrt{\gamma F_{\mathrm{onset}}} \, (1 - c_{e1}\gamma), \qquad E_\gamma = c_{a2} \rho \Omega \gamma F_{\mathrm{turb}} (c_{e2}\gamma - 1),

with ca1=2c_{a1} = 2, ce1=1c_{e1} = 1, ca2=0.06c_{a2} = 0.06, ce2=50c_{e2} = 50, sets in when the vorticity Reynolds number Rev⁡=ρy2S/μ\mathrm{Re}_v = \rho y^2 S/\mu reaches 2.193Reθc⁡2.193\,\mathrm{Re}_{\theta c} (Fonset⁡F_{\mathrm{onset}}). Where transition lies is told by the correlation of turbulence intensity TuTu (in percent) and pressure-gradient parameter λθ=θ2/νdU/ds\lambda_\theta = \theta^2/\nu \, dU/ds:

Reθt⁡={(1173.51−589.428Tu+0.2196Tu2)F(λθ),Tu≤1.3331.50(Tu−0.5658)−0.671F(λθ),Tu>1.3\mathrm{Re}_{\theta t} = \begin{cases} \left(1173.51 - 589.428\,Tu + \dfrac{0.2196}{Tu^2}\right) F(\lambda_\theta), & Tu \le 1.3 \\[1ex] 331.50\,(Tu - 0.5658)^{-0.671}\, F(\lambda_\theta), & Tu > 1.3 \end{cases}

with F=1+0.275(1−e−35λθ)e−Tu/0.5F = 1 + 0.275\,(1 - e^{-35\lambda_\theta})\, e^{-Tu/0.5} for favourable pressure gradient (λθ>0\lambda_\theta > 0) and a cubic polynomial in λθ\lambda_\theta for adverse; θ\theta itself depends on Reθt⁡\mathrm{Re}_{\theta t} and is iterated. Reθc⁡\mathrm{Re}_{\theta c} and Flength⁡F_{\mathrm{length}} are piecewise polynomials in Re⁡˜θt\tilde{\mathrm{Re}}_{\theta t} (Langtry and Menter 2009); at their seams the published pieces meet continuously to within about one percent.

The effective intermittency acts on SST: production of kk times γeff⁡\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}, destruction times min⁡(max⁡(γeff⁡,0.1),1)\min(\max(\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}, 0.1), 1), and F1F_1 stays at 1 in the laminar layer (F1=max⁡(F1,e−(Ry/120)8)F_1 = \max(F_1, e^{-(R_y/120)^8}), Ry=ρyk/μR_y = \rho y\sqrt k/\mu).

What follows. The model is local (only cell quantities and the wall distance) and therefore runs on any mesh. It is as good as its correlation: calibrated for bypass transition on plates and airfoils, not for crossflow or shock-induced transition.

Wall distance and wall functions

All RANS models need the distance dd to the nearest no-slip wall. It is computed exactly: the wall faces are split into triangles (a fan from the face centre), put into a search tree, and every cell centre asks for the nearest point on a triangle – also behind edges and in concave corners, without the approximation of a Poisson or eikonal equation.

If the first cell is too coarse for the sublayer, a wall function replaces resolution. Spalding's law (1961) holds for all y+y^+ at once – it is the inverse, y+y^+ as a function of u+u^+:

y+=u++e−κB(eκu+−1−κu+−(κu+)22−(κu+)36).y^+ = u^+ + e^{-\kappa B} \left( e^{\kappa u^+} - 1 - \kappa u^+ - \frac{(\kappa u^+)^2}{2} - \frac{(\kappa u^+)^3}{6} \right).

For small u+u^+ the first terms of the series of eκu+e^{\kappa u^+} cancel and y+=u++O((κu+)4)y^+ = u^+ + O\big((\kappa u^+)^4\big) remains; for large ones eκ(u+−B)e^{\kappa(u^+ - B)} dominates, i.e. the log law. Given in the wall cell is Re⁡=upyp/ν=u+y+\mathrm{Re} = u_p y_p/\nu = u^+ y^+; u+u^+ follows by Newton (monotone in u+u^+); if Newton does not arrive within 60 steps (for instance at very large Re, where eκu+e^{\kappa u^+} overflows), a bisection between 0 and a doubled bound takes over – u+y+(u+)−Re⁡u^+ y^+(u^+) - \mathrm{Re} increases strictly and is negative at 0, so the bisection always hits. Hence uτ=up/u+u_\tau = u_p/u^+. So that the viscous flux gives exactly the wall shear ρuτ2\rho u_\tau^2, the wall face gets

μt,w=max⁡(ρuτ2ypup−μ,0).\mu_{t,w} = \max\!\left( \frac{\rho u_\tau^2 y_p}{u_p} - \mu, \ 0 \right).

The models take their wall cell from the same uτu_\tau: SST ω=ωvis⁡2+ωlog⁡2\omega = \sqrt{\omega_{\mathrm{vis}}^2 + \omega_{\log}^2} with ωvis⁡=6ν/(β1y2)\omega_{\mathrm{vis}} = 6\nu/(\beta_1 y^2), ωlog⁡=uτ/(β*κy)\omega_{\log} = u_\tau/(\sqrt{\beta^*}\kappa y) (automatic wall treatment after Menter); k-ε after Launder and Spalding ε=Cμ3/4k3/2/(κy)\varepsilon = C_\mu^{3/4} k^{3/2}/(\kappa y) without damping (fμ=1f_\mu = 1); the production of the wall cell blends with φ=tanh⁡((y+/11)4)\varphi = \tanh\big((y^+/11)^4\big) into ρuτ3/(κy)\rho u_\tau^3/(\kappa y).

What follows. With Spalding the wall function is not bound to \(30 < y^+ < 300\): if the first cell lies in the sublayer, \(\mu_{t,w} = 0\) and the model resolves the wall itself. Under separation (\(u_\tau \to 0\)) and strong pressure gradients the law of the wall does not hold; a resolved wall is more accurate there.

Large-eddy simulation

In LES only the smallest part of turbulence is modelled: the field is filtered with the filter width Δ\Delta, the solver computes the large eddies time-accurately, and a subgrid viscosity carries the effect of the small ones. Smagorinsky (1963) assumes that production and dissipation of the small scales are in equilibrium; by dimensional reasoning

νt=(CsΔ)2|S|,|S|=2SijSij,Cs=0.17.\nu_t = (C_s \Delta)^2 |S|, \qquad |S| = \sqrt{2 S_{ij} S_{ij}}, \quad C_s = 0.17 .

In pure shear du/dy=adu/dy = a this equals Cs2Δ2|a|C_s^2\Delta^2 |a| – also at the wall, where turbulence vanishes. WALE (Nicoud and Ducros 1999) builds the model from the traceless symmetric part of the square of the gradient, Sijd=12(gikgkj+gjkgki)−13δijgklglkS^d_{ij} = \frac{1}{2}(g_{ik}g_{kj} + g_{jk}g_{ki}) - \frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij} g_{kl}g_{lk}:

νt=(CwΔ)2(SijdSijd)3/2(SijSij)5/2+(SijdSijd)5/4,Cw=0.5.\nu_t = (C_w \Delta)^2 \frac{(S^d_{ij} S^d_{ij})^{3/2}}{(S_{ij}S_{ij})^{5/2} + (S^d_{ij}S^d_{ij})^{5/4}}, \qquad C_w = 0.5 .

In pure shear g2=0g^2 = 0, so νt=0\nu_t = 0; at the wall νt∝y3\nu_t \propto y^3 like the true turbulent viscosity. In pure strain g=diag⁡(a,−a,0)g = \mathrm{diag}(a, -a, 0) one has SijSij=2a2S_{ij}S_{ij} = 2a^2 and SijdSijd=23a4S^d_{ij}S^d_{ij} = \frac{2}{3}a^4. The filter width is Δ=V1/3\Delta = V^{1/3}, for a single cell layer (planar computation) V/h\sqrt{V/h}.

What follows. LES needs a time-accurate, low-dissipation scheme (central flux) and a fine mesh: the bulk of the energy must be resolved. Smagorinsky without wall damping is too viscous at walls; WALE is not – which makes it the better choice for wall-bounded flows.

Coupling with the flow

The transport equations are solved per step after the flow (loosely, with under-relaxed eddy viscosity, factor 0.5) or fully coupled with the flow in Newton's method; then μt\mu_t enters the residual exactly from the current quantities. The source terms are linearised: the destructive part implicitly, so that the quantities stay positive.

The recalculation of the derivations (cw1c_{w1}, γ1,2\gamma_{1,2}, C1C_1, the sublayer ω solution, the limits of Spalding, WALE in shear and strain, the seams of the transition correlations) is in docs/theorie/rechnung/cfd_turbulenz.py.

Limits

  • All RANS models are eddy-viscosity models: isotropic, without curvature and rotation correction, without secondary flow of the second kind.
  • Spalart-Allmaras without trip term: the boundary layer is turbulent from the start; transition only with SST.
  • Wall functions presuppose the law of the wall; inaccurate under separation and strong pressure gradients.
  • LES without dynamic procedure; the constants are fixed.
