Grid and geometry – background

The computation behind Netz, Netzbauer, Geometrie, Netzpruefung, Umnummerierung and the generators: the face-based finite-volume grid with owner and neighbour, face vector and centroid also of warped faces, volume from pyramids with exactly closed sums, the check with non-orthogonality and skewness, reverse Cuthill-McKee, Delaunay with Ruppert refinement and the boundary layer built from layers – with derivations, assumptions, checks and limits.

What this is about

A cell-centred finite-volume method needs exactly four things from the grid: which cells share a face, the face vector Sf\mathbf S_f (normal times area), the face centre xf\mathbf x_f, and per cell the volume VcV_c and centre xc\mathbf x_c. Everything else – fluxes, gradients, limiters, boundary conditions – runs as a loop over faces. The grid is therefore face-based and stored in flat arrays:

  • Nodes x[3n]x[3n], per cell its type (tetrahedron, pyramid, prism, hexahedron; node order as in VTK/Gmsh) and its nodes as a CSR list (starts and entries).
  • Per face the owner and the neighbour; the face vector points away from the owner. Boundary faces have neighbour −1-1.
  • Interior faces come first, sorted by (owner, neighbour) with owner < neighbour; behind them the boundary faces, contiguous per named boundary.
  • Per cell its faces as a CSR list, derived from owner and neighbour.

A flux loop is then for f in 0 … inner−1: F ← flux(W[owner f], W[neighbour f], S_f); R[owner] −= F; R[neighbour] += F – conservative, because every flux is subtracted exactly once and added exactly once.

Netzbauer: finding and orienting faces

The Netzbauer takes nodes and cells (and optionally named boundary faces) and builds the faces from them. Every local face of a cell – its node ring from the table of the cell type – gets its sorted node numbers as a key. If a key occurs a second time, that cell is the neighbour; a third time is a grid error. Duplicate nodes in a face, boundary faces without a cell and boundary faces in the interior are errors as well. Boundary faces without a name go into the boundary ohne-rand, with a note.

Afterwards every node ring is turned so that its vector points away from the owner: S⋅(xface−xcell)≥0\mathbf S\cdot(\bar{\mathbf x}_{\text{face}} - \bar{\mathbf x}_{\text{cell}}) \ge 0 with the node means. The orientation of the input therefore does not matter – grids from different readers (Gmsh, PLOT3D, CGNS) and generators arrive oriented the same way.

Geometry

Face vector

For a node ring x0,…,xn−1\mathbf x_0, \dots, \mathbf x_{n-1},

S=12∑i=0n−1xi×xi+1(xn=x0).\mathbf S = \tfrac12\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathbf x_i\times\mathbf x_{i+1} \qquad (\mathbf x_n = \mathbf x_0).

For a planar polygon this is the normal times the area (Stokes's formula for the area). The sum does not depend on the reference point: shifting all points by a\mathbf a adds 12∑(a×xi+1+xi×a)\tfrac12\sum(\mathbf a\times\mathbf x_{i+1} + \mathbf x_i\times\mathbf a), which is zero because every point occurs once as xi\mathbf x_i and once as xi+1\mathbf x_{i+1}. For a warped quadrilateral it gives 12(x2−x0)×(x3−x1)\tfrac12(\mathbf x_2 - \mathbf x_0)\times(\mathbf x_3 - \mathbf x_1), the cross product of the diagonals. It is computed as a fan of triangles around the node mean x\bar{\mathbf x}; each triangle has the vector tk=12(xk−x)×(xk+1−x)\mathbf t_k = \tfrac12(\mathbf x_k - \bar{\mathbf x})\times(\mathbf x_{k+1} - \bar{\mathbf x}) and S=∑tk\mathbf S = \sum\mathbf t_k.

Face centre

The face centre is the centroid, not the node mean: the mean of the triangle centroids, weighted with the triangle area projected onto S\mathbf S,

xf=∑kakmk∑kak,ak=tk⋅S|S|,mk=13(x+xk+xk+1).\mathbf x_f = \frac{\sum_k a_k\,\mathbf m_k}{\sum_k a_k}, \qquad a_k = \mathbf t_k\cdot\frac{\mathbf S}{|\mathbf S|}, \qquad \mathbf m_k = \tfrac13(\bar{\mathbf x} + \mathbf x_k + \mathbf x_{k+1}).

For planar polygons this is exactly the centroid of the polygon formula – also for non-convex ones, because the projection subtracts triangles of negative orientation. Example: the quadrilateral (0,0), (4,0), (4,1), (0,3) has area 8 and centroid (5/3, 13/12); the node mean would be (2, 1). If the face has no area (nodes on a straight line), S=0\mathbf S = 0 and the centre is the node mean.

Volume and cell centre

Every cell is decomposed into pyramids: apex at the node mean xs\mathbf x_s of the cell, base one cell face each with the outward vector Sfout=±Sf\mathbf S^{\text{out}}_f = \pm\mathbf S_f (+ for the owner):

Vc=13∑fSfout⋅(xf−xs),xc=1Vc∑fVf(xs+34(xf−xs)).V_c = \tfrac13\sum_{f}\mathbf S^{\text{out}}_f\cdot(\mathbf x_f - \mathbf x_s), \qquad \mathbf x_c = \frac{1}{V_c}\sum_f V_f\,\big(\mathbf x_s + \tfrac34(\mathbf x_f - \mathbf x_s)\big).

The centroid of a pyramid lies on the line from the apex to the centroid of the base, a quarter of the height above the base – hence 34\tfrac34. If the volume is exactly zero (a flattened cell), the centre is the node mean.

Closed sums

Over a closed hull ∑fSfout=0\sum_f\mathbf S^{\text{out}}_f = 0: every edge belongs to two faces and occurs in their rings once as xa×xb\mathbf x_a\times\mathbf x_b and once as xb×xa\mathbf x_b\times\mathbf x_a. Two things the method needs follow from this:

  • Uniform flow is preserved. With a constant state the flux through every face is F⋅Sf\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf S_f, its sum over the cell F⋅∑Sfout=0\mathbf F\cdot\sum\mathbf S^{\text{out}}_f = 0.
  • The apex does not matter: ∑fSfout⋅xs=0\sum_f\mathbf S^{\text{out}}_f\cdot\mathbf x_s = 0, so Vc=13∑fSfout⋅xfV_c = \tfrac13\sum_f\mathbf S^{\text{out}}_f\cdot\mathbf x_f. Summed over all cells the interior faces cancel (they occur with both signs), and the boundary integral remains

∑cVc=13∑f∈boundarySf⋅xf=13∮x⋅dS,\sum_c V_c = \tfrac13\sum_{f \in \text{boundary}}\mathbf S_f\cdot\mathbf x_f = \tfrac13\oint\mathbf x\cdot d\mathbf S,

Gauss's theorem for ∇⋅x=3\nabla\cdot\mathbf x = 3. Because owner and neighbour use the same Sf\mathbf S_f, xf\mathbf x_f, this holds with warped faces too: the cells fill the domain without gaps or overlap.

Grid check

Before computing, Netzpruefung.pruefe checks the grid. Errors (the grid is unusable):

  • cell volume ≤0\le 0 (also exactly zero for a flattened cell);
  • cell not closed: |∑Sout|/∑|S|>10−9|\sum\mathbf S^{\text{out}}| / \sum|\mathbf S| > 10^{-9};
  • domain boundary not closed, by the same measure;
  • interior face whose vector does not point from owner to neighbour: Sf⋅d≤0\mathbf S_f\cdot\mathbf d \le 0 with d=xm−xo\mathbf d = \mathbf x_m - \mathbf x_o (overlapping or folded cells);
  • total volume unequal to the boundary integral, relative 10−910^{-9}.

Warnings: non-orthogonality above 70° and boundary faces without a name. Key figures: smallest and largest volume, largest volume ratio of neighbouring cells, largest non-orthogonality and skewness, closedness, cells per type, boundaries, bandwidth.

The non-orthogonality of an interior face is the angle between face vector and centre connection,

θ=arccos⁡S⋅d|S||d|.\theta = \arccos\frac{\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf d}{|\mathbf S|\,|\mathbf d|}.

The skewness measures how far the point where the connection pierces the face plane lies from the face centre, relative to the length of the connection:

xp=xo+td,t=S⋅(xf−xo)S⋅d,skewness=|xp−xf||d|.\mathbf x_p = \mathbf x_o + t\,\mathbf d, \quad t = \frac{\mathbf S\cdot(\mathbf x_f - \mathbf x_o)}{\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf d}, \qquad \text{skewness} = \frac{|\mathbf x_p - \mathbf x_f|}{|\mathbf d|}.

Two examples by hand: two cubes stacked, all nodes sheared by szs\,z in x – the cells are parallelepipeds, d=(s,0,1)\mathbf d = (s, 0, 1), S=(0,0,1)\mathbf S = (0, 0, 1), so θ=arctan⁡s\theta = \arctan s: 71.57° at s=3s = 3 (warning), 63.43° at s=2s = 2; the piercing point is the face centre, the skewness 0. If only the upper cell is sheared by 0.6 in y, d=(0,0.3,1)\mathbf d = (0, 0.3, 1), θ=16.70°\theta = 16.70°, the piercing point lies 0.15 beside the centre, the skewness 0.15/1.09=0.1440.15/\sqrt{1.09} = 0.144.

An example of the orientation error: two tetrahedra with a common base, the apex of the second pushed below the face. The face is oriented from the first; for the second it now points inward, its centre lies beyond that of the first, and the check reports "interior face does not point from cell 0 to cell 1", together with the open cell and the boundary integral.

Renumbering

Neighbouring cells should have close numbers: the bandwidth of the Jacobian falls (important for ILU and LU-SGS), and neighbours lie close together in memory during the computation. Reverse Cuthill-McKee: per connected region a pseudo-peripheral start cell (after George and Liu: repeated breadth-first search to the cell of largest eccentricity), then a breadth-first search that queues the neighbours by increasing degree, finally the order reversed. Equal degree is decided by the old number – the result is deterministic. The algorithm runs in framework-math (envisionz.math.graph); Netz.umnummeriert carries geometry, boundaries and structure info along.

Generators

Structured grids

Netzgenerator builds boxes from hexahedra, prisms (two per hexahedron), tetrahedra (six per hexahedron, Freudenthal decomposition along the space diagonal) or pyramids (six per hexahedron around a centre point) – all conforming, so that the same manufactured solution runs on every cell type. Optionally the interior nodes are displaced randomly with a fixed seed; the boundary stays planar and the domain volume exact. In addition, planar grids of one cell layer: channel, wedge (oblique shock), plate, step, sector, plate cascade, cylinder, C-grid around an airfoil and the Ringleb flow. Nonsensical input (too few cells, angles above 180°, an odd cell count for the plate cascade) is rejected with a message.

Unstructured: Delaunay and Ruppert

Dreiecksnetz meshes planar domains bounded by closed polylines (outer and any number of holes, every segment carrying the name of its boundary) to a desired edge length h(x,y)h(x, y) and a smallest angle:

  1. The segments are subdivided according to hh.
  2. Delaunay triangulation of the boundary points after Bowyer and Watson: every new point removes the triangles whose circumcircle contains it (the cavity) and connects the cavity boundary to itself. Afterwards no point lies in the circumcircle of a triangle; among all triangulations Delaunay maximises the smallest angle.
  3. Boundary recovery: if a boundary segment is missing, it is split at its midpoint until all are present. Afterwards the boundary segments are constrained edges; no cavity reaches across them (constrained Delaunay property).
  4. Triangles outside (flood fill from the enclosing triangle across non-constrained edges) and in holes are removed.
  5. Ruppert refinement: as long as a triangle has an angle below the smallest or an edge above hh, its circumcentre is inserted – unless it would lie in the diametral circle of a boundary segment or beyond the boundary; then that segment is halved.
  6. Optionally Laplace smoothing of the interior nodes, only if no triangle flips.

Ruppert (1995) proves termination for a smallest angle up to about 20.7° if no two boundary segments meet at less than 60°; in practice almost all domains finish up to 30°. A maximum node count stops any refinement and is reported. The planar grid is extruded into a layer of thickness Δz\Delta z: triangles become prisms, quadrilaterals hexahedra.

Boundary layer from layers

At a wall boundary, Grenzschicht(rand, erste, wachstum, lagen) lays layers of quadrilaterals (hexahedra after extrusion) along the wall normal, the first with thickness h1h_1, every further one thicker by the factor q≥1q \ge 1:

hk=h1qk−1,H=∑k=1Nhk=h1qN−1q−1.h_k = h_1\,q^{k-1}, \qquad H = \sum_{k=1}^{N} h_k = h_1\,\frac{q^N - 1}{q - 1}.

The outer edge of the layer becomes a fixed segment of the triangulation; triangles fill above it. At sharp corners (such as a trailing edge) the layer forms a fan; re-entrant corners above 120° are rejected, as is a neighbouring segment shorter than a good twice the layer. At corners the nodes of the first layer lie on the ray of the averaged normal, stretched by h1/cos⁡h_1/\cos, so that the thickness normal to both walls stays h1h_1.

Assumptions

  • Cells are star-shaped with respect to their node mean – otherwise single pyramids have negative volume; the sum stays right as long as the cell does not fold over.
  • Warped quadrilateral faces are replaced by their fan around the node mean; both neighbours see the same substitute face.
  • Planar grids lie in a plane zz = constant and have one cell layer; the cover faces are symmetry boundaries.

What follows

  • Conservation and uniform flow are a property of the grid, not of the flux: closed sums up to rounding, the same face vectors and centres for owner and neighbour.
  • Cell centre = centroid is the precondition for the cell value of a linear field being its value at the centre and for the reconstruction reaching second order (reconstruction).
  • Non-orthogonality above 70° degrades the viscous fluxes and the convergence; the check warns but computes. Skewness and volume jumps show where a grid should become finer or smoother.
  • A grid with errors is not computed. Most errors arise not in the generator but when deforming (overlapping cells) or in foreign grid files.

How it is checked

  • TheorieC1probe (netz): centroid of the quadrilateral against the polygon formula, degenerate face, flattened cell, sheared column (θ = arctan s, warning from 70°), skewness by hand, overlapping cells with the text of the check, rejection of nonsensical generator input.
  • Netzprobe: volumes against the formulas (tetrahedron, parallelepiped, prism, pyramid), total volume of the distorted box, owner and neighbour, broken grids, RCM against framework-math.
  • Dreiecksnetzprobe (SN32-01 …): area, smallest angle, edge length, Delaunay property, holes, re-entrant corners, boundary layer.
  • Recalculation docs/theorie/rechnung/cfd_netz.py: reference point, diagonal formula, centroid, closed sum, volume from the boundary integral, pyramid centroid, angle and skewness of the probes.

Limits

  • Cell types: tetrahedron, pyramid, prism, hexahedron; general polyhedra are not available.
  • The unstructured generator is planar (2D, one layer); three-dimensional unstructured grids come via Gmsh or CGNS.
  • The check finds overlapping cells only if they share a face or violate the sums; two cells that interpenetrate without a common face are not detected.
