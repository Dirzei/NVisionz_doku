Numerical fluxes – background

The computation behind Roe, Hllc, AusmPlusUp and Zentral: the Euler equations in conservation form, the Riemann problem at every face, Roe's linearised solver with Harten's entropy fix and the low-Mach correction, HLLC with Einfeldt's wave speeds, AUSM+-up with separate splitting of mass flux and pressure, and the central flux for large-eddy simulation – with derivations, assumptions, checks and limits.

What this is about

A finite-volume method keeps the mean Ui\mathbf U_i of the conserved quantities for every cell ii and changes it by what flows in and out through the faces:

VidUidt=−∑f∈∂iF*(WL,WR,nf)|Sf|.V_i\,\frac{d\mathbf U_i}{dt} = -\sum_{f \in \partial i} \mathbf F^{*}\big(\mathbf W_L, \mathbf W_R, \mathbf n_f\big)\,|S_f|.

Left and right of a face there are two different states – the reconstructed values of the two cells (reconstruction). The numerical flux F*\mathbf F^* decides what flows between them. It must satisfy two conditions, otherwise the method is wrong or unstable:

  • Consistency: F*(W,W,n)=F(W)⋅n\mathbf F^*(\mathbf W, \mathbf W, \mathbf n) = \mathbf F(\mathbf W)\cdot\mathbf n – with equal states on both sides, the physical flux.
  • Conservation: F*(WL,WR,n)=−F*(WR,WL,−n)\mathbf F^*(\mathbf W_L, \mathbf W_R, \mathbf n) = -\mathbf F^*(\mathbf W_R, \mathbf W_L, -\mathbf n) – what one cell loses, the other gains. Only then do shocks converge, by Lax and Wendroff, to the right weak solution with the right shock speed.

Beyond that, F*\mathbf F^* should be upwind: information travels along characteristics, and a flux that ignores this oscillates (central differences without damping). The four methods differ in how much of the wave structure they resolve and what that costs.

The Euler equations

For an inviscid ideal gas with isentropic exponent γ\gamma, the conserved quantities and the flux through a face with unit normal n\mathbf n and normal velocity vn=u⋅nv_n = \mathbf u\cdot\mathbf n are

U=(ρρuρE),F⋅n=(ρvnρvnu+pnρvnH),p=(γ−1)(ρE−12ρ|u|2),H=E+pρ.\mathbf U = \begin{pmatrix}\rho\\ \rho\mathbf u\\ \rho E\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n = \begin{pmatrix}\rho v_n\\ \rho v_n\mathbf u + p\,\mathbf n\\ \rho v_n H\end{pmatrix}, \qquad p = (\gamma - 1)\Big(\rho E - \tfrac12\rho|\mathbf u|^2\Big), \quad H = E + \frac{p}{\rho}.

The code works with the primitive state W=(ρ,u,v,w,p)\mathbf W = (\rho, u, v, w, p); c=γp/ρc = \sqrt{\gamma p/\rho} is the speed of sound. The Jacobian ∂(F⋅n)/∂U\partial(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n)/\partial\mathbf U has the eigenvalues vn−cv_n - c, three times vnv_n, and vn+cv_n + c: two acoustic waves, the entropy or contact wave and two shear waves. Because the flux is rotationally invariant, it suffices to solve the one-dimensional problem in the direction n\mathbf n and carry the tangential velocities along.

The Riemann problem at the face

Two constant states meeting at the face at time 0 break up into a fan of three waves: on the left a rarefaction or shock, in the middle the contact discontinuity (pressure and normal velocity continuous, density and tangential velocity jump), on the right again a rarefaction or shock. The flux at the face is the flux of the state lying at x/t=0x/t = 0. The exact solution needs a Newton iteration for the star pressure (Toro, chapter 4); the module has it as a reference (loesung.Riemann), the flux methods approximate it.

Roe: linearised Riemann solver

Roe (1981) replaces the non-linear problem by a linear one, ∂tU+A˜∂xU=0\partial_t\mathbf U + \tilde A\,\partial_x\mathbf U = 0, with a matrix A˜(UL,UR)\tilde A(\mathbf U_L, \mathbf U_R) satisfying three requirements: it is diagonalisable with real eigenvalues, it becomes the Jacobian for equal states, and

A˜(UR−UL)=FR−FL.\tilde A\,(\mathbf U_R - \mathbf U_L) = \mathbf F_R - \mathbf F_L.

This third property makes the solver exact for a single shock (see below). For the ideal gas the Jacobian at the Roe average with weights ρ\sqrt\rho satisfies it:

ρ˜=ρLρR,u˜=ρLuL+ρRuRρL+ρR,H˜ likewise,c˜2=(γ−1)(H˜−12|u˜|2).\tilde\rho = \sqrt{\rho_L\rho_R}, \qquad \tilde{\mathbf u} = \frac{\sqrt{\rho_L}\,\mathbf u_L + \sqrt{\rho_R}\,\mathbf u_R}{\sqrt{\rho_L} + \sqrt{\rho_R}}, \qquad \tilde H \text{ likewise}, \qquad \tilde c^2 = (\gamma - 1)\Big(\tilde H - \tfrac12|\tilde{\mathbf u}|^2\Big).

The derivation: with the parameter vector z=ρ(1,u,H)\mathbf z = \sqrt\rho\,(1, \mathbf u, H), both U\mathbf U and F\mathbf F are quadratic in z\mathbf z; for quadratic functions the difference quotient is exactly the derivative at the arithmetic mean of z\mathbf z – hence the ρ\sqrt\rho weights. The flux is

F*=12(FL+FR−∑k=15|λ˜k|αkK˜k),\mathbf F^* = \tfrac12\Big(\mathbf F_L + \mathbf F_R - \sum_{k=1}^{5} |\tilde\lambda_k|\,\alpha_k\,\tilde{\mathbf K}_k\Big),

with the eigenvalues λ˜k\tilde\lambda_k, the right eigenvectors K˜k\tilde{\mathbf K}_k and the wave strengths αk\alpha_k from the decomposition UR−UL=∑αkK˜k\mathbf U_R - \mathbf U_L = \sum\alpha_k\tilde{\mathbf K}_k. The code computes the wave strengths from the jumps of the primitive quantities (Blazek, section 4.3.3):

α1,5=Δp∓ρ˜c˜Δvn2c˜2,α2=Δρ−Δpc˜2,shear: ρ˜(Δu−Δvnn),\alpha_{1,5} = \frac{\Delta p \mp \tilde\rho\,\tilde c\,\Delta v_n}{2\tilde c^2}, \qquad \alpha_2 = \Delta\rho - \frac{\Delta p}{\tilde c^2}, \qquad \text{shear: } \tilde\rho\,(\Delta\mathbf u - \Delta v_n\,\mathbf n),

and assembles the sum component by component; the energy term of the shear reads ρ˜(u˜⋅Δu−v˜nΔvn)\tilde\rho\,(\tilde{\mathbf u}\cdot\Delta\mathbf u - \tilde v_n\Delta v_n). The probe computes the same flux a second time in Toro's form (section 11.2) – wave strengths from the jumps of the conserved quantities, eigenvectors K˜1…K˜5\tilde{\mathbf K}_1 \dots \tilde{\mathbf K}_5 written out, in the frame rotated to the normal – and finds agreement to 3⋅10−163\cdot 10^{-16}. Both decompositions agree because for the ρ\sqrt\rho averages Δ(ρv)=ρ˜Δv+v˜Δρ\Delta(\rho v) = \tilde\rho\,\Delta v + \tilde v\,\Delta\rho holds exactly.

Harten's entropy fix

At a sonic point in a rarefaction (vn−cv_n - c changes sign) |λ˜1||\tilde\lambda_1| vanishes, and the linear solver leaves an expansion shock – a solution that satisfies the conservation laws but violates the second law. Harten and Hyman replace the modulus of the acoustic eigenvalues near zero by a parabola:

|λ|δ={|λ|,|λ|≥δ,λ2+δ22δ,|λ|<δ,δ=εc˜,ε=0.1 (default).|\lambda|_\delta = \begin{cases}|\lambda|, & |\lambda| \ge \delta,\\[2pt] \dfrac{\lambda^2 + \delta^2}{2\delta}, & |\lambda| < \delta,\end{cases} \qquad \delta = \varepsilon\,\tilde c, \quad \varepsilon = 0.1 \text{ (default)}.

The parabola joins with continuous value and slope at |λ|=δ|\lambda| = \delta and equals δ/2\delta/2 at λ=0\lambda = 0; it is never smaller than |λ||\lambda|, so it only adds dissipation. With ε=0\varepsilon = 0 it is switched off (new Roe(0)).

The stationary shock

For a stationary normal shock FL=FR\mathbf F_L = \mathbf F_R (Rankine-Hugoniot with shock speed 0). The Roe property gives ∑λ˜kαkK˜k=0\sum\tilde\lambda_k\alpha_k\tilde{\mathbf K}_k = 0, so λ˜kαk=0\tilde\lambda_k\alpha_k = 0 for every wave – the only wave with αk≠0\alpha_k \ne 0 has λ˜k=0\tilde\lambda_k = 0, precisely v˜n−c˜=0\tilde v_n - \tilde c = 0. Then F*=FL\mathbf F^* = \mathbf F_L exactly; the shock sits within one cell. The entropy fix lifts exactly this eigenvalue to δ/2\delta/2 and smears the shock: at Mach 2 the flux deviates by 1.7 %.

Low Mach number: lmroe

As M→0M \to 0, the dissipation of the Roe flux in the momentum scales with ρ˜c˜Δvn\tilde\rho\tilde c\,\Delta v_n – a factor 1/M1/M too large against the convective terms. The pressure fluctuations of the discrete solution then grow like MM instead of M2M^2, and the solution does not tend to the incompressible one. Rieper (2011) scales only the jump of the normal velocity in the acoustic waves:

Δvna=min⁡(1,max⁡(ML,MR))Δvn,MK=|uK|/cK.\Delta v_n^{\,a} = \min\big(1, \max(M_L, M_R)\big)\,\Delta v_n, \qquad M_K = |\mathbf u_K|/c_K.

The contact and shear waves are unchanged; in supersonic flow the factor is 1, and lmroe is bit-identical to roe.

HLLC

Harten, Lax and van Leer approximate the fan by two waves SL<SRS_L < S_R with an averaged state between them; the contact is lost. Toro, Spieler and Speares (1994) restore the contact wave S*S^*. Across each of the three waves the jump conditions Fb−Fa=S(Ub−Ua)\mathbf F_b - \mathbf F_a = S\,(\mathbf U_b - \mathbf U_a) hold; across the contact pressure and normal velocity are continuous. This gives the contact speed

S*=pR−pL+ρLvn,L(SL−vn,L)−ρRvn,R(SR−vn,R)ρL(SL−vn,L)−ρR(SR−vn,R)S^* = \frac{p_R - p_L + \rho_L v_{n,L}(S_L - v_{n,L}) - \rho_R v_{n,R}(S_R - v_{n,R})}{\rho_L(S_L - v_{n,L}) - \rho_R(S_R - v_{n,R})}

and the star states

UK*=ρKSK−vn,KSK−S*(1uK+(S*−vn,K)nEK+(S*−vn,K)(S*+pKρK(SK−vn,K))),FK*=FK+SK(UK*−UK).\mathbf U^*_K = \rho_K\,\frac{S_K - v_{n,K}}{S_K - S^*}\begin{pmatrix}1\\ \mathbf u_K + (S^* - v_{n,K})\,\mathbf n\\ E_K + (S^* - v_{n,K})\Big(S^* + \dfrac{p_K}{\rho_K(S_K - v_{n,K})}\Big)\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf F^*_K = \mathbf F_K + S_K(\mathbf U^*_K - \mathbf U_K).

The flux is FL\mathbf F_L, FL*\mathbf F^*_L, FR*\mathbf F^*_R or FR\mathbf F_R, depending on where the tt axis lies in the fan (figure above). The recalculation checks that the same pressure p*=pK+ρK(SK−vn,K)(S*−vn,K)p^* = p_K + \rho_K(S_K - v_{n,K})(S^* - v_{n,K}) holds on both sides of the contact and that FR*−FL*=S*(UR*−UL*)\mathbf F^*_R - \mathbf F^*_L = S^*(\mathbf U^*_R - \mathbf U^*_L).

Einfeldt's wave speeds (1988): SL=min⁡(vn,L−cL,v˜n−c˜)S_L = \min(v_{n,L} - c_L,\ \tilde v_n - \tilde c), SR=max⁡(vn,R+cR,v˜n+c˜)S_R = \max(v_{n,R} + c_R,\ \tilde v_n + \tilde c) with the Roe average. They bound the physical signal speeds; the density stays positive, and no entropy fix is needed. For the stationary shock v˜n−c˜=0\tilde v_n - \tilde c = 0, so SL=0S_L = 0 and F*=FL\mathbf F^* = \mathbf F_L – HLLC resolves it exactly as well.

AUSM+-up

The AUSM family (Liou and Steffen 1993, Liou 2006) splits the flux into a convective part and a pressure part, F*=m˙ψupwind+p1/2(0,n,0)\mathbf F^* = \dot m\,\mathbf{\psi}_{\text{upwind}} + p_{1/2}\,(0, \mathbf n, 0) with ψ=(1,u,H)\mathbf{\psi} = (1, \mathbf u, H), and splits both separately by the Mach number. AUSM+-up uses a common speed of sound at the face, derived from the critical one:

cK*2=2(γ−1)γ+1HK,c^L=cL*2max⁡(cL*,vn,L),c^R=cR*2max⁡(cR*,−vn,R),c1/2=min⁡(c^L,c^R).c^{*2}_K = \frac{2(\gamma - 1)}{\gamma + 1}H_K, \qquad \hat c_L = \frac{c^{*2}_L}{\max(c^*_L, v_{n,L})}, \quad \hat c_R = \frac{c^{*2}_R}{\max(c^*_R, -v_{n,R})}, \qquad c_{1/2} = \min(\hat c_L, \hat c_R).

With MK=vn,K/c1/2M_K = v_{n,K}/c_{1/2} and the polynomials

M4±(M)=±14(M±1)2±β(M2−1)2,P5±(M)=14(M±1)2(2∓M)±αM(M2−1)2(|M|<1),\mathcal M^\pm_4(M) = \pm\tfrac14(M \pm 1)^2 \pm \beta\,(M^2 - 1)^2, \qquad \mathcal P^\pm_5(M) = \tfrac14(M \pm 1)^2(2 \mp M) \pm \alpha\,M\,(M^2 - 1)^2 \qquad (|M| < 1),

(otherwise the upwind split 12(M±|M|)\tfrac12(M \pm |M|) and 12(1±sign⁡M)\tfrac12(1 \pm \mathrm{sign}M)) the Mach number and pressure at the face are

M1/2=M4+(ML)+M4−(MR)−Kpfamax⁡(1−σM2,0)pR−pLρ1/2c1/22,M_{1/2} = \mathcal M^+_4(M_L) + \mathcal M^-_4(M_R) - \frac{K_p}{f_a}\max(1 - \sigma\bar M^2, 0)\,\frac{p_R - p_L}{\rho_{1/2}\,c_{1/2}^2},

p1/2=P5+(ML)pL+P5−(MR)pR−KuP5+P5−(ρL+ρR)fac1/2(vn,R−vn,L),p_{1/2} = \mathcal P^+_5(M_L)\,p_L + \mathcal P^-_5(M_R)\,p_R - K_u\,\mathcal P^+_5\mathcal P^-_5\,(\rho_L + \rho_R)\,f_a c_{1/2}\,(v_{n,R} - v_{n,L}),

with M2=(vn,L2+vn,R2)/(2c1/22)\bar M^2 = (v_{n,L}^2 + v_{n,R}^2)/(2c_{1/2}^2), ρ1/2=(ρL+ρR)/2\rho_{1/2} = (\rho_L + \rho_R)/2, Kp=0.25K_p = 0.25, Ku=0.75K_u = 0.75, σ=1\sigma = 1, β=1/8\beta = 1/8 and

M02=min⁡(1,max⁡(M2,M∞2)),fa=M0(2−M0),α=316(−4+5fa2).M_0^2 = \min\big(1, \max(\bar M^2, M_\infty^2)\big), \qquad f_a = M_0(2 - M_0), \qquad \alpha = \tfrac{3}{16}\big(-4 + 5f_a^2\big).

The mass flux is m˙=c1/2M1/2ρL\dot m = c_{1/2}M_{1/2}\,\rho_L for M1/2>0M_{1/2} > 0, otherwise with ρR\rho_R. The pressure-diffusion term in M1/2M_{1/2} couples pressure and velocity at low Mach number (it prevents checkerboard modes), the velocity-diffusion term in p1/2p_{1/2} damps pressure oscillations. With reference Mach number M∞=1M_\infty = 1 (default), fa=1f_a = 1 and α=3/16\alpha = 3/16, the ordinary AUSM+-up; with M∞<1M_\infty < 1 the dissipation is scaled at low Mach number. The probe recomputes the polynomials in expanded form; the recalculation shows symbolically M4++M4−=M\mathcal M^+_4 + \mathcal M^-_4 = M and P5++P5−=1\mathcal P^+_5 + \mathcal P^-_5 = 1 (consistency follows) and the continuous first derivative at |M|=1|M| = 1.

Central flux for large-eddy simulation

The Riemann solvers damp on coarse grids at the magnitude of the subgrid model; an LES then measures the scheme, not the model. Zentral computes the flux from arithmetic means (⋅)m=12((⋅)L+(⋅)R)(\cdot)_m = \tfrac12\big((\cdot)_L + (\cdot)_R\big) in the splitting of Kennedy and Gruber (2008) – products of means instead of means of products:

m˙=ρmvn,m,Fcentral*=(m˙,m˙um+pmn,m˙Hm).\dot m = \rho_m\,v_{n,m}, \qquad \mathbf F^*_{\text{central}} = \big(\dot m,\ \dot m\,\mathbf u_m + p_m\,\mathbf n,\ \dot m\,H_m\big).

This form conserves kinetic energy discretely in the convective term (except for the pressure part). Added to it is a scalar damping as in Rusanov, controlled by a pressure sensor:

F*=Fcentral*−12eλ(UR−UL),λ=maxK⁡(|vn,K|+cK),e=min⁡(1,ε+20|pR−pL|pR+pL),\mathbf F^* = \mathbf F^*_{\text{central}} - \tfrac12\,e\,\lambda\,(\mathbf U_R - \mathbf U_L), \qquad \lambda = \max_K(|v_{n,K}| + c_K), \qquad e = \min\Big(1,\ \varepsilon + 20\,\frac{|p_R - p_L|}{p_R + p_L}\Big),

with the base damping ε=0.005\varepsilon = 0.005 (adjustable as zentral:EPS). At a shock ee rises up to 1, the full Rusanov flux.

Assumptions

  • Ideal gas with fixed γ\gamma in the formulas on this page; for real gases and mixtures the physics supplies cc and HH to the flux (the speed of sound via kappa).
  • The normal is a unit normal; the solver multiplies by the face area.
  • Roe averages and Einfeldt speeds assume positive density and pressure on both sides; a state with c˜2≤0\tilde c^2 \le 0 is set to 10−30010^{-300}, not reported – reporting is the solver's job, on the cell state.

What follows

  • For shocks and contacts Roe and HLLC are equally sharp; HLLC needs no entropy fix and keeps the density positive. For strong rarefactions (outflow into vacuum, expansion around a corner) use HLLC.
  • The entropy fix costs sharpness at the stationary shock (1.7 % in the flux at Mach 2). Switching it off is safe only if no sonic point in a rarefaction occurs.
  • Below Mach 0.1 use lmroe or AUSM+-up with M∞M_\infty of the order of the free-stream Mach number; the ordinary Roe flux gives too large pressure fluctuations there.
  • For LES the central flux; for shock computations it remains the second choice because it does not keep the density positive.
  • All methods are first order; second order comes only with the reconstructed states WL,WR\mathbf W_L, \mathbf W_R (reconstruction).

How it is checked

  • TheorieC1probe (fluss): Roe against the second form after Toro in four cases (subsonic and supersonic, sonic points on the left and right, oblique normal), HLLC against Toro's computation in all four branches, AUSM+-up against the expanded polynomials with M∞M_\infty 1 and 0.1, stationary shock at Mach 2.
  • Flussprobe (PB2-01 … 07): consistency, mirror symmetry, supersonic flow, stationary contact, central flux by hand, settings, names and copies.
  • Eulerprobe: Sod tube against the exact solution, oblique shock, second order with manufactured solutions, conservation on distorted grids with all methods.
  • Recalculation docs/theorie/rechnung/cfd_fluss.py: equality of both Roe forms in 600 random cases, Roe property, Harten parabola, shock, jump conditions of HLLC, AUSM+-up polynomials.

Limits

  • Roe and the central flux can produce negative density or pressure in strong rarefactions; HLLC with Einfeldt keeps the density positive, the pressure not in every case.
  • The carbuncle at strong shocks on fine aligned grids is known for Roe and HLLC; there is no dedicated remedy (such as a rotated flux).
  • The low-Mach correction is meant for steady computations; it has not been studied for time-accurate acoustic computations at low Mach number.
