Species, mixtures and chemistry – background

The mathematics behind Spezies, Gemisch, GemischTransport and Realgas: transport of mass fractions, diffusion by Schmidt number, Lewis number or mixture-averaged with correction velocity, enthalpy diffusion, thermally ideal mixture with properties from framework-thermo, real gases through an equation of state, stiff chemistry per cell with RODAS4, heat release, loose and full coupling – with derivations, assumptions and limits.

What this is about

A mixture of NN species needs, in addition to the five conserved quantities of the flow, one equation per species for the mass fraction YkY_k:

∂(ρYk)∂t+∇⋅(ρvYk)=−∇⋅jk+ω˙k,∑kYk=1,∑kjk=0,∑kω˙k=0.\frac{\partial (\rho Y_k)}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{v} Y_k) = -\nabla \cdot \mathbf{j}_k + \dot\omega_k, \qquad \sum_k Y_k = 1, \quad \sum_k \mathbf{j}_k = 0, \quad \sum_k \dot\omega_k = 0 .

jk\mathbf{j}_k is the diffusive flux, ω˙k\dot\omega_k the production rate in kg/(m³ s). Summed over all species the equation must give continuity – hence the two sum conditions, and the discretisation depends on them: upwind convection with the mass flux of the numerical flux of the flow (optionally second order with limited gradient), so that ∑Yk\sum Y_k satisfies the same discrete equation as ρ\rho.

There are three ways: steady with a fixed, converged flow; with the implicit solver each flow step is followed by a species step (loose); with the explicit one the partial densities ρYk\rho Y_k run along in the same Runge-Kutta stages (time-accurate).

Diffusion

Three models for ρDk\rho D_k in jk=−ρDk∇Yk\mathbf{j}_k = -\rho D_k \nabla Y_k:

  • Schmidt number (default): ρD=μ/Sc⁡+μt/Sct⁡\rho D = \mu/\mathrm{Sc} + \mu_t/\mathrm{Sc}_t, the same for all species, Sc⁡=0.7\mathrm{Sc} = 0.7, Sct⁡=0.9\mathrm{Sc}_t = 0.9; or a fixed value.
  • Unity Lewis number: ρDk=λ/cp\rho D_k = \lambda/c_p – heat and species diffuse equally fast, Le⁡=λ/(ρcpD)=1\mathrm{Le} = \lambda/(\rho c_p D) = 1.
  • Mixture-averaged after Hirschfelder-Curtiss in Kee's form: per species one coefficient into the mixture from the binary DkjD_{kj}.

Derivation of mixture-averaged diffusion. The Stefan-Maxwell equations for ideal gases read ∇Xk=∑j≠kXkXjDkj(Vj−Vk)\nabla X_k = \sum_{j \ne k} \frac{X_k X_j}{D_{kj}} (\mathbf{V}_j - \mathbf{V}_k). Assuming all other species move together with the mass-averaged velocity (Vj≈0\mathbf{V}_j \approx 0 for j≠kj \ne k in the centre-of-mass frame) leaves ∇Xk=−VkXk∑j≠kXj/Dkj\nabla X_k = -\mathbf{V}_k X_k \sum_{j \ne k} X_j/D_{kj}; with jk=ρYkVk\mathbf{j}_k = \rho Y_k \mathbf{V}_k and referring to the mass fraction follows

Dkm=1−Yk∑j≠kXj/Dkj,jk=−ρDkmMkM∇Xk.D_{km} = \frac{1 - Y_k}{\sum_{j \ne k} X_j / D_{kj}}, \qquad \mathbf{j}_k = -\rho D_{km} \frac{M_k}{\bar M} \nabla X_k .

The binary values DkjD_{kj} come from Chapman-Enskog theory (collision integrals, collision data of the species from framework-thermo), computed once at 1 bar on a table over ln⁡T\ln T (200 K to 6000 K, linear in ln⁡D\ln D) and converted with D∝1/pD \propto 1/p. For a pure species (Yk=1Y_k = 1) the limit with the smallest binary value applies.

Because the approximation does not satisfy ∑kjk=0\sum_k \mathbf{j}_k = 0 by itself, a correction velocity removes the excess proportionally: jk←jk−Yk∑jjj\mathbf{j}_k \leftarrow \mathbf{j}_k - Y_k \sum_j \mathbf{j}_j. Then the sum is exactly zero and the mass fractions keep summing to one.

Assumptions. No thermal diffusion (Soret), no pressure diffusion, no multicomponent solution of the Stefan-Maxwell equations. For light species (H, H₂) in heavy mixtures the mixture-averaged form is accurate to a few percent.

Enthalpy diffusion

When species with different enthalpy diffuse, they carry energy. The heat flux of a mixture is

q=−λ∇T+∑khs,kjk,\mathbf{q} = -\lambda \nabla T + \sum_k h_{s,k} \mathbf{j}_k ,

with the sensible enthalpy hs,kh_{s,k} that matches the energy of the mixture (below). Without the second term a computation with differing DkD_k violates the energy balance: in a hydrogen flame the temperature then becomes visibly wrong.

Viscosity and conductivity of the mixture

Per species Chapman-Enskog theory gives μk\mu_k and λk\lambda_k; they are mixed after Wilke,

μ=∑kXkμk∑jXjΦkj,Φkj=[1+(μk/μj)1/2(Mj/Mk)1/4]28(1+Mk/Mj),\mu = \sum_k \frac{X_k \mu_k}{\sum_j X_j \Phi_{kj}}, \qquad \Phi_{kj} = \frac{\left[ 1 + (\mu_k/\mu_j)^{1/2} (M_j/M_k)^{1/4} \right]^2}{\sqrt{8 (1 + M_k/M_j)}} ,

and likewise λ\lambda. The solver gets both per cell (viscous flow); the Prandtl number follows.

Thermally ideal mixture

Each species is an ideal gas with its own cp,k(T)c_{p,k}(T) from framework-thermo; the mixture has

R=∑kYkRk,cv=∑kYkcp,k(T)−R,p=ρRT.R = \sum_k Y_k R_k, \qquad c_v = \sum_k Y_k c_{p,k}(T) - R, \qquad p = \rho R T .

The solver's energy is the sensible internal energy with reference T0=298.15T_0 = 298.15 K,

es(T)=∑kYk(hk(T)−hk(T0))−R(T−T0)+e0,e0=∑kYk(cp,k(T0)−Rk)T0,e_s(T) = \sum_k Y_k \big( h_k(T) - h_k(T_0) \big) - R (T - T_0) + e_0, \qquad e_0 = \sum_k Y_k \big( c_{p,k}(T_0) - R_k \big) T_0 ,

below T0T_0 linear with cp,k(T0)c_{p,k}(T_0). The chemical energy sits in the mass fractions (∑Ykqk\sum Y_k q_k), not in ese_s. The temperature for ee follows by Newton, T←T−(es(T)−e)/cv(T)T \leftarrow T - (e_s(T) - e)/c_v(T), convergent because ese_s is strictly monotone with derivative cv>0c_v > 0. For the fluxes each cell gets an equivalent gas with γ˜=1+RT/es\tilde\gamma = 1 + RT/e_s, so that p=(γ˜−1)ρesp = (\tilde\gamma - 1)\rho e_s holds exactly.

The sensible enthalpy per species, hs,k=hk(T)−hk(T0)+cp,k(T0)T0h_{s,k} = h_k(T) - h_k(T_0) + c_{p,k}(T_0) T_0, matches it: with the chemical part qk=hk(T0)−cp,k(T0)T0q_k = h_k(T_0) - c_{p,k}(T_0) T_0 one has hs,k+qk=hk(T)h_{s,k} + q_k = h_k(T).

Real gases

For a pure real gas or a mixture of fixed composition (Helmholtz equations, GERG-2008, AGA8, cubic equations) the solver keeps computing with its five conserved quantities. From ρ\rho and ee the equation of state gives pp, TT and the speed of sound aa; each cell gets an equivalent gas

γ˜=1+pρe,R˜=pρT,κ=ρa2p,\tilde\gamma = 1 + \frac{p}{\rho e}, \qquad \tilde R = \frac{p}{\rho T}, \qquad \kappa = \frac{\rho a^2}{p} ,

so that p=(γ˜−1)ρep = (\tilde\gamma - 1)\rho e and T=p/(ρR˜)T = p/(\rho\tilde R) are the values of the equation and the waves run with the correct aa. The solver's energy is e=eEOS⁡−eref⁡e = e_{\mathrm{EOS}} - e_{\mathrm{ref}}; the solver chooses the reference so that γ˜=κ\tilde\gamma = \kappa in the reference state – there the equivalent gas is an ideal gas with the same speed of sound.

Chemistry and heat release

The production rates of a mechanism are sums over reactions with Arrhenius rates k=ATbe−Ea/(RT)k = A T^b e^{-E_a/(RT)} and the law of mass action; in a flame their time scales range from nanoseconds to milliseconds – the system is stiff. The chemistry is therefore integrated per cell on its own after each flow step (operator splitting):

dYkdt=ω˙k(ρ,T,Y)ρ,es+∑kYkqk=const.,T=T(es,Y),\frac{dY_k}{dt} = \frac{\dot\omega_k(\rho, T, \mathbf{Y})}{\rho}, \qquad e_s + \sum_k Y_k q_k = \text{const.}, \qquad T = T(e_s, \mathbf{Y}) ,

at fixed density (cell volume) and fixed total energy: what the reaction releases as chemical energy becomes sensible. Integration uses RODAS4, an L-stable fourth-order Rosenbrock method with step-size control (framework-math), over the cell's time step.

Without a mechanism, with a simple reaction and given formation enthalpies hk0h_k^0, heat release enters the energy equation as the source −∑khk0ω˙k-\sum_k h_k^0 \dot\omega_k; with it the probe measures the speed of an overdriven detonation against Rankine-Hugoniot.

Derivation of the test solution. With uniform velocity uu, first-order reaction ω˙A=−ρkYA\dot\omega_A = -\rho k Y_A and diffusion DD one has DY''−uY'−kY=0D Y'' - u Y' - k Y = 0 with roots λ1,2=(u±u2+4Dk)/(2D)\lambda_{1,2} = \big(u \pm \sqrt{u^2 + 4Dk}\big)/(2D); the probe compares the computation with this closed-form solution and, without diffusion, with YA=e−kx/uY_A = e^{-kx/u}. The first-order discretisation gives from cell to cell Yi=Yi−1/(1+kh/u)Y_i = Y_{i-1}/(1 + k h/u) – exactly, as the probe recomputes.

Loose and full coupling

Loosely coupled, a step solves the flow at fixed composition and then the species at fixed flow (symmetric block Gauss-Seidel, block = species of one cell, with the Jacobian of the production rates implicit). The small block is solved by Gauss elimination with column pivoting. Fully coupled, the species are part of the unknown vector in Newton's method; the Jacobian acts matrix-free through difference quotients, the preconditioner remains the loose step. Where the composition acts strongly back on the flow (mixtures with hydrogen, flames), this converges in far fewer steps.

What follows. The mass fractions keep summing to one as long as the flow is converged and the diffusive fluxes sum to zero – both are ensured in the discretisation, not normalised afterwards. Operator splitting is first order in time; for steady flames that does not matter, for time-accurate ignition the time step must be small against the ignition time.

The recalculation of the derivations (mixture-averaged diffusion from Stefan-Maxwell in the limit, correction velocity, energy and temperature of the mixture, equivalent gas, the closed-form solution of the test problem and the first-order recursion) is in docs/theorie/rechnung/cfd_spezies.py.

Limits

  • No thermal and pressure diffusion, no full multicomponent diffusion.
  • Real gases only with fixed composition; real mixtures with species transport are not available.
  • Chemistry and turbulence without interaction model: the rates use the mean quantities of the cell (laminar chemistry).
  • Operator splitting of first order in time.
