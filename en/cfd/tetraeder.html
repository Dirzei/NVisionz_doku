Tetrahedral grid for arbitrary bodies – background

The computation behind Oberflaeche, Oberflaechenpruefung and Koerpernetz: from a closed triangulated surface a tetrahedral grid whose boundary reproduces the surface exactly – input check, Delaunay in three dimensions, boundary recovery with Steiner points, inside and outside, interior points graded towards the wall, removal of flat cells. With derivations, assumptions, checks and limits.

What this is about

A body is given by its surface: points xi\mathbf x_i and triangles of three point numbers each, every triangle with the name of its group (say einlass, wand). Wanted is a grid of tetrahedra that fills exactly this body – boundary-conforming: every input triangle is the union of boundary faces of the grid, no boundary face sticks out of it, and the boundary faces carry the group name of their triangle. What the probe measures follows at once: the grid volume is the volume of the surface, every group keeps its area, and the boundary hull has the topology of the body.

The surface does not depend on a file format: new Oberflaeche(punkte, dreiecke, gruppen) with 3n3n coordinates, 3m3m point numbers and one name per triangle. A reader for triangle files or meshed CAD faces only has to fill these three arrays. Koerpernetz.vernetze(oberflaeche, h) returns the grid with its key figures; Optionen set growth, largest spacing, surface refinement and the boundary type per group.

Checking the surface

A tetrahedral grid can only be placed in a body that the surface really encloses. Oberflaechenpruefung.pruefe therefore checks before meshing – loudly, with the numbers of the points and triangles concerned, the first ten of each kind individually, the rest as a count:

  1. Numbers: no coordinate NaN, infinite or larger than 101210^{12}.
  2. Welding: points closer than 10−910^{-9} times the diagonal of the bounding box become one point (grid of buckets of that size, neighbouring buckets searched as well). Triangles whose corners coincide in the process are dropped with a note.
  3. Degenerate and duplicate: a triangle with a height below the tolerance, or the same three corners twice, is an error.
  4. Closed and manifold: every edge belongs to exactly two triangles (one: open edge, more: non-manifold edge); the triangles around a point form a single fan (two fans touching only in the point: non-manifold point).
  5. Orientation: across shared edges every triangle is turned so that neighbours traverse the edge in opposite directions (breadth-first search per shell). If that fails, the surface is not orientable – an error. Turned triangles are reported.
  6. Shells and holes: the volume of every shell from Gauss's theorem, V=16∑tat⋅(bt×ct),V = \tfrac16 \sum_{t} \mathbf a_t \cdot (\mathbf b_t \times \mathbf c_t), (reference point the first point, so that large coordinates do not cancel). Whether a shell lies inside another is told by the winding number of one of its points with respect to the other shell, the sum of the solid angles of its triangles divided by 4π4\pi with tan⁡Ω2=a⋅(b×c)|a||b||c|+(a⋅b)|c|+(a⋅c)|b|+(b⋅c)|a|\tan\frac{\Omega}{2} = \frac{\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)}{|\mathbf a||\mathbf b||\mathbf c| + (\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)|\mathbf c| + (\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf c)|\mathbf b| + (\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf c)|\mathbf a|} (van Oosterom and Strackee). A shell at even depth points outwards, at odd depth it is a hole and points inwards; a shell running the wrong way is turned as a whole.
  7. Self-intersection: pairs of triangles whose bounding boxes overlap are searched in a grid of buckets (each pair in one bucket only) and intersected: without a shared point every contact is an intersection, also an edge that meets the other triangle exactly on its boundary and an edge lying in its plane; with one shared point only the opposite edges count, with a shared edge only folding onto each other.
  8. Volume of the whole surface positive. As a note the Euler characteristic χ=V−E+F\chi = V - E + F (sphere 2, torus 0, cube with a cubic hole 4).

Method

Refining the surface

Optionally (default) the surface is brought to the target spacing hh without changing its geometry: every input edge is split into ⌈ℓ/h⌉\lceil \ell/h\rceil equal parts, every input triangle is triangulated in its plane after Delaunay and refined after Ruppert – as long as a sub-triangle has a circumradius above 0.65h0.65\,h or is badly shaped (circumradius above 1.18 times the shortest edge, roughly 25°), its circumcentre is added. If that lies in the diametral sphere of a sub-segment of an edge or outside the triangle, the sub-segment is split instead. All new points lie on the input triangles; they remember which edge or triangle they lie on.

Delaunay in three dimensions

The boundary points are inserted into a large auxiliary tetrahedron after Bowyer and Watson, in the order of a Morton curve, so that the walk to the next point stays short. A new point qq removes all cells in whose circumsphere it lies (the cavity) and connects every boundary face of the cavity with itself. In floating point the cavity is not always star-shaped from qq – with many points in a plane (faces of a cube, trapezoids of a torus) flat or inverted cells would result. Therefore every boundary face must be properly visible from qq:

  • Mandatory cells are the cell containing qq and every cell on whose face qq lies; they stay in the cavity.
  • If a boundary face of another cell is not visible, that cell leaves the cavity and does not come back (shrinking the cavity after Si).
  • If a boundary face of a mandatory cell is not properly visible – qq lies on it, in its plane or behind it –, the cell behind it becomes mandatory as well. The mandatory set only grows, so the method ends. Without this rule, many points on a circle in a plane (rings of a finely divided torus) left a flat cell on an input triangle.

The result is a valid triangulation, at a few places only nearly Delaunay; the boundary recovery afterwards checks whatever depends on it.

Boundary recovery

The Delaunay triangulation of the boundary points does not contain the surface by itself. For the flat oblique body of the probe (a parallelepiped whose faces are split along the longer diagonal) the triangulation of the eight corners lacks six sub-segments and twelve triangles: it chooses the other diagonal in every face. Without recovery the boundary would be wrong.

Recovery works as a conforming Delaunay tetrahedralisation: instead of flipping faces, Steiner points are put on the surface until every sub-segment and every sub-triangle is an edge or face of the Delaunay triangulation. The basis is a simple theorem: a segment whose diametral sphere contains no other point is an edge of every Delaunay triangulation; likewise a triangle whose smallest enclosing sphere is empty. Splitting shrinks the spheres until they are empty. Round by round:

  1. Sub-segments: if a sub-segment of an input edge is missing from the grid, it is split – in the middle, or, if exactly one end is an input corner, at a power-of-two distance from it between one and two thirds of the length. These concentric shells (Ruppert, Shewchuk) make sure that two edges meeting at an acute angle do not split each other endlessly: their split points lie on the same spheres around the corner.
  2. Sub-triangles: if a facet – an input triangle all of whose edges are present – is not covered, that is the sum of the grid faces in it is smaller than its area, a missing sub-triangle is searched in the planar Delaunay triangulation of its points and its circumcentre inserted. If that lies in the diametral sphere of a sub-segment or outside the facet, the sub-segment is split instead.

The rounds end when nothing is missing; after at most 400 rounds the method stops with the number of missing sub-segments and triangles. The grid sees nothing special: the Steiner points are ordinary boundary nodes on edges and faces.

Inside and outside

The cells of the auxiliary tetrahedron are outside. A breadth-first search over the cell neighbours carries the location along and switches it at every face whose three corners lie on the same input triangle. Holes and several shells thus come out right without a special case. If a cell is reached on two paths with different locations, or a cell with a corner of the auxiliary tetrahedron would be inside, the boundary is incomplete – this is loudly rejected, never silently assumed.

Interior points and grading

Points are put into the interior according to the desired spacing

h(d)=min⁡(hmax⁡,h+zd),h(d) = \min\bigl(h_{\max},\; h + z\,d\bigr),

with the wall distance dd (nearest input triangle via a bounding-box tree) and the growth zz (default 0.3). The points come from grids of spacing s=h2ks = h\,2^k, each grid point slightly perturbed; a point belongs to the level whose spacing matches the desired one (\(s \le h(d) < 2s\)). A point closer to the wall than \(0.7\,s\) or than the circumradius of the nearest input triangle is dropped, as is one closer than \(0.6\,s\) to a point already chosen. Afterwards the boundary is recovered again – interior points inside a diametral sphere can push boundary edges out.

Quality measures and slivers

Measured is the smallest dihedral angle of every cell, the angle between two faces at their common edge:

cos⁡θij=n1⋅n2|n1||n2|,n1=(xj−xi)×(xk−xi),n2=(xj−xi)×(xl−xi).\cos\theta_{ij} = \frac{\mathbf n_1\cdot\mathbf n_2}{|\mathbf n_1||\mathbf n_2|},\qquad \mathbf n_1 = (\mathbf x_j-\mathbf x_i)\times(\mathbf x_k-\mathbf x_i),\ \mathbf n_2 = (\mathbf x_j-\mathbf x_i)\times(\mathbf x_l-\mathbf x_i).

Delaunay in three dimensions does not prevent slivers – tetrahedra of four nearly coplanar points on a nearly common sphere, with dihedral angles near 0° and 180°. A cell below 5° counts as a sliver and is removed, at most 40 rounds:

  • If it has an interior point, that point is perturbed by 15 % of its spacing (as in stage 1, Tetraedernetz.quader).
  • If it consists of boundary points only, it is usually a flat ear over the crease between two input triangles. Perturbing does not help here. But a point in its circumsphere destroys it (Delaunay refinement after Shewchuk). The centre of the circumsphere lies close to the wall, often outside; it is moved inwards by 0.7R0.7\,R, 0.85R0.85\,R or 0.97R0.97\,R – still inside the sphere – and placed as soon as it lies inside at least 0.15R0.15\,R from the wall and 0.3R0.3\,R from the nearest point.
  • If that fails too, its edge and face points are perturbed along the edge or within their plane; input corners stay fixed.

After every round everything is triangulated anew and the boundary recovered. For the sphere of 1280 triangles at h=0.15h = 0.15, 606 slivers remained without the ears (smallest angle 0.33°); with them none.

From the result to the grid

The interior cells go into the grid via the Netzbauer; each is ordered so that its triple product (b−a)×(c−a)⋅(d−a)(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)\times(\mathbf c-\mathbf a)\cdot(\mathbf d-\mathbf a) is positive – the VTK orientation. Boundary faces carry the group name of their input triangle; the boundary type comes from the mapping in the options, otherwise from the name itself (einlass, auslass, symmetrie, fernfeld, …), otherwise it is a wall. Unused points are dropped.

Assumptions

  • The surface is closed, manifold and free of self-intersections; the check ensures that or rejects.
  • Input corners are never moved; edge and face points only along their edge or within their triangle. The geometry of the surface is thus exact up to rounding.
  • Floating point without exact predicates; the thresholds are set relative to the edge lengths.

What follows

  • Volume and areas are exact (up to rounding): Gauss volume of the input equals the sum of the cell volumes, every group equals its input area.
  • A fluid at rest stays at rest, a uniform flow stays uniform – with Green-Gauss gradients; with least squares even the uniform flow starts to move on tetrahedra.
  • Run time: sphere of 5120 triangles at h=0.1h = 0.1: 22,198 tetrahedra in 1.3 to 4 s depending on the load of the machine (5,000 to 17,000 cells/s); torus of 2304 triangles at h=0.05h = 0.05: 98,479 tetrahedra in 14 to 24 s. Most of it goes into the sliver rounds, which triangulate anew each time.

How it is checked

  • Koerpernetzprobe [SN60-01 … 05]: cube, L-prism, sphere (icosahedron subdivided twice), torus, cube with a cubic hole. Reference values independent of the method: Gauss volume, area per group, Euler characteristic of the boundary hull (2, 2, 2, 0, 4), every input triangle covered exactly (centroid of every boundary face in exactly one input triangle of its group, area sums equal), all volumes positive in the VTK orientation, grid check without findings.
  • [SN60-06] oblique body: without recovery 6 sub-segments and 12 triangles are missing; [SN60-07] the same bodies without surface refinement, the input points stay nodes.
  • [SN60-08] grading: with growth 0.6 about a quarter fewer cells, twice as large in the middle; [SN60-09] smallest dihedral angle at least 5°.
  • [SN60-10], [SN60-11] input: welded loose triangles, turned shells and triangles, the hole oriented outwards; open surface, shared edge, shared corner, intersection, NaN, 1020010^{200}, degenerate triangle, too small hh – all loudly rejected.
  • [SN60-16] surface finer than hh: torus of 48 × 24 trapezoids at h=0.2h = 0.2 and without refinement at h=0.3h = 0.3.
  • [SN60-12] Euler solver: at rest in the sphere and uniform oblique in the cube, deviation at rounding level; [SN60-13] run time; [SN60-14] boundary types; [SN60-15] plates 1 × 1 × 0.1 at h=0.1h = 0.1 without slivers and 1 × 1 × 0.03 at h=0.2h = 0.2 – volume exact, the remaining slivers reported.
  • Command line: -form:tetrakoerper -koerper:kugel|wuerfel|lprisma|torus|wuerfelloch|schief -h: (Stroemungprobe [SN60-CMD-01]).

Limits

  • Thin parts: if the body is thinner than hh and not refined, slivers remain (a plate 1 × 1 × 0.03 at h=0.2h = 0.2: smallest dihedral angle below 1°). Remedy: adapt hh to the thickness. The grid reports remaining slivers as a note.
  • Acute angles between input triangles (below about 30°) can demand many Steiner points; after 400 rounds recovery stops with a message.
  • No boundary layer of prisms; the grid consists of tetrahedra only, so the choice of gradients above applies to the solver.
  • Interior points keep at least the circumradius of the nearest input triangle away from the wall – without refinement a coarsely triangulated surface therefore stays coarse inside.
  • The surface is neither smoothed nor coarsened; what comes as a triangle stays boundary.
