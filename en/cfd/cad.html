Meshing CAD faces – background

The computation behind CadOberflaeche: from a boundary representation (STEP AP203/AP214, IGES 5.3) to a closed triangle surface for the tetrahedral grid. Edges first and shared, every carrier surface mapped to the plane, Delaunay there with fixed boundary segments, seams, poles and cone apexes, surfaces of revolution over unbounded profiles. With derivations, checks and limits.

What it is about

A CAD body is given by its faces: each lies on a carrier surface (plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus, B-spline, surface of revolution or linear extrusion) and is bounded by loops of edges; each edge lies on a curve between two vertices. Neighbouring faces share their edges. What is wanted is a triangle surface that Koerpernetz accepts: closed, manifold, oriented outwards, the triangles of each face carrying its name as group – exactly what Oberflaechenpruefung demands (see Tetrahedral grid for arbitrary bodies).

The readers in framework-fileformats (StepBrep, IgesBrep) deliver the model as data (CadModell); CadOberflaeche.vernetze(model, h) turns it into the surface, Optionen set the smallest angle (default 25°), the maximum number of points and the Delaunay flip in space. The Ergebnis reports faces, edges, rounds, the smallest angle in space, the largest deviation of the vertices from their curves, and notes.

Edges first, and only once

The surface is only closed if two faces use the same points on their common edge. Every edge is therefore divided exactly once before any face is meshed, and both faces take over that division (in their own direction). The division follows the arc length: a table of 512 sections measures the length LL, the edge is cut into pieces of equal length, so many that none is longer than hh and the tangent turns by no more than 30° over any of them; a closed edge gets at least three. The parameters of the vertices come from projecting the vertex points onto the curve: in closed form for line and circle, otherwise 400 samples and golden-section search; on periodic curves the end parameter runs on in the direction of the edge by at most one period.

Every face to the plane

Meshing happens in a plane w=(a,b)w = (a, b) per face. A map takes the carrier there, as length-preserving as possible, so that a spacing hh in the plane is roughly hh in space:

Boundary points go to the plane through the inverse of the map. Where an edge runs across a seam, the nearest copy is chosen so the loop stays continuous; an edge that occurs twice in one loop (the seam itself) appears on both sides of the strip.

The cone apex

A cone can be developed without distortion: the mantle becomes a circular sector with opening angle Φ=2πsin⁡α\Phi = 2\pi\sin\alpha. The seam is then not a shift but a rotation by Φ\Phi about the apex. If the boundary of a face runs through the apex, it must switch there from the incoming to the outgoing edge, into the copy that encloses an interior angle β\beta with \(0 < \beta \le \Phi\) with the incoming one (counter-clockwise, reversed for a face of opposite sense):

$$\beta_k = \vartheta_{\rm in} - (\vartheta_{\rm out} + k\,\Phi), \qquad 0 < \beta_k \le \Phi .$$

Exactly one kk satisfies this as long as the angles are not taken modulo 2π2\pi. Modulo 2π2\pi, two rotated copies fall into the interval for Φ>2π/3\Phi > 2\pi/3 – on the cone of the probe (Φ=161°\Phi = 161°) the wrong one won with 38°, the boundary stayed open, and the cuts across the apex lay on the seam. That is how the first version did it; found by the probe, fixed.

Poles and the sphere

At a pole of a parametric surface (one parameter line collapses to a point, e.g. the top of a surface of revolution whose profile meets the axis) the boundary runs along the pole line in the plane for a while; all points there become the same point in space. The sphere has a map of its own because its parameters degenerate at both poles: stereographic, projection point outside the face, image plane at the antipode. A full sphere whose boundary is only the seam is split into two hemispheres.

Surfaces of revolution and extrusion over unbounded profiles

STEP often gives the profile of a SURFACE_OF_REVOLUTION or SURFACE_OF_LINEAR_EXTRUSION as an unbounded line (LINE) or as a full circle. Only the boundary of the face says which part is used. For a surface of revolution, every boundary point p\mathbf p is rotated about the axis (point p0\mathbf p_0, direction e\mathbf e) into the plane of the profile – same axial position, same distance –

q=p0+ae±ρe1,a=(p−p0)⋅e,ρ=|(p−p0)−ae|,\mathbf q = \mathbf p_0 + a\,\mathbf e \pm \rho\,\mathbf e_1, \quad a = (\mathbf p - \mathbf p_0)\cdot\mathbf e, \quad \rho = \bigl|(\mathbf p - \mathbf p_0) - a\,\mathbf e\bigr|,

and projected onto the profile; the range runs from the smallest to the largest parameter. The sign (which half of the profile plane) is the one for which all rotated points lie on the profile – the base point of the line may lie on the other side of the axis. For a full circle the parameters lie on a circle; the arc in use lies opposite the largest gap between them, also across the end of the period. For the extrusion of a line c(t)=a+td\mathbf c(t) = \mathbf a + t\,\mathbf d along v\mathbf v only the part across the direction counts: with d⊥=d−(d⋅v^)v^\mathbf d_\perp = \mathbf d - (\mathbf d\cdot\hat{\mathbf v})\hat{\mathbf v}, t=(p−a)⋅d⊥/|d⊥|2t = (\mathbf p - \mathbf a)\cdot\mathbf d_\perp / |\mathbf d_\perp|^2.

By ISO 10303-42, uu is the angle of rotation and vv the profile parameter of a surface of revolution; the normal ∂uS×∂vS\partial_u\mathbf S \times \partial_v\mathbf S is therefore direction of rotation × profile direction. A face with same_sense = .F. reverses it.

Triangles per face

The loops in the plane go to the planar generator Dreiecksnetz (Delaunay after Bowyer-Watson, refinement after Ruppert, see Grid and geometry) with spacing h/m(w)h/m(w) – mm is the scale of the map, for the sphere (1/(1+|w|2/4R2))(1/(1 + |w|^2/4R^2)). All boundary segments are fixed: they are not split, because their points also belong to the neighbouring face. Therein lies the one difficulty: a fixed segment whose ends have no empty circle cannot be contained in the planar triangulation. That happens when points lie close on both sides of the segment – at a narrow gap between two thin webs; beyond a free side there is always an empty circle. Dreiecksnetz then collects all missing fixed segments; CadOberflaeche splits the edge pieces they come from at the middle of their parameter and starts the round again – for all faces on those edges, so they stay shared. If a segment comes from no edge (a cut across a pole or a seam), the face itself is divided more finely. After at most 40 rounds it stops.

The interior points go to space through the map. Optionally (default) interior edges are then flipped when the two opposite angles in space add up to more than 180° and the flip is possible in the plane – Delaunay on the surface rather than in the map. The triangles get their orientation from map, face sense and shell sense; artificial points (pole, apex) are welded to the edge points (10−810^{-8} of the model size).

How it is checked

  • Cadoberflaechenprobe writes its bodies itself as STEP (and one as IGES) and compares with closed-form values: Gauss volume against the exact one, allowed is the sagitta of the longest edge over the whole area; Euler characteristic; area per group; closed and manifold by Oberflaechenpruefung.
  • [SN61-01] cube (faces exact, without names the number of the face), [SN61-02] cylinder analytic and as rational B-spline surface (bottom and top with the same circle division), [SN61-03] cone (apex without slivers), [SN61-04] sphere and hemisphere, [SN61-05] torus with two seams.
  • [SN61-06] elliptic extrusion, frustum as surface of revolution over a LINE – also with the base point beyond the axis, then with as many points –, dome over a full circle (also with the arc across the end of the period), cube with an extrusion over an oblique line and a bottom registered with opposite sense.
  • [SN61-07] box with bore (Euler characteristic 0) and a C of two webs with a 0.005 gap at h=0.2h = 0.2: four rounds of splitting, top face exact.
  • [SN61-08] convergence: the volume deviation of the cylinder falls by a factor 4.09 when hh is halved (second order of the chords).
  • [SN61-09] IGES cube equal to the STEP cube, [SN61-10] hand-over to Koerpernetz (tetrahedral volume = surface volume), [SN61-11] error cases: unknown carrier, open shell, hh zero or NaN.

Limits

  • Poles of surfaces of revolution are meshed through the parameter plane; sharp triangles appear at the pole (dome of the probe: smallest angle 10° instead of 25°), but no slivers.
  • Faces narrower than hh do not get wider: their triangles follow the fixed edge points (webs of 0.005 at h=0.2h = 0.2: 1.4°). The remedy is a smaller hh.
  • Offset surfaces (OFFSET_SURFACE), surfaces on surfaces and curves the reader does not know are rejected by name.
  • Dividing a face more finely for missing cut segments across a pole or seam is a safeguard; no body of the probe needs it.
  • Edges are divided by arc length and curvature, not by the curvature of the adjacent faces; a strongly curved face between straight edges needs a small hh.
