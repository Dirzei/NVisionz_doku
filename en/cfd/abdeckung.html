Methods and test cases – coverage overview

Which methods the solver knows, which of them a case of the collection runs, which only a probe checks and which are missing; together with the test cases for shocks and boundary layers that a transonic compressor rotor calls for.

What this is about

The solver has many switches. Most are options that change nothing when left out. Whether a method is computed correctly is shown by two things: a probe that checks it against a known solution or a second calculation by hand (with counter-probes that a spoiled copy must turn red), and a case of the collection that shows it on a flow with a measurement or reference solution. This page puts both side by side.

The case counts are measured: each of the 120 cases under envisionz-examples/cfd was set up from its case file without computing, and the methods chosen by the solver were read back – that is, what the solver actually chooses, including the defaults chosen from the mesh and those taken from the m-ACHIEVE inputs. As of 2026-11-26.

What Rotor 37 calls for

In the transonic compressor rotor everything comes together at once: a bow shock and a passage shock in the relative frame, the shock meets the turbulent boundary layer on the suction side and separates it, the tip vortex crosses the shock under the casing, all in a rotating frame with rotationally periodic boundaries, and the comparison is against a characteristic and radial profiles. Two gaps stand out. First, no simpler case checks the interaction of a shock with a turbulent boundary layer in a duct or at a corner, and none shows a shock-induced turbulent separation – exactly the phenomenon at which the Rotor 37 calculation falls short of the measurement. Second, all viscous cases other than Rotor 37 run in a stationary frame; tip gap and rotation are checked only on the rotor itself.

The cases of the collection

The collection has 120 cases in eight groups. Of these, 105 carry the state KONVERGIERT (converged) in their ergebnis.properties, 14 time-accurate ones ZEIT_ERREICHT (end time reached), and one (Rotor 37 with SST, fine mesh) is set up but not computed. Of the methods built in 2026-11 for Rotor 37 – skewness correction, GLSQ and WLSQ(G), U-MUSCL, SLAU2, entropy-stable flux, quadratic reconstruction, limiter on the absolute velocity, QCR, EARSM, hub leakage, floor of the CFL number, bound on the residual rise – only the skewness correction runs a case of the collection (the sheared Blasius plate). The others are checked by their probes and measured on Rotor 37, but not shown on any simpler case with a measurement. The compressibility corrections after Sarkar and Wilcox, k-ω after Wilcox 2006 and the Reynolds-stress model SSG/LRR-ω are so far checked only by their probes and not yet computed on Rotor 37; SST-RC has been running there since 2026-11-27 and has not converged yet.

What follows

  1. Shock on a turbulent boundary layer in a duct first. Délery, Bachalo and Johnson and the Sajben diffuser are small (two-dimensional or axisymmetric), measured and check exactly the phenomenon at which Rotor 37 deviates. On them the methods measured only on Rotor 37 (QCR, EARSM, gradients, fluxes) can be compared in minutes instead of hours.
  2. Rotation without a blade. The rotating channel separates the effect of rotation on the turbulence model from the rest of the rotor.
  3. Tip gap without rotation. A cascade with a tip gap checks the meshes of the gap band and the tip vortex without a shock.
  4. Cases for the new options. Where a method runs only in a probe, it belongs in at least one case with a measurement before it is judged on the rotor.

Limits of this overview

  • Counted is what the case files select; how well a case agrees with its measurement is on its validation page, not here.
  • The assignment to phenomena follows the descriptions of the cases and is not evaluated from the result field.
  • The table Which methods the cases run is generated: Stroemung -op:fallinfo sets up each case and reads the features of the solver, a generator then counts; it is updated with every new run of the generator. The other tables and the figure are by hand and reflect the state of 2026-11-26.
