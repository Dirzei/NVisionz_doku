Time integration and solvers – background

The mathematics behind EulerLoeser and Implizit: explicit Runge-Kutta schemes (SSP-RK3, Jameson-RK4), local time steps and the CFL number, LU-SGS with a Rusanov Jacobian and an optional exact boundary derivative, Newton-Krylov with GMRES and block ILU(0) or multigrid, pseudo-time with SER control, dual time stepping for time-accurate implicit runs and the convergence measure – with derivations, assumptions, checks and limits.

What this is about

The spatial discretisation (fluxes and reconstruction) turns the Euler or Navier-Stokes equations into a large system of ordinary differential equations, one per cell and conserved quantity:

VcdUcdt+Rc(U)=0,Uc=(ρ,ρu,ρv,ρw,ρE)c.V_c \frac{d \mathbf{U}_c}{d t} + \mathbf{R}_c(\mathbf{U}) = 0, \qquad \mathbf{U}_c = (\rho, \rho u, \rho v, \rho w, \rho E)_c .

VcV_c is the cell volume, Rc\mathbf{R}_c the sum of the fluxes over the cell faces (positive outward) including sources. Two tasks follow: compute time-accurately (U(t)\mathbf{U}(t) up to an end time) and compute steady states (R(U)=0\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}) = 0). For the first, accuracy in time matters; for the second, time is only a path to the solution, and every cell may advance with its own step.

The module offers four schemes (Einstellungen.zeit): SSPRK3 and JAMESON4 explicit, IMPLIZIT (LU-SGS) and NEWTON (Newton-Krylov) implicit. The implicit ones are steady; they become time-accurate through dual time stepping.

Time step and CFL number

An explicit scheme is stable as long as a wave does not travel further than one cell per step. With the spectral sum of the cell

Λc=∑f∈c(|vf⋅nf|+cf)Sf\Lambda_c = \sum_{f \in c} \left( |\mathbf{v}_f \cdot \mathbf{n}_f| + c_f \right) S_f

(magnitude of the normal velocity plus speed of sound, times face area) the step is

Δtc=CFL⁡VcΛc.\Delta t_c = \mathrm{CFL} \, \frac{V_c}{\Lambda_c} .

With lokal = true every cell uses its own Δtc\Delta t_c – correct for steady states, because the time step no longer appears once R=0\mathbf{R} = 0, and fast, because small cells do not slow down the large ones. With lokal = false all cells take the minimum, additionally limited by the remaining time to the end; bis(t) thus hits the end time exactly. Time-accurate runs with local steps or with an implicit scheme are rejected.

Assumptions. Λc\Lambda_c bounds the spectral radius of the Jacobian of Rc\mathbf{R}_c relative to the volume; the CFL limit of a scheme follows from its stability region (below). On distorted meshes and with limiters the limit is smaller than the linear analysis suggests.

SSP-RK3 (Shu-Osher)

The three-stage scheme of Shu and Osher writes every stage as a convex combination of explicit Euler steps:

U(1)=Un−ΔtVR(Un),U(2)=34Un+14(U(1)−ΔtVR(U(1))),Un+1=13Un+23(U(2)−ΔtVR(U(2))).\begin{aligned} \mathbf{U}^{(1)} &= \mathbf{U}^n - \frac{\Delta t}{V} \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}^n), \\ \mathbf{U}^{(2)} &= \frac{3}{4} \mathbf{U}^n + \frac{1}{4} \left( \mathbf{U}^{(1)} - \frac{\Delta t}{V} \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}^{(1)}) \right), \\ \mathbf{U}^{n+1} &= \frac{1}{3} \mathbf{U}^n + \frac{2}{3} \left( \mathbf{U}^{(2)} - \frac{\Delta t}{V} \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}^{(2)}) \right). \end{aligned}

Order. As a Butcher tableau: c=(0,1,12)c = (0, 1, \tfrac12), a21=1a_{21} = 1, a31=a32=14a_{31} = a_{32} = \tfrac14, b=(16,16,23)b = (\tfrac16, \tfrac16, \tfrac23). The four third-order conditions ∑bi=1\sum b_i = 1, ∑bici=12\sum b_i c_i = \tfrac12, ∑bici2=13\sum b_i c_i^2 = \tfrac13, ∑biaijcj=16\sum b_i a_{ij} c_j = \tfrac16 hold; for R=−λVU\mathbf{R} = -\lambda V \mathbf{U} the amplification factor is 1+z+z2/2+z3/61 + z + z^2/2 + z^3/6 with z=−λΔtz = -\lambda \Delta t. The second stage is evaluated at tn+Δtt^n + \Delta t, the third at tn+Δt/2t^n + \Delta t/2 – as in the code, for time-dependent boundaries.

What follows. Since every stage is a mean of Euler steps with non-negative weights, the scheme inherits every property the Euler step has at CFL⁡≤1\mathrm{CFL} \le 1 (positivity, TVD) with the same CFL limit (SSP coefficient 1). On the imaginary axis (pure convection) the stability region extends to 3\sqrt 3, on the negative real axis to about −2.51-2.51.

Jameson-RK4

The scheme of Jameson, Schmidt and Turkel computes every stage from the initial state and needs only two fields:

U(k)=U(0)−αkΔtVR(U(k−1)),α=(14,13,12,1),Un+1=U(4).\mathbf{U}^{(k)} = \mathbf{U}^{(0)} - \alpha_k \frac{\Delta t}{V} \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}^{(k-1)}), \qquad \alpha = \left( \frac14, \frac13, \frac12, 1 \right), \qquad \mathbf{U}^{n+1} = \mathbf{U}^{(4)} .

Derivation. For a linear equation the amplification factor is nested

P(z)=1+α4z(1+α3z(1+α2z(1+α1z)))=1+z+z22+z36+z424,P(z) = 1 + \alpha_4 z \left( 1 + \alpha_3 z \left( 1 + \alpha_2 z \left( 1 + \alpha_1 z \right) \right) \right) = 1 + z + \frac{z^2}{2} + \frac{z^3}{6} + \frac{z^4}{24},

the classical RK4 polynomial and fourth order. As a Butcher tableau, however, the scheme has only the subdiagonal ak+1,k=αka_{k+1,k} = \alpha_k and b=(0,0,0,1)b = (0, 0, 0, 1). Then ∑bici=α3=12\sum b_i c_i = \alpha_3 = \tfrac12 – second order – but ∑bici2=α32=14≠13\sum b_i c_i^2 = \alpha_3^2 = \tfrac14 \ne \tfrac13: for non-linear R\mathbf{R} the order stays two. Second order depends only on α4=1\alpha_4 = 1 and α3=12\alpha_3 = \tfrac12; α1\alpha_1 and α2\alpha_2 shape only the stability region and the linear order.

What follows. For steady states the order is irrelevant; what counts is the stability region: up to 22≈2.832\sqrt 2 \approx 2.83 on the imaginary axis, to about −2.79-2.79 on the real axis – almost three times that of the Euler step for four evaluations. For time-accurate runs SSPRK3 is the better choice (third order, SSP). The probe measures both: on the same mesh with three CFL numbers in the ratio 4 : 2 : 1 the spatial error cancels from the differences, and

p=log2⁡‖U4−U2‖‖U2−U1‖p = \log_2 \frac{\|\mathbf{U}_4 - \mathbf{U}_2\|}{\|\mathbf{U}_2 - \mathbf{U}_1\|}

is the temporal order (measured 3.0 for SSP-RK3 and 2.2 for Jameson-RK4 with a small perturbation).

Convergence measure

Explicitly, norm() measures the root mean square of the density residual per volume,

r=1N∑c(Rρ,cVc)2,r = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_c \left( \frac{R_{\rho,c}}{V_c} \right)^2 },

before the step. stationaer(n, k) relates it to the largest of the first ten residuals – the start often rises first while waves leave the boundaries – and stops once it has fallen by kk orders of magnitude; with turbulence or species equations, their residuals (each relative to its own start) must have fallen as well. Implicitly, the scaled total residual of all five equations counts, each divided by a reference (largest density, largest momentum, at least ρc0\rho c_0, largest energy), so that CFL control does not depend on the density alone.

Implicit: backward Euler in pseudo-time

For steady problems the explicit CFL limit is the bottleneck: at a boundary layer with cells 10−610^{-6} m high a pressure wave needs millions of steps to cross the domain. The backward Euler step in local pseudo-time, linearised about Un\mathbf{U}^n,

(VΔtI+∂R∂U)ΔU=−R(Un),\left( \frac{V}{\Delta t} I + \frac{\partial \mathbf{R}}{\partial \mathbf{U}} \right) \Delta \mathbf{U} = -\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}^n),

is stable for every Δt\Delta t; for Δt→∞\Delta t \to \infty it is the Newton step for R=0\mathbf{R} = 0. The art lies in solving the system cheaply enough and letting Δt\Delta t grow as fast as the non-linearity permits.

LU-SGS

Following Yoon and Jameson, the first-order Jacobian is built from the Rusanov flux: for Ff=12(F(UL)+F(UR))Sf−12λfSf(UR−UL)F_f = \tfrac12 (F(U_L) + F(U_R)) S_f - \tfrac12 \lambda_f S_f (U_R - U_L)

∂Ff∂UL=12(AL+λfI)Sf,∂Ff∂UR=12(AR−λfI)Sf.\frac{\partial F_f}{\partial U_L} = \frac{1}{2} (A_L + \lambda_f I) S_f, \qquad \frac{\partial F_f}{\partial U_R} = \frac{1}{2} (A_R - \lambda_f I) S_f .

The diagonal of a cell sums 12AcSf\tfrac12 A_c S_f over all faces – that is 12Ac∑fSfnf=0\tfrac12 A_c \sum_f S_f \mathbf{n}_f = 0 for a closed cell – and 12λfSf\tfrac12 \lambda_f S_f. It is therefore scalar:

Dc=VcΔtc+12Λc=Λc(1CFL⁡+12),D_c = \frac{V_c}{\Delta t_c} + \frac{1}{2} \Lambda_c = \Lambda_c \left( \frac{1}{\mathrm{CFL}} + \frac{1}{2} \right),

with the local step from above (plus the diagonal of the dual time derivative). The matrix splits into D+L+UD + L + U by the cell numbering, and a symmetric Gauss-Seidel sweep

(D+L)D−1(D+U)ΔU=−R(D + L) D^{-1} (D + U) \, \Delta \mathbf{U} = -\mathbf{R}

needs only products AjΔUjA_j \, \Delta \mathbf{U}_j of the neighbours – no stored matrix, no block inverses. Forward: \(\Delta \mathbf{U}^*_c = D_c^{-1} \left( -\mathbf{R}_c - \sum_{j < c} \tfrac12 (A_j - \lambda_f I) S_f \Delta \mathbf{U}^*_j \right)\), backward likewise with the neighbours \(j > c\). With luSgsFarben the sweeps run in parallel by the colours of a cell colouring; that is a different but equally valid Gauss-Seidel.

Boundary derivative. In LU-SGS boundary faces first enter only with the diagonal 12λfSf\tfrac12 \lambda_f S_f; the dependence of the ghost state on the interior state is missing. Optionally (luSgsRandableitung) randAbleitung forms a 5×5 block ∂Ff(Uc,G(Uc))/∂Uc\partial F_f(U_c, G(U_c)) / \partial U_c per boundary cell, using the chain rule through the boundary condition GG, and inverts it in the sweeps. This helps where the boundary condition controls convergence (outlets, slip walls).

What follows. LU-SGS is cheap and robust, but the first-order Jacobian with Rusanov dissipation is not that of the second-order scheme: convergence is linear and limited even at large CFL. Fast convergence needs the true Jacobian – Newton.

Newton-Krylov

Following Knoll and Keyes, the linear system is solved with GMRES without assembling the Jacobian. Its product with a vector is a directional derivative:

∂R∂Uv≈R(U+εv)−R(U)ε,ε=10−71+‖U‖‖v‖,\frac{\partial \mathbf{R}}{\partial \mathbf{U}} \, \mathbf{v} \approx \frac{\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U} + \varepsilon \mathbf{v}) - \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U})}{\varepsilon}, \qquad \varepsilon = 10^{-7} \, \frac{1 + \|\mathbf{U}\|}{\|\mathbf{v}\|},

computed in scaled quantities so that density and energy carry equal weight. GMRES thus sees the full second-order discretisation including boundaries and limiter (optionally frozen). Right preconditioning uses

  • block ILU(0) of the first-order Jacobian (5×5 blocks, mesh pattern) – default;
  • LU-SGS (three sweeps per application);
  • an algebraic block multigrid of the same matrix for large meshes.

The GMRES tolerance follows Eisenstat and Walker:

ηk=max⁡(10−8,min⁡(ηmax⁡,0.9(rkrk−1)2)),\eta_k = \max\left( 10^{-8}, \ \min\left( \eta_{\max}, \ 0.9 \left( \frac{r_k}{r_{k-1}} \right)^2 \right) \right),

coarse far from the solution (do not over-solve what the linearisation misses anyway), sharp near it, so that quadratic convergence is preserved.

GMRES itself is the method from framework-math, restarted after gmresNeustart, at most gmresSchritte steps.

Pseudo-time and SER

The CFL number follows the residual (switched evolution relaxation after Mulder and van Leer):

CFLk+1⁡=max⁡(min⁡(CFL0⁡,1),min⁡(CFLmax⁡⁡,CFLk⁡⋅f)),f=min⁡(10,max⁡(0.1,rk−1rk)).\mathrm{CFL}_{k+1} = \max\left( \min(\mathrm{CFL}_0, 1), \ \min\left( \mathrm{CFL}_{\max}, \ \mathrm{CFL}_k \cdot f \right) \right), \qquad f = \min\left( 10, \max\left( 0.1, \frac{r_{k-1}}{r_k} \right) \right).

If the residual falls, the CFL grows in the same ratio and the step approaches Newton; if it rises, it shrinks. If GMRES missed its tolerance, it is at least halved. If the residual stalls (change below 5 %) and cflStillstand > 1, the CFL grows by at least that factor – otherwise SER can sit on a plateau. If a step yields no physical state (negative density or pressure), it is discarded and repeated with a quarter of the CFL; after the first attempt and twelve repetitions or below CFL⁡=10−3\mathrm{CFL} = 10^{-3} the solver stops with location and hint. Each rejection quarters the CFL (counted from the smaller of base and current CFL); after thirteen rejections it stands at 4−13≈1.5⋅10−84^{-13} \approx 1.5 \cdot 10^{-8} times its starting value. The count limit therefore only acts before the lower bound for a large starting CFL (above 413⋅10−3≈6.7⋅1044^{13} \cdot 10^{-3} \approx 6.7 \cdot 10^4).

Assumptions. SER assumes that the residual is a useful measure of the distance to the solution – hence the scaled total residual. In flows with physical unsteadiness (separation, vortex street) it does not fall and the CFL stays small; then the problem must be computed time-accurately.

Dual time stepping

Time-accurate implicit after Jameson (1991): the physical time derivative is discretised as BDF2 and added to the residual as a source,

R*(U)=V32U−2Un+12Un−1Δt+R(U)=0,\mathbf{R}^*(\mathbf{U}) = V \, \frac{\frac32 \mathbf{U} - 2 \mathbf{U}^n + \frac12 \mathbf{U}^{n-1}}{\Delta t} + \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{U}) = 0,

and the equation of the new time level is solved in pseudo-time with LU-SGS or Newton until R*\mathbf{R}^* has fallen by the given orders of magnitude. In the first step and after a change of Δt\Delta t, BDF1 (U−Un\mathbf{U} - \mathbf{U}^n) is used, because the BDF2 weights assume equal steps.

Derivation. Taylor about tn+1t^{n+1}: 32un+1−2un+12un−1=Δtu'(tn+1)+O(Δt3)\frac32 u^{n+1} - 2 u^n + \frac12 u^{n-1} = \Delta t \, u'(t^{n+1}) + O(\Delta t^3); hence second order. The diagonal of the time derivative is ∂R*/∂U∋32V/Δt\partial \mathbf{R}^* / \partial \mathbf{U} \ni \frac32 V/\Delta t (BDF1: 1⋅V/Δt1 \cdot V/\Delta t) and enters DcD_c of LU-SGS and the Newton matrix.

What follows. BDF2 is A-stable; Δt\Delta t follows the physics, not the smallest cell. Accuracy depends on inner convergence: stopping too early does not give BDF2 but a less accurate scheme.

Course of a step

step():
  if IMPLIZIT or NEWTON:
    residual R(U), scales, diagonal D = Λ (1/CFL + 1/2) + a V/Δt_dual
    LU-SGS:  (D+L) D⁻¹ (D+U) ΔU = −R           ; Newton: GMRES with J v ≈ (R(U+εv) − R(U))/ε, η after Eisenstat-Walker
    U ← U + ΔU;  not physical → discard, CFL/4, at most 12 attempts
    CFL ← SER(r_old / r)
  else:
    r = norm(); Δt per cell = CFL V/Λ (global: minimum, limited to the end time)
    JAMESON4: U⁽ᵏ⁾ = U⁰ − α_k Δt/V R(U⁽ᵏ⁻¹⁾), α = 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 1
    SSPRK3:   Shu-Osher, stage times t, t+Δt, t+Δt/2
    t ← t + Δt

How it is checked

New probe envisionz.cfd.loeser.TheorieC2probe (under one second): temporal order of SSP-RK3 and Jameson-RK4 by Richardson comparison on the same mesh (TD22-C2-Z1, Z2), exact end time and rejection of local steps in time-accurate runs (Z3). In addition the existing probes: Implizitprobe (LU-SGS and Newton against Jameson-RK4, isentrope in a channel, quadratic convergence on the Ringleb case, boundary blocks), Navierprobe (total residual, BDF2), Faerbungsprobe, Periodenprobe (CFL stall), Speziesprobe. Four new counter-probes (td22c2-*) corrupt the stage weights and the last partial step; the probe notices all of them. The recalculation docs/theorie/rechnung/cfd_zeit.py checks stage polynomials, order conditions, stability limits, the LU-SGS diagonal, BDF2 and the formula of the measured order.

Limits

  • Jameson-RK4 is only second order in time for non-linear problems; use SSPRK3 for time-accurate runs.
  • Explicit time-accurate runs advance the whole domain with the step of the smallest cell; on boundary-layer meshes dual time stepping is usually cheaper.
  • The preconditioner Jacobian is first order with Rusanov dissipation; boundary profiles (radial equilibrium, mixing plane) stay frozen in it, Newton sees them only through the residual.
  • The abort after the discarded attempts only reports that no physical state was found; the cause (for instance a boundary that is itself unphysical) is found from the location and hint in the message.
  • The directional derivative with fixed ε\varepsilon loses digits at very small residuals; below about 10−1010^{-10} relative no further drop is to be expected.
