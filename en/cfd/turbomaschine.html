Turbomachinery – geometry, mesh and performance

How a blade geometry in MERIDL format becomes a mesh of one blade passage, and how the performance of a blade row follows from the flow field: averaging in measurement planes, pressure and temperature ratio, isentropic efficiency, radial profile and the comparison with measurements – with derivations, checks and limits.

What this is about

A blade row with ZZ identical blades is periodic: it is enough to compute one pitch 2π/Z2\pi/Z, with rotationally periodic boundaries on both sides (periodic boundaries) and in the rotating frame (time and solver). Three steps lead from the geometry to a statement about the machine:

  1. Read the geometry (Meridl): hub and casing contour and the blade in sections from hub to tip.
  2. Generate the mesh (Schaufelpassage): an H mesh over one pitch, the blade in the middle, with a tip gap at the casing.
  3. Evaluate (Turbokennwerte, Messvergleich): means in the inlet and outlet planes, performance of the row, radial profile and the comparison with measured data.

Geometry in MERIDL format

A MERIDL file describes the hub contour rN(z)r_N(z), the casing contour rG(z)r_G(z), the blade count ZZ and, for every blade section, points (z,r,θ1,θ2)(z, r, \theta_1, \theta_2) from leading to trailing edge; θ1,θ2\theta_1, \theta_2 are the circumferential angles of the two surfaces. Lengths are read in inches (factor 0.0254 m) or another unit, angles in radians. Three rules make the reader strict:

  • Count first, then allocate: all numbers in the file are counted and every count in the header is checked against the rest before any array is created. A damaged file allocates no huge array.
  • Ordered surfaces: θ1\theta_1 is always the surface with the larger angle; if they are swapped, they are exchanged and a note is given. Both surfaces must meet at the leading and trailing edge (within 10−410^{-4} rad) and must not cross in between.
  • Contours are interpolated shape-preserving (PCHIP) and continued constant outside the points.

The pitch is 2π/Z2\pi/Z. The sense of rotation in which the row works as a compressor follows from the slope of the camber line θ=(θ1+θ2)/2\bar\theta = (\theta_1 + \theta_2)/2: a compressor turns against the direction in which the camber line moves from leading to trailing edge, sign⁡ω=−sign⁡∂θ/∂z\operatorname{sign}\omega = -\operatorname{sign}\,\partial\bar\theta/\partial z.

The mesh of one pitch

Schaufelpassage builds an H mesh: ii runs axially (upstream part, blade, downstream part), jj circumferentially over one pitch, kk radially from the hub to the blade tip and on through the gap to the casing. The mesh line j=nθ/2j = n_\theta/2 lies on the blade; between the edges its nodes are doubled (one surface below, one above), upstream and downstream it continues on the camber line. The boundaries j=0j = 0 and j=nθj = n_\theta lie half a pitch to either side of the camber line and are rotated against each other by exactly 2π/Z2\pi/Z, node by node – as the rotationally periodic pairing requires.

  • Sections: every blade section is interpolated cubically over the arc length of its camber line and sampled at the same fractions, clustered towards the edges; across the sections over the arc length in the meridional plane. At the hub the blade is extended or shortened to the contour, at the tip it is cut where it lies one gap width below the casing.
  • Gap: cells fill the band between the two surfaces above the blade tip (prisms at the edges, where the surfaces meet); the lower face of the band is the tip (boundary schaufel).
  • Upstream and downstream parts follow hub and casing with the span fraction of the edge nodes. The camber line is continued with the slope at the edge and turns into the axial direction over a length of a few axial chords, so that the mesh does not kink at the edge.

The mesh can be checked against the geometry itself: the volume of all cells must equal the annulus of one pitch between hub and casing minus the blade volume, both computed independently from the file.

Euler's turbine equation as a check

For an inviscid, adiabatic blade row the work per unit mass equals the change of angular momentum:

cp(T0,2−T0,1)=ω(r2cθ,2−r1cθ,1).c_p\,(T_{0,2} - T_{0,1}) = \omega\,\big(r_2\,c_{\theta,2} - r_1\,c_{\theta,1}\big).

An Euler run on the passage mesh must satisfy it in the mass average; at the same time the mass flow through inlet and outlet must be equal. Both check the whole chain of mesh, rotating frame, periodic boundaries and evaluation.

Means and performance

A measurement plane is a boundary (the boundary cells with their faces) or a plane cut through the mesh normal to the axis. Over its elements kk with the area vector Ak\mathbf A_k (in the axial direction) and the mass flow m˙k=ρkwk⋅Ak\dot m_k = \rho_k\,\mathbf w_k\cdot\mathbf A_k the averages are

m˙=∑km˙k,p=∑kpk|Ak|∑k|Ak|,ϕ=∑km˙kϕkm˙for ϕ∈{p0,T0,cθ,cax,α}.\dot m = \sum_k \dot m_k, \qquad \bar p = \frac{\sum_k p_k\,|\mathbf A_k|}{\sum_k |\mathbf A_k|}, \qquad \bar\phi = \frac{\sum_k \dot m_k\,\phi_k}{\dot m} \quad \text{for } \phi \in \{p_0, T_0, c_\theta, c_{ax}, \alpha\}.

The static pressure is area-averaged, total quantities and velocities are mass-averaged. In the rotating frame the velocities of the computation are relative; the absolute ones follow from c=w+ω×r\mathbf c = \mathbf w + \omega\times\mathbf r. The mass flow counts with w\mathbf w, because the plane is at rest in the computational mesh. The swirl angle is α=arctan⁡(cθ/cax)\alpha = \arctan(c_\theta/c_{ax}), positive in the direction of rotation. If the inlet flow runs against the axis, the opposite direction is taken.

From the inlet and outlet planes follow the total pressure ratio Π=p0,2/p0,1\Pi = \bar p_{0,2}/\bar p_{0,1}, the total temperature ratio τ=T0,2/T0,1\tau = \bar T_{0,2}/\bar T_{0,1} and the isentropic efficiency

η=Π(γ−1)/γ−1τ−1\eta = \frac{\Pi^{(\gamma - 1)/\gamma} - 1}{\tau - 1}

With the blade count follows the mass flow of the whole rotor Zm˙Z\,\dot m. Without a temperature change η\eta is undefined and reported as NaN, not as 0 or 1.

Radial profile

For the comparison with probe traverses the plane is divided into bands of equal span, s=(r−rmin⁡)/(rmax⁡−rmin⁡)s = (r - r_{\min})/(r_{\max} - r_{\min}) between the smallest and largest radius of the plane. In every band p0p_0 and T0T_0 are mass-averaged and referred to the inlet mean, together with swirl angle and efficiency per band. This is the form in which exit profiles are given in experiments.

Comparison with measured data

Messvergleich reads a characteristic (mass flow, Π\Pi, τ\tau, η\eta; mass flows in lbm/s are converted with 0.45359237 to kg/s) and profiles over the span in per cent. Compared are

  • the characteristic at the computed mass flow: linear between the two neighbouring measured points; outside the measured range the nearest point, marked as such;
  • the profile at the measured spans: linear between the band centres of the computation, outside the nearest band; the mean deviation as root mean square.

A fixed back pressure does not hold the operating point of a compressor near choke: the mass flow hardly changes there, the pressure ratio depends sensitively on the back pressure. The outlet with radial equilibrium therefore has a throttle (radial equilibrium): the pressure at the hub is pa+Km˙2p_a + K\,\dot m^2 with the mass flow through the outlet, as behind a throttle valve. The operating point then lies at the intersection of characteristic and throttle parabola and can be moved along the characteristic with KK.

Assumptions

  • Total quantities with the ideal gas (γ,R)(\gamma, R) of the solver; for a real gas p0,T0p_0, T_0 and η\eta are only approximations this way.
  • Averages use the cell values of the plane (first order) – accurate enough for the comparison with measurements, not for balances to rounding.
  • The axis of rotation passes through the origin; the geometry has its axis in +z+z.

How it is checked

  • Passagenprobe (SN29-01 … 09): the reader on a reference file and on generated files with nine error cases; the mesh with the defaults (cells and boundary faces counted by hand), periodic over one pitch, gap, volume against annulus minus blade, mesh quality and kink, an Euler run against Euler's turbine equation.
  • Turbomaschinenprobe (SN29-10 … 15): radial equilibrium on the potential vortex by hand, performance and profile, reading and comparing measured data, throttle.

Limits

  • Only one blade row per mesh; stages via the mixing plane or the sliding interface (boundaries).
  • No mesh with fillet or shroud; the gap is radial, the blade ends flat.
  • The performance values assume an averaged, essentially axial plane; with reverse flow in a plane (stall) the mass averages are no longer meaningful.
