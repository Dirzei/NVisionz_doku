Turbomachinery – geometry, mesh and performance

How a blade geometry in MERIDL format becomes a mesh of one blade passage, and how the performance of a blade row follows from the flow field: averaging in measurement planes, pressure and temperature ratio, isentropic efficiency, radial profile and the comparison with measurements – with derivations, checks and limits.

What this is about

A blade row with ZZ identical blades is periodic: it is enough to compute one pitch 2π/Z2\pi/Z, with rotationally periodic boundaries on both sides (periodic boundaries) and in the rotating frame (time and solver). Three steps lead from the geometry to a statement about the machine:

  1. Read the geometry (Meridl): hub and casing contour and the blade in sections from hub to tip.
  2. Generate the mesh (Schaufelpassage): an H mesh over one pitch, the blade in the middle, with a tip gap at the casing.
  3. Evaluate (Turbokennwerte, Messvergleich): means in the inlet and outlet planes, performance of the row, radial profile and the comparison with measured data.

Geometry in MERIDL format

A MERIDL file describes the hub contour rN(z)r_N(z), the casing contour rG(z)r_G(z), the blade count ZZ and, for every blade section, points (z,r,θ1,θ2)(z, r, \theta_1, \theta_2) from leading to trailing edge; θ1,θ2\theta_1, \theta_2 are the circumferential angles of the two surfaces. Lengths are read in inches (factor 0.0254 m) or another unit, angles in radians. Three rules make the reader strict:

  • Count first, then allocate: all numbers in the file are counted and every count in the header is checked against the rest before any array is created. A damaged file allocates no huge array.
  • Ordered surfaces: θ1\theta_1 is always the surface with the larger angle; if they are swapped, they are exchanged and a note is given. Both surfaces must meet at the leading and trailing edge (within 10−410^{-4} rad) and must not cross in between.
  • Contours are interpolated shape-preserving (PCHIP) and continued constant outside the points.

The pitch is 2π/Z2\pi/Z. The sense of rotation in which the row works as a compressor follows from the slope of the camber line θ=(θ1+θ2)/2\bar\theta = (\theta_1 + \theta_2)/2: a compressor turns against the direction in which the camber line moves from leading to trailing edge, sign⁡ω=−sign⁡∂θ/∂z\operatorname{sign}\omega = -\operatorname{sign}\,\partial\bar\theta/\partial z.

The mesh of one pitch

Schaufelpassage builds an H mesh: ii runs axially (upstream part, blade, downstream part), jj circumferentially over one pitch, kk radially from the hub to the blade tip and on through the gap to the casing. The mesh line j=nθ/2j = n_\theta/2 lies on the blade; between the edges its nodes are doubled (one surface below, one above), upstream and downstream it continues on the camber line. The boundaries j=0j = 0 and j=nθj = n_\theta lie half a pitch to either side of the camber line and are rotated against each other by exactly 2π/Z2\pi/Z, node by node – as the rotationally periodic pairing requires.

  • Sections: every blade section is interpolated cubically over the arc length of its camber line and sampled at the same fractions, clustered towards the edges; across the sections over the arc length in the meridional plane. At the hub the blade is extended or shortened to the contour, at the tip it is cut where it lies one gap width below the casing.
  • Gap: cells fill the band between the two surfaces above the blade tip (prisms at the edges, where the surfaces meet); the lower face of the band is the tip (boundary schaufel). Optionally the layers in the gap are clustered towards casing and tip (-wandspalt:), and the band is divided circumferentially into several strips (-kopfzellen:). Their first width is chosen so that the jump to the first cell at the blade next to it equals the largest ratio of neighbouring strips. At the leading and trailing edge, where the band converges to a point, the strips run as chevrons of constant width parallel to both surfaces and meet on the centre line of the band; one or two prisms remain in the middle. If the strips instead ran into the edge point as a fan, the centroid of the outer, thin prism would lie at two thirds of its length and that of the thin cell next to it at half, so that the line between the two centres runs almost along their common face.
  • Gap in the O mesh: in the O mesh (-form:ohpassage) an inner ring and a middle block fill the blade outline above the tip. By default the whole arc around each edge lies on the end edge of the middle block; at a thin tip the ring lines then converge at the leading and trailing edge into a fan of wedges (on Rotor 37 more than a thousand faces with over 70° non-orthogonality). With -netzkopfquer:N the middle block has only NN cells across the blade, its ends lie at the nose, and the wall points of the arc belong to its long sides. The midpoints of the end edges stay where they were; if the sum of the pair numbers with NN is odd, the core takes one cell more. On Rotor 37 with 12 cells across no face in the gap exceeds 70°; at the thin trailing edge the inner ring becomes flatter instead, and the largest volume ratio rises from 9 to 15.
  • Upstream and downstream parts follow hub and casing with the span fraction of the edge nodes. The camber line is continued with the slope at the edge and turns into the axial direction over a length of a few axial chords, so that the mesh does not kink at the edge. Otherwise the clustering towards the blade continues along the extended camber line up to inlet and outlet; with -auslauf:L it turns into the even division with the axial distance dd from the edge, weight of the clustered one (1+cos⁡(πd/L))/2(1 + \cos(\pi d/L))/2 up to d=Ld = L (in axial chords).
  • Wall layers ahead of and behind the blade: with the span fraction of the edge up to inlet and outlet, the first cell height follows the channel height. Where the channel converges behind the blade, it gets smaller there (on Rotor 37 with the channel table from 1.80 to 1.43 µm). For a wall cell a few millimetres long and 1.5 µm high (aspect ratio around 4000), a height change of only 1 % across the cell shifts its centre so far along the wall that the line from wall face to cell centre is more than 80° off the normal. With -netzwandlagen:L each layer kk ahead of and behind the blade changes to the Vinokur distribution fk(z)f_k(z) whose first and last cells are as high as at the respective edge: ek(z)=fk(z)+w(d)(ekE−fk(zE))e_k(z) = f_k(z) + w(d)\,(e_k^{\mathrm{E}} - f_k(z_{\mathrm{E}})), w=(1+cos⁡(πd/L))/2w = (1 + \cos(\pi d/L))/2 for the axial distance \(d < L\) from the edge (\(L\) in chords), otherwise 0. \(e_k\) is thus continuous at the edge, and the height at hub and tip is everywhere that of the edge. On Rotor 37 (SA, \(y^+\) 1, 488,640 cells) the number of wall faces of nabe_nach above 70° drops from 627 to 0, that of interior faces above 70° from 856 to 81, and the largest skewness from 12.2 to 5.5.
  • Wall points around the edges smoothed across the span: each layer distributes the wall points on the arcs around leading and trailing edge after the curvature of its own section. The curvature of the edge, however, changes unsteadily from section to section; on Rotor 37 the spacings jumped by up to 62 % between neighbouring layers. With layers 6 to 9 mm high and wall cells 30 µm wide, the cell centre moves so far along the span that faces between neighbouring cells become up to 86° non-orthogonal. With -netzkantenglatt:N the fractions FkF_k of the arc length are averaged NN times across the layers, Fk←12Fk+12(Fk−1/h−+Fk+1/h+)/(1/h−+1/h+)F_k \leftarrow \tfrac12 F_k + \tfrac12\,(F_{k-1}/h_- + F_{k+1}/h_+)/(1/h_- + 1/h_+), with the radial distances h±h_\pm to the neighbouring layers at the edge; hub and tip stay. Every mixture of increasing fractions increases, so the wall points stay ordered. The weight is the distance, not the layer number: by number, layer 1, only 1.8 µm above the hub, moved to the mean of the span after 200 passes while the hub did not, and the blocks ahead and behind drifted apart. 20 passes are enough on Rotor 37; more gain nothing.
  • First spacing normal to the wall: the lines of the O-ring run within the layer surface. Where the blade stands inclined on it (sweep, lean), the distance of the first node to the blade surface is smaller than measured in the layer by the cosine cc between the line and the blade normal; on Rotor 37 by up to 9.5 % (1.63 instead of 1.80 µm), and differently from layer to layer. If the thickness of a wall cell changes between its two layers, its centre does not lie above the centre of its wall face. With -netzwandsenkrecht:ja the first spacing in the layer is y1/(rc)y_1/(r\,c); the blade normal follows from the tangent along the wall within the layer and the one to the neighbouring layers at the same wall point, and cc is limited below at 0.2. Ahead of and behind the blade (wall layers) the height at hub and casing is held normal to the wall in the same way: radially h1+(dr/dz)2h\sqrt{1 + (\mathrm dr/\mathrm dz)^2}, relative to the slope at the edge. On Rotor 37 (488,640 cells, with -netzwandlagen:1 -netzkantenglatt:20) no wall face of the blade is above 70° any more (before 342), the first height at the blade lies between 1.73 and 1.80 µm and at nabe_vor between 1.758 and 1.764 µm; above 70° remain 4 interior faces (up to 71.3°) and one wall face at nabe_vor (70.0°).
  • Hub: the hub is split into three boundaries: nabe_vor ahead of the blade, nabe below it and nabe_nach behind it. Each part can thus get its own wall condition, for instance a wall at rest in the absolute frame ahead of and behind a rotating rotor. By default the seams lie exactly at the leading and trailing edge at the hub. A rotor, however, usually carries its blades on a platform or disc that extends beyond both edges. With -nabennaht:VOR,NACH the boundary nabe extends from zLE−VOR⁡z_{LE} - \mathrm{VOR} to zTE+NACH⁡z_{TE} + \mathrm{NACH} (in metres, zLEz_{LE} and zTEz_{TE} the edges at the hub). A hub face belongs to a group according to the axial mean of its nodes. If a distance reaches beyond the domain, the boundary ahead or behind disappears. At the seam the wall velocity in the rotating frame jumps by ωr\omega r. Where the seam lies is therefore a statement about the machine, not about the mesh.
  • Channel beyond the file: ahead of the first and behind the last point of the contours of a MERIDL file the generator keeps the radii fixed. If the file ends ahead of the measuring plane, the channel there is too wide wherever the rig keeps converging. -kontur:DATEI continues hub and casing beyond the ends from a table (lines wand;z;r, a source line is required, unit and axial origin selectable). Inside the file its points stay. The curve still runs piecewise cubic and shape-preserving through all points; since the slope at a point depends on its neighbours, only the outermost interval of the file at each end changes. For Rotor 37 the file ends 7.30 cm behind the leading edge at the hub; according to the flow-path table of the rig (R123, Figure 1) at the measuring plane 10.67 cm downstream the hub is 2.4 mm higher and the casing 2.4 mm lower, the annulus area 10 % smaller. Where both apply, file and table differ by at most 0.34 mm. The outermost interval changes by at most 0.012 mm, far behind the blade.
  • Leakage at the seam: in the rig there is a gap between the stationary and the rotating hub. Air flows through it from the cavity below the hub into the passage, or out of it. According to Shabbir et al. (R138) and Denton (R139), this flow explains part of the total-pressure deficit at the hub that the blind computations of Rotor 37 missed. With a third value, -nabennaht:VOR,NACH,SCHLITZ turns a band of width SCHLITZ around the front seam into the group nabe_naht. A hub face belongs to it when its axial centre lies less than SCHLITZ/2 from the seam. There the boundary type nabenleck:MDOT[:DRALL[:TT[:netto|umlauf]]] prescribes the flux itself.
    • Amount: MDOT is for the whole circumference. Each pitch carries m˙/N\dot m/N, where N=2π/θN = 2\pi/\theta follows from the rotation angle θ\theta of the periodic boundaries (cos⁡θ=(trace⁡−1)/2\cos\theta = (\mathrm{trace} - 1)/2). The mass flux is g=m˙/(NA)g = \dot m/(N A), AA the area of the group in the pitch.
    • In (MDOT positive): with u→=ω→×r→\vec u = \vec\omega\times\vec r, the absolute velocity is c→=D(u→−(u→⋅n→)n→)−cnn→\vec c = D\,(\vec u - (\vec u\cdot\vec n)\,\vec n) - c_n\,\vec n. DRALL =D= D is the fraction of ωr\omega r in the circumferential direction (0 no swirl, 1 rotating with the hub); n→\vec n is the normal out of the domain. Total temperature TtT_t (default -t0:), static pressure from inside. Then T=Tt−|c→|2/(2cp)T = T_t - |\vec c|^2/(2 c_p) and ρ=p/(RT)\rho = p/(R T); because cn=g/ρc_n = g/\rho depends on ρ\rho through TT, this is iterated until ρ\rho settles. The solver works with the relative velocity w→=c→−u→\vec w = \vec c - \vec u.
    • Out (MDOT negative): the state from inside with the normal velocity g/ρg/\rho.
    • Circulation (umlauf): the half of the band upstream of its area-weighted axial mean sucks off MDOT, the downstream half blows the same amount in; the net flow is zero. Downstream is the direction of the inflow, not that of the rotation vector, since Rotor 37 rotates about −z-z.
    • Flux: it follows from the state at the face, F=(ρwn,ρwnw→+pn→,wn(E+p))F = (\rho w_n,\ \rho w_n \vec w + p\,\vec n,\ w_n (E + p)). The mass flow through the group is therefore exactly the one requested.
    Where the seam lies in the rig and how large the gap and the leakage are has not yet been established here. The option has not yet been run on Rotor 37.

Without building the mesh, -netzgeometrie:ja gives from the same node lines the largest ratio of neighbouring cell widths per direction with its location (axial, pitch, span, gap, band and the transitions from the last span cell to the first gap layer and from the wall cell of the passage to the first strip of the band) and the half wedge angle of the band at the edges, arctan⁡(t/(2L))\arctan\bigl(t/(2L)\bigr) with the blade thickness tt in the row next to the edge and its distance LL from the edge. -netzknoten:I0-I1,K0-K1 lists zz, rr and the angles of both sides for the nodes of a range.

The mesh can be checked against the geometry itself: the volume of all cells must equal the annulus of one pitch between hub and casing minus the blade volume, both computed independently from the file.

Whether the blade in the mesh carries the angles of the file is checked by the blade comparison (-schaufelabgleich:ja). For a surface θ=f(z,r)\theta = f(z, r) the angle follows from the normal

tan⁡β=r∂f∂z=−nznθ,\tan\beta = r\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial z} = -\frac{n_z}{n_\theta},

area-weighted per side over the boundary faces of the blade without the tip. In the file the least-squares line of θ1\theta_1 or θ2\theta_2 over zz times the mean radius is used, in the same window of the axial chord: leading edge 3 to 8 %, mid chord 45 to 55 %, trailing edge 90 to 97 %; leading and trailing edge come from the file, linear over the span. Compared is, per span 0.1 to 0.9 in a band of ±0.05, the camber line (mean of the two tangents); if a side has fewer than two faces, the entry stays empty. On the default mesh of rotor 37 the largest deviation is 0.42° at the leading edge, 0.77° at mid chord and 1.00° at the trailing edge.

Euler's turbine equation as a check

For an inviscid, adiabatic blade row the work per unit mass equals the change of angular momentum:

cp(T0,2−T0,1)=ω(r2cθ,2−r1cθ,1).c_p\,(T_{0,2} - T_{0,1}) = \omega\,\big(r_2\,c_{\theta,2} - r_1\,c_{\theta,1}\big).

An Euler run on the passage mesh must satisfy it in the mass average; at the same time the mass flow through inlet and outlet must be equal. Both check the whole chain of mesh, rotating frame, periodic boundaries and evaluation.

Means and performance

A measurement plane is a boundary (the boundary cells with their faces) or a plane cut through the mesh normal to the axis. Over its elements kk with the area vector Ak\mathbf A_k (in the axial direction) and the mass flow m˙k=ρkwk⋅Ak\dot m_k = \rho_k\,\mathbf w_k\cdot\mathbf A_k the averages are

m˙=∑km˙k,p=∑kpk|Ak|∑k|Ak|,ϕ=∑km˙kϕkm˙for ϕ∈{p0,T0,cθ,cax,α}.\dot m = \sum_k \dot m_k, \qquad \bar p = \frac{\sum_k p_k\,|\mathbf A_k|}{\sum_k |\mathbf A_k|}, \qquad \bar\phi = \frac{\sum_k \dot m_k\,\phi_k}{\dot m} \quad \text{for } \phi \in \{p_0, T_0, c_\theta, c_{ax}, \alpha\}.

The static pressure is area-averaged, total quantities and velocities are mass-averaged. In the rotating frame the velocities of the computation are relative; the absolute ones follow from c=w+ω×r\mathbf c = \mathbf w + \omega\times\mathbf r. The mass flow counts with w\mathbf w, because the plane is at rest in the computational mesh. The swirl angle is α=arctan⁡(cθ/cax)\alpha = \arctan(c_\theta/c_{ax}), positive in the direction of rotation. If the inlet flow runs against the axis, the opposite direction is taken.

From the inlet and outlet planes follow the total pressure ratio Π=p0,2/p0,1\Pi = \bar p_{0,2}/\bar p_{0,1}, the total temperature ratio τ=T0,2/T0,1\tau = \bar T_{0,2}/\bar T_{0,1} and the isentropic efficiency

η=Π(γ−1)/γ−1τ−1\eta = \frac{\Pi^{(\gamma - 1)/\gamma} - 1}{\tau - 1}

With the blade count follows the mass flow of the whole rotor Zm˙Z\,\dot m. Without a temperature change η\eta is undefined and reported as NaN, not as 0 or 1.

Radial profile

For the comparison with probe traverses the plane is divided into bands of equal span, s=(r−rmin⁡)/(rmax⁡−rmin⁡)s = (r - r_{\min})/(r_{\max} - r_{\min}) between the smallest and largest radius of the plane. In every band p0p_0 and T0T_0 are mass-averaged and referred to the inlet mean, together with swirl angle and efficiency per band. This is the form in which exit profiles are given in experiments.

Comparison with measured data

Messvergleich reads a characteristic (mass flow, Π\Pi, τ\tau, η\eta; mass flows in lbm/s are converted with 0.45359237 to kg/s) and profiles over the span in per cent. Compared are

  • the characteristic at the computed mass flow: linear between the two neighbouring measured points; outside the measured range the nearest point, marked as such;
  • the profile at the measured spans: linear between the band centres of the computation, outside the nearest band; the mean deviation as root mean square.

Next to the measurement a characteristic from the literature can be compared as well (Vergleichskurve, command line -vergleich:): a text file with a mandatory line # quelle:, a header massenstrom or massenstrom/sperre and at least one of Π\Pi, τ\tau, η\eta; an empty cell is no value. Each quantity is evaluated linearly between the nearest points with a value; outside them there is no value — a digitised curve ends where the source lets it end. Computations in the literature often plot their characteristic over the mass flow normalised by their own choke mass flow; the comparison is then made at the same position

x=m˙m˙choke⁡x = \frac{\dot m}{\dot m_{\mathrm{choke}}}

with the choke mass flow of the own computation (-vergleichsperre:), not that of the source: points at the same distance from choke stand side by side. Stored is the characteristic of a RANS computation of Rotor 37 with a 10 % inlet boundary layer (R97, figure 6, digitised from the page image); the source line is printed with every comparison.

A whole characteristic is computed by -op:kennlinie -kennlinie:P1,P2,… (Kennlinie): one steady computation per back pressure, each point started from the result of the previous one. It stops at the first point that stalls: it aborts, at the step limit it does not fall below the limit -kennliniegrenze: (default 10−310^{-3}), or its mass flow lies below the smallest measured one. The residual counts against the reference of the first point – a point started from the previous one begins small and would never fall six orders against its own start. The choke mass flow is the largest mass flow of the settled points; it is choke only if the first back pressure already lies on the choke line. The table gives mass flow, normalised mass flow, PR, TR, η and status per point, with measurement and comparison curve at the same point.

A fixed back pressure does not hold the operating point of a compressor near choke: the mass flow hardly changes there, the pressure ratio depends sensitively on the back pressure. The outlet with radial equilibrium therefore has a throttle (radial equilibrium): the pressure at the hub is pa+Km˙2p_a + K\,\dot m^2 with the mass flow through the outlet, as behind a throttle valve. The operating point then lies at the intersection of characteristic and throttle parabola and can be moved along the characteristic with KK.

Averaged at the probes

The performance figures above average over the whole cross-section, including the boundary layers at hub and casing. A measurement only knows the radii of its probes; whatever happens between the outermost probe and the wall enters with the value of that probe. Averaged over the whole cross-section, pressure ratio and efficiency therefore come out about 0.01 lower each than at the probes (R121, Fig. 3.2) – a difference of the evaluation, not of the flow. With -kennwertemittel:sonden, Sondenmittel additionally computes the figures the way the Rotor 37 data package evaluates them. For probe kk, every element whose radial range [rmin,rmax)[r_{min}, r_{max}) contains the ring radius rk=ri+sk(ra−ri)r_k = r_i + s_k\,(r_a - r_i) counts, with weight g=m˙/(rmax−rmin)g = \dot m / (r_{max} - r_{min}) (mass flux times the arc length of the ring inside the element):

p0,k=(∑gp0κ∑g)1/κ,T0,k=∑gT0∑g,κ=γ−1γp_{0,k} = \left( \frac{\sum g\, p_0^{\kappa}}{\sum g} \right)^{1/\kappa}, \qquad T_{0,k} = \frac{\sum g\, T_0}{\sum g}, \qquad \kappa = \frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}

Across the probes, the annulus between the midpoints of neighbouring probes (the outer ones reaching hub and casing) is weighted with its mass flux (ρvz)k(\rho v_z)_k:

p0=(∑k(ρvz)k(ro,k2−ru,k2)p0,kκ∑k(ρvz)k(ro,k2−ru,k2))1/κp_0 = \left( \frac{\sum_k (\rho v_z)_k\,(r_{o,k}^2 - r_{u,k}^2)\, p_{0,k}^{\kappa}}{\sum_k (\rho v_z)_k\,(r_{o,k}^2 - r_{u,k}^2)} \right)^{1/\kappa}

and T0T_0 likewise. The mass flow is the mean of inlet and outlet plane (R121, Tables 3.2, 3.3 and 3.6). -sondenspannen: gives the probe spans in percent; without it those of the measured profile apply. The lines of the cross-section average stay unchanged; the lines at the probes follow them, with measurement and profile.

Near choke the pressure ratio drops steeply; a small difference in choke mass flow shifts a comparison at equal mass flow a lot. -messungsperre: with the choke mass flow of the computation therefore also compares at the same ratio x=m˙/m˙choke⁡x = \dot m / \dot m_{\mathrm{choke}}: the measurement at xx times its own choke (the choked point of the map, otherwise the largest measured mass flow).

Relative exit angle and deviation

Behind a rotor usually only total pressure, total temperature and the absolute angle α\alpha are measured. How far the flow lags behind the metal angle of the trailing edge is told only by the relative angle β\beta. With swirl-free inflow it follows from Euler's work equation:

U=ωr,cθ=cpT01(T0/T01−1)U,ca=cθtan⁡α,β=arctan⁡U−cθca,δ=β−|κ2|.U = \omega r, \qquad c_\theta = \frac{c_p\,T_{01}\,(T_0/T_{01} - 1)}{U}, \qquad c_a = \frac{c_\theta}{\tan\alpha}, \qquad \beta = \arctan\frac{U - c_\theta}{c_a}, \qquad \delta = \beta - |\kappa_2|.

The radius of span ss is r=rmin+s(rmax−rmin)r = r_{min} + s\,(r_{max} - r_{min}) of the measurement plane, as for the bands of the profile. Measurement and computation go through the same formula: the work of the computation comes from its T0T_0, not from its own swirl. Both sides thus share the assumptions (radius of the stream tube, no heat transfer), and the difference of the two δ\delta is a difference of the flow. Below |α|=0.5∘|\alpha| = 0.5^\circ cac_a is not determined; that span does not count in the mean.

The metal angle comes from the MERIDL file per section: the camber line is the mean of the two sides, θm=(θ1+θ2)/2\theta_m = (\theta_1 + \theta_2)/2; at the edges both sides coincide, so a least-squares line θm(m)\theta_m(m) over 85 to 95 % of the axial chord is used (at the leading edge 5 to 15 %), κ=arctan⁡(rdθm/dm)\kappa = \arctan(\bar r\,\mathrm{d}\theta_m/\mathrm{d}m) with the meridional length mm. Between the sections κ2\kappa_2 is linear over the span. Chord, solidity σ\sigma and position of maximum camber come from the development on the mean radius; with them the bridge gives the deviation after Carter per section.

Measured on rotor 37 at peak efficiency (back pressure 120 kPa, SST with wall function, 90,304 cells): the computation lags the metal angle by 3.1° on average over the span, the back-calculation from the measurement averages 0.1°, between 30 and 90 % span −0.3 to −2.2°. Carter gives 4.6 to 7.7°. Without tip gap the computation stays at 3.0°. With the narrower channel behind the rotor after the channel table of the test rig (back pressure 112 kPa) the number reverses to −2.3°, while pressure ratio and work stay almost the same. The back-calculation therefore depends strongly on radius and axial velocity in the measurement plane and is only useful where the channel there is modelled correctly; comparing the spanwise profiles directly says more.

Blockage after Suder

Behind a transonic rotor, wake, tip-clearance vortex and boundary layers narrow the cross-section through which the core flow passes. Suder (R122) measures this as blockage: the fraction of the area taken up by the displacement thickness. -versperrung:POSITION evaluates it on planes normal to the axis. The plane is sampled on a grid of radii and points across one pitch; each point carries the value of the cell it lies in. At each point the indicator

G=|∂u∂r|ra−ri100+|∂ur∂θ|rP100G = \left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\right| \frac{r_a - r_i}{100} + \left|\frac{\partial u}{r\,\partial\theta}\right| \frac{r P}{100}

with the axial velocity uu, the cell gradient (least squares) and the pitch PP: the change of uu per percent of span and per percent of pitch. Where GG exceeds a threshold, the defect region begins (Eq. 5; Suder quotes 2 to 3 and compares the thresholds 1 and 2.5, the default, in Fig. 3b).

The core flow is the largest connected region below the threshold (neighbours across radius and circumference, periodic in the circumferential direction). Whatever it does not reach belongs to the defect region – including the centre of a wake or vortex, where the gradient is zero. On each radius the inviscid flow is filled in across every run outside the core linearly between the two core points enclosing it; a radius without any core (end-wall zone) is filled in radially from the two nearest core radii. The displacement thickness per radius is then

δ*=∫(1−uuinv)rdθ(Eq. 3),∫(1−TinvuTuinv)rdθ(Eq. 4),∫(1−ρu(ρu)inv)rdθ(Eq. 2)\delta^* = \int \left(1 - \frac{u}{u_{inv}}\right) r\,d\theta \quad\text{(Eq. 3)}, \qquad \int \left(1 - \frac{T_{inv}\,u}{T\,u_{inv}}\right) r\,d\theta \quad\text{(Eq. 4)}, \qquad \int \left(1 - \frac{\rho u}{(\rho u)_{inv}}\right) r\,d\theta \quad\text{(Eq. 2)}

with T=p/ρT = p/\rho, and the blockage of a span range (Eq. 7)

B=∫δ*drπ(r22−r12)/NB.B = \frac{\int \delta^*\,dr}{\pi (r_2^2 - r_1^2)/N_B}.

Suder's Table 2 is computed from the measurements with Eq. 3.

-versperrungbezug:FILE puts the values next to a table of measured blockage with a source line; -versperrungcsv: writes the blockage per radius.

Measured on Rotor 37 (Spalart-Allmaras without wall function, 480,768 cells, converged at 20.43 kg/s), plane at 130 % chord at midspan: core 20 to 80 % 9.1 %, end-wall zone 84 to 98 % 14.2 % (threshold 2.5; with 1: 9.2 % and 14.5 %). Suder measures 8 to 10 % in the core and 17 to 18 % in the end-wall zone at design speed. The computation therefore does not block too much; on the plane at 130 % of the hub chord it is 6.5 % and 13.9 %.

Shock position

-stosslage:P1,... (spans in percent) averages the blade wall pressure in a band of ±5 % around the span, per side, along the axial chord in 25 bins, area-weighted; leading and trailing edge come from the MERIDL file. The suction side is the side with the lower mean pressure between 10 and 90 % chord. The shock lies at the largest pressure rise per chord fraction between two consecutive bins with a value; empty bins – the grid lines drift in span along the chord – are skipped.

On the same computation the shock at 70 % span lies at 0.48 of the axial chord (0.40 to 0.52 across the span, further aft towards the tip). Suder sees it there at 35 to 50 % chord at low and at 45 to 65 % at high flow.

Blade row as a source

Instead of meshing the blades, a row can act as a force in a zone: cells whose centre lies between FROM and TO along the source axis aa. Results of a through-flow calculation (metal angles, pitch) can thus be carried into a CFD run without resolving the blade. Two models, both options (off by default):

Actuator disc (momentum theory): spread evenly per volume

F=Δpa+fttL,SE=F⋅u,L=TO−FROM.\mathbf{F} = \frac{\Delta p\,\mathbf{a} + f_t\,\mathbf{t}}{L}, \qquad S_E = \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{u}, \qquad L = \text{TO} - \text{FROM}.

Integrated over the zone, the momentum jump per area is Δp\Delta p along the axis and ftf_t along the circumferential direction t\mathbf{t}; the disc does work (fan). Not in a rotating frame.

Body force after Hall (per mass): the metal angle κ\kappa varies linearly over the zone from κ1\kappa_1 to κ2\kappa_2; the camber-surface normal is n=−sin⁡κa+cos⁡κt\mathbf{n} = -\sin\kappa\,\mathbf{a} + \cos\kappa\,\mathbf{t}, the deviation δ=arcsin⁡(W⋅n/|W|)\delta = \arcsin(\mathbf{W}\cdot\mathbf{n}/|\mathbf{W}|). Then

fn=−Kδ|W|22s|cos⁡κ|m^,fp=−cf|W|2s|cos⁡κ|e^W,\mathbf{f}_n = -\frac{K\,\delta\,|\mathbf{W}|^2}{2\,s\,|\cos\kappa|}\,\hat{\mathbf{m}}, \qquad \mathbf{f}_p = -\frac{c_f\,|\mathbf{W}|^2}{s\,|\cos\kappa|}\,\hat{\mathbf{e}}_W,

with m^\hat{\mathbf{m}} perpendicular to W\mathbf{W} in the plane of W\mathbf{W} and n\mathbf{n}, pitch ss (fixed for a linear cascade, s=2πr/Bs = 2\pi r/B in an annulus, t=a×r/|a×r|\mathbf{t} = \mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{r}/|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{r}|), K=2πK = 2\pi by default. The source is ρ(fn+fp)\rho(\mathbf{f}_n + \mathbf{f}_p) in the momentum; there is no energy source. The normal force is perpendicular to W\mathbf{W} and does no work; friction removes total pressure, not total enthalpy (in a rotor the rothalpy is kept).

Check against the stream tube. In a periodic channel of constant cross-section the solution depends on xx only; with m=ρum = \rho u and the source S\mathbf{S} per volume, mass, momentum and total enthalpy give

dudx=(γ−1)uc2−u2SE−vSy−γγ−1uSxm,dvdx=Sym,dpdx=Sx−mdudx.\frac{du}{dx} = \frac{(\gamma-1)\,u}{c^2 - u^2}\,\frac{S_E - v S_y - \frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1} u S_x}{m}, \qquad \frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{S_y}{m}, \qquad \frac{dp}{dx} = S_x - m\frac{du}{dx}.

The probe integrates this with RK4 from a cell upstream of the zone and compares the exit. Body force 0 to 30 degrees: error 5.1·10⁻³ with 40 and 1.6·10⁻³ with 80 cells, exit angle 28.7 degrees, total enthalpy to 10⁻⁷; disc: 6.3·10⁻⁴ and 3.1·10⁻⁴.

Limits: steady, no loss correlation beyond cfc_f; the disc only in a stationary frame; no coupling to the through-flow code of framework-turbo in the program — metal angles and pitch are passed as numbers; from a plane-cascade design the bridge derives them (Carter, loss coefficient as cfc_f, cascade figures back). A comparison with m-ACHIEVE is not possible, it has no such model.

Assumptions

  • Total quantities with the ideal gas (γ,R)(\gamma, R) of the solver; for a real gas p0,T0p_0, T_0 and η\eta are only approximations this way.
  • Averages use the cell values of the plane (first order) – accurate enough for the comparison with measurements, not for balances to rounding.
  • The axis of rotation passes through the origin; the geometry has its axis in +z+z.

How it is checked

  • Passagenprobe (SN29-01 … 09): the reader on a reference file and on generated files with nine error cases; the mesh with the defaults (cells and boundary faces counted by hand), periodic over one pitch, gap, volume against annulus minus blade, mesh quality and kink, an Euler run against Euler's turbine equation.
  • Turbomaschinenprobe (SN29-10 … 15): radial equilibrium on the potential vortex by hand, performance and profile, reading and comparing measured data, throttle.
  • Schaufelabgleichprobe (SN84-01 … 05): default mesh of rotor 37 below a limit per window, a twisted blade is detected at the trailing edge, slope of the file by hand, rejections, table.
  • Minderumlenkungprobe (SN85-01 … 05): metal angles, chord and pitch of a blade on cylinders by hand, relative angle from the work, back-calculation at three spans, CSV, radii about an oblique axis.
  • Vergleichskurveprobe (SN87-01 … 04): stored characteristic from the literature with source and gaps, comparison line normalised to the own choke, curve in kg/s, eleven faulty files rejected.
  • Sondenmittelprobe (SN106-01 … 04): rings per probe by hand from the primitive variables (also with elements of unequal height and a probe on the border of two layers), plane average by hand, two uniform states with pressure ratio 2, cut equal to boundary, choke of the measurement and comparison normalised to it, rejections.
  • Versperrungprobe (SN104-01 … 04): sampling on the ring sector (cell value per point, indicator (G) on a linear field), displacement thickness by hand on an invented grid with an island, a defect region across the pitch boundary and an end-wall zone, a wake with exactly known blockage, also with reversed axis and across ±180°, Suder's table and rejections. Stosslageprobe (SN104-11 … 13): on the Rotor 37 mesh a pressure from the chord fraction with a jump at 0.52 – suction side, bin means by hand, position of the jump, gaps.
  • Kanalkonturprobe (SN111-01 … 03): contour table in mm, cm and inches with axial origin, source over several lines, thirteen rejections; continuation on a small geometry: bit-identical at all points of the file and between its second and second-to-last point, outside exactly through the points of the table; the stored table from R123 against the MERIDL file of Rotor 37 (at most 0.34 mm, 10 % less annulus area at the measuring plane, blade untouched).
  • Nabenleckageprobe (SN105-01 … 03): band nabe_naht from the axial centre of each hub face by hand, bit-identical without a slot; boundary formula at one face: mass flux, total temperature, swirl and relative velocity, circulation per half; solver on the small Rotor 37 passage: leakage exactly −m˙/36-\dot m/36, balance, circulation net zero, downstream from the inflow, rejections.
  • Kennlinieprobe (SN83-01 … 04): stall at the step limit, abort and mass flow below the measurement end the characteristic, choke only from settled points, table with measurement and comparison curve; on the command line a characteristic on the smallest mesh of the blade passage up to stall.

Limits

  • Only one blade row per mesh; stages via the mixing plane or the sliding interface (boundaries).
  • No mesh with fillet or shroud; the gap is radial, the blade ends flat.
  • The performance values assume an averaged, essentially axial plane; with reverse flow in a plane (stall) the mass averages are no longer meaningful.
