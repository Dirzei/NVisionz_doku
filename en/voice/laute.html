Vowels, consonants and the phoneme chain – background

From the formant table to the spoken word: vowels as standing formants, consonants as events (fricatives, plosives, nasals), level balancing and soft limiter, pseudocode of PhonemeSynth.render (Vowel, Phoneme, Phonemes, WordParser, PhonemeSynth).

Vowels: position of the formants

A vowel is, when you form it, a posture of the vocal tract, and in the signal a group of formant positions. The first two formants carry the identity: F1 rises with the opening of the mouth (open /a/ high, close /i/ and /u/ low), F2 rises the further forward the tongue lies (/i/ high, /u/ low). Plotting F2 to the left and F1 downwards gives the familiar vowel trapezoid.

The formant bandwidths are the same for all vowels: 80, 110 and 160 Hz for F1, F2 and F3 (higher formants are wider). The values are approximations from the phonetics literature for a neutral reference voice; the pitch range is scaled by the profile through the tract scale ss: all formant frequencies are multiplied by ss.

German distinguishes short (lax) and long (tense) vowels. There are therefore additional lax variants (a_lax to oe_lax, 0.10 s long) with different formants, reduction vowels (short schwa 0.07 s, the ending -er as one sound, vocalised r) and diphthong components (short nucleus plus non-syllabic offglide). A full vowel is 0.16 s long: full vowels of equal 200 ms made the flow of a sentence sluggish; the rhythm now arises from the contrast between full vowel and schwa.

Consonants are events

Consonants are not standing formants but events with a time structure of their own. The same formant filters carry vowels and consonants; only the source (voiced pulse, noise, both) and the target of the formants are switched. Three families, by their physics:

  • Fricatives (s, sh, f, ch, h, w, voiced s): noise through formant filters. The “formants” of a fricative are the noise centres: /s/ lies at 4 to 8 kHz (bright), /sh/ at 2 to 3.6 kHz, /f/ is diffuse. In addition there is the noise colouring cc (0.05 for /s/, 0.30 for /sh/).
  • Plosives (p, t, k and b, d, g): a short silence (closure), then a short burst of noise. The place of articulation lies in the locus frequencies towards which the neighbouring vowel glides: labial (p, b) low, alveolar (t, d) mid-high, velar (k, g) with converging F2 and F3. German voiceless plosives are aspirated: a breath phase before the vowel onset.
  • Nasals (m, n; l and r simplified): voiced, a low nasal formant at about 250 Hz and damped higher formants.

Why the place of a plosive hangs on the transition and not on the burst: the burst is short and noisy and similar for all places. The formant trajectory of the following vowel, on the other hand, moves from a place-typical starting point (locus) to the vowel target. During the aspiration phase the formant target is therefore already set to the next sound: the place information lies audibly in the transition.

Level balancing and limiting

Sibilants and bursts are perceptually much louder than vowels of equal energy. With normalisation to the peak value, short consonant peaks would “crush” the vowels and the word would sound like hissing. Three measures:

  1. Segment weights: vowels are multiplied by vowelBoost (default 1.5), nasals by 1.15; the consonant noise enters only through consonantGain (default 0.12).
  2. Percentile normalisation: instead of the peak, the 95th percentile of the magnitude is normalised, target 0.9: xi←xi⋅0.9P95(|x|)x_i \leftarrow x_i\cdot\frac{0.9}{P_{95}(|x|)}
  3. Soft limiter with knee (knee 0.9, ceiling 1.0): below the knee the signal is unchanged, above it it approaches the ceiling continuously and without a kink: y=sgn⁡(x)[k+(c−k)tanh⁡|x|−kc−k],|x|>k=0.9,c=1.0y = \operatorname{sgn}(x)\Big[k + (c-k)\tanh\!\frac{|x|-k}{c-k}\Big],\qquad |x| > k = 0.9,\ c = 1.0

In addition, segments get soft “shoulders” (attack and release arcs of 12 and 20 ms, deliberately not down to zero); a vowel after another voiced sound (nasal or vowel) gets an onset dip of 35 ms (gain from 0.15 to 1.0 in a cosine rise) so that the syllable is audibly set off from the previous one and voiced sounds of equal pitch do not merge into a drone; and the whole gets a fade in and out of 5 ms.

Source: envisionz-framework-voice/src/envisionz/voice/core/PhonemeSynth.java; sound table text/Phonemes.java, vowel table text/Vowel.java. The adjustment parameters and their limits: consonantGain 0 to 1 (default 0.12), vowelBoost 1.0 to 2.5 (1.5), plosiveAspiration 0 to 0.08 s (0.030 s), vowelGlide 0.002 to 0.05 (0.018).

Decisions in the framework

Decision in the framework: The length of the output buffer is computed in advance from the segments, including the breath phase of voiceless plosives, with one sample of rounding reserve per segment (a plosive consists of two to three segments). An earlier version did not count the breath phase; the buffer was then too small, and the end of the sentence was dropped without an error message – the more plosives, the more was missing. Corrected on 11 Aug 2026.
Decision in the framework: The limiter starts its arc at the knee. The earlier version ceil · tanh(x/ceil) was discontinuous at its own threshold (at x=cx = c it gives ctanh⁡1=0.76cc\tanh 1 = 0.76\,c); with c=0.98c = 0.98 the signal jumped by −0.23-0.23 at one point and crackled on loud vowels. As measured, about 4 % of all samples exceed 0.98 after percentile normalisation. The knee form is continuous, its derivative at the knee is 1, and the ceiling 1.0 is never exceeded. Corrected on 11 Aug 2026.
Decision in the framework: Consonant level and vowel boost are adjustment parameters, not hidden constants: in one measurement “louder” was not better but worse. The defaults are set by ear and measurement and remain adjustable.

Worked example

Limiter. For |x|=0.95|x| = 0.95: y=0.9+0.1tanh⁡(0.5)=0.9+0.1⋅0.4621=0.9462y = 0.9 + 0.1\tanh(0.5) = 0.9 + 0.1\cdot 0.4621 = 0.9462; for |x|=0.9|x| = 0.9 y=0.9y = 0.9 (continuous at the knee), for very large |x||x| yy approaches 1.0.

Length of a voiceless plosive /t/ with defaults: closure 0.05 s + burst 0.010 s + aspiration 0.030 s = 0.090 s, which at 22,050 Hz is about 1985 samples.

Normalisation. If the 95th percentile of the magnitude before normalisation is 0.45, the signal is multiplied by 0.9/0.45=20.9/0.45 = 2; the loudest 5 % of the samples then lie above 0.9 and are folded by the limiter into the room up to 1.0.

How it is checked

  • Lautkettenprobe (18 checks) pins down what PhonemeSynth itself promises: the length as the sum of the segments (a voiceless plosive made of closure, burst and breath) – so that the error of 11 Aug 2026 cannot return (seven voiceless plosives, nothing cut off); the limits of the four adjustment parameters; the 95th percentile at 0.9, the ceiling 1.0, the linear 5 ms fades; silence stays silent; the same seed gives bit-identical output. The ceiling 1.0 is exactly reached (tanh saturates in double); what is promised is only that it is never exceeded. Eleven counter-experiments, all red, among them the length error itself.
  • VoiceTest: every sound that the grapheme-to-phoneme conversion produces must be in the sound table (otherwise the synthesizer cannot speak it); sounds have positive duration, an unknown symbol is not reported, silence has the requested duration.
  • Stimmprobe: the length of an utterance is duration times sampling rate; the peak is at 0.9; no NaN; ends are faded; the same seed gives the same output.

Limits

  • Words that begin with a long fricative (/s/, /h/) remain the difficult case; fricatives and bursts are perceptually too loud and pull vowels down.
  • The syllable intelligibility of a pure formant synthesis is hard to tune blind; acceptance of the voice is by ear, not by a test.
  • The sound table is for German; l and r are simplified as a nasal and as a noisy fricative respectively (approximations from the literature, tuned by ear).
  • WordParser is a simple word splitter with longest match first (“sch” before “s”) for test words and syllables; for arbitrary text the grapheme-to-phoneme conversion of the next page is used.
