From text to speech: normalisation, pronunciation, sentence melody – background

The chain behind Voice.speak: text normalisation, rule-based grapheme-to-phoneme conversion for German, prosody with declination, question rise, pauses and final lengthening, speaking rate (TextNormalizer, RuleG2pDe, Prosody, Voice).

What this is about

A sentence such as “Bereit? 21 Fehler.” is not yet a sound. To be spoken, a sequence of sounds must be made from the writing (spell out numbers, read punctuation as pauses, translate letters into sounds), and a melody and rhythm must be laid over it. Together they contribute more to intelligibility than sound quality: a question that falls like a statement is not heard as a question, and a sentence without word pauses becomes a blur.

The chain is fixed and not optional: normalize → convert → shape → render. RuleG2pDe.convert expects normalised text; only normalisation turns the question mark into the marker __Q__. Whoever skips the step gets no error but a question that sounds like a statement – the melody falls instead of rising.

Text normalisation

TextNormalizer.normalize does, in this order:

  1. everything to lower case (German rules), trim the edges;
  2. resolve a few abbreviations (“z.b.” → “zum beispiel”, “d.h.”, “usw.”, “etc.”, “bzw.”; “&” → “und”, “%” → “prozent”);
  3. spell out numbers: 0 to 19 from a word list, 20 to 99 in German order (“einundzwanzig”: units, “und”, tens); anything larger digit by digit (“100” → “eins null null”);
  4. punctuation into pause markers: “?” → __Q__, “.” and “!” → __S__, “,” “;” “:” → __K__; brackets and quotation marks are dropped, hyphens become spaces.

The markers are capital letters in plain ASCII. A single special character for the pauses was mangled into a question mark by a character set change – a real bug that a test uncovered. Markers in capitals also cannot be confused with text, which is lower case throughout.

Grapheme-to-phoneme conversion

German is more regular than English, which is why it comes first. RuleG2pDe works word by word from left to right and chooses at each position the longest matching rule (so “sch” wins over “s” and “ei” over “e”). Rules may depend on context: word start, word end, preceding vowel, number of following consonants.

Sentence melody and rhythm

Prosody turns the units into a flat sound sequence and, per sound, a pitch factor (1.0 = fundamental frequency of the profile). Each sentence is treated on its own, the contour starts anew at the beginning of a sentence. With the relative position t∈[0,1]t \in [0,1] of the sound in the sentence (only sound-producing phonemes count) and the melody depth dd of the profile:

statement: b(t)=1.08−0.26tquestion: b(t)={1.05−0.15t0.75t<0.750.90+0.55t−0.750.25t≥0.75f(t)=1+d(b(t)−1)\text{statement: } b(t) = 1.08 - 0.26\,t\qquad \text{question: } b(t) = \begin{cases} 1.05 - 0.15\,\dfrac{t}{0.75} & t < 0.75\\[2mm] 0.90 + 0.55\,\dfrac{t-0.75}{0.25} & t \ge 0.75\end{cases}\qquad f(t) = 1 + d\,\big(b(t) - 1\big)

At d=0.2d = 0.2 (humanity 0) only 20 % of the excursion remains; this is how the voice speaks “almost monotonically”.

Pauses structure the sentence: 0.14 s between words (with phonemes of 160 to 270 ms the word boundary was inaudible at 0.05 s, and the sentence became a blur), 0.20 s at the comma, 0.22 s at the sentence end, 0.18 s after a question. Final lengthening: before every phrase boundary the last full syllable is lengthened, glide vowels before it by half as much; at the sentence end by 1+0.45d1 + 0.45\,d, at the comma by 1+0.25d1 + 0.25\,d. Humans lengthen noticeably before boundaries; their absence is immediately audible as mechanical.

Word stress exists as a rule (the first full vowel of the word is lengthened by 25 % and raised slightly) but is switched off (STRESS_ENABLED = false): a measurement with an automatic speech recogniser showed the lengthening to be harmful because it blurs the rhythm.

Speaking rate. speak(text, rate) with rate∈[0.4;1.5]\text{rate} \in [0.4;\,1.5] lengthens the full vowels by 1/rate1/\text{rate} and the pauses by min⁡(2.2;1/rate)\min(2.2;\,1/\text{rate}). Consonants keep their length: made slower they would only blur.

Decisions in the framework

Decision in the framework: The pause tiers are word 0.14 s < comma 0.20 s < sentence end 0.22 s (after a question 0.18 s).
Decision in the framework: The melody depth dd of the profile scales not only the contour but also the final lengthening; dd hangs on the humanity hh (d=0.2+0.8hd = 0.2 + 0.8\,h). Sentence melody carries more intelligibility than sound quality, so it cannot be switched off, only weakened (smallest depth 0.2).
Decision in the framework: Numbers are spoken as words only up to 99; larger numbers digit by digit. Decimal numbers are split: “3.5” becomes “drei __S__ fünf”, i.e. with a sentence end in the middle, because each run of digits is spelled out on its own and the full stop in between then becomes the sentence-end marker. This is unsuitable for measured values such as “3.85”; changing it is an open design question.

Worked example

Normalisation. “Bereit? 21 Fehler.” becomes bereit __Q__ einundzwanzig fehler __S__. “Test 3.5 und 100” becomes test drei __S__ fuenf und eins null null. “Ja, bitte!” becomes ja __K__ bitte __S__.

Pronunciation (computed from the code): zugriff → t, s, u_lax, g, r, i_lax, f; bereit → b, schwa_k, r, a_d, i_g, t; schule → sch, u, l, schwa_k; sprechen → sch, p, r, e_lax, ch, schwa_k, n; fehler → f, e, l, er_end; rad and rat → r, a, t.

Contour. For “Das Haus ist groß.” (19 units including pauses) the pitch factor for the reference voice (d=0.68d = 0.68) lies between 0.878 and 1.054: 1+0.68⋅(1.08−1)=1.0541 + 0.68\cdot(1.08-1) = 1.054 at the start, 1+0.68⋅(0.82−1)=0.8781 + 0.68\cdot(0.82-1) = 0.878 at the end. With melody depth 1.0 a statement runs from 1.08 to 0.82 and a question ends at 1.45.

How it is checked

  • VoiceTest: results are not empty; letters are lower case; a sentence end appears as the marker __S__, a comma as __K__; digits are spelled out; empty input does not give null; “schon” has fewer sounds than letters; “rad” and “rat” end alike; every sound that the conversion produces for a series of words is in the sound table; question and statement are marked as such.
  • Stimmprobe (sentence melody): the trap is itself a check – unnormalised text carries no sentence end, normalised text does. A statement falls by at least a tenth, a question rises to 1.45; omitting the declination is detected because the check demands the tenth (word stress alone would already let it fall somewhat).

Limits

  • German only. There is no exception lexicon for names, loanwords and English rules; words that do not follow the rules are pronounced wrongly in accordance with the rules.
  • Numbers only up to 99 as words; decimals, dates and units are not spelled out (see above).
  • Pronunciation is rule-based and knows no syllable boundaries or word stress; the word-stress rule is switched off.
  • The melody knows only statement and question; exclamations are statements (“!” is treated like “.”).
