The Enigma – background

The computation behind Enigma.Maschine: rotors as permutations, the path of the current through plugboard, rotors and reflector, the stepping with the double step, and what follows for reciprocity and key space.

Rotors as permutations

A rotor connects 26 contacts on one side to 26 on the other, each to exactly one – mathematically a permutation π\pi of the letters 0 to 25. The wiring is stored in the source as a 26-character string: rotor I is EKMFLGDQVZNTOWYHXUSPAIBRCJ, so π(A)=E\pi(A) = E, π(B)=K\pi(B) = K, and so on. For the return path of the current the inverse permutation π−1\pi^{-1} is needed; Enigma creates it once when the machine is built.

Two settings shift the rotor against the input. The rotor position pp (the letter in the window) turns the core, the ring setting rr turns the lettered ring against the core. Only the difference d=p−rd = p - r is effective. A contact cc passes through the rotor like this:

c'=(π(c+d)−d)mod26,back: c'=(π−1(c+d)−d)mod26c' = \bigl(\pi(c + d) - d\bigr) \bmod 26, \qquad \text{back: } c' = \bigl(\pi^{-1}(c + d) - d\bigr) \bmod 26

The reflector UU is a permutation of 13 pairs: it sends the current back on a different contact, has no fixed point and is its own inverse, U=U−1U = U^{-1}. The plugboard PP is likewise an exchange of pairs; the remaining letters stay in place.

The path of a letter

With MM for the passage through all rotors from right to left, a key press xx, after the rotors have stepped, gives:

S(x)=P(M−1(U(M(P(x))))),M=πleft∘πmiddle∘πrightS(x) = P\bigl(M^{-1}\bigl(U\bigl(M(P(x))\bigr)\bigr)\bigr), \qquad M = \pi_{\text{left}} \circ \pi_{\text{middle}} \circ \pi_{\text{right}}

Setting Q=M∘PQ = M \circ P, we have S=Q−1∘U∘QS = Q^{-1} \circ U \circ Q. A permutation conjugated by another keeps its cycle structure: SS also consists of 13 two-cycles. Two well-known properties follow without the source enforcing them explicitly:

  • The machine is its own counterpart: S=S−1S = S^{-1}. Decrypting is encrypting with the same setting; entschluessele calls verschluessele, and reziprok() returns true.
  • No letter is ever encrypted to itself: SS has no fixed point. Codebreakers used this weakness for probable words.

Stepping and the double step

The right rotor steps one position at every key press. Each rotor has one or two turnover notches (rotor I: Q, II: E, III: V, IV: J, V: Z, VI to VIII: Z and M); if a rotor is on its notch at the key press, it takes its left neighbour along. If the middle rotor itself stands on its notch, it steps together with the left one – even if the right rotor is not at its notch. That is the double step: the middle rotor thereby steps at two consecutive key presses (keys 3 and 4 in the example below).

Pseudocode – Enigma.Maschine

// setup: per rotor i (left → right) from the wiring
vor[i][k] ← letter k of the wiring;  rück[i][vor[i][k]] ← k
kerbe[i][letter] ← true for each notch of the rotor
ring[i], pos[i] ← ring setting, start position (0..25)

schalte():                                  r = right, m = middle, l = left of the three stepping rotors
    mitte  ← kerbe[m][pos[m]];  rechts ← kerbe[r][pos[r]]
    if mitte:   pos[l] ← pos[l] + 1;  pos[m] ← pos[m] + 1       double step
    else if rechts:  pos[m] ← pos[m] + 1
    pos[r] ← pos[r] + 1                       all modulo 26

durch(i, x, hin):
    d ← pos[i] − ring[i]
    e ← (x + d) mod 26
    a ← hin ? vor[i][e] : rück[i][e]
    return (a − d) mod 26

taste(x):
    schalte()                                step first, then compute
    c ← stecker[x]
    for i ← n−1 down to 0:  c ← durch(i, c, hin)
    c ← ukw[c]
    for i ← 0 to n−1:        c ← durch(i, c, back)
    return stecker[c]

Source: envisionz-framework-chiffre/src/envisionz/chiffre/maschine/Enigma.java. With four rotors (M4), l,m,rl, m, r denote the right three; the extra rotor at the far left never steps.

Convention in the framework: Stepping happens before the computation, the notch is tested at the displayed position of the rotor, and the ring setting enters only into the shift dd. Model and rotors are checked: the Enigma I knows only rotors I to V, the M4 demands Beta or Gamma on the left and admits both only there. Positions and plugs may be given as letters or as numbers 01 to 26 (1/20 for a plug pair); a letter may appear in one pair only.

Worked examples

Double step, by hand and from the probe. M3, rotor order left → right III II I, start position KDO. The right rotor I has its notch at Q, the middle II at E. Four key presses:

1K D Ono notch: right onlyK D P

4K E Rmiddle on notch E: left and middle step (double step)L F S

The probe expects exactly this sequence KDP KDQ KER LFS. Note the fourth step: the middle rotor moves from E to F although the right rotor is not on its notch.

Check value. Enigma I, reflector B, rotors I II III, rings AAA, start position AAA, no plugs: AAAAA⁡↦BDZGO⁡\mathrm{AAAAA} \mapsto \mathrm{BDZGO}, and conversely BDZGO⁡↦AAAAA⁡\mathrm{BDZGO} \mapsto \mathrm{AAAAA}. This is a published value; none of the five outputs is an A.

Key space. For Enigma I with ten plug pairs: 60 rotor orders (5⋅4⋅35 \cdot 4 \cdot 3), 263=1757626^3 = 17576 start positions and, for ten pairs among 26 letters,

26!(26−20)!10!210=150738274937250\frac{26!}{(26-20)!\;10!\;2^{10}} = 150\,738\,274\,937\,250

plugboards. The plugboard thus contributes most of the key count, but it is a fixed exchange and does not change the frequency structure of the text. This calculation is arithmetic, not part of the code.

How it is checked

Verfahrensprobe (162 checks for the whole module, under 5 s) holds the Enigma against published examples:

  • the Wikipedia check value AAAAA⁡↔BDZGO⁡\mathrm{AAAAA} \leftrightarrow \mathrm{BDZGO} in both directions,
  • the double step (KDP KDQ KER LFS),
  • historical radio messages: Barbarossa 1941 in two parts, one with the rings as numbers (02 21 12), Scharnhorst 1943 with rotors VI and VIII, and a Kriegsmarine message of the M4 with plugs as numbers. The text must match character by character; the message key is decrypted first and then used for the text.

The Enigmabrecherprobe additionally checks the replica of the machine in the breaker against Enigma.Maschine on 600 keys, including the double step, and that the M4 with rotor Beta in position A computes like an M3.

Limits

  • The input is prepared for the Latin alphabet: capital letters, umlauts as AE, OE, UE, ß as SS; all other characters are dropped. The form (spaces, characters, case) can be laid over the result again by Aufbereitung.formErhalten.
  • There is no variant with a rotating reflector, no Uhr plugboard, no Enigma D, K or T; the catalogue names Enigma I, M3 and M4.
  • The module computes one configured machine. How many positions an attack must try is shown on the page on breaking.
