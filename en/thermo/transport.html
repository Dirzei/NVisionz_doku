Transport properties of dilute gases – background

The computation behind Gastransport and Kollisionsintegrale: viscosity and binary diffusion after Chapman-Enskog with Lennard-Jones and Stockmayer collision integrals, thermal conductivity after Warnatz (Chemkin model) and after Eucken, mixing rules after Wilke and Mason-Saxena, mixture-averaged diffusion. Formulas, pseudocode, hand calculation, check against Cantera, limits.

What this is about

Reynolds, Prandtl and Schmidt numbers of hot exhaust gases, heat transfer in combustors and diffusion flames need viscosity μ\mu, thermal conductivity λ\lambda and diffusion coefficients DijD_{ij} of gas mixtures up to far above 2000 K. Measurements hardly exist there. The kinetic theory of gases after Chapman and Enskog provides them from two parameters per molecule: the depth ε\varepsilon and the zero crossing σ\sigma of the Lennard-Jones potential, plus the dipole moment for polar molecules. This is the approach Chemkin and Cantera use as well; Gastransport applies it to the species of FundamentalSpecies and takes the heat capacity from the partition functions.

Collision integrals

The transport coefficients depend on the potential through the reduced collision integrals Ω(l,s)*\Omega^{(l,s)*}. They are functions of the reduced temperature and the reduced dipole moment:

T*=kTε,δ*=μD22⋅4πε0εσ3T^* = \frac{kT}{\varepsilon}, \qquad \delta^* = \frac{\mu_D^2}{2\cdot 4\pi\varepsilon_0\,\varepsilon\,\sigma^3}

δ*=0\delta^* = 0 is the pure Lennard-Jones (12-6) potential, δ*>0\delta^* > 0 the Stockmayer potential. The values come from the tables of Monchick and Mason (1961): Ω(2,2)*\Omega^{(2,2)*} and A*=Ω(2,2)*/Ω(1,1)*A^* = \Omega^{(2,2)*}/\Omega^{(1,1)*} over 37 and 39 values of T*T^* (0.1 to 100 and 0 to 500) and 8 values of δ*\delta^* (0 to 2.5), taken over by script from Cantera 3.2.0. Ω(1,1)*=Ω(2,2)*/A*\Omega^{(1,1)*} = \Omega^{(2,2)*}/A^*.

Intermediate values (own choice, not Cantera's): quadratic Lagrange interpolation in δ*\delta^* over the three nearest columns, then quadratic in ln⁡T*\ln T^* over the three nearest rows:

L(x)=y0(x−x1)(x−x2)(x0−x1)(x0−x2)+y1(x−x0)(x−x2)(x1−x0)(x1−x2)+y2(x−x0)(x−x1)(x2−x0)(x2−x1)L(x) = y_0\frac{(x - x_1)(x - x_2)}{(x_0 - x_1)(x_0 - x_2)} + y_1\frac{(x - x_0)(x - x_2)}{(x_1 - x_0)(x_1 - x_2)} + y_2\frac{(x - x_0)(x - x_1)}{(x_2 - x_0)(x_2 - x_1)}

The three support points are centred around the wanted value where possible; outside the table the edge rows are extrapolated. At a table point the result is exactly the table value.

Pure gases

Viscosity (Chapman-Enskog, first approximation), with the molecular mass m=M/NAm = M/N_A:

μ=516πmkTπσ2Ω(2,2)*(T*,δ*)\mu = \frac{5}{16}\,\frac{\sqrt{\pi m k T}}{\pi\sigma^2\,\Omega^{(2,2)*}(T^*, \delta^*)}

Binary diffusion with the reduced mass mij=mimj/(mi+mj)m_{ij} = m_im_j/(m_i + m_j) and the combining rules σij=(σi+σj)/2\sigma_{ij} = (\sigma_i + \sigma_j)/2, εij=εiεj\varepsilon_{ij} = \sqrt{\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j}:

Dij=3162πk3T3/mijpπσij2Ω(1,1)*(Tij*,δij*)D_{ij} = \frac{3}{16}\,\frac{\sqrt{2\pi k^3T^3/m_{ij}}}{p\,\pi\sigma_{ij}^2\,\Omega^{(1,1)*}(T^*_{ij}, \delta^*_{ij})}

If both partners are polar, δij*\delta^*_{ij} is formed from both dipole moments. If exactly one is polar, the correction of the Chemkin transport model (Kee et al.) applies, as in Cantera:

ξ=1+14αn*μp*2εpεn,εij→εijξ2,σij→σijξ−1/6\xi = 1 + \frac14\,\alpha^*_n\,\mu^{*2}_p\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_p}{\varepsilon_n}}, \qquad \varepsilon_{ij} \to \varepsilon_{ij}\,\xi^2, \qquad \sigma_{ij} \to \sigma_{ij}\,\xi^{-1/6}

with the reduced polarisability of the non-polar partner αn*=αn/σn3\alpha^*_n = \alpha_n/\sigma_n^3 and the reduced dipole moment of the polar one μp*2=μp2/(4πε0εpσp3)\mu^{*2}_p = \mu_p^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_p\sigma_p^3); δ*=0\delta^* = 0.

Thermal conductivity after Warnatz (Chemkin model with rotational relaxation, as in Cantera). Translation, rotation and the remaining internal degrees of freedom are treated separately and coupled through self-diffusion:

λ=μMR(ftr⁡cv,tr⁡R+frot⁡cv,rot⁡R+fint⁡cv,int⁡R),cv,tr⁡R=32,cv,rot⁡R=0,1,32(atom, linear, non-linear)\lambda = \frac{\mu}{M}\,R\left(f_{\mathrm{tr}}\,\frac{c_{v,\mathrm{tr}}}{R} + f_{\mathrm{rot}}\,\frac{c_{v,\mathrm{rot}}}{R} + f_{\mathrm{int}}\,\frac{c_{v,\mathrm{int}}}{R}\right), \qquad \frac{c_{v,\mathrm{tr}}}{R} = \frac32, \quad \frac{c_{v,\mathrm{rot}}}{R} = 0,\ 1,\ \frac32\ (\text{atom, linear, non-linear})

fint⁡=ρDkkμ=MRTpDkkμ,A=52−fint⁡,B=Zrot⁡F(298Kk/ε)F(T*)+2π(53cv,rot⁡R+fint⁡)f_{\mathrm{int}} = \frac{\rho D_{kk}}{\mu} = \frac{M}{RT}\,\frac{p D_{kk}}{\mu}, \qquad A = \frac52 - f_{\mathrm{int}}, \qquad B = Z_{\mathrm{rot}}\frac{F(298\ \mathrm{K}\,k/\varepsilon)}{F(T^*)} + \frac{2}{\pi}\left(\frac53\frac{c_{v,\mathrm{rot}}}{R} + f_{\mathrm{int}}\right)

frot⁡=fint⁡(1+2πAB),ftr⁡=52(1−2πABcv,rot⁡cv,tr⁡),cv,int⁡R=cpR−52−cv,rot⁡Rf_{\mathrm{rot}} = f_{\mathrm{int}}\left(1 + \frac{2}{\pi}\frac{A}{B}\right), \qquad f_{\mathrm{tr}} = \frac52\left(1 - \frac{2}{\pi}\frac{A}{B}\frac{c_{v,\mathrm{rot}}}{c_{v,\mathrm{tr}}}\right), \qquad \frac{c_{v,\mathrm{int}}}{R} = \frac{c_p}{R} - \frac52 - \frac{c_{v,\mathrm{rot}}}{R}

with Parker's temperature factor of rotational relaxation

F(T*)=1+π3/2T*(12+1T*)+(π24+2)1T*F(T^*) = 1 + \frac{\pi^{3/2}}{\sqrt{T^*}}\left(\frac12 + \frac{1}{T^*}\right) + \left(\frac{\pi^2}{4} + 2\right)\frac{1}{T^*}

and the rotational collision number Zrot⁡Z_{\mathrm{rot}} at 298 K from the collision data. cpc_p comes as the central difference of H−H0H - H_0 from the partition functions (step 10−3T10^{-3}T). For comparison waermeleitfaehigkeitEucken computes the classical Eucken correction, λ=(μ/M)(cp+54R)\lambda = (\mu/M)(c_p + \tfrac54 R) (molar), for atoms λ=154(R/M)μ\lambda = \tfrac{15}{4}(R/M)\mu.

Mixtures

Viscosity after Wilke, thermal conductivity after Wassiljewa with the coefficients of Mason and Saxena – the same formula with the same Φij\Phi_{ij}:

wmix⁡=∑ixiwi∑jxjΦij,Φij=[1+(μi/μj)1/2(Mj/Mi)1/4]28(1+Mi/Mj)w_{\mathrm{mix}} = \sum_i\frac{x_i\,w_i}{\sum_j x_j\,\Phi_{ij}}, \qquad \Phi_{ij} = \frac{\left[1 + \left(\mu_i/\mu_j\right)^{1/2}\left(M_j/M_i\right)^{1/4}\right]^2}{\sqrt{8\,(1 + M_i/M_j)}}

with w=μw = \mu or w=λw = \lambda. Diffusion into the mixture (averaged, y mass fractions):

Dkm=1−yk∑j≠kxj/DkjD_{km} = \frac{1 - y_k}{\sum_{j\ne k} x_j/D_{kj}}

// viskositaet(sp, T)
d ← collision data(sp)                    throws if there is no source (H2S, SO, SO2)
m ← M/N_A;  σ ← σ[Å]·10⁻¹⁰
Ω ← omega22(T/(ε/k), δ*(d, d))
return 5/16·√(π·m·k·T)/(π·σ²·Ω)

// waermeleitfaehigkeit(sp, T): Warnatz
μ ← viskositaet(sp, T);  cvRot ← 0 | 1 | 1.5
cp/R ← (H(T + 10⁻³T) − H(T − 10⁻³T))/(2·10⁻³T)/R
pD ← diffusion(sp, sp, T, p = 1 Pa)        self-diffusion, without ξ
f_int ← M/(R·T)·pD/μ;  A ← 5/2 − f_int
B ← Z_rot·F(298/(ε/k))/F(T/(ε/k)) + 2/π·(5/3·cvRot + f_int)
c1 ← 2/π·A/B
return μ/M·R·(2.5·(1 − c1·cvRot/1.5)·1.5 + f_int(1 + c1)·cvRot + f_int·(cp/R − 2.5 − cvRot))

// wilke(sp, x, μ, w)
for i with x_i > 0:  s += x_i·w_i / Σ_j x_j·Φ_ij
return s

Convention in the framework: collision data (geometry, ε/k in K, σ in Å, dipole moment in debye, polarisability in Å³, Zrot) from GRI-Mech 3.0, read from Cantera 3.2.0's gri30.yaml, for 20 of the 23 species; H2S, SO, SO2 throw IllegalArgumentException until a source is available. Constants: kk and NAN_A fixed SI values, ε0=8.854187812773345⋅10−12\varepsilon_0 = 8.854187812773345\cdot10^{-12} F/m as in Cantera, 1 debye = 10⁻²¹/c C m. Units: μ in Pa s, λ in W/(m K), D in m²/s.

How it is checked

test/envisionz/thermo/transport/Transportprobe.java, 26 checks:

  • Table points: Ω(2,2)*\Omega^{(2,2)*} and A*A^* at five table points exactly (10⁻¹⁴), an intermediate value between its neighbours.
  • Against Cantera 3.2.0 with the same collision data: viscosity of all 20 species at 300, 1000, 2000 K to 0.2 %; thermal conductivity (Warnatz) for the 15 species whose cp in FundamentalSpecies and in the GRI-Mech NASA polynomials agree to 0.5 %, to 1 %; binary diffusion to 0.1 %; Wilke with Cantera's pure viscosities. Measured according to the module description: μ 0.12 %, D 0.06 %, λ 0.7 %, Wilke 7·10⁻⁶. The tolerances are wider because Cantera evaluates through fitted polynomials in T and interpolates the table differently.
  • Against reference correlations for dilute gases (CoolProp 8.0.0 at 1 kPa, e.g. Lemmon-Jacobsen for N2, O2, Ar): the accuracy of kinetic theory with Lennard-Jones data, μ to 6 % (7 gases, 13 points), λ to 8 % (6 gases without water).
  • Limiting cases: mixture of a single species, symmetry Dij=DjiD_{ij} = D_{ji}, D∝1/pD \propto 1/p, Warnatz equals Eucken for atoms, rejection of H2S, SO, SO2 and of δ*\delta^* outside 0 to 2.5; 8 counter-probes red.

Limits

  • Dilute gases: no density correction; not applicable at high pressure or near condensation.
  • Water vapour: λ is 22–35 % too high with the GRI data; use Wassertransport (IAPWS, IF97 – background).
  • cp deviations are inherited: where FundamentalSpecies deviates from the NASA polynomials (CH4 −6 % at 2000 K, CH3O +10 % at 300 K, H2O2 and NO2 2–3 %), λ deviates too.
  • No collision data for H2S, SO, SO2. The Lennard-Jones sets in machieve are partly different numbers (e.g. CH4 3.758 Å/148.6 K instead of 3.746 Å/141.4 K); unification comes with WS12.
  • Table range: outside 0.1 ≤ T* ≤ 100 extrapolation; δ* above 2.5 is rejected.
  • Mixing rules are approximations; no multicomponent diffusion and no thermal diffusion.
