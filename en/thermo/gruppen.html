Group additivity after Benson – background

The computation behind Smiles, Gruppenwerte and Benson: from structure string to molecular graph, decomposition into groups per heavy atom, sum of contributions with symmetry and optical isomers, NASA polynomial by fitting.

What it is about

If measured properties of a species are missing, they can be estimated from its structure. Benson showed that enthalpy of formation, entropy and heat capacity of ideal gases are well represented as a sum of group contributions: a group is a polyvalent atom with the kind of its neighbours, e.g. C-(C)(H)₃ for a methyl group on carbon.

From SMILES to the graph

The reader knows carbon, oxygen and aromatic carbon, bonds - = # :, parentheses for branches and ring closures 1 to 9 (also preceded by a bond symbol). Two aromatic atoms without a bond symbol are bonded aromatically, otherwise singly. Hydrogens are added from the valence:

nH,i=vi−∑j∼ioij,vC=4,vO=2,oij∈{1,2,3,32}n_{\mathrm H,i} = v_i - \sum_{j \sim i} o_{ij}, \qquad v_{\mathrm C} = 4,\ v_{\mathrm O} = 2, \qquad o_{ij} \in \{1, 2, 3, \tfrac32\}

with the bond order oijo_{ij}, aromatic 32\tfrac32. A negative or non-integer remainder is an error in the structure text (for example a five-bonded carbon or an aromatic ring without partner) and is rejected with the atom number; likewise square brackets, charges and other elements. Every ring closure yields its ring as the shortest path between the two atoms without the closing bond – the ring correction depends on it.

Contributions and corrections

Each heavy atom is named by its kind (Cs saturated, Cd on a double bond, Ct on a triple bond, Cb aromatic, CO carbonyl, O) and with the kinds of its neighbours forms the group; the 22 groups and the ring correction for cyclohexane come from a freely available group database (source noted per group). Then

ΔfH(298)=∑iΔHi,S(298)=∑iSi−Rln⁡σ+Rln⁡nopt,cp(T)=∑icp,i(T)\Delta_f H(298) = \sum_i \Delta H_i, \qquad S(298) = \sum_i S_i - R\ln\sigma + R\ln n_{opt}, \qquad c_p(T) = \sum_i c_{p,i}(T)

with the symmetry number σ\sigma and the number of optical isomers noptn_{opt}; cpc_p is given at the points 300 to 1500 K. nasa7() fits a NASA polynomial through these values and sets the coefficients a6a_6, a7a_7 from enthalpy and entropy; spezies(name) returns the result as SpeciesProperties for the rest of the thermodynamics.

Why groups and why the corrections. Benson orders additivity in levels: atoms (zeroth order, exact only for the mass), bonds (first order) and groups (second order) – an atom with its immediate neighbours. The second level captures the interaction of neighbouring bonds, which for hydrocarbons makes up most of the deviation from bond additivity; what reaches further (gauche positions, 1,5 repulsion, ring strain) is added as a correction. The group entropies hold for an imagined molecule without symmetry. The rotational partition function of a molecule with symmetry number σ\sigma (external rotations times internal rotors) is σ\sigma times smaller, hence −Rln⁡σ-R\ln\sigma; noptn_{opt} mirror-image forms make an ideal mixture of equal parts, hence +Rln⁡nopt+R\ln n_{opt}. Example isobutane: external axis 3 times three methyl rotors 333^3, σ=81\sigma = 81.

NASA fit. Sought are a1…a5a_1 \dots a_5 with cp/R=∑kakTk−1c_p/R = \sum_k a_k T^{k-1} in the least-squares sense over the seven points; the calculation uses x=T/1000x = T/1000 in the normal equations, which would otherwise be ill-conditioned at T4≈1012T^4 \approx 10^{12}. The coefficients for enthalpy and entropy follow from the values at 298.15 K:

a6=ΔfH(298)R−∑k=15akT0kk,a7=S(298)R−a1ln⁡T0−∑k=25akT0k−1k−1,T0=298.15Ka_6 = \frac{\Delta_f H(298)}{R} - \sum_{k=1}^{5}\frac{a_k\,T_0^k}{k}, \qquad a_7 = \frac{S(298)}{R} - a_1\ln T_0 - \sum_{k=2}^{5}\frac{a_k\,T_0^{k-1}}{k-1}, \qquad T_0 = 298.15\ \mathrm K

The polynomial holds with the same coefficients over the whole range; outside 300–1500 K it is an extrapolation.

Worked example

Isobutane, Benson.schaetze("CC(C)C", 81, 1): groups C-(C)₃(H) once, C-(C)(H)₃ three times; ΔfH\Delta_f H = −135.98 kJ/mol (measured −134.2), SS = 294.67 J/(mol K), cpc_p = 96.69 J/(mol K) at 300 K (measured 97.15) and 227.44 at 1000 K (228.45); the NASA polynomial matches the seven points to 0.28 %. Ethanol ("CCO", σ\sigma = 3): −242.18 kJ/mol against measured −234.0. Toluene ("Cc1ccccc1", σ\sigma = 6): 49.41 kJ/mol against 50.1.

Computed with a small Java program against the classes (as of 18.10.2026); measured values as in the probe after the NIST Chemistry WebBook.

How it is checked

The recomputation with sympy (derivations and worked examples of this page) is in the source tree under docs/theorie/rechnung/thermo_gruppen.py.

  • 14 hydrocarbons against the NIST Chemistry WebBook: ΔfH\Delta_f H to 2 kJ/mol (measured at most 1.8).
  • 7 oxygen compounds to 9 kJ/mol, on average 4.5 (ethanol −8.2, formaldehyde +7.3 – the group values there rest on older measurements or other computational methods).
  • Entropy to 0.5 and 1.5 J/(mol K); cpc_p from 300 to 1000 K for hydrocarbons without cyclohexane to 2.5 %, for all to 7.5 %; NASA fit to 1 %; comparison with the thermo file of GRI-Mech.

Limits

Only C, H, O; rings other than cyclohexane and benzene and unknown groups are rejected by name; gauche and 1,5 corrections are missing.

Assumptions and validity

  • Ideal gas at 1 bar, ground-state conformer; conformer mixtures and hindered rotation are contained only as far as they are contained in the groups of the database.
  • Second order: accurate for unbranched and singly branched molecules; heavily branched (neopentane-like) and strained molecules need the missing corrections.
  • Mixed origin: the oxygen groups of the database are quantum-chemical (CBS-QB3), the carbon groups measured after Benson – hence the larger scatter of the oxygen compounds.
  • Cost: linear in the number of atoms; one estimate including the NASA fit costs microseconds.

What follows

  • For hydrocarbons without group data in the mechanism the estimate is usable to about 2 kJ/mol in the enthalpy of formation and 2.5 % in cpc_p – more accurate than the uncertainty of many equilibrium constants in large mechanisms.
  • For oxygen compounds expect up to 9 kJ/mol; that shifts KcK_c at 1000 K by up to a factor of 3.
  • The symmetry number is no detail: Rln⁡81=36.5R\ln 81 = 36.5 J/(mol K) for isobutane.
