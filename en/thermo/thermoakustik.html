Duct modes and thermoacoustics – background

The equations behind Kanalmoden and Netz: acoustic modes in circular and annular ducts with flow, cut-off frequency and the circumferential orders after Tyler and Sofrin; and the network of plane waves with heat source and flame, complex eigenfrequencies and the Rayleigh criterion.

What it is about

In engines and combustors sound matters in two ways. In the annular ducts of compressor and fan the blade rows create pressure patterns that travel as modes – whether they leave the duct or decay is decided by the cut-off frequency. In combustors the flame itself can excite sound when its heat release fluctuates in step with the pressure; the result is combustion instability. The page pipe networks describes the transfer matrix of plane waves; here the higher-order modes and the source are added.

Modes in circular and annular ducts

In a duct with hard walls and uniform axial flow of Mach number MM the convected wave equation separates into

p=(Jm(krr)+QYm(krr))eimθeikxxe−iωt,∂p∂r=0 at the walls.p = \big(J_m(k_r r) + Q\,Y_m(k_r r)\big)\,e^{i m\theta}\,e^{i k_x x}\,e^{-i\omega t}, \qquad \frac{\partial p}{\partial r} = 0 \text{ at the walls}.

In the circular duct (radius RR) YmY_m drops out and the boundary condition is Jm'(krR)=0J_m'(k_r R) = 0: the eigenvalues are the zeros j'm,nj'_{m,n} of the derivative of the Bessel function, for m=0m = 0 starting with the plane wave kr=0k_r = 0. In the annular duct with σ=ri/ra\sigma = r_i/r_a and x=krrax = k_r r_a both walls require ∂p/∂r=0\partial p/\partial r = 0, so

Jm'(x)Ym'(σx)−Jm'(σx)Ym'(x)=0.J_m'(x)\,Y_m'(\sigma x) - J_m'(\sigma x)\,Y_m'(x) = 0 .

The class samples this function and brackets each zero with Brent. Two limits pin it down: for σ→0\sigma \to 0 the annulus becomes the circle (probe: σ=10−6\sigma = 10^{-6} to 7e-12), for σ→1\sigma \to 1 the flat gap of height h=ra−rih = r_a - r_i with kr2≈(m/rm)2+(nπ/h)2k_r^2 \approx (m/r_m)^2 + (n\pi/h)^2.

Axial wavenumber and cut-off frequency

Inserting into the convected wave equation with k=ω/ck = \omega/c gives the dispersion relation (k−Mkx)2=kx2+kr2(k - M k_x)^2 = k_x^2 + k_r^2 and from it

kx±=−Mk±k2−(1−M2)kr21−M2.k_x^\pm = \frac{-M k \pm \sqrt{k^2 - (1 - M^2)\,k_r^2}}{1 - M^2}.

If the radicand is positive, both wavenumbers are real and the mode propagates ("cut-on"); otherwise it decays exponentially in both directions. The limit lies at

fc=ckr1−M22π,f_c = \frac{c\,k_r\sqrt{1 - M^2}}{2\pi},

so the flow lowers the cut-off frequency. Example from the probe: mode (1,0)(1, 0), ra=0.5r_a = 0.5 m, M=0.4M = 0.4: fc=182.627f_c = 182.627 Hz.

Circumferential orders after Tyler and Sofrin

A rotor with BB blades ahead of VV vanes produces, at the nn-th harmonic of the blade passing frequency nBΩnB\Omega, only the circumferential orders

m=nB+kV,k∈Z,Ωm=nBΩm.m = nB + kV, \qquad k \in \mathbb Z, \qquad \Omega_m = \frac{nB\,\Omega}{m}.

The mode spins at Ωm\Omega_m, against the rotor for negative mm. For B=24B = 24, V=56V = 56 and the first harmonic, m=−32,24,80m = -32, 24, 80 arise with speeds −0.75-0.75, 11 and 0.30.3 of the rotor speed. Since the circumferential speed of the pattern at the wall grows with |Ωm||\Omega_m|, a mode with small |m||m| and large |Ωm||\Omega_m| is the dangerous one: the rotor mode m=Bm = B in the example propagates from a tip Mach number of about 1.048. This is the basis of the rule to choose V>2BV > 2B – then no interaction mode with |m|<B|m| < B occurs at the first harmonic.

The thermoacoustic network

For low frequencies plane waves suffice. The state variables are acoustic pressure pp and volume flow u=Svu = Sv, time dependence e−iωte^{-i\omega t}; the elements are multiplied in sequence as with the transfer matrix. New is that ω\omega may be complex:

  • pipe with complex k=ω/ck = \omega/c and Z=ρc/SZ = \rho c/S: (cos⁡kLiZsin⁡kLisin⁡kL/Zcos⁡kL)\begin{pmatrix}\cos kL & iZ\sin kL\\ i\sin kL/Z & \cos kL\end{pmatrix}, determinant 1;
  • compact heat source: p2=p1p_2 = p_1, u2=u1(1+Geiωτ)u_2 = u_1\,(1 + G\,e^{i\omega\tau}) – the volume source of the fluctuating heat release, linearised, with gain GG and time lag τ\tau;
  • flame after the nn-τ\tau model: source with G=(T2/T1−1)nG = (T_2/T_1 - 1)\,n, behind it hotter gas at equal pressure: ρ2=ρ1T1/T2\rho_2 = \rho_1 T_1/T_2, c2=c1T2/T1c_2 = c_1\sqrt{T_2/T_1}.

Eigenfrequencies. Ends are open (p=0p = 0) or hard (u=0u = 0). At the inlet the free variable is set to 1 and multiplied through the network; the boundary condition at the outlet is the characteristic function F(ω)F(\omega), whose complex zeros the secant method finds in complex arithmetic. With this time dependence Im⁡ω>0\mathrm{Im}\,\omega > 0 is growth, the growth rate in 1/s. Starting values come from the network without sources: there FF is real on the real axis, and the zeros are sampled and bracketed with Brent.

Temperature jump. A closed-open tube of a cold part of length aa and a hot one of length bb has the eigenvalue equation tan⁡(k1a)tan⁡(k2b)=Z1/Z2\tan(k_1 a)\tan(k_2 b) = Z_1/Z_2; at T2/T1=4T_2/T_1 = 4 the probe finds all six eigenfrequencies up to 1500 Hz to 1e-12.

Growth: Rijke tube and Rayleigh criterion

In a tube of length LL open at both ends with a source at xf=ξLx_f = \xi L the characteristic function reads

sin⁡kL+Geiωτcos⁡(kxf)sin⁡(k(L−xf))=0.\sin kL + G\,e^{i\omega\tau}\cos(k x_f)\,\sin\big(k(L - x_f)\big) = 0 .

If eiωτ=−1e^{i\omega\tau} = -1, it has a real root k*k^* of sin⁡kL−Gcos⁡(kxf)sin⁡(k(L−xf))=0\sin kL - G\cos(kx_f)\sin(k(L - x_f)) = 0: at the time lag τ*=π/(ck*)\tau^* = \pi/(ck^*) the mode is neutral, shorter and longer lags excite or damp. In the example (ξ=1/4\xi = 1/4, G=0.3G = 0.3) τ*=3.0892\tau^* = 3.0892 ms at 161.85 Hz, with growth rates +10.1+10.1 and −11.3-11.3 1/s at 95 % and 105 % of τ*\tau^*. For small GG perturbation about the fundamental k0=π/Lk_0 = \pi/L gives

Im⁡ω≈cG2Lsin⁡(ω0τ)sin⁡(2πξ),\mathrm{Im}\,\omega \approx \frac{c\,G}{2L}\,\sin(\omega_0\tau)\,\sin(2\pi\xi),

which the probe meets at G=10−3G = 10^{-3} to 2e-3 relative – the remainder is of order GG.

Rayleigh criterion. A heat source excites when its fluctuation is on average in phase with the pressure (Rayleigh 1878). The class measures at the first source

R=Re⁡(pq)|p||q|,q=Geiωτu1,R = \frac{\mathrm{Re}(\bar p\,q)}{|p|\,|q|}, \qquad q = G\,e^{i\omega\tau}\,u_1,

the cosine of the phase angle between pressure and source strength. In 40 random flames of the probe RR has the same sign as the growth rate in all 39 cases with |R|≥0.05|R| \ge 0.05. The criterion is necessary, not sufficient: losses at the ends can consume an excitation.

Pseudocode – eigenfrequency of the network

ω₀ ← zero of F without sources (real, sampling + Brent)
ω₁ ← ω₀ (1 + 10⁻⁴) + small shift
repeat
    s ← F(ω₁) (ω₁ − ω₀) / (F(ω₁) − F(ω₀))         complex arithmetic
    ω₀ ← ω₁;  ω₁ ← ω₁ − s
until |s| < 10⁻¹³ |ω₁|
f ← Re ω₁ / 2π;  growth ← Im ω₁;  R ← Rayleigh index at the source

Source: envisionz-framework-thermo/src/envisionz/thermo/akustik/Netz.java.

How it is checked

  • Kanalmodenprobe (6 checks): zeros j'm,nj'_{m,n} against table 9.5 of Abramowitz and Stegun (4e-6, the table has six digits); annulus with σ=10−6\sigma = 10^{-6} equal to the circle; narrow annulus against the flat gap; cut-off frequency and dispersion relation; Tyler-Sofrin with speeds and propagation threshold; error cases.
  • Thermoakustikprobe (8): complex pipe equal to the real transfer matrix, determinant 1; eigenfrequencies without source (open-open nc/2Lnc/2L, closed-open (2n−1)c/4L(2n-1)c/4L); temperature jump; source in the middle neutral; Rijke tube with neutral time lag and sign change; first order in GG; Rayleigh criterion; error cases.

Counter-checks (sign in the annulus function, sign of convection, cut-off frequency without the root, m=nB−kVm = nB - kV, sign in the complex sine, conjugated lag factor, c∼Tc \sim T instead of T\sqrt T, imaginary instead of real part in the Rayleigh index) are detected.

Limits

  • Duct modes: hard walls, uniform flow without swirl and without boundary layer, no liner.
  • Network: plane waves, no mean flow in the network, ends ideally open or closed (no impedance), flame compact and linear; limit cycles of combustion instability do not follow from it.
