Radiation and phase change – background

The computation behind Sichtfaktor, Austausch, Wsgg, Stefan and Sieden: view factors in closed form, with crossed strings and as a double integral; radiosities in a grey enclosure; emissivity of combustion gases; the Stefan problem after Neumann and with the enthalpy method; critical heat flux and nucleate boiling.

What it is about

At high temperatures radiation often carries more heat than convection – between the walls of a furnace, from a flame to the combustor wall. In phase change (melting, solidification, boiling) latent heat is released or absorbed at a moving front. Both fields have classical solutions against which numerical methods can be checked.

View factors and exchange

The view factor F12F_{12} is the fraction of the energy diffusely emitted by surface 1 that hits surface 2. Closed forms exist for coaxial discs, opposite parallel rectangles and perpendicular rectangles with a common edge; for two-dimensional configurations Hottel's crossed-string rule applies; for arbitrarily placed rectangles the double integral is evaluated with Gauss-Legendre. The summation rule ∑jFij=1\sum_j F_{ij} = 1 in a closed enclosure and reciprocity AiFij=AjFjiA_i F_{ij} = A_j F_{ji} hold.

Where the formulas come from

A diffuse surface emits with intensity I=E/πI = E/\pi uniformly in all directions; the element dA2dA_2 at distance rr subtends the solid angle cos⁡θ2dA2/r2\cos\theta_2\,dA_2/r^2 seen from dA1dA_1, and the projection of dA1dA_1 contributes cos⁡θ1\cos\theta_1. Hence

F12=1A1∫A1∫A2cos⁡θ1cos⁡θ2πr2dA2dA1.F_{12} = \frac{1}{A_1}\int_{A_1}\!\int_{A_2} \frac{\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2}{\pi r^2}\,dA_2\,dA_1 .

The kernel is symmetric in both surfaces, hence reciprocity A1F12=A2F21A_1F_{12} = A_2F_{21} – also for the quadrature sum itself when both directions use the same nodes. For opposite rectangles (sides aa, bb, distance LL, X=a/L\bar X = a/L, Y=b/L\bar Y = b/L) the fourfold integral can be done in closed form:

F12=2πXY[ln⁡(1+X2)(1+Y2)1+X2+Y2+X1+Y2arctan⁡X1+Y2+Y1+X2arctan⁡Y1+X2−Xarctan⁡X−Yarctan⁡Y].F_{12} = \frac{2}{\pi \bar X\bar Y}\Big[\ln\sqrt{\frac{(1+\bar X^2)(1+\bar Y^2)}{1+\bar X^2+\bar Y^2}} + \bar X\sqrt{1+\bar Y^2}\arctan\frac{\bar X}{\sqrt{1+\bar Y^2}} + \bar Y\sqrt{1+\bar X^2}\arctan\frac{\bar Y}{\sqrt{1+\bar X^2}} - \bar X\arctan\bar X - \bar Y\arctan\bar Y\Big].

Perpendicular rectangles with a common edge (Incropera, table 13.2) and coaxial discs (Ri=ri/hR_i = r_i/h, S=1+(1+R22)/R12S = 1 + (1+R_2^2)/R_1^2) follow in the same way:

F12discs=12(S−S2−4(R2/R1)2).F_{12}^{\text{discs}} = \tfrac12\Big(S - \sqrt{S^2 - 4(R_2/R_1)^2}\Big).

In a cube one parallel and four perpendicular factors add up to 1 (0.19982 + 4 · 0.20004) – the probe uses this as an independent check of both formulas.

Crossed strings. In the plane (surfaces infinitely long normal to the drawing) two segments with the crossed connections a1b2\overline{a_1b_2}, b1a2\overline{b_1a_2} and the uncrossed a1a2\overline{a_1a_2}, b1b2\overline{b_1b_2} form two closed hulls of taut strings; summation rule and reciprocity in both give Hottel's rule

F12=(a1b2+b1a2)−(a1a2+b1b2)2L1,F_{12} = \frac{(\overline{a_1b_2} + \overline{b_1a_2}) - (\overline{a_1a_2} + \overline{b_1b_2})}{2\,L_1},

so in a triangle with sides L1,L2,L3L_1, L_2, L_3 F12=(L1+L2−L3)/(2L1)F_{12} = (L_1 + L_2 - L_3)/(2L_1).

What follows. The double integral is accurate only for separated surfaces: at a common edge r→0r \to 0, the integrand becomes singular, and Gauss-Legendre converges only like about n−0.75n^{-0.75} (with n=64n = 64 per direction the cube is still 7 % off). For configurations with a common edge use the closed forms or the summation rule – or the contour form.

Contour form (since 2026-10-19). By Stokes' theorem the area integral becomes a contour integral over the boundaries (Mitalas and Stephenson):

A1F12=12π∮C1∮C2ln⁡rds1⋅ds2=12π∑i,j(ei⋅ej)∫01∫01ln⁡|Pi(s)−Qj(t)|dsdtA_1F_{12} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{C_1}\oint_{C_2}\ln r\;\mathrm d\mathbf s_1\cdot\mathrm d\mathbf s_2 = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{i,j}(\mathbf e_i\cdot\mathbf e_j)\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1\ln\lvert\mathbf P_i(s) - \mathbf Q_j(t)\rvert\,\mathrm ds\,\mathrm dt

over the four edges ei\mathbf e_i and ej\mathbf e_j of each surface. Perpendicular edge pairs drop out. The inner integral is closed: with the foot of the perpendicular at distance cc from the line, ∫12ln⁡(σ2+c2)dσ=12(σln⁡(σ2+c2)−2σ+2carctan⁡(σ/c))\int\tfrac12\ln(\sigma^2 + c^2)\,\mathrm d\sigma = \tfrac12\bigl(\sigma\ln(\sigma^2 + c^2) - 2\sigma + 2c\arctan(\sigma/c)\bigr); cc is obtained from the cross product, otherwise it cancels to 10−810^{-8} for two edges on one line. The outer integral uses tanh-sinh, split where the second edge is closest to a corner of the first. The logarithm is integrable at a common edge: the perpendicular rectangles match the closed form to rounding, where the double integral with n=64n = 64 is still 6 % off. The precondition is a free view – each surface lies entirely in front of the other; otherwise the transformation does not hold and kontur rejects.

In an enclosure of grey diffuse surfaces the radiosities are unknown:

Ji=εiσTi4+(1−εi)∑jFijJj,Qi=Ai(Ji−∑jFijJj);J_i = \varepsilon_i\,\sigma T_i^4 + (1 - \varepsilon_i)\sum_j F_{ij}J_j, \qquad Q_i = A_i\Big(J_i - \sum_j F_{ij}J_j\Big);

Derivation: the radiosity is the outgoing radiation, J=εEb+(1−ε)GJ = \varepsilon E_b + (1-\varepsilon)G with Eb=σT4E_b = \sigma T^4 and the irradiation Gi=1Ai∑jAjFjiJj=∑jFijJjG_i = \frac{1}{A_i}\sum_j A_jF_{ji}J_j = \sum_j F_{ij}J_j (reciprocity); the net flow is Q=A(J−G)Q = A(J - G). Eliminating GG gives

Qi=Eb,i−Ji(1−εi)/(εiAi),Qij=Ji−Jj1/(AiFij),Q_i = \frac{E_{b,i} - J_i}{(1-\varepsilon_i)/(\varepsilon_i A_i)}, \qquad Q_{ij} = \frac{J_i - J_j}{1/(A_iF_{ij})},

i.e. a network of surface resistances and space resistances (Oppenheim). If a surface has a prescribed heat flow instead of a temperature (e.g. adiabatic, Q=0Q = 0), its row reads Ji−∑jFijJj=Qi/AiJ_i - \sum_j F_{ij}J_j = Q_i/A_i, and the temperature follows afterwards from

σTi4=Ji+1−εiεiQiAi.\sigma T_i^4 = J_i + \frac{1-\varepsilon_i}{\varepsilon_i}\,\frac{Q_i}{A_i}.

Two parallel plates give q=σ(T14−T24)/(1/ε1+1/ε2−1)q = \sigma(T_1^4 - T_2^4)/(1/\varepsilon_1 + 1/\varepsilon_2 - 1), a small body in a large room Q=ε1σA1(T14−T24)Q = \varepsilon_1\sigma A_1(T_1^4 - T_2^4). The system is regular for ε>0\varepsilon > 0 because I−(1−ε)FI - (1-\varepsilon)F is strictly diagonally dominant in its row sums; black surfaces (ε=1\varepsilon = 1) give J=σT4J = \sigma T^4 directly, an isothermal enclosure Q=0Q = 0 regardless of the emissivities (Kirchhoff).

Gas radiation after WSGG

The weighted-sum-of-grey-gases model writes the emissivity of an H₂O-CO₂ mixture as

ε=∑iai(T)(1−e−kipL),ai(T)=∑jbijTj−1,\varepsilon = \sum_i a_i(T)\big(1 - e^{-k_i\,p\,L}\big), \qquad a_i(T) = \sum_j b_{ij}T^{j-1},

with three grey gases and one clear gas (coefficients after Smith, Shen and Friedman), valid for 600 to 2400 K and 0.001≤pL≤100.001 \le pL \le 10 atm·m. The coefficients are taken from a secondary source and not checked against the original; the probe checks properties only (limits, monotonicity).

Each grey gas ii covers part of the spectrum with a fixed absorption coefficient kik_i, the clear gas (k0=0k_0 = 0) the rest with weight a0=1−∑aia_0 = 1 - \sum a_i. This gives the limits: for thin layers ε→∑iaikipL\varepsilon \to \sum_i a_i k_i\,pL (optically thin, linear in pLpL), for thick ones \(\varepsilon \to \sum_i a_i < 1\). Smith, Shen and Friedman give five tables for \(p_{H_2O}/p_{CO_2}\) → 0, 1/2, 2, → ∞ (traces) and water vapour at 1 atm; in between the nearest table is taken piecewise (limits at water fractions 1/4, 7/12, 5/6), so the emissivity jumps there.

The Stefan problem

If a semi-infinite layer melts at a wall of fixed temperature, the front moves like s(t)=2λαlts(t) = 2\lambda\sqrt{\alpha_l t}; λ\lambda follows from Neumann's transcendental equation (one-phase: λeλ2erf⁡λ=St⁡/π\lambda e^{\lambda^2}\operatorname{erf}\lambda = \mathrm{St}/\sqrt\pi, two-phase with the heat flux into the solid).

Derivation. In both phases the heat equation Tt=αTxxT_t = \alpha T_{xx} holds; without a length scale of their own the solutions depend only on η=x/(2αt)\eta = x/(2\sqrt{\alpha t}), so

Tl=Tw−(Tw−Tm)erf⁡(x/2αlt)erf⁡λ,Ts=Ti+(Tm−Ti)erfc⁡(x/2αst)erfc⁡(λν),ν=αl/αs,T_l = T_w - (T_w - T_m)\frac{\operatorname{erf}\big(x/2\sqrt{\alpha_l t}\big)}{\operatorname{erf}\lambda}, \qquad T_s = T_i + (T_m - T_i)\frac{\operatorname{erfc}\big(x/2\sqrt{\alpha_s t}\big)}{\operatorname{erfc}(\lambda\nu)}, \qquad \nu = \sqrt{\alpha_l/\alpha_s},

and the front lies at s=2λαlts = 2\lambda\sqrt{\alpha_l t}, because T=TmT = T_m must hold there for all tt. The Stefan condition ρLs˙=−klTl,x+ksTs,x\rho L\,\dot s = -k_l T_{l,x} + k_s T_{s,x} at the front gives the equation for λ\lambda:

e−λ2πerf⁡λ−ksν(Tm−Ti)kl(Tw−Tm)e−ν2λ2πerfc⁡(νλ)=λStl⁡,Stl⁡=cl(Tw−Tm)L.\frac{e^{-\lambda^2}}{\sqrt\pi\operatorname{erf}\lambda} - \frac{k_s\,\nu\,(T_m - T_i)}{k_l\,(T_w - T_m)}\,\frac{e^{-\nu^2\lambda^2}}{\sqrt\pi\operatorname{erfc}(\nu\lambda)} = \frac{\lambda}{\mathrm{St}_l}, \qquad \mathrm{St}_l = \frac{c_l(T_w - T_m)}{L}.

The left side falls from +∞+\infty in λ\lambda, the right side rises – there is exactly one root, which bisection on [10−8,5][10^{-8}, 5] finds safely. For Ti=TmT_i = T_m the one-phase form remains; for small Stefan numbers λ≈St⁡/2\lambda \approx \sqrt{\mathrm{St}/2} (quasi-steady: linear profile in the melt). The heat flux at the wall is qw=kl(Tw−Tm)/(παlterf⁡λ)q_w = k_l(T_w - T_m)/(\sqrt{\pi\alpha_l t}\,\operatorname{erf}\lambda), its time mean over [0,t][0, t] twice that.

For arbitrary initial and boundary conditions the enthalpy method computes explicitly with finite volumes in the volumetric enthalpy; the temperature follows from it and stays at the melting temperature in the two-phase region.

Hin+1−HinΔt=qi−1/2−qi+1/2Δx,qi−1/2=−2ki−1kiki−1+kiTi−Ti−1Δx,q1/2=−k1T1−TwΔx/2,\frac{H_i^{n+1} - H_i^n}{\Delta t} = \frac{q_{i-1/2} - q_{i+1/2}}{\Delta x}, \qquad q_{i-1/2} = -\frac{2k_{i-1}k_i}{k_{i-1}+k_i}\,\frac{T_i - T_{i-1}}{\Delta x}, \qquad q_{1/2} = -k_1\frac{T_1 - T_w}{\Delta x/2},

with T(H)T(H) piecewise linear (solid below H=0H = 0, two-phase region 0≤H≤ρL0 \le H \le \rho L, liquid above). The flux form conserves energy exactly; the explicit step is stable for Δt≤Δx2/(2αmax)\Delta t \le \Delta x^2/(2\alpha_{max}), computed with 0.4 instead of 0.5. Because the front is smeared within one volume and the temperature stands still there, the method is only first order in Δx\Delta x – the probe measures exactly that (error halves with the grid).

Boiling

The surface tension of water comes from the IAPWS equation

σ=235.8mN/mτ1.256(1−0.625τ),τ=1−T/Tc,\sigma = 235.8\ \text{mN/m}\,\tau^{1.256}(1 - 0.625\,\tau), \qquad \tau = 1 - T/T_c ,

(the exponent 1.256 is close to the universal critical value); the critical heat flux after Zuber

qmax=Chlvρv(σg(ρl−ρv)ρv2)1/4,C=0.149 (Lienhard and Dhir) or π/24;q_{max} = C\,h_{lv}\,\rho_v\Big(\frac{\sigma g(\rho_l - \rho_v)}{\rho_v^2}\Big)^{1/4}, \qquad C = 0.149 \text{ (Lienhard and Dhir) or } \pi/24;

follows from the stability of the vapour columns above the heater: the critical wavelength of the Rayleigh-Taylor instability is λT∼σ/(gΔρ)\lambda_T \sim \sqrt{\sigma/(g\Delta\rho)}, the Helmholtz instability limits the vapour velocity to uv∼(σgΔρ/ρv2)1/4u_v \sim (\sigma g\Delta\rho/\rho_v^2)^{1/4}, and qmax=ρvhlvuvq_{max} = \rho_v h_{lv} u_v times an area fraction – hence the fourth root and qmax∼g1/4q_{max} \sim g^{1/4}. Nucleate boiling after Rohsenow

q=μlhlv(g(ρl−ρv)σ)1/2(cp,lΔTCsfhlvPrln⁡)3q = \mu_l h_{lv}\Big(\frac{g(\rho_l - \rho_v)}{\sigma}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\frac{c_{p,l}\,\Delta T}{C_{sf}\,h_{lv}\,\mathrm{Pr}_l^{\,n}}\Big)^3

is a similarity form with the bubble departure length σ/(gΔρ)\sqrt{\sigma/(g\Delta\rho)} as length: q∼ΔT3q \sim \Delta T^3, ∼Csf−3\sim C_{sf}^{-3} and ∼g1/2\sim g^{1/2}. Because of the third power an error of 30 % in CsfC_{sf} means a factor 2 in heat flux – conversely the wall superheat for a given qq is fairly certain. Properties of water from IF97 and the IAPWS transport equations.

What follows. Where the Rohsenow curve crosses the critical heat flux (for water, 1 atm and Csf=0.013C_{sf} = 0.013 between 15 and 25 K superheat), nucleate boiling ends; a heated body with prescribed heat flux jumps there into film boiling, often by hundreds of kelvin.

How it is checked

  • Summation rule for cube and cuboid to 1e-12, double integral against the closed forms, discs, crossed strings, parallel plates, small body in a large room, Oppenheim network.
  • Neumann one- and two-phase against the enthalpy method (order 1); in doing so the probe found an inverted ratio of thermal diffusivities in the first version. The Stefan condition of the two-phase solution is recomputed from the profiles to 1e-13, the mean wall heat flux as well.
  • Reciprocity of the double integral for skew rectangles, crossed strings in an arbitrary triangle, black and isothermal enclosure, thin gas layer.
  • σ(100°C)=58.91\sigma(100\,°C) = 58.91 mN/m; qmax=1.26q_{max} = 1.26 MW/m² for water at 1 atm (textbook value).

Limits

Grey diffuse surfaces; WSGG unchecked against the original. Stefan problem one-dimensional. Film and impingement cooling (gap list LV24) deferred because no independent reference values were available.
