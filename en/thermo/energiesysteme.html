Steam power, refrigeration and solar position – background

The computation behind Dampfprozess and Sonnenstand: Rankine cycle with water after IF97 and reheat, the same cycle with pure fluids (ORC), vapour-compression refrigeration; solar position after Meeus with refraction and parallax, angle of incidence, sunrise and sunset, extraterrestrial radiation.

What it is about

Steam power plants, organic Rankine units for waste heat and refrigerators rest on cycles with phase change; their key figures depend on accurate properties in the wet-steam region. For solar plants the solar position is the basic quantity: from it follow angles of incidence on collectors and the radiation available outside the atmosphere.

Rankine and ORC

Pump inlet saturated liquid at condenser pressure (1). The pump raises isentropically to boiler pressure, with efficiency ηP\eta_P: h2=h1+(h2s−h1)/ηPh_2 = h_1 + (h_{2s} - h_1)/\eta_P. The boiler produces saturated or superheated steam (3); the turbine expands with ηT\eta_T: h4=h3−ηT(h3−h4s)h_4 = h_3 - \eta_T(h_3 - h_{4s}). With reheat, steam is heated again after a high-pressure stage and expanded further. Then

ηth=wT−wPqin,back-work ratio=wPwT.\eta_{th} = \frac{w_T - w_P}{q_{in}}, \qquad \text{back-work ratio} = \frac{w_P}{w_T}.

Why this form. For a steady flow process without kinetic and potential energy the work per mass is the enthalpy difference; the isentropic pump work for a nearly incompressible liquid is ≈vΔp\approx v\,\Delta p and therefore small (back work ratio below 2 %, unlike the gas turbine cycle). The energy balance over the cycle gives wT−wP=qin−qoutw_T - w_P = q_{in} - q_{out}, i.e. ηth=1−qout/qin\eta_{th} = 1 - q_{out}/q_{in}. Because all heat is added below the maximum temperature and rejected at condenser temperature, ηth\eta_{th} lies below Carnot 1−T1/T31 - T_1/T_3; reheat raises the mean temperature of heat addition and the quality at the turbine exit.

Water uses IF97; the same cycle with a pure fluid of the Helmholtz table (isopentane, toluene, …) is an ORC. Because the table only knows (T,ρ)(T, \rho) and (T,p)(T, p), (p,s)(p, s) and (p,h)(p, h) are inverted here: in the wet region via saturation and quality, otherwise with Brent in temperature. In the wet region (p,s)(p, s) or (p,h)(p, h) is linear in the quality,

x=s−s's''−s',h=h'+x(h''−h'),x = \frac{s - s'}{s'' - s'}, \qquad h = h' + x\,(h'' - h'),

outside s(T)s(T) at fixed pressure is strictly monotonic (∂s/∂T|p=cp/T>0\partial s/\partial T|_p = c_p/T > 0), so Brent finds exactly one root between saturation and maximum temperature.

Vapour-compression refrigeration

Compressor inlet saturated or superheated vapour at evaporator pressure (1), compression with isentropic efficiency (2), condenser down to saturated or subcooled liquid (3), isenthalpic throttle (4, h4=h3h_4 = h_3). Coefficient of performance of the refrigerator q0/w=(h1−h4)/(h2−h1)q_0/w = (h_1 - h_4)/(h_2 - h_1), of the heat pump (h2−h3)/w(h_2 - h_3)/w – larger by exactly 1 because of the isenthalpic throttle.

The energy balance qout=q0+wq_{out} = q_0 + w gives this directly. The upper bound is the reversed Carnot cycle, \(\varepsilon_R < T_0/(T_c - T_0)\) with the saturation temperatures of evaporator and condenser; the throttle is the irreversible point where the cycle loses most against it.

Solar position

From the Julian date follow mean longitude and anomaly of the sun, the equation of centre, the apparent longitude and the obliquity of the ecliptic (simplified method after Meeus, as in the NOAA calculator); from them declination δ\delta and equation of time.

JD=⌊365.25(Y+4716)⌋+⌊30.6001(M+1)⌋+D+B−1524.5,B=2−A+⌊A/4⌋,A=⌊Y/100⌋,JD = \lfloor 365.25(Y + 4716)\rfloor + \lfloor 30.6001(M + 1)\rfloor + D + B - 1524.5, \qquad B = 2 - A + \lfloor A/4\rfloor, \ A = \lfloor Y/100\rfloor,

(January and February as months 13 and 14 of the previous year, BB the Gregorian correction). The declination follows from the apparent longitude λ\lambda and the obliquity ε\varepsilon as sin⁡δ=sin⁡εsin⁡λ\sin\delta = \sin\varepsilon\sin\lambda; the equation of time is the difference between true and mean solar time, fed by the eccentricity of the orbit and the obliquity.

With the hour angle hh and the latitude φ\varphi:

cos⁡θz=sin⁡φsin⁡δ+cos⁡φcos⁡δcos⁡h,azimuth=atan2⁡(sin⁡h,cos⁡hsin⁡φ−tan⁡δcos⁡φ)+180°.\cos\theta_z = \sin\varphi\sin\delta + \cos\varphi\cos\delta\cos h, \qquad \text{azimuth} = \operatorname{atan2}\big(\sin h,\ \cos h\sin\varphi - \tan\delta\cos\varphi\big) + 180°.

The zenith formula is the cosine rule in the spherical triangle of pole, zenith and sun. The elevation is reduced by the parallax (8.794″) and increased by refraction after the SPA formula (with air pressure and temperature). The angle of incidence on a tilted surface is cos⁡θ=cos⁡θzcos⁡β+sin⁡βsin⁡θzcos⁡(γs−γ)\cos\theta = \cos\theta_z\cos\beta + \sin\beta\sin\theta_z\cos(\gamma_s - \gamma).

Sunrise and sunset (elevation −0.8333°: half the solar diameter and mean refraction at the horizon) are iterated because declination and equation of time change over the day.

The daily extraterrestrial radiation on a horizontal surface after Duffie and Beckman is

H0=24⋅3600Gscπ(1+0.033cos⁡2πn365)(cos⁡φcos⁡δsin⁡ωs+ωssin⁡φsin⁡δ).H_0 = \frac{24\cdot 3600\,G_{sc}}{\pi}\Big(1 + 0.033\cos\frac{2\pi n}{365}\Big)\Big(\cos\varphi\cos\delta\sin\omega_s + \omega_s\sin\varphi\sin\delta\Big).

It is the integral of Goncos⁡θzG_{on}\cos\theta_z over the hour angle from −ωs-\omega_s to ωs\omega_s (with dt=12h/πdωdt = 12\,\text{h}/\pi\,d\omega), and cos⁡ωs=−tan⁡φtan⁡δ\cos\omega_s = -\tan\varphi\tan\delta is the sunset hour angle. If its magnitude exceeds 1, the sun does not rise (ωs=0\omega_s = 0, polar night) or does not set (ωs=π\omega_s = \pi, polar day). Here the declination is computed after Cooper, δ=23.45°sin⁡(2π(284+n)/365)\delta = 23.45°\sin(2\pi(284 + n)/365), as in the textbook – not with the more accurate method above.

How it is checked

  • Textbook examples (Cengel and Boles): simple cycle 3 MPa / 350 °C / 75 kPa – η=0.2602\eta = 0.2602 (0.260), x4=0.8861x_4 = 0.8861; with reheat 15 MPa / 600 °C, 4 MPa / 600 °C, 10 kPa – η=0.4499\eta = 0.4499 (0.450), x6=0.8962x_6 = 0.8962; refrigeration with R134a 0.14 / 0.8 MPa – COP 3.968 (3.97).
  • In addition: pump and turbine at efficiency 1 isentropic (1e-9), cycles below Carnot, energy balance with reheat to 1e-12; Julian date at table values, zenith up to the parallax, angle of incidence in the limiting cases, polar night and polar day.
  • Water after IF97 against IAPWS-95 in the same cycle to 1e-5; ORC with isopentane below the Carnot efficiency, energy balance closed; pump and turbine efficiencies act exactly as required.
  • Solar position: reference example of the SPA (Reda and Andreas) – zenith 50.1135° (50.11162), azimuth 194.3426° (194.34024), angle of incidence to 0.01°, equation of time to 0.02 min, sunrise and transit to seconds; the sunset of the example (17:20:19) belongs to the previous evening local time, where 17:20:18 results. Duffie-Beckman ex. 1.10.1: H0H_0 = 33.77 MJ/m² (33.8).

Limits

Cycles without feedwater heating and without pressure losses in the heat exchangers; ORC below the critical pressure. Solar position with the simplified method (about 0.01° between 1800 and 2100), no atmosphere apart from refraction.
