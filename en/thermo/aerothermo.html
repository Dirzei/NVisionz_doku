Aero-thermodynamics – background

The computation behind AeroThermo: mixing rule for fuel-air and water-air ratio, entropy function, inversions, gas dynamics with temperature-dependent cp, isentropic and polytropic efficiencies, standard atmosphere and flight speeds. Pseudocode following the source, worked examples, checks and limits.

What this is about

In gas turbine performance calculations the working fluid is not air but a mixture of air, combustion products and water vapour whose composition changes from component to component. Classical practice (ENVISIONZ-2020, ThermFunctions) describes this mixture with two numbers: the fuel-air ratio FAR (kg fuel per kg dry air) and the water-air ratio WAR (kg water per kg dry air). All properties are then functions of TT, FAR and WAR.

AeroThermo keeps exactly this interface and mixing rule but computes the properties of the components from the partition functions (background partition functions) instead of polynomials. On top of it sit the gas dynamics (static ↔ total at a given Mach number, flow function), the efficiency definitions for compressor and turbine, the standard atmosphere and the conversion of flight speeds. None of these calculations assumes a constant γ\gamma: wherever the textbook formula Tt/T=1+γ−12M2T_t/T = 1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2 would appear, the enthalpy and entropy function of the real mixture are used.

Mixture of air and stoichiometric products

A gas with 0≤FAR⁡≤FARst⁡0 \le \mathrm{FAR} \le \mathrm{FAR}_{st} is regarded as a mass mixture of pure air and stoichiometric combustion products. Burning the fuel mass FAR per kg of air uses the same amount of fuel that FAR⁡/FARst⁡\mathrm{FAR}/\mathrm{FAR}_{st} kg of air would burn stoichiometrically, producing (1+FARst⁡)FAR⁡/FARst⁡(1 + \mathrm{FAR}_{st})\,\mathrm{FAR}/\mathrm{FAR}_{st} kg of products. Relative to the total mass 1+FAR⁡1 + \mathrm{FAR}, the mass fraction of products is

w(FAR⁡)=FAR⁡1+FAR⁡⋅1+FARst⁡FARst⁡w(\mathrm{FAR}) = \frac{\mathrm{FAR}}{1 + \mathrm{FAR}}\cdot\frac{1 + \mathrm{FAR}_{st}}{\mathrm{FAR}_{st}}

and every mass-specific quantity X=cp,h,φX = c_p, h, \varphi mixes linearly:

X(T,FAR⁡)=Xair⁡(T)+w(FAR⁡)[Xst(T)−Xair⁡(T)]X(T, \mathrm{FAR}) = X_{\mathrm{air}}(T) + w(\mathrm{FAR})\,\bigl[X_{st}(T) - X_{\mathrm{air}}(T)\bigr]

The stoichiometric products follow from one mole of air (N2 0.78084, O2 0.209476, Ar 0.009365, CO2 0.000319) and kerosene with 86.2 % C and 13.8 % H (mass), FARst⁡=0.0681061\mathrm{FAR}_{st} = 0.0681061:

nC=FARst⁡⋅28.9647⋅0.86212.0107,nH=FARst⁡⋅28.9647⋅0.1381.00794,nCO2=0.000319+nC,nH2O=nH2n_C = \mathrm{FAR}_{st}\cdot 28.9647\cdot\frac{0.862}{12.0107}, \quad n_H = \mathrm{FAR}_{st}\cdot 28.9647\cdot\frac{0.138}{1.00794}, \quad n_{\mathrm{CO_2}} = 0.000319 + n_C,\quad n_{\mathrm{H_2O}} = \frac{n_H}{2}

Oxygen is zero at stoichiometry; the product mole fractions are ni/∑nn_i/\sum n, the properties XstX_{st} are mole-fraction weighted sums of the partition-function values, divided by the mean molar mass. For the nine NJFCP fuels the same calculation exists with a fuel-specific FARst⁡\mathrm{FAR}_{st} and product vector (AeroThermo.FuelGas.of(fuel); nF=xO2/(nC+nH/4)n_F = x_{\mathrm{O_2}}/(n_C + n_H/4), FARst⁡=nFMapp⁡/28.9647\mathrm{FAR}_{st} = n_F M_{\mathrm{app}}/28.9647).

Humidity (WAR). Water vapour is mixed in on a mass basis, again ideal from the partition function of H2O:

X(T,FAR⁡,WAR⁡)=(1+FAR⁡)X(T,FAR⁡)+WAR⁡⋅XH2O(T)1+FAR⁡+WAR⁡X(T, \mathrm{FAR}, \mathrm{WAR}) = \frac{(1 + \mathrm{FAR})\,X(T, \mathrm{FAR}) + \mathrm{WAR}\cdot X_{\mathrm{H_2O}}(T)}{1 + \mathrm{FAR} + \mathrm{WAR}}

With WAR ≤ 0 all three-argument forms reduce exactly to the two-argument forms. The gas constant mixes the same way: R(FAR⁡)=Rair⁡+w(Rst−Rair⁡)R(\mathrm{FAR}) = R_{\mathrm{air}} + w\,(R_{st} - R_{\mathrm{air}}), then with WAR and RH2OR_{\mathrm{H_2O}}.

Convention in the framework: the FAR mixing rule is that of ThermFunctions – deliberately, so that maps and cycles from ENVISIONZ-2020 stay comparable. The gas dynamics and efficiencies use Rair⁡=287.049R_{\mathrm{air}} = 287.049 J/(kg K) also for FAR > 0, as the original did; only the WAR family (gammaOfT(t, far, war), sOfTP) uses rGas(far, war). γ(T,FAR⁡)=cp/(cp−Rair⁡)\gamma(T, \mathrm{FAR}) = c_p/(c_p - R_{\mathrm{air}}).

Entropy function and isentrope

For an ideal gas the specific entropy separates into a temperature part and a pressure part. AeroThermo calls the temperature part φ(T)\varphi(T) – it is s∘(T)s^\circ(T) at 1 bar, mass-specific:

s(T,p)=φ(T)−Rln⁡pp0,φ(T)=s∘(T),p0=105Pa⁡s(T, p) = \varphi(T) - R\,\ln\frac{p}{p_0}, \qquad \varphi(T) = s^\circ(T), \qquad p_0 = 10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}

Two states on the same isentrope therefore satisfy

φ(T2)−φ(T1)=Rln⁡p2p1⇒p2p1=exp⁡(φ(T2)−φ(T1)R)\varphi(T_2) - \varphi(T_1) = R\,\ln\frac{p_2}{p_1} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{p_2}{p_1} = \exp\left(\frac{\varphi(T_2) - \varphi(T_1)}{R}\right)

This is the formula used for all pressure ratios of the class – exact for any cp(T)c_p(T), without an averaged isentropic exponent. sOfTP(t, p, far, war) returns the absolute entropy for T-s diagrams.

Inversions T(h) and T(φ)

// tOfH(h, far): Newton from a fixed start
T ← 300
for i ← 1 to 40:
    f ← h(T, far) − h
    T ← T − f / cp(T, far)
    if |f| < 10⁻⁴·max(1, |h|): stop              the last step has already been taken
return T

// tOfPhi(φ, far): same with dφ/dT = cp/T
T ← 300
for i ← 1 to 40:
    f ← φ(T, far) − φ
    T ← T − f / (cp(T, far)/T)
    if |f| < 10⁻⁶·max(1, |φ|): stop

Source: envisionz-framework-thermo/src/envisionz/thermo/AeroThermo.java, tOfH, tOfPhi (also with WAR). The stopping test checks the residual before the last Newton step; the returned value has therefore had one more step. Example: tOfH(hOfT(1234.5 K, 0.03), 0.03) hits to 10−910^{-9} K.

Gas dynamics with cp(T)

Between the total state (Tt,ptT_t, p_t) and the static state (Ts,psT_s, p_s), adiabatic and loss-free flow conserves energy and entropy; the Mach number links the velocity to the speed of sound of the static state:

h(Tt)=h(Ts)+v22,v=Mγ(Ts)RTs,ptps=exp⁡(φ(Tt)−φ(Ts)R)h(T_t) = h(T_s) + \frac{v^2}{2}, \qquad v = M\sqrt{\gamma(T_s)\,R\,T_s}, \qquad \frac{p_t}{p_s} = \exp\left(\frac{\varphi(T_t) - \varphi(T_s)}{R}\right)

Usually TsT_s is wanted for given MM and TtT_t. The defining equation

f(Ts)=2[h(Tt)−h(Ts)]−M2γ(Ts)RTs=0f(T_s) = 2\,\bigl[h(T_t) - h(T_s)\bigr] - M^2\,\gamma(T_s)\,R\,T_s = 0

decreases in TsT_s and is solved by bisection between 40 K and TtT_t, 60 steps (interval width afterwards Tt⋅2−60T_t\cdot 2^{-60}). For M<0.01M < 0.01 the velocity is formed with γ(Tt)\gamma(T_t) and TsT_s is found via tOfH – there the bisection is poorly conditioned.

Flow function. With m˙=ρsvA\dot m = \rho_s v A and ρs=ps/(RTs)\rho_s = p_s/(R T_s):

Qa=m˙TtApt=vTtRTs(pt/ps)[kg⁡KPam2s]Q_a = \frac{\dot m\sqrt{T_t}}{A\,p_t} = \frac{v\,\sqrt{T_t}}{R\,T_s\,(p_t/p_s)}\quad \left[\frac{\mathrm{kg}\,\sqrt{\mathrm K}}{\mathrm{Pa\,m^2\,s}}\right]

defined for 10−4<M≤110^{-4} < M \le 1. The inverse mnOfQa bisects on the subsonic branch between M=10−4M = 10^{-4} and 1 (60 steps); aOfMn, mnOfA and ptOfMn (area, Mach number, total pressure from mass flow) build on it. mnOfPq goes the other way via the entropy function: TsT_s from φ(Ts)=φ(Tt)−Rln⁡(pt/ps)\varphi(T_s) = \varphi(T_t) - R\ln(p_t/p_s), then v=2(ht−hs)v = \sqrt{2(h_t - h_s)} and M=v/γRTsM = v/\sqrt{\gamma R T_s}.

Pseudocode – AeroThermo.tsOfMn(M, Tt, far) and qaOfMn

if M < 0: return NaN

Isentropic and polytropic efficiencies

Isentropic efficiency, compressor ηs=(h2s−h1)/(h2−h1)\eta_s = (h_{2s} - h_1)/(h_2 - h_1), turbine ηs=(h1−h2)/(h1−h2s)\eta_s = (h_1 - h_2)/(h_1 - h_{2s}). With the entropy function:

Polytropic efficiency – the efficiency of an infinitesimal compression step, dhs=ηpdh\mathrm{d}h_s = \eta_p\,\mathrm{d}h. For the ideal gas with dhs=vdp=RTdp/p\mathrm{d}h_s = v\,\mathrm{d}p = RT\,\mathrm{d}p/p and dh=Tdφ\mathrm{d}h = T\,\mathrm{d}\varphi, along the process Rdln⁡p=ηpdφR\,\mathrm{d}\ln p = \eta_p\,\mathrm{d}\varphi (compressor), integrated

Rln⁡π=ηp[φ(T2)−φ(T1)](compressor),Rln⁡π=φ(T2)−φ(T1)ηp(turbine)R\ln\pi = \eta_p\,\bigl[\varphi(T_2) - \varphi(T_1)\bigr] \quad(\text{compressor}), \qquad R\ln\pi = \frac{\varphi(T_2) - \varphi(T_1)}{\eta_p} \quad(\text{turbine})

From this pqOfDhqtPoly, texOfPqPoly (φ(T2)=φ1+Rln⁡π/ηp\varphi(T_2) = \varphi_1 + R\ln\pi/\eta_p) and the conversions etaPolyOfIsen and etaIsenOfPoly (both for the compressor) are computed:

// etaPolyOfIsen(Tt, π, ηs, far)
h1 ← h(Tt);  φ1 ← φ(Tt)
h2s ← h(tOfPhi(φ1 + R·ln π))
T2 ← tOfH(h1 + (h2s − h1)/ηs)                    real exit
return R·ln π / (φ(T2) − φ1)

// etaIsenOfPoly(Tt, π, ηp, far)
T2 ← tOfPhi(φ1 + R·ln(π^(1/ηp)))                 real exit, polytropic
h2s ← h(tOfPhi(φ1 + R·ln π))
return (h2s − h1)/(h(T2) − h1)

Source: AeroThermo.java, section "Wirkungsgrade".

Convention in the framework: the class expects Δh/T\Delta h/T positive for compressor and turbine and pqOfDhqt returns a pressure ratio greater than 1 (compressor p2/p1p_2/p_1, turbine p1/p2p_1/p_2), like the existing maps. dhqtOfPq, however, returns (h2−h1)/Tt(h_2 - h_1)/T_t with sign, i.e. negative for the turbine, and pqOfDhqtPoly returns p2/p1<1p_2/p_1 < 1 for the turbine (see limits).

How it is checked

  • test/ThermoTest.java: cp(T,FAR⁡)c_p(T, \mathrm{FAR}) at 400, 800, 1200, 1600, 2000 K and FAR 0; 0.02; 0.05; 0.0681 against stored target values to 0.05 % (regression); round trip T→h→TT \to h \to T for 15 points to 10−410^{-4} %; 1<γ<5/31 < \gamma < 5/3, cp>0c_p > 0, cpc_p rises with FAR, γ\gamma falls with TT.
  • Validation reports against the old ThermFunctions (test/gasdynamik-vs-thermfunctions.txt, test/pqofdhqt-vs-thermfunctions.txt): gas dynamics to 0.2 %, pqOfDhqt at most 0.33 % (compressor 250–900 K, turbine 900–1800 K), round trip 3⋅10−93\cdot10^{-9}. The reports predate the anharmonic rework; according to ThermoTest today's cpc_p values lie up to 1.0 % above the report (2000 K, FAR 0.0681) – as an improvement, not an error.
  • AeroThermo.pqOfDhqt(far = 0) and AirFunctions.pqOfDhqt agree to about 3⋅10−83\cdot10^{-8} relative (in this example 1.4187901 against 1.4187900, both 1.418790 to six places); they are not bit-identical, because AirFunctions searches the temperature with a different start and stopping rule.

Limits

  • Rich mixture: for FAR > FARst, w>1w > 1 and the mixing rule extrapolates beyond the stoichiometric products; it is physically meaningful only up to FARst. Dissociation is not included – use Combustion or equilibrium.
  • No table path for FAR > 0: air comes from the AirFunctions table, the products are computed exactly from the partition functions at every call (including H2O with the Watson rotor). Calls with FAR > 0 are therefore slow.
  • Newton without bracketing: tOfH and tOfPhi start at a fixed 300 K. Robust in the operating range, but unprotected against absurd inputs.
  • Subsonic: qaOfMn is defined only for M≤1M \le 1, mnOfQa returns only the subsonic branch.
  • Sign conventions of the efficiency functions are not uniform: pqOfDhqt returns p1/p2>1p_1/p_2 > 1 for the turbine, pqOfDhqtPoly p2/p1<1p_2/p_1 < 1 (e.g. 1.49983 against 0.66803 at Δh/T\Delta h/T = 100, η\eta = 0.9, 1500 K, FAR 0.02); dhqtOfPq returns a negative value for the turbine.
  • Humidity: specHumOfRelHum uses an empirical approximation for the saturation pressure over water – not a Magnus formula, as the class documentation said until 2026-09-28 – which lies 0.6–1.0 % above IAPWS-IF97 between 273 and 373 K (2358.6 instead of 2339.2 Pa at 20 °C); If97.psat is more accurate (water).
