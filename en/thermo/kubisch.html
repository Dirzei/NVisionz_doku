Real gases – cubic equations of state Peng-Robinson and SRK

The computation behind Kubisch and Reinstoffe: the general cubic form, Ωa, Ωb and Zc from criticality, mixing rules, stable root computation, fugacity coefficients and departure functions, saturation pressure between the spinodals, flash with Michelsen's stability test and Rachford-Rice. Pseudocode, examples, check against CoolProp, limits.

What this is about

At high pressure and close to condensation pv=RTpv = RT no longer holds: natural gas in a pipeline, refrigerants, CO2 near the critical point, fuel condensing in a line. Cubic equations of state are the simplest approach that describes gas and liquid with one equation. They need only three numbers per substance – critical temperature, critical pressure, acentric factor ω – and therefore suit mixtures of any composition. Their strength is vapour pressures and phase equilibria; liquid densities and the vicinity of the critical point are described only roughly. For reference accuracy there are the Helmholtz equations (Helmholtz – background).

Peng-Robinson (1976) and Soave-Redlich-Kwong (1972) are special cases of

For the pure substance

Ωa, Ωb and Zc are not measured values. They follow from the condition that the cubic in Z has a triple root at the critical point. Comparing coefficients with (Z−Zc)3(Z - Z_c)^3 gives Zc=[1+(1−u)Ωb]/3Z_c = [1 + (1 - u)\Omega_b]/3, for Ωb\Omega_b the cubic

(u+2)3Ωb3−3(u2−5u−9w−5)Ωb2+3(u+2)Ωb−1=0(u + 2)^3\,\Omega_b^3 - 3(u^2 - 5u - 9w - 5)\,\Omega_b^2 + 3(u + 2)\,\Omega_b - 1 = 0

and Ωa=Ωb2(u2+u−3w+1)/3+Ωb(u+2)/3+1/3\Omega_a = \Omega_b^2(u^2 + u - 3w + 1)/3 + \Omega_b(u + 2)/3 + 1/3. The class solves the cubic at load time by Newton (start 0.08, until |Δ|≤10−17Ωb|\Delta| \le 10^{-17}\Omega_b); uu and ww are stored exactly, not formed from a rounded √2. Only the two m(ω) polynomials are empirical.

Mixtures after van der Waals with binary parameters kijk_{ij} (default 0; there is no table with sources, the caller provides them, symmetric):

Roots and properties

Cardano alone loses the small roots at low pressure through cancellation. Hence: largest real root by Cardano (trigonometric for three real roots), Newton polish, then the remaining quadratic with the stable formula, both roots polished. Only roots Z>BZ > B are valid; the phase selects the smallest (liquid), the largest (vapour) or the one with lower Gibbs energy

gRRT=Z−1−ln⁡(Z−B)−ABL,L=1Δ1−Δ2ln⁡Z+Δ1BZ+Δ2B\frac{g^R}{RT} = Z - 1 - \ln(Z - B) - \frac{A}{B}L, \qquad L = \frac{1}{\Delta_1 - \Delta_2}\ln\frac{Z + \Delta_1 B}{Z + \Delta_2 B}

Fugacity coefficients and departure functions at equal (T, p):

ln⁡φi=bib(Z−1)−ln⁡(Z−B)−AB(2∑jxjaija−bib)L\ln\varphi_i = \frac{b_i}{b}(Z - 1) - \ln(Z - B) - \frac{A}{B}\left(\frac{2\sum_j x_j a_{ij}}{a} - \frac{b_i}{b}\right)L

h−hig=RT(Z−1)+Tda/dT−abL,s−sig=Rln⁡(Z−B)+da/dTbLh - h^{ig} = RT(Z - 1) + \frac{T\,\mathrm da/\mathrm dT - a}{b}\,L, \qquad s - s^{ig} = R\ln(Z - B) + \frac{\mathrm da/\mathrm dT}{b}\,L

with aij=aiaj(1−kij)a_{ij} = \sqrt{a_ia_j}(1 - k_{ij}) and dαi/dT=−miαi/TTc,i\mathrm d\alpha_i/\mathrm dT = -m_i\sqrt{\alpha_i}/\sqrt{T T_{c,i}}. The ideal part does not belong to the cubic equation; the class returns only the residual parts, molar.

// wurzeln(A, B)
c2 ← uB − B − 1;  c1 ← A + wB² − uB − uB²;  c0 ← −(AB + wB² + wB³)

z1 ← Newton(z1)                          at most 8 steps, improving steps only

    r ← −½·(q1 + sign(q1)·√(q1² − 4q0));  z2 ← r;  z3 ← q0/r   stable formula

Saturation pressure of the pure substance

Below TcT_c the isotherm p(v)p(v) has a local minimum and maximum, the spinodals. Only between them do liquid and vapour roots exist at the same time; the saturation pressure is where both have equal fugacity, f(ln⁡p)=ln⁡φL−ln⁡φV=0f(\ln p) = \ln\varphi_L - \ln\varphi_V = 0.

// saettigungsdruck(i, T), T < T_c
spinodals: sign changes of ∂p/∂v on 4000 log-spaced points between b(1+10⁻⁹) and 10⁷·b,
           Brent (10⁻¹⁵) per change → v_min, v_max → p_min, p_max
lb ← ln(p_max·(1 − 10⁻⁶))                      there f < 0: vapour more stable
la ← lb;  while f(la) ≤ 0: la ← max(la − ln 10, ln(p_min·(1 + 10⁻⁶)))   decades downwards
return exp(Brent(f, la, lb, 10⁻¹⁴))

The distance 10⁻⁶ from the spinodals prevents two roots from merging so that the calculation sees only one.

Flash: isothermal-isobaric split

Given TT, pp and the feed composition zz; wanted is whether the mixture splits into two phases, and if so the vapour fraction β\beta and the compositions xx (liquid), yy (vapour).

1. Start K values without an empirical formula, from the equation itself: Ki=ps,i(T)/pK_i = p_{s,i}(T)/p for 0.3Tc<T<Tc0.3\,T_c < T < T_c; outside, ln⁡(ps/pc)\ln(p_s/p_c) is continued linearly in 1/T1/T through the point at 0.7Tc0.7\,T_c and the critical point (Clausius-Clapeyron line).

2. Michelsen's stability test (tangent plane): with di=ln⁡zi+ln⁡φi(z)d_i = \ln z_i + \ln\varphi_i(z) a trial phase WW is iterated by successive substitution Wi←exp⁡(di−ln⁡φi(W/∑W))W_i \leftarrow \exp(d_i - \ln\varphi_i(W/\sum W)) (until ∑(Δln⁡W)2<10−20\sum(\Delta\ln W)^2 < 10^{-20}, at most 1000), once vapour-like (start ziKiz_iK_i), once liquid-like (start zi/Kiz_i/K_i). The feed is unstable if the trial phase does not fall back to zz (distance ≥ 10⁻⁶) and

tm*=1−∑iWi<−10−10tm^* = 1 - \sum_i W_i < -10^{-10}

The K values are then reset from the trial phase.

3. Rachford-Rice and successive substitution. For given K values β\beta is the root of

∑izi(Ki−1)1+β(Ki−1)=0,11−Kmax⁡<β<11−Kmin⁡\sum_i \frac{z_i(K_i - 1)}{1 + \beta(K_i - 1)} = 0, \qquad \frac{1}{1 - K_{\max}} < \beta < \frac{1}{1 - K_{\min}}

(Brent between the poles), then xi=zi/(1+β(Ki−1))x_i = z_i/(1 + \beta(K_i - 1)), yi=Kixiy_i = K_ix_i, normalised, and new K values ln⁡Ki=ln⁡φiL(x)−ln⁡φiV(y)\ln K_i = \ln\varphi_i^L(x) - \ln\varphi_i^V(y), until ∑(Δln⁡K)2<10−26\sum(\Delta\ln K)^2 < 10^{-26} (at most 2000 steps).

// flash(T, p, z)
ln K ← ln startK(T, p)
d ← ln z + ln φ(z, stable root)
for direction ∈ (vapour-like, liquid-like), while not unstable:
    W ← stability(d, start z·K or z/K)
    if W unstable: ln K from W;  unstable ← true
if not unstable: return single phase (β = 1 vapour or 0 liquid)
repeat until Σ(Δ ln K)² < 10⁻²⁶ (at most 2000):
    β ← RachfordRice(z, K);  x, y from β, K;  normalise
    ln K ← ln φL(x) − ln φV(y)
β ← RachfordRice(z, K)
if β ≤ 0 or β ≥ 1: return single phase
return (β, x, y, two-phase, iterations)

How it is checked

  • Constants: SRK Ωb = (21/3 − 1)/3, Ωa = 1/(9(21/3 − 1)), Zc = 1/3 to 10⁻¹⁵; Peng-Robinson against the exact values of Bell and Deiters (2021) in CoolProp to 10⁻¹⁵; Z at the critical point of methane.

According to the module description PR to 10⁻¹¹, SRK to 10⁻⁸.
  • Own consequences: at the own saturation pressure ln⁡φL=ln⁡φV\ln\varphi_L = \ln\varphi_V to 10⁻¹⁰ (5 substances, T/Tc 0.5 to 0.99); sR=−∂gR/∂Ts^R = -\partial g^R/\partial T to 10⁻⁷ and hR=gR+TsRh^R = g^R + Ts^R; symmetry of ∂ln⁡φi/∂nj\partial\ln\varphi_i/\partial n_j (10⁻⁶); ideal gas at 1 mPa |Z−1|<10−10|Z - 1| < 10^{-10}.

Found during construction (finding in CoolProp): Ωa and Ωb for SRK with transposed digits (4·10⁻¹⁰), Ωb for PR with a missing digit (5·10⁻¹⁴); CoolProp iterates the saturation pressure only to about 2·10⁻⁸.

Limits

  • Liquid densities are rough (typically percent to above 10 %); at the critical point Zc of the equation (0.307 or 1/3) does not match that of the substance. No volume translation (Péneloux) implemented.
  • No kij table: without input 0 applies; for polar and associating substances (water, alcohols, NH3) cubic equations with van der Waals mixing are weak anyway.
  • Two phases: the flash knows only vapour-liquid, no liquid-liquid or three-phase equilibrium.
  • Residual parts only: absolute caloric values additionally need an ideal-gas model.
  • Saturation only below TcT_c; near TcT_c the spinodals close in and the bracket becomes narrow.
