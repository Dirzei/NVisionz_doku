Water hammer, Windkessel and duct acoustics – background

The computation behind Druckstoss, Windkessel and Transfermatrix: the method of characteristics for pipe networks with reservoirs, junctions and valves; Windkessel models with two to four elements; plane sound waves with transfer matrices and the transmission loss of silencers.

What it is about

Three tasks, one idea – waves in conduits. If a valve closes fast, a pressure wave runs through the pipe network (water hammer). The arterial tree buffers the pulsating flow of the heart; the simplest model for this is the Windkessel. And in intake and exhaust ducts, area changes and side branches decide which frequencies pass.

Water hammer by the method of characteristics

For head HH and flow QQ in a pipe with wave speed aa and area AA the compatibility conditions along the characteristics dx/dt=±adx/dt = \pm a are

C±:HP=HA,B∓B(QP−QA,B)∓RQA,B|QA,B|,B=agA,R=fΔx2gDA2.C^\pm:\quad H_P = H_{A,B} \mp B\,(Q_P - Q_{A,B}) \mp R\,Q_{A,B}|Q_{A,B}|, \qquad B = \frac{a}{gA},\ R = \frac{f\,\Delta x}{2 g D A^2}.

Derivation. Continuity and momentum in an elastic pipe, Ht+a2gAQx=0H_t + \frac{a^2}{gA}Q_x = 0 and Qt+gAHx+f2DAQ|Q|=0Q_t + gA\,H_x + \frac{f}{2DA}Q|Q| = 0, are hyperbolic with eigenvalues ±a\pm a. The combination "first equation ±\pm BB times the second" with B=a/(gA)B = a/(gA) becomes along dx/dt=±adx/dt = \pm a an ordinary equation dH/dt±BdQ/dt±af2gDA2Q|Q|=0dH/dt \pm B\,dQ/dt \pm \frac{a f}{2gDA^2}Q|Q| = 0; integrated over one step with friction evaluated at the foot point this gives the compatibility conditions above. Two points A (left) and B (right) give two equations for HPH_P and QPQ_P at the new point P:

HP=12(CP+CM),QP=CP−CM2B,CP=HA+BQA−RQA|QA|,CM=HB−BQB+RQB|QB|.H_P = \tfrac12\big(C_P + C_M\big), \quad Q_P = \frac{C_P - C_M}{2B}, \qquad C_P = H_A + BQ_A - RQ_A|Q_A|, \ C_M = H_B - BQ_B + RQ_B|Q_B|.

A steady flow satisfies these equations exactly if the head loss per segment is RQ|Q|RQ|Q| – that is Darcy-Weisbach with fΔx/Df\Delta x/D. The initial state from the network solver therefore stays put with the valve held fixed.

With Δx=aΔt\Delta x = a\,\Delta t (Courant 1) the characteristics meet exactly at the grid points; the wave speed is adjusted slightly per pipe for this and reported. Boundaries: reservoir (HH fixed), junction (continuity, equal head), valve with Q=τ(t)KH−HdownQ = \tau(t)K\sqrt{H - H_{down}} in every step. The initial state is the steady one at τ(0)\tau(0), computed with the network solver from framework-math. On sudden closure the head jumps by ΔH=aΔV/g\Delta H = a\,\Delta V/g (Joukowsky).

This follows directly from C+C^+ at the valve with QP=0Q_P = 0: HP−HA=BQA=aV0/gH_P - H_A = BQ_A = aV_0/g. At a junction all connected pipes share the same head; continuity ∑Q=0\sum Q = 0 gives HP=∑(C/B)/∑(1/B)H_P = \sum (C/B)/\sum(1/B), so at an area change the transmission factor 2B2/(B1+B2)2B_2/(B_1 + B_2) for the pressure wave. If the valve closes in a time tst_s longer than the reflection time 2L/a2L/a, the relief wave from the reservoir returns before the valve is shut; the peak falls roughly like 2LV0/(gts)2LV_0/(gt_s) (Michaud).

Windkessel

The peripheral resistance RR lies in parallel with the compliance CC (two elements); upstream the characteristic impedance ZcZ_c (three elements) and in parallel with it the inertance LL (four elements after Stergiopulos). The input impedance is

Z(ω)=iωLZcZc+iωL+R1+iωRC(without L:Zc+R1+iωRC),Z(\omega) = \frac{i\omega L\,Z_c}{Z_c + i\omega L} + \frac{R}{1 + i\omega RC} \qquad (\text{without } L: Z_c + \tfrac{R}{1 + i\omega RC}),

with Z(0)=R+ZcZ(0) = R + Z_c (four elements: RR) and Z(∞)=ZcZ(\infty) = Z_c. The formula is the series and parallel connection of complex impedances (1/(iωC)1/(i\omega C) for the compliance, iωLi\omega L for the inertance); for L→∞L \to \infty nothing flows through the inertance and three elements remain, for L→0L \to 0 it short-circuits ZcZ_c and two remain. In the time domain pCp_C and the flow through LL are states: Cp˙C=Q−pC/RC\dot p_C = Q - p_C/R, Lq˙L=Zc(Q−qL)L\dot q_L = Z_c(Q - q_L), p=pC+Zc(Q−qL)p = p_C + Z_c(Q - q_L); integrated with Dormand-Prince up to the periodic steady state. In diastole (Q=0Q = 0) the pressure falls like e−t/RCe^{-t/RC}.

Because the system is linear, the periodic steady response to a periodic flow Q=∑kQ^keikωtQ = \sum_k \hat Q_k e^{ik\omega t} is exactly p=∑kZ(kω)Q^keikωtp = \sum_k Z(k\omega)\hat Q_k e^{ik\omega t}; the probe compares time domain and superposition. The mean pressure is RQR\,\bar Q (for ω=0\omega = 0 without ZcZ_c in the four-element model).

Plane waves with transfer matrices

With sound pressure pp and volume velocity u=Svu = S v, a straight duct of length LL and characteristic impedance Z=ρc/SZ = \rho c/S connects both ends by

(p1u1)=(cos⁡kLiZsin⁡kLisin⁡kL/Zcos⁡kL)(p2u2);\begin{pmatrix} p_1 \\ u_1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos kL & i Z\sin kL \\ i\sin kL/Z & \cos kL \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} p_2 \\ u_2 \end{pmatrix};

It follows from the solution p=p+e−ikx+p−eikxp = p^+e^{-ikx} + p^-e^{ikx}, u=(p+e−ikx−p−eikx)/Zu = (p^+e^{-ikx} - p^-e^{ikx})/Z of the wave equation by eliminating the wave amplitudes between both ends. Its determinant is cos2⁡kL+sin2⁡kL=1\cos^2kL + \sin^2kL = 1 – an expression of reciprocity; a half-wave duct (kL=πkL = \pi) gives −I-I and is transparent.

An area change is the identity matrix, a side branch with impedance ZbZ_b the matrix [[1,0],[1/Zb,1]][[1, 0], [1/Z_b, 1]] (closed tube: Zb=−iZcot⁡kLbZ_b = -iZ\cot kL_b; Helmholtz resonator: Zb=i(ωρl/Sh−ρc2/(ωV))Z_b = i(\omega\rho l/S_h - \rho c^2/(\omega V))). The transmission loss with anechoic termination is

TL=20lg⁡|T11+T12/Zo+ZiT21+ZiT22/Zo|2+10lg⁡ZoZi;TL = 20\lg\frac{|T_{11} + T_{12}/Z_o + Z_i T_{21} + Z_i T_{22}/Z_o|}{2} + 10\lg\frac{Z_o}{Z_i};

for the expansion chamber with area ratio mm the closed form is TL=10lg⁡(1+14(m−1/m)2sin2⁡kL)TL = 10\lg\big(1 + \tfrac14(m - 1/m)^2\sin^2 kL\big).

The transmission loss is the ratio of incident to transmitted power: with anechoic termination p2=Zau2p_2 = Z_a u_2 the incident wave at the inlet is p+=(p1+Zeu1)/2p^+ = (p_1 + Z_e u_1)/2. It is never negative for a lossless chain, since ducts and blind side branches (purely imaginary ZbZ_b) cannot create power, only reflect it. The chamber is most effective at kL=π/2kL = \pi/2, a quarter wavelength, and not at all at kL=πkL = \pi – a single-chamber muffler has periodic pass bands.

How it is checked

  • Water hammer: Joukowsky exact, square wave with period 4L/a4L/a; passage through an area change with 2B2/(B1+B2)2B_2/(B_1 + B_2) exact; steady friction loss; slow closure stays below Joukowsky; valve equation in every step to 1e-12.
  • Windkessel: impedance limits; periodic response to harmonics against superposition with Z(kω)Z(k\omega) to 1e-9 mmHg for all three models; diastole exact.
  • In addition: the steady state stays put under the characteristic equations over the computing time; limits of the inertance in the Windkessel; 500 random lossless chains without negative loss, half-wave duct transparent.
  • Acoustics: duct without loss, det⁡T=1\det T = 1; area change 10lg⁡((1+m)2/(4m))10\lg((1+m)^2/(4m)); expansion chamber against the formula to 1e-10 dB; quarter-wave branch and Helmholtz resonator with stop frequency.

Limits

Water hammer without cavitation and with quasi-steady friction; Windkessel linear; acoustics without mean flow, without wall damping, plane waves only (below the first cross mode).
