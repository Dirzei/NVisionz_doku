Reactors, analysis, reduction and flames – background

The computation behind the package reaktor: stirred reactor steady and transient, plug flow, partially stirred reactor with IEM mixing; ignition delay as an event and its sensitivities, reaction fluxes; reduction with DRG, DRGEP and quasi-steady states; thermal NO after Zeldovich and a two-equation soot model; the free flat flame with the mass flux as eigenvalue and the counterflow flame as a similarity solution.

What it is about

A reaction mechanism (species, reactions, rate laws) says nothing yet about a combustor. Only a reactor model connects it with flow and mixing: perfectly stirred, without back-mixing (plug flow), partially mixed, or as a flame with diffusion. Added are tools that understand and shrink a mechanism, and models for pollutants. Computation works with every mechanism framework-thermo reads; concentrations in mol/cm³ as in the mechanism. Validated against Cantera 3.2 with the same mechanism files.

Stirred reactor, plug flow, PaSR

The adiabatic stirred reactor (PSR) at fixed pressure, with the residence time referred to the mass in the reactor (τ=m/m˙\tau = m/\dot m), satisfies transiently

dYkdt=Yk,in−Ykτ+ωkMkρ,cpdTdt=∑kYk,in[hk(Tin)−hk(T)]τ−∑khkωkMkρ\frac{\mathrm dY_k}{\mathrm dt} = \frac{Y_{k,\text{in}} - Y_k}{\tau} + \frac{\omega_k M_k}{\rho}, \qquad c_p\frac{\mathrm dT}{\mathrm dt} = \sum_k \frac{Y_{k,\text{in}}\,[h_k(T_{\text{in}}) - h_k(T)]}{\tau} - \sum_k \frac{h_k\,\omega_k M_k}{\rho}

with hkh_k per mass. Derivation of the temperature equation: the energy balance mdh/dt=m˙(hin−h)m\,\mathrm dh/\mathrm dt = \dot m(h_{\text{in}} - h) with h=∑kYkhk(T)h = \sum_k Y_k h_k(T) gives cpdT/dt+∑khkdYk/dt=(hin−h)/τc_p\,\mathrm dT/\mathrm dt + \sum_k h_k\,\mathrm dY_k/\mathrm dt = (h_{\text{in}} - h)/\tau; inserting the species equation, the terms in Ykhk(T)Y_k h_k(T) cancel down to the formula above. In steady state there remain

Yk,in−Yk+τωkMkρ=0,h=hinY_{k,\text{in}} - Y_k + \tau\,\frac{\omega_k M_k}{\rho} = 0, \qquad h = h_{\text{in}}

solved with damped Newton: Jacobian as forward difference (temperature 10−6T10^{-6}T, mass fractions 10−8+10−6|Y|10^{-8} + 10^{-6}|Y|), dense LU, temperature step at most 200 K, mass fractions not below zero; stop at a residual below 10−1010^{-10}. The temperature equation is normalised by cpTc_pT so that both kinds of rows are comparable. The start comes from the time history with RODAS4 over 30 residence times from a hot state. The S-curve reduces τ\tau by a factor as long as the steady state keeps more than 30 % of the temperature rise, and then brackets the blow-out limit by geometric bisection.

Plug flow takes the position as independent variable. At fixed mass flux g=ρug = \rho u and fixed pressure

dYkdx=ωkMkg,dTdx=ρgdTdt|batch,dtdx=ρg\frac{\mathrm dY_k}{\mathrm dx} = \frac{\omega_k M_k}{g}, \qquad \frac{\mathrm dT}{\mathrm dx} = \frac{\rho}{g}\,\frac{\mathrm dT}{\mathrm dt}\Big|_{\text{batch}}, \qquad \frac{\mathrm dt}{\mathrm dx} = \frac{\rho}{g}

– a fluid particle experiences exactly the history of the closed reactor in the time t(x)=∫0xρ/gdx't(x) = \int_0^x \rho/g\,\mathrm dx'; the probe compares both. The partially stirred reactor (PaSR, Correa) holds NN particles of equal mass and makes three sub-steps per time step: on average NΔt/τN\Delta t/\tau random particles are replaced by fresh gas, then all mix by IEM, integrated exactly, towards the mean, then every particle reacts by itself at fixed pressure (RODAS4):

dϕdt=−ϕ−ϕτmix⇒ϕ←ϕ+(ϕ−ϕ)e−Δt/τmix,ϕ∈{Yk,h}\frac{\mathrm d\phi}{\mathrm dt} = -\frac{\phi - \bar\phi}{\tau_{mix}} \;\Rightarrow\; \phi \leftarrow \bar\phi + (\phi - \bar\phi)\,e^{-\Delta t/\tau_{mix}}, \qquad \phi \in \{Y_k, h\}

Mixing is in enthalpy and mass fractions, the temperature follows from hh (Newton). Limits: τmix→0\tau_{mix} \to 0 is the PSR, τmix→∞\tau_{mix} \to \infty segregated flow, in which every particle is a batch reactor with an exponential residence-time distribution. The random generator has a fixed seed; the calculation is bit-identically repeatable.

Ignition delay, sensitivity, fluxes

The ignition delay is the time at which T=T0+ΔTT = T_0 + \Delta T – located exactly as an event of the RODAS solver (re-integration in the last step), not on the step grid. The sensitivity comes from central differences with a mechanism that scales forward and reverse rate of reaction jj by 1±h1 \pm h:

Sj=∂ln⁡τ∂ln⁡kj≈ln⁡τ(1+h)−ln⁡τ(1−h)ln⁡(1+h)−ln⁡(1−h)S_j = \frac{\partial\ln\tau}{\partial\ln k_j} \approx \frac{\ln\tau(1 + h) - \ln\tau(1 - h)}{\ln(1 + h) - \ln(1 - h)}

Both runs use tolerance 10−1010^{-10} so that the difference is not lost in the integration error; forward and reverse rate are scaled together, which leaves the equilibrium untouched. The logarithmic denominator makes the formula exact for finite hh for a pure power law.

Reaction fluxes. For each reaction with net progress qjq_j the flow of element ee is distributed proportionally from each reactant aa to each product bb (in the opposite direction for qj<0q_j < 0):

Fab←Fab+|qj|ν'ajne,aν''bjne,bNe,j,Ne,j=∑aν'ajne,a,∑b(Fab−Fba)=−ne,aωaF_{ab} \leftarrow F_{ab} + |q_j|\,\frac{\nu'_{aj}n_{e,a}\,\nu''_{bj}n_{e,b}}{N_{e,j}}, \qquad N_{e,j} = \sum_a \nu'_{aj}n_{e,a}, \qquad \sum_b (F_{ab} - F_{ba}) = -n_{e,a}\,\omega_a

Derivation of the identity: the outflow from aa is ∑j|qj|ν'ajne,a∑bν''bjne,b/Ne,j=∑j|qj|ν'ajne,a\sum_j |q_j|\nu'_{aj}n_{e,a}\sum_b\nu''_{bj}n_{e,b}/N_{e,j} = \sum_j |q_j|\nu'_{aj}n_{e,a}, because every reaction conserves the element (∑bν''bjne,b=Ne,j\sum_b\nu''_{bj}n_{e,b} = N_{e,j}); the inflow likewise with ν''aj\nu''_{aj}. The difference is −ne,a∑jqj(ν''aj−ν'aj)=−ne,aωa-n_{e,a}\sum_j q_j(\nu''_{aj} - \nu'_{aj}) = -n_{e,a}\omega_a. integriert sums the fluxes over a history with the trapezoidal rule.

Reduction: DRG, DRGEP, QSSA

The directed relation graph (DRG, Lu and Law) rates how strongly the formation of aa depends on bb; DRGEP (Pepiot-Desjardins and Pitsch) uses net rates and refers to the larger of production PaP_a and consumption CaC_a:

rabDRG⁡=∑j|νajqjδbj|∑j|νajqj|,rabDRGEP⁡=|∑jνajqjδbj|max⁡(Pa,Ca),Ra=maxpaths⁡∏rr_{ab}^{\mathrm{DRG}} = \frac{\sum_j |\nu_{aj}\,q_j\,\delta_{bj}|}{\sum_j |\nu_{aj}\,q_j|}, \qquad r_{ab}^{\mathrm{DRGEP}} = \frac{\bigl|\sum_j \nu_{aj}\,q_j\,\delta_{bj}\bigr|}{\max(P_a, C_a)}, \qquad R_a = \max_{\text{paths}}\prod r

(δbj=1\delta_{bj} = 1 if bb takes part in reaction jj). DRG keeps what is reachable from the targets over edges r≥εr \ge \varepsilon (breadth-first search); DRGEP finds for each species the largest path product with Dijkstra on −ln⁡r-\ln r and keeps R≥εR \ge \varepsilon. The values are maximised over a sample of states. On GRI-Mech the textbook method removes almost nothing; three additions were necessary: carriers such as N₂ without outgoing edges, samples only from 1 % of the temperature rise and a scaling per state. With them 29 of 53 species remain at 1400 K with a 1.8 % deviation in ignition delay. The skeleton keeps the species list and property data and sets every reaction with a removed species to factor zero.

Quasi-steady state sets ωk=0\omega_k = 0 for the chosen species and solves for their concentrations at every evaluation (Newton, started from the last result); lifetimes help with the choice:

ωk(cQSS,crest)=0(k∈QSS),τk=1|∂ωk/∂ck|\omega_k(c_{\text{QSS}}, c_{\text{rest}}) = 0 \quad (k \in \text{QSS}), \qquad \tau_k = \frac{1}{|\partial\omega_k/\partial c_k|}

The error of the approximation is the mass share of the QSS species missing in the integrator; it must be small. For DRM19 with CH₂, CH₂(S), HCO, CH₃O the ignition delay changes by less than 0.5 %.

Thermal NO and soot

The extended Zeldovich mechanism (O + N₂ ⇌ NO + N, N + O₂ ⇌ NO + O, N + OH ⇌ NO + H), forward rates after Hanson and Salimian, reverse rates from the equilibrium constants of the property data (all without change in mole number, Kc=KpK_c = K_p). With N quasi-steady (r1=r2+r3r_1 = r_2 + r_3) and a=[NO⁡]/[NO⁡]eqa = [\mathrm{NO}]/[\mathrm{NO}]_{eq}:

d[NO⁡]dt=2k1[O][N2](1−a2)1+Ka,K=k1r[NO⁡]eqk2[O2]+k3[OH⁡],[NO⁡]eq=K1K2[N2][O2]\frac{\mathrm d[\mathrm{NO}]}{\mathrm dt} = \frac{2k_1[\mathrm O][\mathrm N_2]\,(1 - a^2)}{1 + Ka}, \qquad K = \frac{k_{1r}[\mathrm{NO}]_{eq}}{k_2[\mathrm O_2] + k_3[\mathrm{OH}]}, \qquad [\mathrm{NO}]_{eq} = \sqrt{K_1K_2[\mathrm N_2][\mathrm O_2]}

The closed form holds exactly if OH and H are absent or if H and OH are in the partial equilibrium [H]/[OH⁡]=(K3/K2)[O]/[O2][\mathrm H]/[\mathrm{OH}] = (K_3/K_2)[\mathrm O]/[\mathrm O_2] (otherwise a remainder proportional to K3[O][OH⁡]−K2[H][O2]K_3[\mathrm O][\mathrm{OH}] - K_2[\mathrm H][\mathrm O_2] is left; the recomputation shows it). At a fixed state the time to the fraction aa follows by separation of variables:

t(a)=[NO⁡]eq2k1[O][N2](artanh⁡a+K2ln⁡11−a2)t(a) = \frac{[\mathrm{NO}]_{eq}}{2k_1[\mathrm O][\mathrm N_2]}\Bigl(\operatorname{artanh} a + \frac K2\ln\frac{1}{1 - a^2}\Bigr)

zeitBis takes no H and is therefore exact if OH is absent or H is in partial equilibrium; with OH and H = 0 the reverse of N + OH ⇌ NO + H is missing and the true formation rate lies above the closed one (at 2200 K, 1 atm, xOH⁡=3⋅10−3x_{\mathrm{OH}} = 3\cdot10^{-3}: 1.3 % at a=0.3a = 0.3, 9.5 % at a=0.9a = 0.9). For changing states and with H, nachlauf computes exactly.

nachlauf integrates NO over a reactor history with implicit Euler in sub-steps (Newton on the scalar), with NO in mol per gram of mixture so that density changes do not distort anything; the combustion is untouched, NO is a trace species.

The two-equation soot model (mass fraction YsY_s and particle number b=N/ρb = N/\rho) after the design of Leung, Lindstedt and Jones assumes spherical particles of equal diameter d=(6ρYs/(πρsN))1/3d = (6\rho Y_s/(\pi\rho_sN))^{1/3} with surface A=πd2NA = \pi d^2N per volume:

R1=k1[C2H2],R2=k2A[C2H2],R3=k3TA[O2]R_1 = k_1[\mathrm{C_2H_2}], \quad R_2 = k_2\sqrt A\,[\mathrm{C_2H_2}], \quad R_3 = k_3\sqrt T A[\mathrm{O_2}]

d(ρYs)dt=MC(2R1+2R2−R3),dNdt=2NAR1Cmin⁡−2Cad6kBTρsN2\frac{\mathrm d(\rho Y_s)}{\mathrm dt} = M_C(2R_1 + 2R_2 - R_3), \quad \frac{\mathrm dN}{\mathrm dt} = \frac{2N_AR_1}{C_{\min}} - 2C_a\sqrt d\sqrt{\frac{6k_BT}{\rho_s}}N^2

(SI, concentrations in kmol/m³). The sub-cases have closed solutions that the probe uses: coagulation only N−5/6=N0−5/6+56KtN^{-5/6} = N_0^{-5/6} + \tfrac56Kt, growth only m2/3m^{2/3} linear in tt, oxidation only m1/3m^{1/3} falling linearly. The constants are taken from secondary sources and not checked against the original.

Free flame and counterflow flame

The free planar premixed flame is steady in the moving frame; unknowns are T(x)T(x), Yk(x)Y_k(x) and the mass flux M=ρuM = \rho u as eigenvalue:

MdYkdx+djkdx=ωkMk,McpdTdx−ddx(λdTdx)+∑kjkcp,kdTdx+∑khkωkMk=0,jk=−ρDkmMkMdXkdx−Yk∑ijiM\frac{dY_k}{dx} + \frac{d j_k}{dx} = \omega_k M_k, \qquad M c_p\frac{dT}{dx} - \frac{d}{dx}\Big(\lambda\frac{dT}{dx}\Big) + \sum_k j_k c_{p,k}\frac{dT}{dx} + \sum_k h_k\omega_k M_k = 0, \qquad j_k = -\rho D_{km}\frac{M_k}{\bar M}\frac{dX_k}{dx} - Y_k\sum_i j_i

Convection upwind, diffusion central on the non-uniform grid; the correction term −Yk∑iji-Y_k\sum_i j_i makes the sum of the diffusion fluxes zero (mass conservation). Inflow with diffusion on the left (MYk,in=MYk+jkMY_{k,\text{in}} = MY_k + j_k), no gradients on the right; a temperature fix point (20 % of the rise) replaces the equation for MM there, elsewhere MM is constant. The diffusion fluxes follow mixture-averaged transport as in Chemkin (Wilke, Warnatz, Mathur, Dkm=(1−Yk)/∑jXj/DkjD_{km} = (1 - Y_k)/\sum_j X_j/D_{kj}). The counterflow flame uses Kee's similarity solution: uu, V=v/rV = v/r, TT, YkY_k and the pressure eigenvalue Λ=(1/r)∂p/∂r\Lambda = (1/r)\,\partial p/\partial r with

d(ρu)dz+2ρV=0,ρudVdz+ρV2=−Λ+ddz(μdVdz),dΛdz=0\frac{\mathrm d(\rho u)}{\mathrm dz} + 2\rho V = 0, \qquad \rho u\frac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dz} + \rho V^2 = -\Lambda + \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dz}\Big(\mu\frac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dz}\Big), \qquad \frac{\mathrm d\Lambda}{\mathrm dz} = 0

and mass fluxes at both nozzles. The solution uses damped Newton (Deuflhard's monotonicity test: a step is accepted if the simplified next Newton step becomes smaller), a Jacobian from differences in three colours (every third grid point perturbed at once, because only neighbours couple), block-tridiagonal Thomas, pseudo-time steps as fallback and grid refinement by slope and curvature.

coarse grid; start from a ramp to the PSR state
repeat:
    freeze transport on the current state
    damped Newton (block Thomas); if it fails: pseudo-time steps, then Newton again
    refine the grid where slope or curvature of a quantity is too large
until no new point is needed

Cost. Per Newton step one Jacobian from 3nv3n_v residuals (nv=ns+2n_v = n_s + 2 unknowns per point) and one block-Thomas solution with O(Nnv3)O(N n_v^3) for NN grid points – linear in the grid size, cubic in the number of species. DRM19 with a few hundred points thus takes minutes; GRI-Mech with 53 species about twenty times as much per point.

Limits

Adiabatic and isobaric; flames one-dimensional, without radiation and without Soret effect. Upwinding on a non-uniform grid conserves element flux only to about 5e-3 (as Cantera with the same method). Soot constants unchecked.

Assumptions and validity

  • Perfectly stirred or perfectly without back-mixing: PSR and PFR are limits; the PaSR lies between, with a mixing time that must come from the flow (IEM knows no length scale).
  • Mixture-averaged transport instead of multicomponent diffusion; for flames with much hydrogen the Soret effect (thermal diffusion of light species) is missing.
  • Reduction holds in the sample range: a skeleton obtained from ignition histories at 1400 K must be checked again for flames or other pressures.
  • Zeldovich NO is only the thermal path; prompt NO and the N₂O path are missing (not negligible in lean, cool flames).

What follows

  • The S-curve gives the smallest residence time of a combustor; the margin against blow-out is the distance of the operating residence time from the extinction limit.
  • Sensitivities show which rates determine an ignition delay; a reduction that keeps these reactions matches the ignition delay.
  • Thermal NO grows with e−38370/Te^{-38370/T}: a few tens of kelvin less peak temperature lower the formation rate noticeably – the lever of lean premixing.
