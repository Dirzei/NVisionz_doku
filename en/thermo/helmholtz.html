Real gases – Helmholtz reference equations, IAPWS-95, GERG-2008

The computation behind Helmholtz, Restanteil, Idealanteil, Reinfluid, Iapws95, Reinfluide and Gerg2008: properties from the reduced Helmholtz energy, the term forms, saturation after Akasaka, density search at (T, p), the mixture equation GERG-2008 with reducing functions and departure function. Pseudocode, examples, checks, limits.

What this is about

For water, CO2, hydrogen, natural gas components and refrigerants there are equations of state of reference quality: they reproduce density, speed of sound, heat capacity and vapour pressure over the whole fluid region within the measurement uncertainty – also in the supercritical region, where cubic equations (Peng-Robinson and SRK) are off by ten percent. All these equations are built the same way: as a reduced Helmholtz energy α=a/(RT)\alpha = a/(RT) over the reduced density δ\delta and the inverse reduced temperature τ\tau. All properties are derivatives of it.

The package therefore has a general core: Restanteil and Idealanteil know all term forms of the reference equations, Helmholtz computes the properties from them. The data: IAPWS-95 for water, 34 further pure substances and air as a pseudo-pure fluid (coefficients as in CoolProp 8.0.0), and GERG-2008 for natural gas mixtures of 21 components. Via HelmholtzZustandsgleichung they sit behind the contract of the flow solvers (equations of state).

Properties from α(δ, τ)

With α=α0+αr\alpha = \alpha^0 + \alpha^r, δ=ρ/ρr\delta = \rho/\rho_r, τ=Tr/T\tau = T_r/T, the specific gas constant R/MR/M and subscripts as partial derivatives (relations as in table 3 of the IAPWS-95 release):

pρRT=1+δαδr,uRT=τ(ατ0+ατr),hRT=τ(ατ0+ατr)+1+δαδr\frac{p}{\rho RT} = 1 + \delta\alpha^r_\delta, \qquad \frac{u}{RT} = \tau(\alpha^0_\tau + \alpha^r_\tau), \qquad \frac{h}{RT} = \tau(\alpha^0_\tau + \alpha^r_\tau) + 1 + \delta\alpha^r_\delta

sR=τ(ατ0+ατr)−α0−αr,cvR=−τ2(αττ0+αττr)\frac{s}{R} = \tau(\alpha^0_\tau + \alpha^r_\tau) - \alpha^0 - \alpha^r, \qquad \frac{c_v}{R} = -\tau^2(\alpha^0_{\tau\tau} + \alpha^r_{\tau\tau})

cpR=cvR+(1+δαδr−δταδτr)21+2δαδr+δ2αδδr,w2MRT=1+2δαδr+δ2αδδr−(1+δαδr−δταδτr)2τ2(αττ0+αττr)\frac{c_p}{R} = \frac{c_v}{R} + \frac{(1 + \delta\alpha^r_\delta - \delta\tau\alpha^r_{\delta\tau})^2}{1 + 2\delta\alpha^r_\delta + \delta^2\alpha^r_{\delta\delta}}, \qquad \frac{w^2 M}{RT} = 1 + 2\delta\alpha^r_\delta + \delta^2\alpha^r_{\delta\delta} - \frac{(1 + \delta\alpha^r_\delta - \delta\tau\alpha^r_{\delta\tau})^2}{\tau^2(\alpha^0_{\tau\tau} + \alpha^r_{\tau\tau})}

Plus g/(RT)=1+α+δαδrg/(RT) = 1 + \alpha + \delta\alpha^r_\delta, (∂p/∂ρ)T=RT(1+2δαδr+δ2αδδr)(\partial p/\partial\rho)_T = RT(1 + 2\delta\alpha^r_\delta + \delta^2\alpha^r_{\delta\delta}), (∂p/∂T)ρ=ρR(1+δαδr−δταδτr)(\partial p/\partial T)_\rho = \rho R(1 + \delta\alpha^r_\delta - \delta\tau\alpha^r_{\delta\tau}). Every part returns its value and the derivatives up to second order (record Ableitungen).

The term forms

Residual part. A general exponential term covers power, exponential, Gaussian and double-exponential terms (as in CoolProp):

nδdτtexp⁡(u),u=−cδl−ωτm−η(δ−ε)2−β(τ−γ)2n\,\delta^d\tau^t\exp(u), \qquad u = -c\,\delta^l - \omega\,\tau^m - \eta(\delta - \varepsilon)^2 - \beta(\tau - \gamma)^2

Power term: all factors zero; exponential term: c=1c = 1, l>0l > 0; Gaussian term: η,ε,β,γ\eta, \varepsilon, \beta, \gamma; double exponential: c,l,ω,mc, l, \omega, m. Then the non-analytic term for the critical region (IAPWS-95 terms 55 and 56, CO2 after Span and Wagner),

nΔbδψ,Δ=θ2+B[(δ−1)2]a,θ=(1−τ)+A[(δ−1)2]1/(2β),ψ=exp⁡[−C(δ−1)2−D(τ−1)2]n\,\Delta^b\delta\,\psi, \qquad \Delta = \theta^2 + B[(\delta - 1)^2]^a, \qquad \theta = (1 - \tau) + A[(\delta - 1)^2]^{1/(2\beta)}, \qquad \psi = \exp[-C(\delta - 1)^2 - D(\tau - 1)^2]

with the derivatives after table 5 of the IAPWS-95 release, and the Gao term (ammonia, Gao et al. 2020): nτtexp⁡[1/(b+β(τ−γ)2)]δdexp⁡[η(δ−ε)2]n\,\tau^t\exp[1/(b + \beta(\tau - \gamma)^2)]\,\delta^d\exp[\eta(\delta - \varepsilon)^2].

Ideal part of a pure substance:

α0=ln⁡δ+a1+a2τ+alog⁡ln⁡τ+∑nτt+∑nln⁡(c+deθτ)+αcp0(τ)\alpha^0 = \ln\delta + a_1 + a_2\tau + a_{\log}\ln\tau + \sum n\,\tau^t + \sum n\ln\bigl(c + d\,e^{\theta\tau}\bigr) + \alpha^0_{c_p}(\tau)

The Planck-Einstein term nln⁡(1−e−γτ)n\ln(1 - e^{-\gamma\tau}) is the case θ=−γ\theta = -\gamma, c=1c = 1, d=−1d = -1; the sinh and cosh terms of Aly and Lee are special cases too. αcp0\alpha^0_{c_p} is the twice-integrated heat capacity cp0/R=∑cTtc_p^0/R = \sum c\,T^t with its own reference temperature; a shift of the reference point of enthalpy and entropy enters a1,a2a_1, a_2.

Saturation after Akasaka

In phase equilibrium pressure and Gibbs energy of both phases are equal. With

J(δ)=δ(1+δαδr),K(δ)=δαδr+αr+ln⁡δJ(\delta) = \delta(1 + \delta\alpha^r_\delta), \qquad K(\delta) = \delta\alpha^r_\delta + \alpha^r + \ln\delta

(at fixed τ\tau) the conditions read J(δ')=J(δ'')J(\delta') = J(\delta'') and K(δ')=K(δ'')K(\delta') = K(\delta'') – the ideal part cancels. Akasaka solves this by Newton in both densities:

Jδ=1+2δαδr+δ2αδδr,Kδ=2αδr+δαδδr+1δJ_\delta = 1 + 2\delta\alpha^r_\delta + \delta^2\alpha^r_{\delta\delta}, \qquad K_\delta = 2\alpha^r_\delta + \delta\alpha^r_{\delta\delta} + \frac1\delta

// saettigungT(T), T_triple ≤ T < T_c·(1 − 10⁻⁶)
δ' ← ρ'_ancillary(T)/ρ_r;  δ'' ← ρ''_ancillary(T)/ρ_r
if not δ' > δ'' > 0: δ' ← 2ρ_c/ρ_r;  δ'' ← 0.5ρ_c/ρ_r       ancillaries out of range
for k ← 1 to 200:
    J', J'', K', K'', J'δ, J''δ, K'δ, K''δ from αr
    det ← J''δK'δ − J'δK''δ
    Δ' ← ((K'' − K')J''δ − (J'' − J')K''δ)/det
    Δ'' ← ((K'' − K')J'δ − (J'' − J')K'δ)/det
    g ← 1;  while δ' + gΔ' ≤ 0 or δ'' + gΔ'' ≤ 0: g ← g/2
    δ' += gΔ';  δ'' += gΔ''
    s ← max(|Δ'|/δ', |Δ''|/δ'')
    if s ≤ 10⁻¹³ or (s < 10⁻⁷ and s > ½·s_previous): stop   rounding noise
if not δ' > δ''(1 + 10⁻⁸): throw "trivial solution"
return (T, p from the vapour, state(δ'), state(δ''))

The pressure is computed from the vapour: on the liquid side the steep slope ∂p/∂ρ\partial p/\partial\rho turns 10⁻¹³ in density into 10⁻⁷ in pressure. The ancillary equations of the saturation line are only start values; the result is the Maxwell criterion of the equation itself. saettigungP(p) starts from the ancillary (Brent in T) and iterates Newton in T with Clausius-Clapeyron, dp/dT=(s''−s')/(v''−v')\mathrm dp/\mathrm dT = (s'' - s')/(v'' - v'), until |ps−p|≤10−13p|p_s - p| \le 10^{-13}p.

State at (T, p). Below TcT_c the saturation pressure decides the phase (exactly on it: liquid). Then the density is searched on the stable branch between bounds within which p(ρ)p(\rho) rises: vapour between a halving bound below the ideal-gas density and the dew density, liquid from the bubble density upwards (×1.1 steps until p(ρ)≥pp(\rho) \ge p). dichteImIntervall is a safeguarded Newton: if a step leaves the interval or shrinks too slowly it bisects; stop at |Δρ|≤10−14ρ|\Delta\rho| \le 10^{-14}\rho.

Pseudo-pure air. The equation describes a mixture of fixed composition; dew and bubble line separate and do not follow from the Maxwell criterion. As in CoolProp the ancillaries for bubble and dew pressure decide the phase (to about 1 %); in between it is rejected.

GERG-2008 for natural gas mixtures

GERG-2008 (Kunz and Wagner 2012, also AGA 8 Part 2) describes mixtures of 21 components: methane to n-decane, N2, CO2, H2, O2, CO, H2O, H2S, He, Ar. The reduced Helmholtz energy of the mixture is

α(δ,τ,x)=∑ixi[αoi0(ρ,T)+ln⁡xi]+∑ixiαoir(δ,τ)+∑i∑j>ixixjFijαijr(δ,τ)\alpha(\delta, \tau, x) = \sum_i x_i\bigl[\alpha^0_{oi}(\rho, T) + \ln x_i\bigr] + \sum_i x_i\,\alpha^r_{oi}(\delta, \tau) + \sum_{i}\sum_{j>i} x_i x_j F_{ij}\,\alpha^r_{ij}(\delta, \tau)

with the pure-fluid equations αoi\alpha_{oi}, binary departure functions αijr\alpha^r_{ij} (ten mixture models) and weights FijF_{ij}. Reduction uses composition-dependent quantities:

1ρr(x)=∑i∑jxixjβv,ijγv,ijxi+xjβv,ij2xi+xj⋅18(ρc,i−1/3+ρc,j−1/3)3\frac{1}{\rho_r(x)} = \sum_i\sum_j x_ix_j\,\beta_{v,ij}\gamma_{v,ij}\frac{x_i + x_j}{\beta_{v,ij}^2 x_i + x_j}\cdot\frac18\left(\rho_{c,i}^{-1/3} + \rho_{c,j}^{-1/3}\right)^3

Tr(x)=∑i∑jxixjβT,ijγT,ijxi+xjβT,ij2xi+xjTc,iTc,jT_r(x) = \sum_i\sum_j x_ix_j\,\beta_{T,ij}\gamma_{T,ij}\frac{x_i + x_j}{\beta_{T,ij}^2 x_i + x_j}\sqrt{T_{c,i}T_{c,j}}

(for i=ji = j the parameters are 1). Gerg2008 is transferred line by line from the NIST reference code (AGA8CODE/C/GERG2008.cpp, version 2.01, git 3bdb9ab8), with one change: without NIST's caches (last composition, last temperature), hence stateless and thread-safe. Units as in the NIST code: T in K, p in kPa, D in mol/l, J/mol.

Density at (T, p) (DensityGERG): Newton in ln⁡v\ln v with ln⁡p\ln p as target, step Δln⁡v=(ln⁡p(D)−ln⁡p)p/(D∂p/∂D)\Delta\ln v = (\ln p(D) - \ln p)\,p/(D\,\partial p/\partial D), stop at |Δln⁡v|<10−7|\Delta\ln v| < 10^{-7}, at most 50 steps. Start: ideal-gas density (vapour) or three times the pseudo-critical density (liquid). If the state is two-phase (∂p/∂D<0\partial p/\partial D < 0) or it takes too long (steps 20, 30, 40), it restarts at 3, 2.5 and 2 times the pseudo-critical density; without success the ideal-gas density is returned with an error flag. The equation has no phase equilibrium; in the two-phase region a metastable state may result.

Convention in the framework: the coefficients of the pure substances and air are generated by script from the CoolProp 8.0.0 fluid files, IAPWS-95 from the text of release R6-95(2018) (number for number equal to CoolProp), GERG-2008 from the NIST code; every equation keeps its own gas constant (it differs in the fifth digit depending on the year of origin). Gerg2008.komponente maps lines of the machieve file gascomp.inp to the 21 components via molar mass and θ4.

How it is checked

test/envisionz/thermo/helmholtz/Helmholtzprobe.java, 244 checks:

  • IAPWS-95: tables 6 to 8 of the release (derivatives at 500 K, 11 single-phase states, saturation at 275, 450, 625 K) to the nine digits of the tables; triple-point pressure and h′ at the triple point.
  • Against CoolProp 8.0.0 (backend HEOS) for all substances: states at (T, ρ) without iteration (according to the module description 10⁻¹²), density at (T, p), saturation without "superancillaries" (2·10⁻¹⁰); air without saturation.
  • GERG-2008: NIST's verification values (GERG2008_test_01.cpp, 10⁻¹⁴) and 33 cases from the translated C++ code itself (all 21 pure fluids, seven mixture models, two cases without convergence; 2·10⁻¹⁵).
  • Derivatives of every term form against central differences; consequences: equal Gibbs energy and pressure in equilibrium, Clausius-Clapeyron, inversions, cp as derivative of h.
  • IF97 against IAPWS-95 in the common range: the density deviation must stay below the uncertainty of IF97 (figure 3 of the release). Plus the EquationOfState contract for pure fluid and GERG mixture; 16 counter-probes red.

Found during construction: CoolProp computes the iterative saturation pressure from the liquid side and is therefore up to 8·10⁻⁹ off (ethanol, 248 K); the comment test run in GERG2008.cpp gives 1130.48 for d²p/dD², code and test files 1129.53 – the comment is outdated.

Limits

  • Saturation only between triple point and Tc(1−10−6)T_c(1 - 10^{-6}); right at the critical point the Jacobian becomes singular and the call is rejected.
  • GERG-2008 without phase equilibrium: no dew and bubble point calculation, possibly a metastable density in the two-phase region; temperature range behind the contract 60–700 K (extended range of the equation).
  • Air as a pseudo-pure fluid: phase boundaries only via ancillaries (about 1 %), no solution between dew and bubble line.
  • Validity limits of each equation (Tmax, pmax per substance) are carried along; outside, the equation extrapolates with growing uncertainty.
  • Run time: (T, p) and (ρ, e) are iterations over many terms; in the flow solver the start value from the thread's last result helps.
