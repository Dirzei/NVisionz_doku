Boundary layer, gas dynamics, airfoils and rotors – background

The computation behind Grenzschicht, Gasdynamik, Panel, Wirbelgitter and Blattelement: integral methods after Thwaites and Head, shock relations and Prandtl-Meyer, the minimum-length nozzle, the Hess-Smith panel method, horseshoe vortices and the Trefftz plane, blade element momentum theory with losses.

What it is about

Before computing a flow on a mesh, classical methods answer many design questions in seconds: where does the boundary layer separate? How strong is a shock? What lift does an airfoil have, what induced drag a wing, what power a rotor? The classes compute without a mesh on lines, panels and annular elements.

Boundary layer after Thwaites and Head

Laminar (Thwaites): for a given outer velocity U(x)U(x)

θ2U6=θ02U06+0.45ν∫U5dx,λ=θ2U'ν,cf=2νl(λ)Uθ;\theta^2 U^6 = \theta_0^2 U_0^6 + 0.45\,\nu\int U^5\,dx, \qquad \lambda = \frac{\theta^2 U'}{\nu}, \qquad c_f = \frac{2\nu\,l(\lambda)}{U\theta};

Where the integral comes from. Kármán's momentum equation dθ/dx+(2+H)θU'/U=cf/2d\theta/dx + (2 + H)\,\theta U'/U = c_f/2 is multiplied by θU/ν\theta U/\nu; with λ=θ2U'/ν\lambda = \theta^2U'/\nu and cf=2νl/(Uθ)c_f = 2\nu l/(U\theta) it becomes

Uddx(θ2ν)=2[l(λ)−(2+H(λ))λ]=F(λ)≈0.45−6λ.U\,\frac{d}{dx}\Big(\frac{\theta^2}{\nu}\Big) = 2\big[l(\lambda) - (2 + H(\lambda))\lambda\big] = F(\lambda) \approx 0.45 - 6\lambda .

Thwaites' observation is that FF is nearly linear in λ\lambda for all known exact solutions. With the linear form the equation for θ2U6\theta^2U^6 can be integrated exactly – that is the formula above. H(λ)H(\lambda) and l(λ)l(\lambda) after Cebeci and Bradshaw, recomputed into FF, agree to 0.03; at separation λ=−0.09\lambda = -0.09 ll, i.e. wall shear, vanishes.

The separation point is additionally found exactly by bisection on λ(x)+0.09=0\lambda(x) + 0.09 = 0.

Turbulent (Head): entrainment d(UθH1)/dx=0.0306U(H1−3)−0.6169d(U\theta H_1)/dx = 0.0306\,U(H_1 - 3)^{-0.6169} with H1(H)H_1(H), the momentum equation and skin friction after Ludwieg and Tillmann cf=0.246⋅10−0.678HReθ−0.268c_f = 0.246\cdot 10^{-0.678H}Re_\theta^{-0.268}; separation at H=2.4H = 2.4.

Head closes the momentum equation with a second equation for the entrainment of outer fluid: the volume flow in the boundary layer U(δ−δ1)=UθH1U(\delta - \delta_1) = U\theta H_1 grows at a rate depending only on H1H_1. θ\theta and UθH1U\theta H_1 are integrated with Dormand-Prince; HH follows from inverting H1(H)H_1(H). Because H1H_1 must not drop below 3.3 and the equations become stiff before separation, the integration proceeds in substeps and stops at H=2.4H = 2.4.

Shocks, Prandtl-Meyer, nozzle

The normal shock gives pressure, density and temperature ratios from M1M_1; the oblique one follows from the θ-β-M relation

tan⁡θ=2cot⁡βM12sin2⁡β−1M12(γ+cos⁡2β)+2,\tan\theta = 2\cot\beta\,\frac{M_1^2\sin^2\beta - 1}{M_1^2(\gamma + \cos 2\beta) + 2},

with weak and strong branch and the detachment limit θmax\theta_{max}.

Derivation. Mass, momentum and energy across a normal shock (Rankine-Hugoniot) give

p2p1=1+2γγ+1(M12−1),ρ2ρ1=(γ+1)M12(γ−1)M12+2,M22=1+γ−12M12γM12−γ−12,\frac{p_2}{p_1} = 1 + \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}(M_1^2 - 1), \qquad \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{(\gamma+1)M_1^2}{(\gamma-1)M_1^2 + 2}, \qquad M_2^2 = \frac{1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_1^2}{\gamma M_1^2 - \frac{\gamma-1}{2}},

and from s2−s1=cvln⁡[(p2/p1)(ρ1/ρ2)γ]s_2 - s_1 = c_v\ln\big[(p_2/p_1)(\rho_1/\rho_2)^\gamma\big] the total pressure loss p0,2/p0,1=(ρ2/ρ1)γ/(γ−1)(p2/p1)−1/(γ−1)p_{0,2}/p_{0,1} = (\rho_2/\rho_1)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}(p_2/p_1)^{-1/(\gamma-1)}. Equivalent is Prandtl's relation M1*M2*=1M_1^*M_2^* = 1 for Mach numbers based on the critical speed of sound. Across an oblique shock the tangential velocity is conserved; it is therefore a normal shock of the normal component M1sin⁡βM_1\sin\beta, and M2=Mn,2/sin⁡(β−θ)M_2 = M_{n,2}/\sin(\beta - \theta). The θ-β-M relation follows from the density ratio via tan⁡β/tan⁡(β−θ)=ρ2/ρ1\tan\beta/\tan(\beta - \theta) = \rho_2/\rho_1; θ\theta vanishes at the Mach wave β=arcsin⁡(1/M1)\beta = \arcsin(1/M_1) and at the normal shock and has a maximum in between, found by golden section; the weak branch lies left of it, the strong one right.

The Prandtl-Meyer function ν(M)=γ+1γ−1arctan⁡γ−1γ+1(M2−1)−arctan⁡M2−1\nu(M) = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}}\arctan\sqrt{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}(M^2-1)} - \arctan\sqrt{M^2-1} is inverted with Newton. It follows from the compatibility condition of an expansion wave, dθ=M2−1dV/Vd\theta = \sqrt{M^2-1}\,dV/V, with dV/V=dM/(M(1+γ−12M2))dV/V = dM/\big(M(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2)\big); exactly this derivative dν/dMd\nu/dM is used by the Newton step, and the start value 1+2ν1/31 + 2\nu^{1/3} follows from ν∼(M−1)3/2\nu \sim (M-1)^{3/2} near M=1M = 1.

The minimum-length nozzle results from the method of characteristics with a sharp corner at the throat and nn characteristics; its area ratio approaches the isentropic

AA*=1M[2γ+1(1+γ−12M2)]γ+12(γ−1)\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac1M\Big[\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\Big(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2\Big)\Big]^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}}

as nn grows, which has its minimum 1 at the throat (M=1M = 1). The corner at the throat produces a fan of nn expansion waves with total angle ν(Me)/2\nu(M_e)/2; each is reflected at the axis and cancelled at the wall, where the contour turns the flow parallel to the axis.

Panel method and vortex lattice

Hess and Smith: per panel a source of constant strength, on all panels a vortex of equal strength; no normal velocity at the panel midpoints and the Kutta condition (equal tangential velocity on the two trailing-edge panels) give a linear system. For thin wings the vortex lattice places a horseshoe vortex per panel (bound at 1/4, control point at 3/4 of the chord); lift follows from Kutta-Joukowski, induced drag from the Trefftz plane. At large aspect ratio the lift slope tends to 2π2\pi, the elliptic wing has Oswald factor 1.

Why it works. Sources and vortices solve the Laplace equation; every superposition does as well, and the boundary condition v→⋅n→=0\vec v\cdot\vec n = 0 at the panel midpoints fixes the source strengths. The circulation remains free – only the Kutta condition (smooth outflow at the trailing edge) makes it unique. Lift then follows from Kutta-Joukowski, cl=2Γ/(U∞c)c_l = 2\Gamma/(U_\infty c), with Γ=γ⋅perimeter\Gamma = \gamma\cdot\text{perimeter}; integrated over the pressure distribution the same value results up to the discretisation error (below 0.5 % at 400 panels).

In the vortex lattice each piece of a horseshoe gives its velocity by Biot-Savart. That the bound vortex lies at 1/4 and the control point at 3/4 of the chord (Pistolesi) is no accident: for the flat plate exactly this placement with a single element gives the exact slope 2π2\pi. Induced drag follows in the Trefftz plane far behind the wing from the circulation of the trailing vortices, cd,i=cl2/(πeΛ)c_{d,i} = c_l^2/(\pi e\Lambda).

Blade element momentum theory

Per annular element of a rotor axial and tangential momentum are equated with the blade forces (Glauert), with Prandtl's tip and hub loss and Buhl's correction for high induction (a>0.4a > 0.4). It is not solved with the usual fixed-point iteration in aa, a'a' – it jumped onto a spurious branch at the inner annuli – but after Ning in one equation for the inflow angle ϕ\phi, which has a root in a bracket for sure. Without swirl and losses the Betz optimum cP=16/27c_P = 16/27 at a=1/3a = 1/3 results.

Derivation. Axial momentum through an annulus gives dT=4πrρV2a(1−a)FdrdT = 4\pi r\rho V^2 a(1-a)F\,dr, angular momentum dQ=4πr3ρVΩa'(1−a)FdrdQ = 4\pi r^3\rho V\Omega\,a'(1-a)F\,dr; the blade forces with solidity σ=Bc/(2πr)\sigma = Bc/(2\pi r) and coefficients cnc_n, ctc_t normal and parallel to the rotor plane give

a1−a=σcn4Fsin2⁡ϕ,a'1+a'=σct4Fsin⁡ϕcos⁡ϕ,tan⁡ϕ=1−aλr(1+a'),\frac{a}{1-a} = \frac{\sigma c_n}{4F\sin^2\phi}, \qquad \frac{a'}{1+a'} = \frac{\sigma c_t}{4F\sin\phi\cos\phi}, \qquad \tan\phi = \frac{1-a}{\lambda_r(1+a')},

and Ning substitutes the first two into the third: sin⁡ϕ/(1−a)−cos⁡ϕ(1−k')/λr=0\sin\phi/(1-a) - \cos\phi(1-k')/\lambda_r = 0 is an equation in ϕ\phi alone that changes sign on (0,π/2](0, \pi/2]. Above a=0.4a = 0.4 momentum theory no longer holds (turbulent wake state); Buhl's parabola CT=8/9+(4F−40/9)a+(50/9−4F)a2C_T = 8/9 + (4F - 40/9)a + (50/9 - 4F)a^2 joins there continuously with continuous slope. Prandtl's tip loss F=2πarccos⁡e−B(R−r)/(2rsin⁡ϕ)F = \frac2\pi\arccos e^{-B(R-r)/(2r\sin\phi)} replaces the finite blade number by a reduction near the edge.

For cP=4a(1−a)2c_P = 4a(1-a)^2 (without swirl and losses) the maximum lies at dcP/da=0dc_P/da = 0, i.e. a=1/3a = 1/3 and cP=16/27c_P = 16/27; with swirl every finite tip speed ratio retains less (Glauert).

How it is checked

  • Boundary layer: Thwaites in closed form and against Falkner-Skan (flat plate 1 %, stagnation point −6 %); Howarth separation 0.123 L (exact 0.120); Head against Schultz-Grunow to 3 %.
  • Gas dynamics: normal shock in closed form; M=2M = 2, θ=10°\theta = 10°: β=39.31°\beta = 39.31°, M2=1.64M_2 = 1.64, p2/p1=1.707p_2/p_1 = 1.707; θmax=22.97°\theta_{max} = 22.97°; ν(2)=26.38°\nu(2) = 26.38°, ν(3)=49.76°\nu(3) = 49.76°; nozzle with 80 characteristics to 0.07 %.
  • Panel: cylinder to 1e-14, Kármán-Trefftz airfoil against the conformal map (400 panels 0.12 %); vortex lattice with aspect ratio 1000 to 0.4 % of 2π2\pi, elliptic circulation.
  • Blade element: Betz exact, Glauert optimum, losses, Buhl continuous.
  • In addition: Thwaites coefficients against 0.45−6λ0.45 - 6\lambda; Prandtl's relation M1*M2*=1M_1^*M_2^* = 1 to 1e-14; oblique equal to the normal shock of the normal component; dν/dMd\nu/dM against differences; lift from pressure against Kutta-Joukowski.

Limits

Two-dimensional or quasi-two-dimensional, incompressible for panel and vortex lattice, no transition in the boundary layer (laminar or turbulent prescribed), airfoil polars for the blade element from the user.
