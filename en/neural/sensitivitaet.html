Sensitivity analysis – Sobol indices, permutation importance, model-free estimation

The computation behind SobolAnalyzer, PermutationImportance and DataSensitivity: how the spread of an output is distributed over the inputs, how the shares are estimated after Saltelli and Jansen and why the estimators must be centred, how permutation and binning arrive at similar statements without a model – with pseudocode following the source, examples with known solutions, checks and limits.

What this is about

A surrogate for the efficiency has four inputs. Which one determines the result, which one hardly matters, and do two act only together? Local derivatives answer this at a single point only. Variance-based sensitivity analysis asks globally: if all inputs scatter over their range, which share of the output's spread goes back to which input?

The module offers three tools that check each other. The Sobol analysis calls a function – usually the surrogate model – at points it chooses itself and decomposes the variance completely, interactions included. The permutation importance works on the real data with the model and measures how much worse it gets when one input is scrambled. The model-free estimation needs no model at all and reads the main effects directly from the data.

Decomposing the variance

For a function Y=f(X1,…,Xd)Y = f(X_1, \dots, X_d) with independent inputs, the variance can be decomposed uniquely into shares of single inputs, of pairs, triples and so on:

V(Y)=∑iVi+∑i<jVij+…+V12…d,Vi=V(E[Y∣Xi]).V(Y) = \sum_i V_i + \sum_{i<j} V_{ij} + \dots + V_{12\dots d}, \qquad V_i = V\bigl(\mathrm{E}[Y \mid X_i]\bigr).

ViV_i is the variance of what one knows about YY on average when only XiX_i is known. The indices follow:

Si=ViV(Y),STi=E[V(Y∣X∼i)]V(Y)=1−V(E[Y∣X∼i])V(Y),Sij=VijV(Y).S_i = \frac{V_i}{V(Y)}, \qquad S_{Ti} = \frac{\mathrm{E}\bigl[V(Y \mid X_{\sim i})\bigr]}{V(Y)} = 1 - \frac{V\bigl(\mathrm{E}[Y \mid X_{\sim i}]\bigr)}{V(Y)}, \qquad S_{ij} = \frac{V_{ij}}{V(Y)} .

SiS_i, the first-order index (main effect), is the share XiX_i explains on its own. STiS_{Ti}, the total index, is the share that would remain if all inputs except XiX_i were held fixed – the main effect plus all interactions in which XiX_i takes part. Si≤STiS_i \le S_{Ti} holds; 1−∑iSi1 - \sum_i S_i is the total interaction share. An input with STi≈0S_{Ti} \approx 0 can be held fixed without the output changing noticeably.

The estimators after Saltelli and Jansen

Two independent sample matrices AA and BB with nn rows each are drawn, uniformly distributed over [loi,hii][lo_i, hi_i]. In addition, per input ii, a mixed matrix AB(i)A_B^{(i)}: all columns from AA, only column ii from BB.

With f0f_0 and VV, mean and variance of all 2n2n values of f(A)f(A) and f(B)f(B), the code computes

V^i=1n∑j=1n(f(B)j−f0)(f(AB(i))j−f(A)j)(Saltelli 2010),\hat V_i = \frac1n \sum_{j=1}^{n} \bigl(f(B)_j - f_0\bigr)\bigl(f(A_B^{(i)})_j - f(A)_j\bigr) \quad \text{(Saltelli 2010)},

V^Ti=12n∑j=1n(f(A)j−f(AB(i))j)2(Jansen 1999),S^i=V^i/V,S^Ti=V^Ti/V.\hat V_{Ti} = \frac{1}{2n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} \bigl(f(A)_j - f(A_B^{(i)})_j\bigr)^2 \quad \text{(Jansen 1999)}, \qquad \hat S_i = \hat V_i / V, \quad \hat S_{Ti} = \hat V_{Ti} / V .

Why this works: f(AB(i))f(A_B^{(i)}) and f(B)f(B) share XiX_i and nothing else; their product therefore measures the covariance through XiX_i, which is ViV_i. f(A)f(A) and f(AB(i))f(A_B^{(i)}) differ only in XiX_i; half the mean squared difference is E[V(Y∣X∼i)]\mathrm{E}[V(Y \mid X_{\sim i})].

Second order additionally needs BA(i)B_A^{(i)} (all columns from BB, column ii from AA). f(BA(i))f(B_A^{(i)}) and f(AB(j))f(A_B^{(j)}) share exactly XiX_i (from AA) and XjX_j (from BB). That yields the "closed" share of both together, from which the two main effects are subtracted (Saltelli 2002):

V^ijc=1n∑k(f(BA(i))k−f0)(f(AB(j))k−f0),S^ij=V^ijcV−S^i−S^j.\hat V^c_{ij} = \frac1n \sum_{k} \bigl(f(B_A^{(i)})_k - f_0\bigr)\bigl(f(A_B^{(j)})_k - f_0\bigr), \qquad \hat S_{ij} = \frac{\hat V^c_{ij}}{V} - \hat S_i - \hat S_j .

The cost is (d+2)n(d+2)\,n function calls without and (2d+2)n(2d+2)\,n with second order. The estimation error falls with 1/n1/\sqrt n.

Why centring is necessary

The estimator for ViV_i is unbiased even without subtracting f0f_0, since E[f(AB(i))−f(A)]=0\mathrm{E}[f(A_B^{(i)}) - f(A)] = 0. Its variance, however, depends on it: without centring, every summand is weighted with f(B)jf(B)_j instead of f(B)j−f0f(B)_j - f_0, and the Monte Carlo noise grows with the ratio |f0|/σ|f_0|/\sigma. For the efficiency of a compressor the mean is around 0.87 at a spread of 0.02 – a ratio above 40. According to the source comment, the uncentred estimator gave S1≈−0.55S_1 \approx -0.55 there instead of +0.03+0.03. Centred, the noise depends on σ\sigma only. Jansen's estimator for STS_T works with differences and was never affected.

Framework convention: S1S_1 and SijS_{ij} are estimated centred, with f0f_0 from A∪BA \cup B. Small negative values are estimation noise and are not clipped. The sample is uniformly random over the given ranges, not quasi-random. The inputs are assumed independent.

Permutation importance

The Sobol analysis evaluates the model on a box, also where there never were data. Permutation importance stays with the data: one input column is shuffled row-wise – its distribution is preserved, its relation to the target destroyed – and the increase of the error is measured:

Ioi=MSEo(column i shuffled)−MSEo(baseline)V(to).I_{oi} = \frac{\text{MSE}_o(\text{column } i \text{ shuffled}) - \text{MSE}_o(\text{baseline})}{V(t_o)} .

Dividing by the target variance puts II on the same scale as 1−R21 - R^2 and the Sobol indices. For classification accuracyDrop measures the drop in accuracy. Where Sobol indices and permutation importance agree, the statement is robust; permutation, however, does not separate main effect and interaction.

baseline ← MSE per output of f on the data
V ← variance of the targets per output           divisor n
for each column i:
    X' ← data, column i Fisher-Yates shuffled with Random(seed + i)
    I[o][i] ← (MSE_o(f on X') − baseline_o) / V_o   NaN for a constant target

Model-free from the data: binned main effects

DataSensitivity.binnedFirstOrder estimates Si=V(E[Y∣Xi])/V(Y)S_i = V(\mathrm{E}[Y \mid X_i])/V(Y) without a model. The data are sorted by XiX_i and split into MM equally populated bins; the mean of the target per bin estimates E[Y∣Xi]\mathrm{E}[Y \mid X_i] in that range, and the spread of the bin means estimates ViV_i. With the sums of squares between and within the bins

SSB=∑bnb(yb−y)2,SSW=∑b∑k∈b(yk−yb)2,SST=∑k(yk−y)2\text{SSB} = \sum_b n_b (\bar y_b - \bar y)^2, \qquad \text{SSW} = \sum_b \sum_{k \in b} (y_k - \bar y_b)^2, \qquad \text{SST} = \sum_k (y_k - \bar y)^2

SSB/SST would be too large: pure noise also produces differing bin means. From the analysis of variance, E[SSB]=nVbetween+(M−1)σwithin2\mathrm{E}[\text{SSB}] = n\,V_\text{between} + (M-1)\,\sigma^2_\text{within}; with σwithin2≈SSW/(n−M)\sigma^2_\text{within} \approx \text{SSW}/(n-M) the corrected estimator follows:

S^i=SSB−(M−1)SSW/(n−M)SST.\hat S_i = \frac{\text{SSB} - (M-1)\,\text{SSW}/(n-M)}{\text{SST}} .

It captures arbitrary, also non-monotonic main effects. spearman adds the rank correlation (Pearson correlation of the ranks, average ranks for ties) as a hint at direction and monotonicity – blind to a parabola around an optimum, which yields ρ≈0\rho \approx 0 despite a large influence. That is why both measures belong together.

Framework convention: At least 16 rows. The automatic number of bins is round⁡(n)\operatorname{round}(\sqrt n), clamped to [4,50][4, 50] and at most max⁡(2,⌊n/4⌋)\max(2, \lfloor n/4 \rfloor) – 10 bins for 100 rows, 6 for 40 rows, 50 for 4000 rows. A number of bins given explicitly is capped at n/2n/2. The indices apply to the distribution in which the data are given, including their correlations.

How it is checked

  • Sobol against Ishigami (TestMain [11]): a=7a = 7, b=0.1b = 0.1, n=16,384n = 16{,}384, seed 7, with second order. S1S_1 and STS_T of all three inputs within 0.05 of the analytical values, S13S_{13} and S12S_{12} within 0.08.
  • Centring: the same function shifted by +100 (mean far above the spread) must yield the same indices within the same tolerances. The run gave exactly the same rounded values.
  • Permutation: Ishigami with a fourth, ineffective input, 800 rows: the importance of the dead input must be below 10−1210^{-12}, x2x_2 must rank above x3x_3, I1>0.1I_1 > 0.1, I2>0.3I_2 > 0.3. Measured: 1.080, 0.879, 0.493, 0.0000.
  • Binned and Spearman (TestMain [12]): 4000 Ishigami rows with a dead fourth input; the binned indices within 0.06 of the exact ones, the dead input within 0.06 of zero, ρ1>0.3\rho_1 > 0.3 and |ρ2|<0.1|\rho_2| < 0.1.

Limits

  • Sobol presupposes independent, uniformly distributed inputs. If the inputs are in reality coupled – say speed and mass flow along an operating line –, the analysis evaluates the model in regions where it has never seen data, and the statement applies to the model, not to the machine.
  • The statements apply to the range given. Too wide a range queries the surrogate outside its training data.
  • Monte Carlo error ∼1/n\sim 1/\sqrt n; in the linear example S^2\hat S_2 deviates by 0.013 from the exact value at n=8192n = 8192 – indices of that order cannot be told apart from noise.
  • With coupled inputs permutation also creates unrealistic combinations, and it does not separate interactions.
  • The model-free estimate captures main effects only, measurement noise enters fully, and it applies only to the distribution of the data at hand.
