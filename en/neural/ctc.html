CTC – learning sequences without alignment

The computation behind Ctc and SequenzTrainer: how Connectionist Temporal Classification sums over all alignments of a sound sequence, why that works in O(T⋅S)O(T \cdot S) with forward and backward variables, where the simple gradient with respect to the logits comes from and how the existing network learns sequences with it – with pseudocode following the source, an example worked by hand, the checks and the limits. Decoding with beam search and the error rate are on the page Beam search.

What this is about

An acoustic model sees a hundred time windows per second and is to deliver a word of five sounds. It could be trained like an ordinary classifier only if one knew which window belongs to which sound. Nobody knows this assignment – the alignment; entering it by hand costs many times the recording.

Connectionist Temporal Classification turns the question around: instead of assuming one alignment, it sums the probability over all alignments that produce the correct output. What is trained is the probability of the sound sequence, regardless of how it is spread over the windows. The network learns the alignment by itself on the way.

Paths, blank, collapsing

For each time window t=1,…,Tt = 1, \dots, T the network delivers logits over KK characters; index 0 is the blank, written ε\varepsilon in formulas and "_" in examples. After the softmax, yt(k)y_t(k) is the probability of character kk in window tt. Choosing one character per window gives a path π\pi of length TT. The path becomes the output in two steps: first merge equal neighbours into one, then drop the blanks.

a a _ a b b   →   a _ a b   →   a a b
_ a a b b _   →   _ a b _   →   a b

The blank is necessary: without it "a a" could never be output, since two neighbouring a's would always merge. The order matters – drop blanks first, then merge, and "a _ a" would give a single a. Calling the mapping B\mathcal B,

P(l∣x)=∑π:B(π)=l∏t=1Tyt(πt),L=−ln⁡P(l∣x).P(l \mid x) = \sum_{\pi\,:\,\mathcal B(\pi) = l}\ \prod_{t=1}^{T} y_t(\pi_t), \qquad L = -\ln P(l \mid x).

The product presupposes that the outputs of the windows are mutually independent given the input. With K=20K = 20 and T=100T = 100 there are 2010020^{100} paths; they cannot be enumerated, but they meet in few states.

The extended sequence and the lattice

The target sequence ll of length LL is extended: a blank goes before, between and after every character, l'=(ε,l1,ε,l2,…,lL,ε)l' = (\varepsilon, l_1, \varepsilon, l_2, \dots, l_L, \varepsilon) with S=2L+1S = 2L + 1 positions. Every admissible path traverses l'l' from left to right and may, per window,

  • stay on its position (the same character once more),
  • move one position on,
  • jump two positions – only if a blank is skipped and the characters before and after it are different: l's≠εl'_s \ne \varepsilon and l's≠l's−2l'_s \ne l'_{s-2}.

The last condition is the difference between a correct and an almost correct implementation: were the two characters equal, they would merge when collapsing, and the path would yield a different label. A path starts at position 0 or 1 and ends at S−1S-1 or S−2S-2.

How many windows does a label need at least? One per character plus one between any two equal neighbours: T≥L+#{i:li=li−1}T \ge L + \#\{i : l_i = l_{i-1}\}. "a a b" needs four windows, "a b" two. Ctc.darstellbar checks exactly this.

Forward and backward variables

The forward variable αt(s)\alpha_t(s) is the sum of the probabilities of all path beginnings up to window tt that end at position ss:

α1(0)=y1(ε),α1(1)=y1(l1),αt(s)=(αt−1(s)+αt−1(s−1)+[jump allowed]αt−1(s−2))yt(l's).\alpha_1(0) = y_1(\varepsilon), \qquad \alpha_1(1) = y_1(l_1), \qquad \alpha_t(s) = \Bigl(\alpha_{t-1}(s) + \alpha_{t-1}(s-1) + [\text{jump allowed}]\,\alpha_{t-1}(s-2)\Bigr)\, y_t(l'_s).

Mirror-inverted, the backward variable βt(s)\beta_t(s) is the sum over all path endings from window tt that start at position ss – in this implementation including the emission yt(l's)y_t(l'_s):

βT(S−1)=yT(ε),βT(S−2)=yT(lL),βt(s)=(βt+1(s)+βt+1(s+1)+[jump to s+2 allowed]βt+1(s+2))yt(l's).\beta_T(S-1) = y_T(\varepsilon), \qquad \beta_T(S-2) = y_T(l_L), \qquad \beta_t(s) = \Bigl(\beta_{t+1}(s) + \beta_{t+1}(s+1) + [\text{jump to } s+2 \text{ allowed}]\,\beta_{t+1}(s+2)\Bigr)\, y_t(l'_s).

The total probability is P(l∣x)=αT(S−1)+αT(S−2)P(l \mid x) = \alpha_T(S-1) + \alpha_T(S-2). The cost is O(T⋅S)O(T \cdot S) instead of O(KT)O(K^T).

In the logarithm. α\alpha is a product of up to TT probabilities. With T=100T = 100 and values around 0.1 that would be 10−10010^{-100} – still representable –, with T=400T = 400 no longer (the smallest positive double is about 4.9⋅10−3244.9 \cdot 10^{-324}). Everything is therefore computed in ln⁡α\ln\alpha, ln⁡β\ln\beta; products become sums, sums the stable form

ln⁡(ea+eb)=max⁡(a,b)+ln⁡(1+e−|a−b|),\ln(e^a + e^b) = \max(a, b) + \ln\bigl(1 + e^{-|a-b|}\bigr),

with ln⁡0=−∞\ln 0 = -\infty as the neutral element, passed through without NaN.

The gradient with respect to the logits

αt(s)βt(s)\alpha_t(s)\,\beta_t(s) is the sum over all admissible paths that are at position ss in window tt – with the emission yt(l's)y_t(l'_s) counted twice, once from α\alpha and once from β\beta. Dividing by it once and summing over all positions carrying character kk gives the probability that the path emits character kk in window tt, given the correct label:

γt(k)=1P(l∣x)∑s:l's=kαt(s)βt(s)yt(k).\gamma_t(k) = \frac{1}{P(l \mid x)} \sum_{s\,:\,l'_s = k} \frac{\alpha_t(s)\,\beta_t(s)}{y_t(k)} .

Differentiating L=−ln⁡PL = -\ln P through the softmax with respect to the logits zt(k)z_t(k) leaves

∂L∂zt(k)=yt(k)−γt(k).\frac{\partial L}{\partial z_t(k)} = y_t(k) - \gamma_t(k).

This has the same form as for softmax cross-entropy (p−yp - y), except that the target is not a fixed class but the distribution γt\gamma_t, which the network computes itself from the sum over all alignments. Because ∑kyt(k)=∑kγt(k)=1\sum_k y_t(k) = \sum_k \gamma_t(k) = 1, every row of the gradient sums to zero – one of the few properties that expose a sign or index error at once.

Pseudocode – Ctc.rechne(logits, label)

check T ≥ 1, K ≥ 2, all rows equally wide, label without blank, 0 < character < K
lnY ← row-wise log-softmax of the logits              subtracting the largest
g ← extend(label)                                     S = 2L + 1
if not representable(label, T):
    return (loss +∞, gradient 0)                          no path leads to the target
lnα ← forward(lnY, g);   lnβ ← backward(lnY, g)
lnP ← logsumexp(lnα[T][S−1], lnα[T][S−2])
for t ← 1 to T:
    lnγ[k] ← −∞ for all k
    for s ← 0 to S−1:
        lnγ[g[s]] ← logsumexp(lnγ[g[s]], lnα[t][s] + lnβ[t][s] − lnY[t][g[s]])
    for k:  grad[t][k] ← exp(lnY[t][k]) − exp(lnγ[k] − lnP)
return (−lnP, grad)

function forward(lnY, g):
    lnα ← −∞ everywhere;  lnα[1][0] ← lnY[1][g[0]];  lnα[1][1] ← lnY[1][g[1]]
    for t ← 2 to T,  s ← 0 to S−1:
        sum ← lnα[t−1][s]
        if s ≥ 1:  sum ← logsumexp(sum, lnα[t−1][s−1])
        if s ≥ 2 and g[s] ≠ blank and g[s] ≠ g[s−2]:  sum ← logsumexp(sum, lnα[t−1][s−2])
        lnα[t][s] ← sum + lnY[t][g[s]]

Source: envisionz-framework-neural/src/de/envisionz/neural/seq/Ctc.java. backward is the mirror image of forward. verlust computes the forward variables only; gierig and falte are the simple decoding and the collapsing.

Framework convention: The blank is always index 0 (Ctc.LEER), so nothing needs to be agreed. The loss is −ln⁡P-\ln P for the whole utterance, not per window; the logits are raw, Ctc computes the softmax itself. If a label cannot be represented with the given number of windows, the loss is +∞+\infty and the gradient zero – not NaN.

Sequence training with the existing network

Sequences seem to call for a network with memory. CTC, however, presupposes precisely that the outputs per window are independent. A model following this assumption needs no memory but context: an input in which the neighbouring windows are already contained. That is exactly what the context window of the feature chain in framework-audio provides. Every time window is then an ordinary sample, and NeuralNet fits unchanged.

The second building block was also already there: the layers accumulate their gradients (see The network). For an utterance this means: one backward pass per time window with the corresponding row of the CTC gradient, and at the end the gradient of the whole utterance sits in the layers.

Pseudocode – SequenzTrainer.trainiere(net, training, valid, opt, settings)

check training not empty, input width = feature width, ≥ 2 outputs, output layer LINEAR
measure ← valid or, if none, training
opt.init(net);  rnd ← Random(seed);  best ← net.snapshot();  bestRate ← +∞
for epoch ← 1 to epochs:
    shuffle the order of the utterances with rnd
    net.zeroGrads();  inBatch ← 0;  windows ← 0
    for each utterance b:
        n ← oneStep(net, b)                              0 if not representable: skip
        if n = 0:  continue
        windows ← windows + n;  inBatch ← inBatch + 1
        if inBatch ≥ batch:
            net.scaleGrads(1 / windows);  opt.step(net, 1);  net.zeroGrads()
            inBatch ← 0;  windows ← 0
    if inBatch > 0:  net.scaleGrads(1 / windows);  opt.step(net, 1);  net.zeroGrads()
    rate ← error rate on measure (beam width from the settings, 0 = greedy)
    loss ← mean CTC loss per time window on measure
    if rate < bestRate − 10⁻⁹:  remember rate, loss, epoch;  best ← net.snapshot()
    if patience > 0 and epoch − bestEpoch ≥ patience:
        net.restore(best);  return (epoch, loss, rate, stopped early)
net.restore(best);  return (epochs, loss, rate, not stopped)

function oneStep(net, b):
    logits ← net.run(b.input[t])  for all t
    e ← Ctc.rechne(logits, b.label);   if not e.possible:  return 0
    for t ← 1 to T:
        net.forward(b.input[t])                           once more: the network holds one sample only
        net.backward(e.gradient[t])                       accumulates
    return T

Source: envisionz-framework-neural/src/de/envisionz/neural/seq/SequenzTrainer.java. The gradient is divided by the number of windows in the batch, not by the number of utterances – long utterances weigh correspondingly more.

The forward pass is computed twice – once for the logits, once per window immediately before the backward step, because a layer only holds the activations of the sample seen last. That costs roughly a third more computing time and saves keeping the activations of the whole sequence.

Framework convention: The best network is selected by error rate, not by loss. A CTC model becomes sharper during training and puts its probability on fewer and fewer alignments; the loss, which sums over all of them, can rise while the decoded output improves. Recorded in the source comment: the validation loss rose from 0.058 to 0.133 while the error rate kept falling. Einstellung.ueblich(): 200 epochs, batch of 8 utterances, patience 25, seed 20260824, no reports, beam width 12 when rating. The output layer must be LINEAR – otherwise its derivative would be applied a second time; the trainer rejects such a network. Learning-rate schedules and dropout are not used here.

Worked example

Two windows, two characters (blank and a), label "a". The logits are chosen so that y1=(0.25,0.75)y_1 = (0.25,\ 0.75) and y2=(23,13)y_2 = (\tfrac23,\ \tfrac13). Of the four paths, three yield "a":

PathOutputProbability
a aa0.75⋅13=0.250.75 \cdot \tfrac13 = 0.25
a _a0.75⋅23=0.50.75 \cdot \tfrac23 = 0.5
_ aa0.25⋅13=0.08330.25 \cdot \tfrac13 = 0.0833
_ _(empty)0.25⋅23=0.16670.25 \cdot \tfrac23 = 0.1667

P(a)=0.8333P(\text{a}) = 0.8333, L=−ln⁡0.8333=0.1823L = -\ln 0.8333 = 0.1823. In window 1 the paths "a a" and "a _" emit an a: γ1(a)=0.75/0.8333=0.9\gamma_1(\text{a}) = 0.75/0.8333 = 0.9, γ1(ε)=0.1\gamma_1(\varepsilon) = 0.1. In window 2 "a a" and "_ a" emit an a: γ2(a)=0.3333/0.8333=0.4\gamma_2(\text{a}) = 0.3333/0.8333 = 0.4, γ2(ε)=0.6\gamma_2(\varepsilon) = 0.6. The gradient:

∂L∂z1=(0.25−0.1,0.75−0.9)=(0.15,−0.15),∂L∂z2=(0.6667−0.6,0.3333−0.4)=(0.0667,−0.0667).\frac{\partial L}{\partial z_1} = (0.25 - 0.1,\ 0.75 - 0.9) = (0.15,\ -0.15), \qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial z_2} = (0.6667 - 0.6,\ 0.3333 - 0.4) = (0.0667,\ -0.0667).

Ctc.rechne delivers exactly these values, Ctc.verlust gives −ln⁡0.1667-\ln 0.1667 for the empty label; both probabilities add up to 1. The gradient pushes more strongly towards a in window 1 than in window 2 – there the a is already likely, but the blank carries more alignments.

How it is checked

  • Against enumeration (CtcSelfTest): with a small alphabet all KTK^T paths can be enumerated – an independent truth that shares nothing with the dynamic programming except the collapsing. For K=2,3,4K = 2, 3, 4, T=2…6T = 2 \dots 6 and nine labels (85 cases in the run) the loss must agree to 10−1210^{-12}; measured 1.8⋅10−151.8 \cdot 10^{-15}.
  • Distribution: the probabilities of all reachable outputs sum to 1, and each individual one agrees with Ctc, to 10−1210^{-12}.
  • Gradient: 30 cases against the central difference quotient with h=10−4h = 10^{-4} (for this function the minimum of truncation and rounding error; with 10−610^{-6} the derivative was demonstrably worse). Relative below 10−710^{-7}, absolute below 10−810^{-8}, every row sums to zero within 10−1210^{-12}. Measured: 3.6⋅10−83.6 \cdot 10^{-8} and 3.2⋅10−103.2 \cdot 10^{-10}.
  • Edge cases: "aa" needs three windows, "ab" two; an impossible label gives +∞+\infty instead of NaN; the empty label is the pure blank path; a blank in the label, characters outside the alphabet and an alphabet of only one character are rejected. 22 checks in total.
  • Parameter gradient (SequenzSelfTest): the gradient with respect to the weights, as it sits in the network after einSchritt, against the numerical derivative of the CTC loss – relative below 10−610^{-6}, measured 1.0⋅10−71.0 \cdot 10^{-7}. This covers whether the right gradient row belongs to the right window.
  • The trainer learns the alignment: an artificial task with five characters as points in an eight-dimensional feature space, four windows with noise per character, silence in between, a label without any timing; network 8–24–6. The error rate on held-back utterances must fall below 0.35 and drop by more than a factor of five. Measured: 1.678 before, 0.222 after (greedy 0.267), 139 epochs. With varying durations and repetitions: 0.275 (greedy 0.482), required below 0.5. An impossible label in the training set must not abort training.

Limits

  • The outputs of the windows count as independent. What came before and after a window must be contained in the input (context window); knowledge about admissible sound sequences only enters at decoding.
  • A label longer than the number of windows allows is skipped in training.
  • The forward pass runs twice per utterance; for very long utterances keeping the activations would be cheaper.
  • Selection by error rate decodes the whole measuring set in every epoch, with beam search – more expensive than a loss computation.
  • The package is checked, but has no callers outside the checks yet.
