Wavelets and Hilbert transform – background

The computation behind Daubechies, Wavelet, Cwt and Hilbert: orthogonal filters by spectral factorisation, the multi-level discrete transform, the Morlet scalogram and the analytic signal with envelope and instantaneous frequency – with derivations and recomputed examples.

What it is about

The Fourier transform says which frequencies a signal contains, but not when. Wavelets decompose by time and scale; the analytic signal gives amplitude and frequency at each instant for a narrow-band signal – for instance the envelope of a bearing defect or the course of a run-up.

Daubechies filters

The low-pass dbN with NN vanishing moments has length 2N2N. It results from spectral factorisation: the polynomial P(y)=∑k<N(N−1+kk)ykP(y) = \sum_{k<N}\binom{N-1+k}{k}y^k is written in zz via y=(2−z−z−1)/4y = (2 - z - z^{-1})/4, its roots are found from the companion matrix (QR method), those inside the unit circle are kept and multiplied by (1+z)N(1 + z)^N; normalised to ∑h=2\sum h = \sqrt2. The high-pass is gn=(−1)nhL−1−ng_n = (-1)^n h_{L-1-n}.

Where the polynomial comes from (Daubechies, Chapter 6). With H(ω)=∑nhne−inωH(\omega) = \sum_n h_n e^{-in\omega}, orthonormality of the shifted filters requires

|H(ω)|2+|H(ω+π)|2=2.|H(\omega)|^2 + |H(\omega + \pi)|^2 = 2.

NN vanishing moments of the high-pass mean that HH has an NN-fold zero at ω=π\omega = \pi: H(ω)=2(1+e−iω2)NL(ω)H(\omega) = \sqrt 2\,\big(\tfrac{1 + e^{-i\omega}}{2}\big)^N L(\omega). With y=sin2⁡(ω/2)y = \sin^2(\omega/2), |1+e−iω2|2=1−y|\tfrac{1 + e^{-i\omega}}{2}|^2 = 1 - y, and the condition becomes

(1−y)NP(y)+yNP(1−y)=1,P(y)=|L(ω)|2.(1 - y)^N P(y) + y^N P(1 - y) = 1, \qquad P(y) = |L(\omega)|^2.

The solution of lowest degree is the polynomial above (Bézout's theorem; recomputed symbolically for N=1…6N = 1 \dots 6). PP is a squared modulus; to obtain LL itself one takes the “square root” in zz: every root comes with its mirror image 1/z1/\bar z, one of each is kept. Choosing those inside the unit circle gives the minimum-phase filter – the Daubechies filters in the narrow sense.

Example db2 (N=2N = 2): h=(1+3,3+3,3−3,1−3)/(42)h = \big(1 + \sqrt 3,\ 3 + \sqrt 3,\ 3 - \sqrt 3,\ 1 - \sqrt 3\big)/(4\sqrt 2). Recomputed: ∑h=2\sum h = \sqrt 2, ∑hk2=1\sum h_k^2 = 1, h0h2+h1h3=0h_0 h_2 + h_1 h_3 = 0; the high-pass g=(h3,−h2,h1,−h0)g = (h_3, -h_2, h_1, -h_0) has ∑gn=∑ngn=0\sum g_n = \sum n\,g_n = 0, but ∑n2gn≠0\sum n^2 g_n \ne 0 – exactly two vanishing moments.

Discrete and continuous transform

The discrete transform filters periodically and halves per level:

aj+1[k]=∑nh[n]aj[2k+n],dj+1[k]=∑ng[n]aj[2k+n].a_{j+1}[k] = \sum_n h[n]\,a_j[2k + n], \qquad d_{j+1}[k] = \sum_n g[n]\,a_j[2k + n].

One level is a matrix TT whose rows are the filters hh and gg shifted by two each. The orthonormality conditions above say exactly that these rows are orthonormal – TT is orthogonal, the inverse is TTT^{\mathsf T}: lossless, energy preserved (Parseval). Cost: one level costs LL multiplications per value, the next works on half the length, together at most 2Ln2Ln – linear in nn, cheaper than an FFT.

Vanishing moments at work. If the signal is a polynomial of degree below NN on the support of a filter, the detail there is zero; only jumps and kinks leave details. That is why the decomposition separates smooth trends from disturbances. At periodic boundaries the filter meets the jump between end and start – hence “interior” in the check.

The continuous transform with the Morlet wavelet (ω0=6\omega_0 = 6) is computed after Torrence and Compo per scale via the FFT: in the frequency domain the wavelet is ψ^(sω)=2πs/Δtπ−1/4e−(sω−ω0)2/2\hat\psi(s\omega) = \sqrt{2\pi s/\Delta t}\,\pi^{-1/4} e^{-(s\omega - \omega_0)^2/2} for ω>0\omega > 0, else 0; per scale one multiplication and one inverse FFT. The scale ss corresponds to the Fourier period

λ=4πsω0+2+ω02,ω0=6:λ=1.033s.\lambda = \frac{4\pi s}{\omega_0 + \sqrt{2 + \omega_0^2}}, \qquad \omega_0 = 6:\ \lambda = 1.033\, s.

Derivation. A cosine of angular frequency ω\omega produces at scale ss the power ∝se−(sω−ω0)2\propto s\,e^{-(s\omega - \omega_0)^2} (the factor ss comes from the normalisation). Differentiating with respect to ss and setting to zero gives sω=(ω0+ω02+2)/2s\omega = \big(\omega_0 + \sqrt{\omega_0^2 + 2}\big)/2, and with λ=2π/ω\lambda = 2\pi/\omega the formula above. Without the normalisation factor the maximum would be at sω=ω0s\omega = \omega_0, i.e. λ=2πs/ω0=1.047s\lambda = 2\pi s/\omega_0 = 1.047\,s – a difference of just over one per cent.

Analytic signal

The Hilbert transform shifts every frequency by a quarter period: in the spectrum it multiplies by −isgn⁡ω-i\,\operatorname{sgn}\omega, so Hcos⁡=sin⁡\mathcal H\cos = \sin. The analytic signal z=x+iHxz = x + i\,\mathcal H x therefore has the spectrum (1+sgn⁡ω)X(1 + \operatorname{sgn}\omega)\,X: negative frequencies to 0, positive ones doubled, DC and Nyquist single. That is how it is computed via the FFT, for any length (Bluestein for non-powers of two). From it

A(t)=|z(t)|,φ(t)=arg⁡z(t),f(t)=12πdφdt,A(t) = |z(t)|, \qquad \varphi(t) = \arg z(t), \qquad f(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{d\varphi}{dt},

the envelope, the unwrapped phase and the instantaneous frequency.

When the envelope is right. For x=a(t)cos⁡(ωct)x = a(t)\cos(\omega_c t), A=|a|A = |a| holds exactly when the spectrum of aa lies entirely below ωc\omega_c (Bedrosian's theorem). An AM tone with modulation far below the carrier satisfies this, and the envelope is then exactly 1+0.5cos⁡1 + 0.5\cos. If the bands overlap – modulation as fast as the carrier –, |z||z| is no longer an envelope in the intuitive sense.

Instantaneous frequency. The phase is unwrapped (jumps of 2π2\pi removed) and differentiated. For a linear chirp φ\varphi is quadratic and ff linear; at the edges the periodicity of the FFT distorts the course, so only the middle part is compared.

How it is checked

  • Haar, db2 in closed form, db4 after Daubechies' table to 1e-10; db1 to db10 with ∑h=2\sum h = \sqrt2, orthonormal shifts and vanishing moments.
  • db4 over five levels lossless with Parseval; a quadratic signal under db3 without interior details.
  • Hcos⁡=sin⁡\mathcal H\cos = \sin at 512 and 1000 values; envelope of an AM tone exact; instantaneous frequency of a linear chirp to 1 %; Morlet period and separation of two tones in time.

Limits

Periodic boundaries (no mirroring); no biorthogonal wavelets; Hilbert transform with the edge effects of the FFT.
