Numerical integration – background

The computation behind GaussLegendre, GaussKronrod, TanhSinh and Stuetzstellen: degree of exactness, Gauss nodes, the Kronrod pair with the QUADPACK error estimate, adaptive subdivision, infinite limits, the double-exponential substitution with tail estimate and the rules for measured data – with pseudocode following the source, worked examples, checks and limits.

What this is about

We want I=∫abf(x)dxI = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx – an enthalpy difference from cp(T)c_p(T), a force from a pressure distribution, a mean over a characteristic curve. Every numerical quadrature rule replaces the integral by a weighted sum of function values:

I≈Q=∑i=1Nwif(xi).I \approx Q = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i\, f(x_i).

The quality of a rule is measured by its degree of exactness: the highest polynomial degree it integrates exactly. If ff is smooth it behaves locally like a polynomial, and a rule of high degree quickly becomes accurate. If ff is not smooth – a kink, a peak, a square-root singularity at the end –, a high degree helps little; then the rule must become finer where ff is difficult (adaptive), or the variable must be substituted so that the difficulty disappears.

Gauss-Legendre

With nn nodes and nn weights a rule has 2n2n free quantities; they can be chosen so that all polynomials up to degree 2n−12n - 1 are integrated exactly – more is impossible. On [−1,1][-1, 1] these nodes are the roots of the Legendre polynomial PnP_n, the weights

wi=2(1−xi2)Pn'(xi)2.w_i = \frac{2}{(1 - x_i^2)\, P_n'(x_i)^2}.

PnP_n and its derivative come from the three-term recurrence

kPk(x)=(2k−1)xPk−1(x)−(k−1)Pk−2(x),Pn'(x)=n(xPn(x)−Pn−1(x))x2−1,k\, P_k(x) = (2k - 1)\, x\, P_{k-1}(x) - (k - 1)\, P_{k-2}(x), \qquad P_n'(x) = \frac{n\,\big(x P_n(x) - P_{n-1}(x)\big)}{x^2 - 1},

and the roots from Newton's method starting at cos⁡(π(i+3/4)/(n+1/2))\cos\big(\pi (i + 3/4)/(n + 1/2)\big). For an interval [a,b][a, b] the map is affine: ∫abf=b−a2∑iwif(a+b2+b−a2xi)\int_a^b f = \frac{b-a}{2}\sum_i w_i f\big(\frac{a+b}{2} + \frac{b-a}{2} x_i\big).

Gauss-Kronrod, adaptive

A single Gauss rule does not tell how accurate it is. Kronrod added n+1n + 1 further nodes to the nn Gauss nodes such that the extended rule with 2n+12n + 1 nodes has the highest possible degree. The pair G7/K15: 7 Gauss nodes (degree 13) inside 15 Kronrod nodes (degree 22). Both rules share the function values; 15 evaluations give an accurate value KK and a coarser comparison value GG.

The raw difference |K−G||K - G| usually overestimates the error of KK enormously – it is the error of the worse rule. QUADPACK (routine QK15) therefore reshapes it. With resasc⁡≈∫|f−f|\mathrm{resasc} \approx \int |f - \bar f| (mean deviation from the mean value, computed with the Kronrod weights) and resabs⁡≈∫|f|\mathrm{resabs} \approx \int |f|:

error⁡=resasc⁡⋅min⁡(1,(200|K−G|resasc⁡)3/2),error⁡≥50εresabs⁡.\mathrm{error} = \mathrm{resasc} \cdot \min\!\Big(1,\ \Big(\frac{200\,|K - G|}{\mathrm{resasc}}\Big)^{3/2}\Big), \qquad \mathrm{error} \ge 50\,\varepsilon\,\mathrm{resabs}.

The exponent 3/2 reflects that the true error of KK falls with a higher power than |K−G||K - G|; the lower bound accounts for what rounding alone causes.

Adaptive means: all subintervals sit in a priority queue ordered by their estimated error. The worst one is halved, both halves get their own K15 evaluation, until the sum of the errors reaches the target:

∑subintervalserrorj⁡≤max⁡(absTol⁡,relTol⁡⋅|Q|).\sum_{\text{subintervals}} \mathrm{error}_j \le \max(\mathrm{absTol},\ \mathrm{relTol}\cdot|Q|).

The subintervals thus gather by themselves at kinks, peaks and end-point singularities.

Pseudocode – GaussKronrod.adaptiv(f, a, b, absTol, relTol, max)

(K, e) ← k15(f, a, b);  if not finite: end “integrand not finite at x=…”
queue ← {[a, b]};  sum ← K;  error ← e
repeat:
    if error ≤ max(absTol, relTol · |sum|): converged, stop
    if |queue| ≥ max: message “maximum number of subintervals”, stop
    s ← subinterval with largest error;  m ← midpoint
    if m not strictly inside or width ≤ 100 ε max(|a_s|, |b_s|):
        message “rounding limit … cannot be divided further”, stop
    (K_l, e_l) ← k15(left);  (K_r, e_r) ← k15(right)             not finite → message
    sum ← sum + K_l + K_r − K_s;  error ← error + e_l + e_r − e_s
sum value and error afresh over all subintervals
if converged but freshly summed above the target: “tolerance missed after resummation”

Source: envisionz-framework-math/src/envisionz/math/quadratur/GaussKronrod.java, adaptiv and k15. Nodes and weights are those tabulated in QUADPACK, to 33 digits.

Convention in the framework: at most 2000 subintervals (MAX_TEILINTERVALLE), adjustable. Both tolerances 0, negative or NaN are rejected. b<ab < a gives the negative integral, a=ba = b the value 0 without evaluation. If the integrand returns NaN or infinity at a node, the computation ends with a message and location – it does not silently continue.

Infinite limits

An infinite limit is mapped to the unit interval as in QUADPACK QAGI:

∫a∞f(x)dx=∫01f(a+1−tt)dtt2,∫−∞∞f(x)dx=∫01(f(x)+f(−x))dtt2,x=1−tt.\int_a^\infty f(x)\,dx = \int_0^1 f\Big(a + \frac{1 - t}{t}\Big)\,\frac{dt}{t^2}, \qquad \int_{-\infty}^\infty f(x)\,dx = \int_0^1 \Big(f(x) + f(-x)\Big)\frac{dt}{t^2},\ x = \frac{1-t}{t}.

The Kronrod nodes never hit the interval ends, so t=0t = 0 is never evaluated. The count of evaluations takes into account that with both limits infinite each node costs two function values. Examples from the probe: ∫0∞e−x=1\int_0^\infty e^{-x} = 1 with 135 evaluations, ∫−∞∞e−x2=π\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-x^2} = \sqrt\pi with 390, true error 4.4⋅10−164.4 \cdot 10^{-16}.

Tanh-sinh: singularities at the ends

An integrand such as 1/x1/\sqrt x or ln⁡x\ln x is unbounded at the end. Gauss-Kronrod reaches it only by repeated halving towards the end – for ∫01x−1/2\int_0^1 x^{-1/2} that is 1965 evaluations. The double-exponential substitution of Takahasi and Mori (1974) removes the problem at its root:

x(t)=tanh⁡(π2sinh⁡t),x'(t)=π2cosh⁡tcosh2⁡(π2sinh⁡t),∫−11f(x)dx=∫−∞∞f(x(t))x'(t)dt.x(t) = \operatorname{tanh}\!\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{sinh} t\Big), \qquad x'(t) = \frac{\frac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{cosh} t}{\operatorname{cosh}^2\!\big(\frac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{sinh} t\big)}, \qquad \int_{-1}^1 f(x)\,dx = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f\big(x(t)\big)\,x'(t)\,dt.

The weight x'(t)x'(t) falls double-exponentially for |t|→∞|t| \to \infty, faster than any end-point singularity of the form |x∓1|p|x \mp 1|^{p}, p>−1p > -1, can grow. The new integrand is smooth and decays rapidly; for such functions the plain trapezoidal rule in tt with step hh is extraordinarily accurate – the number of correct digits roughly doubles with each halving of hh.

Accuracy at the limit and the tail estimate

Near the limit 1−|x|1 - |x| is not formed as a difference (cancellation) but directly: with u=π2sinh⁡tu = \frac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{sinh} t and e=exp⁡(−2u)e = \exp(-2u), 1−tanh⁡u=2e/(1+e)1 - \operatorname{tanh} u = 2e/(1 + e), and the weight is π2cosh⁡(t)⋅4e/(1+e)2\frac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{cosh}(t)\cdot 4e/(1+e)^2. The nodes lie at a+b−a2(1−x)a + \tfrac{b-a}{2}(1 - x) and b−…b - \ldots.

An integrand that only knows xx, however, does not see the exact distance at a limit a≠0a \ne 0: x=a+δx = a + \delta is rounded to a multiple of the rounding step of aa, and points closer than one rounding step disappear completely. For ∫12(x−1)−1/2dx=2\int_1^2 (x-1)^{-1/2}\,dx = 2 about 2⋅10−82 \cdot 10^{-8} is missing because of this, and the levels among themselves do not notice – they all cut off at the same place. The error estimate therefore has three parts:

  1. the difference of the last two levels (cautious, the true error is rather its square);
  2. the tail estimate: from the two outermost evaluated points on each side (distances s1<s2s_1 < s_2, magnitudes f1,f2f_1, f_2) a local power law |f|≈Csp|f| \approx C s^p is estimated, p=ln⁡(f1/f2)/ln⁡(s1/s2)p = \ln(f_1/f_2)/\ln(s_1/s_2), and the rest beyond s1s_1 is taken as 2f1s1/(1+min⁡(p,0))2\,f_1 s_1/(1 + \min(p, 0)) – infinite for p≤−1p \le -1, where the integral diverges;
  3. a rounding floor 50εh∑wi|fi|50\,\varepsilon\,h\sum w_i|f_i|.

Anyone needing full accuracy passes the integrand as a Randfunktion (x,zuA⁡,zuB⁡)→f(x, \mathrm{zuA}, \mathrm{zuB}) \to f: it receives the distances to both limits exactly, and ∫12(x−1)−1/2\int_1^2 (x-1)^{-1/2} computed with zuA instead of x − 1 is accurate to 2⋅10−162 \cdot 10^{-16}.

Pseudocode – TanhSinh.rechne(f, randgenau, a, b, absTol, relTol, maxStufen)

I ← h · Σ

Convention in the framework: |t|≤6.56|t| \le 6.56 (there 1−|x|1 - |x|, at about 10−48110^{-481}, is far below 10−30010^{-300} and already 0 in double precision); start with h=1h = 1, at most 12 halvings (adjustable 1 to 20); convergence at the earliest from the third level, because an accidentally small difference between two coarse levels can fake convergence. Measured data: trapezoid and Simpson

If only value pairs (xi,yi)(x_i, y_i) are available – a measured curve, a table column –, there is no function that could be evaluated at arbitrary points. Stuetzstellen integrates over the given points, also on uneven grids:

  • Trapezoid (exact for straight lines): ∑i12(xi−xi−1)(yi+yi−1)\sum_i \tfrac12 (x_i - x_{i-1})(y_i + y_{i-1}); kumulativTrapez gives the running integral, F0=0F_0 = 0.
  • Simpson (exact for parabolas): each pair of intervals with widths h0,h1h_0, h_1 under the parabola through three points, ∫x0x2≈h0+h16[(2−h1h0)y0+(h0+h1)2h0h1y1+(2−h0h1)y2],\int_{x_0}^{x_2} \approx \frac{h_0 + h_1}{6}\Big[\Big(2 - \frac{h_1}{h_0}\Big) y_0 + \frac{(h_0 + h_1)^2}{h_0 h_1}\, y_1 + \Big(2 - \frac{h_0}{h_1}\Big) y_2\Big], for equal widths the well-known rule h3(y0+4y1+y2)\tfrac h3 (y_0 + 4 y_1 + y_2). With an odd number of intervals the last interval is integrated with the parabola through the last three points.

The abscissae must be strictly monotone; decreasing ones give the integral with reversed sign. Example: y=x2y = x^2 at x=0,0.5,1.5,2x = 0,\ 0.5,\ 1.5,\ 2 (three uneven intervals). Simpson gives 8/3=2.66678/3 = 2.6667 exactly, the trapezoidal rule 2.8752.875.

The result: no silent failure

The adaptive methods return an Integral: value, error estimate, number of evaluations of the original integrand, subintervals or levels, konvergiert and a message. Not converged always means: with a reason – maximum reached, integrand not finite (with location), rounding limit, tail dominates. The error estimate should exceed the true error, also without convergence; the probe checks this on every one of its reference integrals.

Worked example

∫01e−x2dx=π2erf⁡(1)=0.746824132812427…\int_0^1 e^{-x^2}\,dx = \tfrac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\operatorname{erf}(1) = 0.746824132812427\ldots with GaussKronrod.integriere(f, 0, 1, 1e-13, 1e-10): value 0.74682413281242710.7468241328124271, reported error 8.3⋅10−158.3 \cdot 10^{-15}, 15 evaluations, one subinterval – the K15 rule alone suffices. ∫01x−1/2dx=2\int_0^1 x^{-1/2}\,dx = 2 with TanhSinh: value 1.99999999999999981.9999999999999998, reported error 2.6⋅10−142.6 \cdot 10^{-14}, 75 evaluations in 3 levels (Gauss-Kronrod: 1965).

How it is checked

The Quadraturprobe (266 checks) relies on degrees of exactness instead of tables and on integrals in closed form. Every reference check requires true error ≤ reported error.

  • Gauss-Legendre: nodes and weights for n=1,2,3n = 1, 2, 3 against the known values (10−1510^{-15}); for 21 node counts up to n=1000n = 1000: weight sum 2, symmetry, nodes strictly increasing in (−1,1)(-1, 1), weights positive, exact up to degree 2n−12n - 1 and at degree 2n2n exactly the remainder; x5x^5 on [1,3][1, 3] with n=3n = 3 exact; composite rule; rejection of n=0n = 0, n=4097n = 4097, infinite limit.
  • Kronrod pair: K15 exact up to degree 22 (deviation below 10−1410^{-14}), no longer at degree 24; G7 up to degree 13, at degree 14 the error estimate rises (1.6⋅10−21.6 \cdot 10^{-2}).
  • Gauss-Kronrod (NM7-GK-01 … 15, relTol 10−1010^{-10}, absTol 10−1310^{-13}): sin⁡\sin on [0,π][0, \pi] (15 evaluations), x\sqrt x, 1/x1/\sqrt x (1965), ln⁡x\ln x, cos⁡(100x)\cos(100x), peak 1/((x−0.3)2+10−4)1/((x - 0.3)^2 + 10^{-4}), kink |x−0.7||x - 0.7|, four infinite limits; divergent 1/x1/x, NaN, too few subintervals → not converged with message, error estimate honest nevertheless.
  • Tanh-sinh (NM7-TS-01 … 10, NM7-TR-01 … 03): 1/x1/\sqrt x, ln⁡x\ln x, x−0.9x^{-0.9}, −ln⁡x\sqrt{-\ln x}, exe^x each to 10−1210^{-12} relative, limits never evaluated; 1/x−11/\sqrt{x-1} and 1/1−x21/\sqrt{1-x^2} via xx only: honestly not converged (true 2.2⋅10−82.2 \cdot 10^{-8}, reported 6⋅10−86 \cdot 10^{-8}); the same with end-point accuracy to 10−1210^{-12}; ln⁡(x−3)ex\ln(x-3)\,e^x on [3,4][3, 4].
  • Measured data (NM7-ST-01 … 04): trapezoid exact for lines, Simpson exact for parabolas on an uneven grid with even and odd interval count, Simpson much more accurate than trapezoid (1.1⋅10−41.1 \cdot 10^{-4} versus 1.6⋅10−21.6 \cdot 10^{-2}); rejection of non-monotone abscissae.

The 34 reference cases are stored with fixed identifiers (NM7-GK-01 … NM7-ST-04) under envisionz-examples/math/quadratur; 13 counter-probes, all red.

Limits

  • Gauss-Kronrod has no extrapolation (QUADPACK QAGS, Wynn ε\varepsilon); end-point singularities cost a few hundred to a thousand more evaluations – use tanh-sinh for them.
  • Interior singularities must be known to the caller, who splits the interval there; otherwise they are only approached adaptively, and at non-integrable points the computation ends with a message.
  • Tanh-sinh only with finite limits. With a limit ≠0\ne 0 and an integrand that only knows xx, the accuracy is limited to about the square root of the rounding step – then use the Randfunktion.
  • Strongly oscillating integrands over long intervals (sin⁡(ωx)\sin(\omega x), ω⋅(b−a)\omega \cdot (b-a) large) need many subintervals; there is no dedicated rule for oscillation (Filon, QAWO).
  • Stuetzstellen has no error estimate; the accuracy depends on the measurement density.
