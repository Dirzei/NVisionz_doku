Sequences, images, circuits, optics, celestial mechanics – background

The computation behind Ausrichtung, Segmentierung, Schaltung, Optik, Bahn and Mehrkoerper: dynamic programming after Gotoh, Otsu threshold and connected regions, modified nodal analysis with trapezoidal companion models, ray matrices and thin films, Kepler's equation, Lambert's problem and symplectic integration.

What it is about

Five small fields, each a complete compute core with closed-form reference cases: align two strings optimally; separate a grey image into foreground and background; compute an electrical network in DC, AC and time domain; compute lens systems, laser beams and layer stacks; determine orbits of satellites and celestial bodies.

Alignment after Gotoh

With score s(a,b)s(a, b), gap opening oo and extension ee Gotoh keeps three tables (pair MM, gap in the second sequence XX, in the first YY):

Mij=max⁡(M,X,Y)i−1,j−1+s(ai,bj),Xij=max⁡(Mi−1,j−o,Xi−1,j−e),Yij=max⁡(Mi,j−1−o,Yi,j−1−e).M_{ij} = \max(M, X, Y)_{i-1,j-1} + s(a_i, b_j), \quad X_{ij} = \max(M_{i-1,j} - o,\ X_{i-1,j} - e), \quad Y_{ij} = \max(M_{i,j-1} - o,\ Y_{i,j-1} - e).

Globally (Needleman-Wunsch) the borders start with gap penalties; locally (Smith-Waterman) MM is cut at 0 and the maximum of the whole table is sought. The traceback gives the alignment, bewerte recomputes its score independently.

Why three tables. A gap of kk characters costs g(k)=o+(k−1)eg(k) = o + (k - 1)\,e – opening once, each extension cheaper. A single table cannot do this: whether the next gap step costs oo or ee depends on how the cell was reached. The three tables remember exactly this state (last a pair, gap above, gap left); the recursion stays local, and the optimality principle of dynamic programming holds. Cost O(nm)O(nm) in time and, because of the traceback over all three tables, also in memory. With o=eo = e it becomes Needleman-Wunsch with linear gap penalty. Scoring GATTACA\mathtt{GATTACA} against GCATGCU\mathtt{GCATGCU} with match +1+1, mismatch and gap −1-1 gives the optimum 0.

Otsu and regions

The Otsu threshold maximises over the histogram the between-class variance

σB2(t)=wBwF(μB−μF)2=wBμB2+wFμF2−μ2,wB+wF=1.\sigma_B^2(t) = w_B\,w_F\,(\mu_B - \mu_F)^2 = w_B\mu_B^2 + w_F\mu_F^2 - \mu^2, \qquad w_B + w_F = 1 .

Because the total variance σ2=σW2+σB2\sigma^2 = \sigma_W^2 + \sigma_B^2 does not depend on the threshold, this is the same as the smallest spread within the classes – without computing the class variances; with running sums over the histogram one pass suffices, O(L)O(L) for LL levels. Counts are used instead of fractions; that changes only a constant factor, not the position of the maximum.

For three classes with the sums Sk=wkμkS_k = w_k\mu_k, σB2=∑kSk2/wk−μ2\sigma_B^2 = \sum_k S_k^2/w_k - \mu^2 is maximised – over all pairs of thresholds, with prefix sums in O(L2)O(L^2). The form follows from σB2=∑kwk(μk−μ)2=∑kwkμk2−μ2\sigma_B^2 = \sum_k w_k(\mu_k - \mu)^2 = \sum_k w_k\mu_k^2 - \mu^2.

Connected regions are labelled by breadth-first search with 4- or 8-neighbourhood; region growing adds neighbours as long as their value lies within a tolerance. Whether two pixels connected only at a corner belong together is decided by the neighbourhood alone – which is why the same pattern splits into more regions with four neighbours than with eight.

Modified nodal analysis

Unknown are the node voltages and the currents through voltage sources (and inductors in the DC case). The system has the block form

(GBCD)(vj)=(ie):\begin{pmatrix} G & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} v \\ j \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} i \\ e \end{pmatrix}:

GG the nodal conductance matrix (a resistor between pp and qq stamps +1/R+1/R into GppG_{pp}, GqqG_{qq} and −1/R-1/R into GpqG_{pq}, GqpG_{qp}), BB and CC the assignment of the extra currents to their nodes, ii the impressed currents, ee the source voltages; a controlled source stamps its factor into the row of its current. Ground (node 0) is not an unknown – without it GG would be singular.

In AC the conductances are complex (iωCi\omega C, 1/(iωL)1/(i\omega L)), computed in real embedding: (Gr+iGi)(vr+ivi)=ir+iii(G_r + iG_i)(v_r + iv_i) = i_r + i\,i_i becomes the real system of twice the size with blocks (Gr−GiGiGr)\begin{pmatrix} G_r & -G_i \\ G_i & G_r\end{pmatrix}. For the RC low-pass this gives u2/u1=1/(1+iωRC)u_2/u_1 = 1/(1 + i\omega RC).

In the time domain the trapezoidal rule replaces each capacitor by a companion model (conductance 2C/Δt2C/\Delta t in parallel with a current source from the last step), each inductor likewise; initial values from the DC case at t=0t = 0 (capacitors open, inductors shorted). Derived from Cu˙=iC\,\dot u = i and Li˙=uL\,\dot i = u, each integrated with the trapezoidal rule over one step:

in+1=2CΔtun+1−(2CΔtun+in),un+1−2LΔtin+1=−un−2LΔtin.i_{n+1} = \frac{2C}{\Delta t}\,u_{n+1} - \Big(\frac{2C}{\Delta t}\,u_n + i_n\Big), \qquad u_{n+1} - \frac{2L}{\Delta t}\,i_{n+1} = -u_n - \frac{2L}{\Delta t}\,i_n .

The capacitor thus becomes a conductance with a current source from the old state; the inductor gets its own row with its current. The trapezoidal rule is A-stable but not L-stable: fast components are not damped but ring with alternating sign. Order 2 of the trapezoidal rule shows only with smooth sources – a step in a source drops it to 1.

Ray matrices, Gaussian beam, thin films

Paraxial elements are 2×2 matrices: distance [[1,d],[0,1]][[1, d], [0, 1]], thin lens [[1,0],[−1/f,1]][[1, 0], [-1/f, 1]], interface [[1,0],[(n1−n2)/(Rn2),n1/n2]][[1, 0], [(n_1 - n_2)/(R n_2), n_1/n_2]]; a system is their product, the focal length −1/C-1/C. The interface follows from the law of refraction at small angles: the ray at height yy meets the surface normal tilted by y/Ry/R, n1(θ+y/R)=n2(θ'+y/R)n_1(\theta + y/R) = n_2(\theta' + y/R), so θ'=n1n2θ+n1−n2n2Ry\theta' = \frac{n_1}{n_2}\theta + \frac{n_1 - n_2}{n_2 R}\,y. Two such surfaces with thin glass between give the lensmaker's formula 1/f=(n−1)(1/R1−1/R2)1/f = (n - 1)(1/R_1 - 1/R_2); two thin lenses at distance dd the focal length 1/f=1/f1+1/f2−d/(f1f2)1/f = 1/f_1 + 1/f_2 - d/(f_1 f_2). The image distance bb is the distance after which B=0B = 0: then the image height no longer depends on the angle – imaging. With f=0.1f = 0.1 and g=0.3g = 0.3, b=0.15b = 0.15.

A Gaussian beam is described by the complex beam parameter qq, 1/q=1/R−iλ/(πw2)1/q = 1/R - i\lambda/(\pi w^2); it passes a system as q'=(Aq+B)/(Cq+D)q' = (Aq + B)/(Cq + D). In free space from the waist q(z)=z+izRq(z) = z + i z_R with the Rayleigh length zR=πw02/λz_R = \pi w_0^2/\lambda; hence

w(z)=w01+(z/zR)2,R(z)=z(1+(zR/z)2).w(z) = w_0\sqrt{1 + (z/z_R)^2}, \qquad R(z) = z\big(1 + (z_R/z)^2\big).

Thin films use the characteristic matrix per layer [[cos⁡δ,isin⁡δ/n],[insin⁡δ,cos⁡δ]][[\cos\delta, i\sin\delta/n], [i n\sin\delta, \cos\delta]], δ=2πnd/λ\delta = 2\pi n d/\lambda; from the product follow reflectance and transmittance (normal incidence, lossless):

(BC)=∏kMk(1ns),R=|n0B−Cn0B+C|2,T=4n0ns|n0B+C|2.\begin{pmatrix} B \\ C \end{pmatrix} = \prod_k M_k\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ n_s \end{pmatrix}, \qquad R = \Big|\frac{n_0 B - C}{n_0 B + C}\Big|^2, \qquad T = \frac{4 n_0 n_s}{|n_0 B + C|^2}.

Without a layer B=1B = 1, C=nsC = n_s, and the Fresnel formula R=((n0−ns)/(n0+ns))2R = ((n_0 - n_s)/(n_0 + n_s))^2 remains – for glass with ns=1.52n_s = 1.52 that is 4.26 %. A quarter-wave layer (δ=π/2\delta = \pi/2) has M=(0i/n1in10)M = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & i/n_1 \\ i n_1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, so B=ins/n1B = i n_s/n_1, C=in1C = i n_1 and R=0R = 0 exactly for n1=n0nsn_1 = \sqrt{n_0 n_s}: the anti-reflection coating. Because the layers are lossless, R+T=1R + T = 1.

Kepler, Lambert, N-body

Kepler's equation M=E−esin⁡EM = E - e\sin E is solved by Newton with a bracket [M−e,M+e][M - e, M + e] – plain Newton ran away at high eccentricity; hyperbolic M=esinh⁡F−FM = e\sinh F - F. The bracket is derived: |esin⁡E|≤e|e\sin E| \le e, so the solution lies in [M−e,M+e][M - e, M + e], and f(E)=E−esin⁡E−Mf(E) = E - e\sin E - M is strictly increasing because f'=1−ecos⁡E>0f' = 1 - e\cos E > 0 for e<1e < 1. A Newton step leaving the bracket is replaced by bisection; the bracket tightens with every function value.

Orbital elements follow from (r,v)(r, v) via angular momentum and eccentricity vector,

h=r×v,e=1μ((v2−μr)r−(r⋅v)v),h = r \times v, \qquad e = \frac{1}{\mu}\Big(\big(v^2 - \tfrac{\mu}{r}\big)\,r - (r\cdot v)\,v\Big),

and back via the rotation from the orbital plane. The line of nodes is z^×h\hat z \times h; it is missing for equatorial orbits, and node and perigee are then set to 0.

Lambert's problem (orbit from r1r_1 to r2r_2 in Δt\Delta t) is solved with the universal variable zz and Stumpff functions after Curtis, algorithm 5.2:

y(z)=r1+r2+AzS(z)−1C(z),(yC)3/2S+Ay=μΔt,A=sin⁡Δθr1r21−cos⁡Δθ,y(z) = r_1 + r_2 + A\,\frac{z S(z) - 1}{\sqrt{C(z)}}, \qquad \Big(\frac{y}{C}\Big)^{3/2} S + A\sqrt y = \sqrt\mu\,\Delta t, \qquad A = \sin\Delta\theta\sqrt{\frac{r_1 r_2}{1 - \cos\Delta\theta}},

with C(z)=(1−cos⁡z)/zC(z) = (1 - \cos\sqrt z)/z, S(z)=(z−sin⁡z)/z3S(z) = (\sqrt z - \sin\sqrt z)/\sqrt{z}^{\,3} (hyperbolic for z<0z < 0, at z=0z = 0 the series 12−z/24\tfrac12 - z/24, 16−z/120\tfrac16 - z/120). From yy follow the Lagrange coefficients f=1−y/r1f = 1 - y/r_1, g=Ay/μg = A\sqrt{y/\mu}, g˙=1−y/r2\dot g = 1 - y/r_2 and the velocities v1=(r2−fr1)/gv_1 = (r_2 - f r_1)/g, v2=(g˙r2−r1)/gv_2 = (\dot g\,r_2 - r_1)/g. At Δθ=180°\Delta\theta = 180°, A=0A = 0 and the orbital plane is undetermined; there is no solution for that.

For several bodies Mehrkoerper integrates symplectically with Yoshida's 4th-order method (three leapfrog steps with w1=1/(2−21/3)w_1 = 1/(2 - 2^{1/3}), w0=−21/3w1w_0 = -2^{1/3}w_1); energy and momentum are conserved.

The weights solve the order conditions 2w1+w0=12w_1 + w_0 = 1 (consistency) and 2w13+w03=02w_1^3 + w_0^3 = 0 (the third-order error of the symmetric method cancels): w1=1.3512w_1 = 1.3512, w0=−1.7024w_0 = -1.7024 – one sub-step runs backwards. Symplectic means: energy is not held exactly, but its error stays bounded and does not grow with time. The acceleration with Plummer softening ε\varepsilon,

ai=G∑j≠imjxj−xi(|xj−xi|2+ε2)3/2,a_i = G\sum_{j \ne i} m_j\,\frac{x_j - x_i}{\big(|x_j - x_i|^2 + \varepsilon^2\big)^{3/2}},

is computed once per pair and distributed to both with opposite sign – so momentum is conserved up to rounding.

How it is checked

  • GATTACA against GCATGCU (optimum 0), exhaustive search for short sequences, local search, affine gap as one block.
  • Otsu against exhaustive search, three classes, 4/8-neighbourhood on a hand pattern.
  • Voltage divider, Wheatstone bridge and non-inverting amplifier by hand; RC low-pass and resonant circuit in AC and time domain with order 2.
  • Lens equation, lens pair, thick lens, Gaussian beam after Self (1983), quarter-wave layer without reflection, Bragg mirror in closed form.
  • Curtis ex. 4.3 and 5.2 (Lambert: v1=(−5.9925;1.9254;3.2456)v_1 = (-5.9925;\ 1.9254;\ 3.2456) km/s); circular orbit after one revolution (order 4); figure-eight of three bodies after Chenciner and Montgomery back after one period to 3e-8.

Limits

Alignment with memory O(nm)O(nm); circuits linear; optics paraxial, layers at normal incidence and lossless; Lambert for less than one revolution; at Δθ=0°\Delta\theta = 0° or 180°180° the orbital plane is undetermined and the call is rejected; N-body with direct summation O(n2)O(n^2), without step size control (in close encounters only the softening helps).
