Generalised eigenvalues, sparse factorisations, events and matrix exponential – background

The computation behind Verallgemeinert, DuenneCholesky, DuenneQr, nested dissection, Ereignisse with DAE up to index 3 and Matrixexponential – extensions of the numerics needed by the structural, rotor and control modules.

What it is about

Structural dynamics requires Kx=ω2MxK x = \omega^2 M x instead of Ax=λxA x = \lambda x; large finite-element systems require sparse factorisations with little fill-in; multibody and switching processes require that integration stops at events and keeps algebraic constraints; control and Markov chains need eAte^{At}. This page collects the building blocks.

Generalised eigenproblem

Ax=λBxA x = \lambda B x with symmetric AA and positive definite BB is reduced densely via the Cholesky decomposition B=LLTB = L L^T to L−1AL−Ty=λyL^{-1}AL^{-T}y = \lambda y, x=L−Tyx = L^{-T}y; the vectors are B-normalised (xTBx=1x^T B x = 1, unit modal mass). In general (also singular BB) Francis QR runs on (A−σB)−1B(A - \sigma B)^{-1}B with λ=σ+1/θ\lambda = \sigma + 1/\theta; infinite eigenvalues are left out and counted. For large problems shift-invert Lanczos in the B inner product (symmetric) or Arnoldi (non-symmetric) run, with a dense or sparse factorisation of A−σBA - \sigma B and explicit restart.

The reduction. With B=LLTB = L L^T and y=LTxy = L^T x, Ax=λBxA x = \lambda B x becomes

L−1AL−Ty=λy,C=L−1AL−T=CT.L^{-1} A L^{-T}\, y = \lambda\, y, \qquad C = L^{-1} A L^{-T} = C^T.

CC is symmetric, so it has real eigenvalues and orthonormal eigenvectors yiy_i. For xi=L−Tyix_i = L^{-T} y_i we then have xiTBxj=yiTL−1LLTL−Tyj=yiTyj=δijx_i^T B x_j = y_i^T L^{-1} L L^T L^{-T} y_j = y_i^T y_j = \delta_{ij}: the mode shapes are B-orthonormal, in structural dynamics modal mass 1. L−1AL−TL^{-1}AL^{-T} is never formed as an inverse but by forward and backward substitution with LL.

Shift-invert. From (A−σB)x=(λ−σ)Bx(A - \sigma B)\,x = (\lambda - \sigma)\,B x it follows that

(A−σB)−1Bx=θx,θ=1λ−σ,λ=σ+1θ.(A - \sigma B)^{-1} B\, x = \theta\, x, \qquad \theta = \frac{1}{\lambda - \sigma}, \qquad \lambda = \sigma + \frac{1}{\theta}.

Eigenvalues λ\lambda close to σ\sigma become the θ\theta of largest modulus – exactly those a Krylov method finds first. The operator S=(A−σB)−1BS = (A - \sigma B)^{-1}B is not symmetric, but it is self-adjoint in the B inner product ⟨u,v⟩B=uTBv\langle u, v\rangle_B = u^T B v: ⟨Su,v⟩B=uTB(A−σB)−1Bv=⟨u,Sv⟩B\langle S u, v\rangle_B = u^T B (A - \sigma B)^{-1} B v = \langle u, S v\rangle_B, because AA and BB are symmetric. That is why Lanczos runs there with the same three-term recurrence as in the ordinary case (mode 3 of ARPACK). Each step costs one solve with the once-factored matrix A−σBA - \sigma B and one product with BB.

Singular BB. If Bx=0B x = 0, then θ=0\theta = 0 and λ=∞\lambda = \infty: a degree of freedom without mass. The general dense path recognises such θ\theta by ‖Bx‖\|B x\| close to zero, leaves them out and reports their number.

Worked example: the bar. Longitudinal vibration with linear elements of length hh, stiffness K=1htridiag⁡(−1,2,−1)K = \frac1h\,\mathrm{tridiag}(-1, 2, -1), consistent mass M=h6tridiag⁡(1,4,1)M = \frac h6\,\mathrm{tridiag}(1, 4, 1), both ends fixed. The sine vectors with tj=jπht_j = j\pi h are eigenvectors of both matrices, and

λj=6h21−cos⁡tj2+cos⁡tj=(jπ)2(1+(jπh)212+O(h4)).\lambda_j = \frac{6}{h^2}\,\frac{1 - \cos t_j}{2 + \cos t_j} = (j\pi)^2\Big(1 + \frac{(j\pi h)^2}{12} + O(h^4)\Big).

The consistent mass thus approaches the continuous value (jπ)2(j\pi)^2 from above, with order 2 – exactly what the probe measures for λ1→π2\lambda_1 \to \pi^2.

Sparse Cholesky, sparse QR, nested dissection

The sparse Cholesky factorisation PAPT=LLTPAP^T = LL^T works like Davis's cs_chol: elimination tree, row pattern via reachability in the tree, a symbolic pass for the column counts, then the numbers. The sparse QR for min⁡‖Ax−b‖\min\|Ax - b\| rotates row by row with Givens (George and Heath) without storing QQ. How much fills in is decided by the ordering PP: nested dissection (George) splits the graph into two halves with a level-set separator, numbers the separator last and repeats in the halves; small parts are ordered by AMD. On a 160 × 160 grid it fills in less than AMD (616 084 against 638 350 entries); growth follows k2log⁡kk^2\log k.

The elimination tree. For the Cholesky factorisation the parent of column jj is

parent⁡(j)=min⁡{i>j:lij≠0}.\mathrm{parent}(j) = \min\{\, i > j : l_{ij} \ne 0 \,\}.

It condenses the whole dependency structure of the elimination: the non-zeros of row ii of LL are exactly the nodes reached from the non-zeros aika_{ik}, k<ik < i, by walking up the tree until just before ii (Liu 1990; Davis, ch. 4). This gives the structure of the code: first the tree from the pattern of AA, then the reach per row – which gives the column lengths of LL without a single floating-point operation –, only then the numbers. A shallow tree means many columns are independent of each other.

Why dissection helps. If a separator SS splits the graph into two parts with no edge between them and the nodes of SS are numbered last, the block of LL between the two parts stays empty: eliminating a node fills only between its neighbours, and they lie in its own part or in the separator. On a k×kk \times k grid a plane separator has O(k)O(k) nodes; applied recursively this gives O(k2log⁡k)O(k^2 \log k) entries in LL and O(k3)O(k^3) operations, against O(k3)O(k^3) entries for a band ordering (George 1973). For three dimensions with k3k^3 nodes: separator O(k2)O(k^2), entries O(k4)O(k^4), operations O(k6)O(k^6).

Sparse QR with Givens. A rotation with r=a2+b2r = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}, c=a/rc = a/r, s=b/rs = b/r turns the pair (a,b)(a, b) into (r,0)(r, 0). Each row of AA is rotated entry by entry into the growing triangle RR, the right-hand side rotates along; QQ is never stored. Since QQ is orthogonal, RTR=ATAR^T R = A^T A: up to signs RR is the Cholesky factor of ATAA^T A, and its pattern is at most that pattern – which is why the column order is determined on the graph of ATAA^T A.

Events and DAE up to index 3

After each accepted step the event functions g(t,y)g(t, y) are evaluated; a sign change in the requested direction stops, the Illinois method finds the root, and every value y(τ)y(\tau) comes from a short integration with the same method (as accurate as the integration). The action stops or sets a new state (impact, switching). For higher-index DAE, Radau IIA carries the index per unknown (1, 2, 3); error norm and Newton stop weight these components with hh at index 2 and with h2h^2 at index 3 – the code divides their scale by hh and h2h^2 (as RADAU5 by Hairer and Wanner). Without this information the pendulum in Cartesian coordinates fails after four steps.

after each step [t, t+h]:
    for each event function g: if sign g(t) ≠ sign g(t+h) in the requested direction:
        τ ← Illinois on g(s, y(s)), y(s) by integration from t
    earliest τ: action (stop or new state), continue from τ

The Illinois method. Between aa and bb with different signs of gg the secant gives

c=b−gbb−agb−ga.c = b - g_b\,\frac{b - a}{g_b - g_a}.

Plain regula falsi keeps one end fixed when gg is curved and then converges only linearly. Illinois halves the stored function value of the end that stays put for the second time in a row; this pulls the secant across to the root, and the order of convergence becomes 31/3≈1.4423^{1/3} \approx 1.442 (Dowell and Jarratt 1971). The bracket is always kept – unlike the secant method, the root cannot be lost.

The pendulum as a DAE. In Cartesian coordinates with rod length 1 and rod force λ\lambda:

x'=u,y'=v,u'=−λx,v'=−λy−g,0=x2+y2−1.x' = u,\quad y' = v,\quad u' = -\lambda x,\quad v' = -\lambda y - g,\qquad 0 = x^2 + y^2 - 1.

The constraint does not contain λ\lambda. Differentiated once in time: xu+yv=0x u + y v = 0; once more, with the equations of motion: u2+v2−λ(x2+y2)−gy=0u^2 + v^2 - \lambda(x^2 + y^2) - g y = 0, hence λ=u2+v2−gy\lambda = u^2 + v^2 - g y – only the third derivative determines λ\lambda, the system has index 3. The position has index 1, the velocities index 2, the force index 3. For implicit Runge-Kutta methods the errors of the index-2 and index-3 components are larger by the factor 1/h1/h and 1/h21/h^2 than those of the others (Hairer and Wanner, ch. VII); that is why they enter the error norm with the weight hh and h2h^2. Without this weight the step size control considers them inaccurate and shrinks hh until nothing works any more.

Matrix exponential

Scaling and squaring with Padé approximation after Higham (2005, algorithm 2.3): depending on the 1-norm order 3, 5, 7, 9 or 13 (bounds θm\theta_m); at order 13 the matrix is divided by 2s2^s first and squared ss times at the end:

eA≈(qm(A/2s)−1pm(A/2s))2s.e^A \approx \big(q_m(A/2^s)^{-1}p_m(A/2^s)\big)^{2^s}.

The Padé approximant. rm=pm/qmr_m = p_m/q_m is the rational function with numerator and denominator of degree mm whose Taylor series agrees with that of exe^x up to x2mx^{2m}. Its coefficients are known in closed form:

pm(x)=∑k=0m(2m−k)!m!(2m)!k!(m−k)!xk,qm(x)=pm(−x).p_m(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{m} \frac{(2m-k)!\; m!}{(2m)!\; k!\; (m-k)!}\, x^k, \qquad q_m(x) = p_m(-x).

The code computes them from the recursion c0=1c_0 = 1, ck=ck−1(m−k+1)/(k(2m−k+1))c_k = c_{k-1}\,(m - k + 1)/\big(k\,(2m - k + 1)\big). It never goes above 13, and the bounds θm\theta_m (θ3≈0.015\theta_3 \approx 0.015, θ5≈0.254\theta_5 \approx 0.254, θ7≈0.950\theta_7 \approx 0.950, θ9≈2.098\theta_9 \approx 2.098, θ13≈5.372\theta_{13} \approx 5.372) are chosen such that for ‖A‖1≤θm\|A\|_1 \le \theta_m the approximant is the exact exponential of a slightly perturbed matrix, rm(A)=eA+ΔAr_m(A) = e^{A + \Delta A} with ‖ΔA‖≤u‖A‖\|\Delta A\| \le u\,\|A\|, u=2−53u = 2^{-53} (Higham 2005). Above θ13\theta_{13} the matrix is divided by 2s2^s, s=⌈log2⁡(‖A‖1/θ13)⌉s = \lceil \log_2(\|A\|_1/\theta_{13}) \rceil, and the result squared ss times – using eA=(eA/2s)2se^A = (e^{A/2^s})^{2^s}. The squarings are where accuracy is lost for large norms; that is why Higham chooses m=13m = 13 with as few squarings as possible.

Needed for the zero-order hold in control and the course of Markov chains.

How it is checked

  • Bar with consistent mass: discrete spectrum in closed form, all 60 values to 1e-12, XTMX=IX^TMX = I; n = 3000 sparse; damped oscillators against s=−ζω±iω1−ζ2s = -\zeta\omega \pm i\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}; singular BB with an infinite value reported. Since TD3: with a search space of 8, shift-invert Lanczos finds the four smallest values of diag(1 … 200) only after restarts (to 10−1510^{-15}); with no restart allowed it reports “not converged”, and for A=B=IA = B = I an invariant space with one value.
  • Cholesky with manufactured solution under four orderings, every entry equal to the dense factorisation; 30³ unknowns; QR equal to the dense least-squares solution to 1e-12.
  • Bouncing ball: ten impact times in closed form to 1e-10; pendulum as DAE of index 3 and 2 against the angle equation to 1e-6, constraint to 1e-10; lowest point after K(sin⁡45°)/gK(\sin 45°)/\sqrt g to 1e-7.
  • Matrix exponential: rotation and diagonal over all orders to 1e-13, nilpotent and Jordan block in closed form, eAe−A=Ie^A e^{-A} = I.

Limits

Sparse factorisations only for positive definite or full-rank matrices; two events in one step go unnoticed (set a maximum step); matrix exponential dense.
