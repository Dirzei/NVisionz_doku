Eigenvalues and singular value decomposition – dense matrices

The computation behind EigenQr, GolubKahan and Singulaerwertzerlegung: balancing, Hessenberg form, Francis QR with double shift, eigenvectors and their normalisation, bidiagonalisation and implicit QR for the SVD, rank, condition and minimum-norm solution – with pseudocode following the source, worked examples, checks and limits. Large operators are treated on the page Lanczos and Arnoldi.

What this is about

An eigenpair (λ,x)(\lambda, x) of a square matrix satisfies Ax=λxA x = \lambda x, x≠0x \ne 0: the matrix only stretches xx without changing its direction. For the engineer eigenvalues appear in three places:

  • Stability: a linearised model y˙=Ay\dot y = A y – for instance the engine dynamics around an operating point – is stable if all eigenvalues have negative real part. A complex pair σ±iω\sigma \pm i\omega is an oscillation with angular frequency ω\omega and growth rate σ\sigma.
  • Mode shapes: for Kx=ω2MxK x = \omega^2 M x (stiffness and mass) the eigenvalues are the squares of the natural angular frequencies, the vectors the mode shapes.
  • Roots: the roots of a polynomial are the eigenvalues of its companion matrix.

A non-symmetric real matrix generally has complex eigenvalues that occur in conjugate pairs. Computing via the characteristic polynomial det⁡(A−λI)=0\det(A - \lambda I) = 0 would be numerically hopeless – the roots of a polynomial depend extremely sensitively on its coefficients. One goes the other way: similarity transformations A→Q−1AQA \to Q^{-1} A Q do not change the eigenvalues and bring the matrix step by step into a form from which they can be read off.

The singular value decomposition A=UΣVTA = U \Sigma V^T is the counterpart for arbitrary, also rectangular matrices: UU and VV are orthogonal, Σ\Sigma diagonal with the singular values σ1≥σ2≥…≥0\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge 0. It answers the questions of rank, condition and best approximate solution – see section singular value decomposition.

All eigenvalues: Francis QR

EigenQr is the sequence of the EISPACK routines balanc, orthes/ortran and hqr2, as also used by JAMA and MATLAB's eig for general matrices. The idea of the QR algorithm (Francis and Kublanovskaya 1961): factor Ak=QkRkA_k = Q_k R_k and set Ak+1=RkQk=QkTAkQkA_{k+1} = R_k Q_k = Q_k^T A_k Q_k. Each step is an orthogonal similarity transformation, and the sequence converges to the real Schur form: an upper triangular matrix with 1×11 \times 1 blocks (real eigenvalues) and 2×22 \times 2 blocks (conjugate pairs) on the diagonal. Three ingredients turn this into a usable method.

1. Balancing

Badly scaled matrices – entries from 10−610^{-6} to 101010^{10} in one matrix, as they arise when linearising models with mixed units – lose accuracy in the QR algorithm because the rounding errors depend on the norm of the whole matrix. Balancing (Parlett and Reinsch 1969) computes with B=D−1ADB = D^{-1} A D, DD diagonal with powers of two. This is a similarity transformation – the eigenvalues do not change – and since only powers of 2 are involved it is exact in floating point. For each row ii the sum of off-diagonal magnitudes of the column, cc, is compared with that of the row, rr, and a factor f=2kf = 2^k is sought with which cf2c f^2 lies between r/2r/2 and 2r2r:

c⋅f2≈r.c \cdot f^2 \approx r.

The factor is only taken if it reduces the sum noticeably: (cf2+r)/f<0.95(c+r)(c f^2 + r)/f < 0.95\,(c + r).

Row ii is divided by ff, column ii multiplied by ff; the passes repeat until nothing is gained any more (at most 100). The eigenvectors are transformed back with DD at the end. In probe NM8-QR-04 balancing reduces the error for a badly scaled matrix from 62.862.8 to 6.7⋅10−156.7 \cdot 10^{-15}.

2. Hessenberg form

A QR step on a full matrix costs O(n3)O(n^3). On an upper Hessenberg matrix – only zeros below the first subdiagonal – it costs O(n2)O(n^2), and the form is preserved. Therefore AA is brought to this form once with n−2n - 2 Householder reflections (EISPACK orthes); the reflections are accumulated in VV (ortran) because the eigenvectors are transformed back through them at the end.

3. Francis double step with implicit shift

Without a shift QR converges as slowly as the ratios |λi+1/λi||\lambda_{i+1}/\lambda_i|. With a shift A−σIA - \sigma I close to an eigenvalue the element hn,n−1h_{n,n-1} falls quadratically. The shifts are the two eigenvalues of the trailing 2×22 \times 2 block. If they are complex, a single shift would lead into complex arithmetic; Francis' double step applies both conjugate shifts at once, implicitly: from the first column of (H−σ1I)(H−σ2I)(H - \sigma_1 I)(H - \sigma_2 I), which has only three entries,

p=(x−z)(y−z)−whm+1,m+hm,m+1,q=hm+1,m+1−z−(x−z)−(y−z),r=hm+2,m+1,p = \frac{(x - z)(y - z) - w}{h_{m+1,m}} + h_{m,m+1}, \quad q = h_{m+1,m+1} - z - (x - z) - (y - z), \quad r = h_{m+2,m+1},

a 3×33 \times 3 Householder reflection is formed; it creates a “bulge” below the subdiagonal that further reflections chase down the diagonal until Hessenberg form is restored. Everything stays real.

Deflation: when a subdiagonal element becomes negligible,

|hl,l−1|<ε(|hl−1,l−1|+|hl,l|)orhl,l−1=0,|h_{l,l-1}| < \varepsilon\,(|h_{l-1,l-1}| + |h_{l,l}|) \quad \text{or} \quad h_{l,l-1} = 0,

the matrix splits. A deflated 1×11 \times 1 block is a real eigenvalue. A 2×22 \times 2 block (abcd)\left(\begin{matrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{matrix}\right) gives, with p=(a−d)/2p = (a - d)/2, q=p2+bcq = p^2 + b c, either two real values (q≥0q \ge 0, then the block is additionally rotated to triangular form) or the pair d+p±i−qd + p \pm i\sqrt{-q}.

Exceptional shifts: if nothing deflates after 10 steps, the computation continues with an ad-hoc shift from the last subdiagonal entries (x=y=0.75s,w=−0.4375s2)(x = y = 0.75\,s,\ w = -0.4375\,s^2), after 30 steps with a second rule. After 100 steps without deflation the method throws an ArithmeticException.

Eigenvectors, ordering, residuals

All methods of the package return their result through a common final stage (Nachbereitung) as Eigenpaare:

  • Ordering by the Auswahl; for EigenQr it is BETRAG_GROESSTE: by |λ||\lambda| descending, on ties by real then imaginary part. A conjugate pair sits side by side, the value with +i+i first.
  • Vectors as columns; a pair occupies two columns, real and imaginary part of the vector for λ=σ+iω\lambda = \sigma + i\omega.
  • Normalisation: length 1 (for pairs ‖u‖2+‖w‖2=1\|u\|^2 + \|w\|^2 = 1), and the component of largest magnitude is real and positive – for a pair the vector is rotated by a complex phase to achieve this. The representation is thus unique and comparable between runs.
  • Residuals ‖Ax−λx‖\|A x - \lambda x\| are recomputed independently with the original matrix at the end, complex for pairs: ‖(Au−σu+ωw)+i(Aw−σw−ωu)‖\|(A u - \sigma u + \omega w) + i(A w - \sigma w - \omega u)\|.
Convention in the framework: EigenQr.zerlege(a) balances by default and computes vectors; werte(a) computes without vectors (the same values, bit-identical, check NM8-QR-03). With zerlege(a, mitVektoren, false) balancing can be switched off – sensible if small entries are only noise and would gain weight through balancing.

Worked example

Roots of x3−6x2+11x−6=(x−1)(x−2)(x−3)x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6 = (x-1)(x-2)(x-3) via the companion matrix:

C=(6−116100010),det⁡(C−λI)=−(λ3−6λ2+11λ−6).C = \left(\begin{matrix} 6 & -11 & 6 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{matrix}\right), \qquad \det(C - \lambda I) = -(\lambda^3 - 6\lambda^2 + 11\lambda - 6).

EigenQr.zerlege(C) returns after 6 QR steps the values 2.99999999999999822.9999999999999982, 2.00000000000000042.0000000000000004, 1.00000000000000091.0000000000000009, all with imaginary part 0, ordered by magnitude. The rotation (0−110)\left(\begin{matrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{matrix}\right) gives the pair +i,−i+i, -i in this order; here the 2×22 \times 2 block deflates immediately, and p=0p = 0, q=−1q = -1 gives ±i1\pm i\sqrt{1}.

Singular value decomposition after Golub, Kahan and Reinsch

Every matrix AA (m×nm \times n) can be written as

A=UΣVT=∑iσiuiviT,Avi=σiui,ATui=σivi.A = U \Sigma V^T = \sum_{i} \sigma_i\, u_i v_i^T, \qquad A v_i = \sigma_i u_i, \qquad A^T u_i = \sigma_i v_i.

Geometrically: AA maps the unit sphere onto an ellipsoid; the σi\sigma_i are its semi-axes, viv_i the pre-image and uiu_i the image directions. Everything else follows: ‖A‖2=σ1\|A\|_2 = \sigma_1, κ2=σ1/σmin⁡\kappa_2 = \sigma_1/\sigma_{\min}, the rank is the number of non-zero σi\sigma_i, and the best approximation of rank kk is the sum truncated after kk terms.

Why not via ATAA^T A

The eigenvalues of ATAA^T A are σi2\sigma_i^2. Computing them, the rounding error is of size εσ12\varepsilon \sigma_1^2. A singular value σi\sigma_i is then only recognisable if σi2\sigma_i^2 is clearly larger than εσ12\varepsilon \sigma_1^2, that is σi\sigma_i clearly larger than εσ1≈10−8σ1\sqrt\varepsilon\,\sigma_1 \approx 10^{-8}\sigma_1. For a matrix with condition 101210^{12} the smallest singular value is completely lost on this route (check NM8-SV-02: via ATAA^T A computed as 0, relatively wrong by 1). Golub-Kahan works on AA itself and hits every singular value to about εσ1\varepsilon\,\sigma_1; in the probe each of the four values 1…10−121 \dots 10^{-12} is within 1.5⋅10−171.5 \cdot 10^{-17}.

Method

  1. Bidiagonalisation A=U1BV1TA = U_1 B V_1^T: alternately a Householder reflection from the left (zeroes a column below the diagonal) and from the right (zeroes a row to the right of the superdiagonal). BB has only the diagonal ss and the upper superdiagonal ee. This is a finite amount of work, about 4mn24 m n^2.
  2. Implicit QR on BB: a QR step on BTBB^T B without forming BTBB^T B. The shift is the Wilkinson shift – the eigenvalue of the trailing 2×22 \times 2 block of BTBB^T B closer to the last diagonal element. The step starts with a Givens rotation from (sk2−shift,skek)(s_k^2 - \text{shift},\ s_k e_k) and chases the resulting bulge down the diagonal with alternating rotations from the right and the left.
  3. Deflation after LINPACK dsvdc: a superdiagonal element counts as zero if |ek|≤tiny+ε(|sk|+|sk+1|)|e_k| \le \text{tiny} + \varepsilon(|s_k| + |s_{k+1}|) with ε=2−52\varepsilon = 2^{-52}, tiny=2−966\text{tiny} = 2^{-966}. If instead a diagonal element sks_k becomes negligible, the neighbouring superdiagonal entry is chased away with rotations (cases 1 and 2); case 3 is the QR step, case 4 the convergence of a value.
  4. Finishing each converged value: move a negative sign into VV, swap the value to its place in the descending sequence.

Pseudocode – GolubKahan.hoch(a, vektoren), m ≥ n

Householder alternately left/right → s (diagonal), e (superdiagonal), U, V
p ← n;  iter ← 0
while p > 0:
    k ← largest k < p−1 with |e[k]| ≤ tiny + ε(|s[k]| + |s[k+1]|)  →  e[k] ← 0
    if k = p−2:                      case ← 4                        s[p−1] converged
    else look for ks in (k, p−1] with |s[ks]| ≤ tiny + ε·(neighbouring e):
        none → case 3;   ks = p−1 → case 1;   otherwise case 2
    case 1: chase e[p−2] upwards with rotations from the right (V)
    case 2: chase e[k−1] downwards with rotations from the left (U)
    case 3: iter ← iter + 1;  if iter > 75: throw ArithmeticException
            shift ← Wilkinson from trailing 2×2 of BᵀB (scaled)
            Givens chain from k to p−2, alternately right (V) and left (U)
    case 4: if s[k] < 0: s[k] ← −s[k], negate column k of V
            swap s[k] into descending order (with U, V);  iter ← 0;  p ← p − 1

Source: envisionz-framework-math/src/envisionz/math/eigen/GolubKahan.java (structure after LINPACK dsvdc). Wide matrices (m<nm < n) are factored transposed and UU, VV swapped; werte(a) computes the same values without UU and VV (about half the cost).

Rank, condition, minimum-norm solution

Singulaerwertzerlegung is the thin form: UU is m×km \times k, VV is n×kn \times k, k=min⁡(m,n)k = \min(m, n). From it:

  • norm2() =σ1= \sigma_1; konditionszahl() =σ1/σk= \sigma_1/\sigma_k, infinite if σk=0\sigma_k = 0.
  • rang(): number of σi\sigma_i above the bound max⁡(m,n)⋅ε⋅σ1\max(m, n) \cdot \varepsilon \cdot \sigma_1 with ε=2−52\varepsilon = 2^{-52} – the same bound as MATLAB's rank.
  • loese(b): x=Vdiag⁡(1/σi)UTbx = V \operatorname{diag}(1/\sigma_i) U^T b, omitting all σi\sigma_i below the bound. With full rank this is the ordinary least-squares solution, with rank deficiency the one with smallest ‖x‖\|x\| among all minimisers of ‖Ax−b‖\|A x - b\| – it is perpendicular to the null space.
  • pseudoinverse(): A+=Vdiag⁡(1/σi)UTA^+ = V \operatorname{diag}(1/\sigma_i) U^T with the same bound; it satisfies the four Penrose conditions AA+A=AA A^+ A = A, A+AA+=A+A^+ A A^+ = A^+, (AA+)T=AA+(A A^+)^T = A A^+, (A+A)T=A+A(A^+ A)^T = A^+ A.

A=(3045)A = \left(\begin{matrix} 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 \end{matrix}\right): ATA=(25202025)A^T A = \left(\begin{matrix} 25 & 20 \\ 20 & 25 \end{matrix}\right) has the eigenvalues 45 and 5, hence σ=(35,5)=(6.7082…,2.2360…)\sigma = (3\sqrt 5, \sqrt 5) = (6.7082\ldots,\ 2.2360\ldots), condition 3. GolubKahan.zerlege returns exactly these values after one QR step.

A=(122436)A = \left(\begin{matrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \\ 3 & 6 \end{matrix}\right) has rank 1: σ=(70,0)\sigma = (\sqrt{70}, 0), rang() = 1. For b=(1,2,3)b = (1, 2, 3) all xx with x1+2x2=1x_1 + 2 x_2 = 1 are exact solutions; loese(b) returns the shortest of them, x=(0.2,0.4)=v1(u1Tb)/σ1x = (0.2,\ 0.4) = v_1\,(u_1^T b)/\sigma_1 with v1=(1,2)/5v_1 = (1, 2)/\sqrt 5.

How it is checked

The Eigenprobe (84 checks) computes only against spectra in closed form (NM8-P01 … P13, as Matrix Market files and reference spectra under envisionz-examples/math/eigen); residuals and operator calls are recomputed independently. For the dense methods:

  • QR-01, 02: rotation → ±i\pm i exactly, +i+i first, residual 0; companion matrix with roots 1…51 \dots 5 and ±2i\pm 2i to 7.6⋅10−147.6 \cdot 10^{-14}.
  • QR-03: QTQTQ T Q^T, non-normal, twelve values with three pairs to 1.8⋅10−151.8 \cdot 10^{-15}; residuals below 100nε‖A‖100\,n\,\varepsilon\,\|A\|; reported = recomputed residuals; normalisation; ordering; werte() bit-identical to zerlege().
  • QR-04: badly scaled: with balancing 6.7⋅10−156.7 \cdot 10^{-15}, without 62.862.8 (at least 1000 times worse required); vectors transformed back correctly despite the scaling.
  • QR-05 … 10: triangular matrix, Jordan block (both values 2 to ε\sqrt\varepsilon), Markov chain (eigenvalue 1, eigenvector = stationary distribution), symmetric 80×8080 \times 80 against QL (tql2) to 6.8⋅10−146.8 \cdot 10^{-14}, random 100×100100 \times 100, zero and 1×11 \times 1 matrix.
  • SV-01 … 08: six shapes (tall, wide, square, row, column) with A=USVTA = U S V^T to 3⋅10−163 \cdot 10^{-16}; graded 1…10−121 \dots 10^{-12} each value to 1.5⋅10−17≤10nεs01.5 \cdot 10^{-17} \le 10\,n\,\varepsilon\,s_0 and the counterpart via ATAA^T A; integer matrix of rank 3; least squares 20×620 \times 6 with AT(Ax−b)=0A^T(Ax - b) = 0 to 6.7⋅10−156.7 \cdot 10^{-15}; rank deficiency perpendicular to the null space; Penrose conditions; zero matrix; ATA^T with swapped UU, VV.

11 counter-probes, all red. The Eigenbausteinprobe additionally checks the normalisation of a complex pair and the ordering per Auswahl directly on Nachbereitung.

Limits

  • Dense: cost about 10n310 n^3 without, 25n325 n^3 with vectors (EigenQr), 4mn2+8n34 m n^2 + 8 n^3 (SVD with vectors). Up to a few hundred rows; for a few values of large matrices use Lanczos and Arnoldi.
  • Condition of an eigenvalue: the method is backward stable – the values are exact for some A+EA + E with ‖E‖≈nε‖A‖\|E\| \approx n\,\varepsilon\,\|A\|. How far an eigenvalue moves because of that depends on the matrix: for non-normal matrices and multiple eigenvalues with a Jordan block (2×22 \times 2 block, perturbation ε\varepsilon) by ε\sqrt\varepsilon. The residual still stays small – it says nothing about the error of the eigenvalue.
  • Multiple eigenvalues with a Jordan block have only one eigenvector; the two vectors returned are then almost parallel.
  • After 100 QR steps per eigenvalue (EigenQr) or 75 per singular value (SVD) an ArithmeticException is thrown; this does not occur in the probes.
  • The rank bound is relative to σ1\sigma_1; anyone evaluating measured data with a known noise level passes an own bound with rang(schranke) or loese(b, schranke).
