Scattered data and geometry – background

The computation behind Delaunay, Voronoi, NatuerlicheNachbarn, Variogramm, Kriging, Vieleck and Huelle3d: triangulation after Bowyer-Watson, clipped Voronoi cells, the in-circle test via the paraboloid, Sibson interpolation, ordinary kriging with derivation and variance, Boolean operations by edge classification and the convex hull in space.

What it is about

Measurements rarely lie on a grid: probes in a duct, boreholes in terrain, test points on a surface. Interpolating between them needs a neighbourhood structure (Delaunay, Voronoi) and an interpolation method – geometric (natural neighbours) or statistical with error estimate (kriging). For areas and bodies, set operations and hulls are added.

Delaunay and Voronoi

Bowyer-Watson inserts point by point (random order with fixed seed): all triangles whose circumcircle contains the new point form a cavity; it is removed and re-triangulated from the new point. The enclosing triangle is found by “walking” through neighbours from the last created one. Result: counter-clockwise triangles with neighbours; Euler gives 2n−2−h2n - 2 - h triangles for hh hull points. The Voronoi cell of a point is the intersection of the half-planes against its Delaunay neighbours, clipped to a rectangle (Sutherland-Hodgman).

The in-circle test

Everything hinges on one question: does dd lie in the circumcircle of the counter-clockwise triangle abcabc? Lifting every point onto the paraboloid, (x,y)↦(x,y,x2+y2)(x, y) \mapsto (x, y, x^2 + y^2), puts the points of a circle on a plane and the inside of the circle below it. The question becomes the orientation of four points in space, a determinant:

inCircle⁡(a,b,c;d)=det⁡(ax−dxay−dy(ax−dx)2+(ay−dy)2bx−dxby−dy(bx−dx)2+(by−dy)2cx−dxcy−dy(cx−dx)2+(cy−dy)2)>0.\operatorname{inCircle}(a, b, c; d) = \det\begin{pmatrix} a_x - d_x & a_y - d_y & (a_x - d_x)^2 + (a_y - d_y)^2 \\ b_x - d_x & b_y - d_y & (b_x - d_x)^2 + (b_y - d_y)^2 \\ c_x - d_x & c_y - d_y & (c_x - d_x)^2 + (c_y - d_y)^2 \end{pmatrix} > 0 .

It is computed relative to dd because the differences stay small where the coordinates are large; this reduces cancellation but does not remove it. There are no exact predicates (Shewchuk) – for points almost on a circle the sign can come out wrong; the triangulation is then only nearly Delaunay in that corner.

How many triangles? Derived from Euler's formula n−k+f=2n - k + f = 2 for the planar subdivision with nn points, kk edges and f=T+1f = T + 1 faces (the outer one included): every triangle has three edges, every inner edge belongs to two triangles, each of the hh hull edges to one, so 3T+h=2k3T + h = 2k. Hence

T=2n−2−h.T = 2n - 2 - h .

For the 21 × 21 grid with h=80h = 80 hull points that is 800 triangles.

Construction and cost. It starts from an auxiliary triangle 10410^4 times the extent of the point cloud; its triangles are removed at the end. If it is too small, triangles at the edge of the hull go missing. The triangle containing the new point is found by walking from triangle to triangle, not with the location structure of de Berg et al.; the expected O(nlog⁡n)O(n \log n) proven there therefore does not apply directly. The cavity is connected and star-shaped as seen from the new point – which is why searching through neighbours from the found triangle and joining the cavity border to the point suffices.

The Voronoi cell of pp is the set of locations qq closer to pp than to any other point. Against a neighbour xix_i this means, after expanding |q−p|2≤|q−xi|2|q - p|^2 \le |q - x_i|^2:

q⋅(xi−p)≤12(|xi|2−|p|2),q \cdot (x_i - p) \le \tfrac12\big(|x_i|^2 - |p|^2\big) ,

a half-plane bounded by the perpendicular bisector. The Delaunay neighbours suffice: the Voronoi diagram is the dual graph of the Delaunay triangulation (de Berg et al., ch. 7 and 9).

Natural neighbours and kriging

Sibson: if the query point pp is inserted notionally, its cell V(p)V(p) steals area from the old cells; the weights are

λi=|V(p)∩Vold(i)||V(p)|,w^(p)=∑iλiwi.\lambda_i = \frac{|V(p)\cap V_{old}(i)|}{|V(p)|}, \qquad \hat w(p) = \sum_i \lambda_i\, w_i .

They are non-negative because they are areas; they sum to 1 because the stolen pieces tile V(p)V(p) without gaps. That moreover ∑iλixi=p\sum_i \lambda_i x_i = p – the method reproduces linear fields exactly – is a theorem of Sibson (from the literature). Only points whose cell gives something to pp contribute: these are the natural neighbours, the corners of the cavity that inserting pp would create. It is computed without inserting: the cell of pp against these neighbours, then for each neighbour its old cell inside it.

Kriging (ordinary, after Matheron) chooses the weights from the variogram γ(h)\gamma(h) (spherical, exponential, Gaussian, linear, with nugget):

(Γ11T0)(λμ)=(γ01),z^=∑λizi,σ2=∑λiγi0+μ.\begin{pmatrix}\Gamma & \mathbf 1\\ \mathbf 1^T & 0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda\\ \mu\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}\gamma_0\\ 1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \hat z = \sum\lambda_i z_i, \qquad \sigma^2 = \sum\lambda_i\gamma_{i0} + \mu.

Where the system comes from. Let the field ZZ be stationary with unknown mean; the variogram γ(h)=12E[(Z(x+h)−Z(x))2]\gamma(h) = \tfrac12 E\big[(Z(x + h) - Z(x))^2\big] describes how dissimilar values at distance hh are. We seek z^=∑λizi\hat z = \sum\lambda_i z_i with ∑λi=1\sum\lambda_i = 1 (then the estimator is unbiased whatever the mean) and smallest error variance. With ∑λi=1\sum\lambda_i = 1 the error variance can be written in terms of γ\gamma alone, E[(z^−Z0)2]=2∑iλiγi0−∑i,jλiλjγijE[(\hat z - Z_0)^2] = 2\sum_i\lambda_i\gamma_{i0} - \sum_{i,j}\lambda_i\lambda_j\gamma_{ij}; differentiating with the Lagrange multiplier μ\mu for the constraint gives exactly the system above, and substituting leaves the variance σ2=∑λiγi0+μ\sigma^2 = \sum\lambda_i\gamma_{i0} + \mu (Cressie, ch. 3).

What follows. At a data point the right-hand side is a column of Γ\Gamma; the solution is λ=ei\lambda = e_i, μ=0\mu = 0, variance 0 – kriging is exact there. The diagonal of Γ\Gamma is 0, also with a nugget (γ(0)=0\gamma(0) = 0); the nugget therefore acts as a jump: exact at the data point, immediately beside it uncertain by the nugget. It is not a smoothing that removes measurement error. In one dimension with linear variogram γ=ch\gamma = c\,h and two data points a<ba < b the system gives exactly linear interpolation, with

σ2(x)=2c(x−a)(b−x)b−a,\sigma^2(x) = \frac{2c\,(x - a)(b - x)}{b - a},

the variance of the Brownian bridge – a closed-form anchor used by the probe.

The system is factorised once (LU, O(n3)O(n^3)) and solved for each query point (O(n2)O(n^2)).

Variogram. The models, with nugget c0c_0, partial sill cc and range aa, t=h/at = h/a:

γ(h)=c0+c⋅{32t−12t3(t<1),1(t≥1)spherical1−e−3texponential1−e−3t2Gaussian(h>0),γ(0)=0.\gamma(h) = c_0 + c\cdot\begin{cases} \tfrac32 t - \tfrac12 t^3 \ (t < 1),\ 1 \ (t \ge 1) & \text{spherical} \\ 1 - e^{-3t} & \text{exponential} \\ 1 - e^{-3t^2} & \text{Gaussian} \end{cases} \qquad (h > 0), \qquad \gamma(0) = 0 .

The factor 3 makes aa the “practical” range: there 1−e−3=95%1 - e^{-3} = 95\,\% of the sill is reached. Linear is γ=c0+ch\gamma = c_0 + c\,h without a range.

The empirical variogram after Matheron averages per distance class γ^(hk)=12Nk∑(i,j)∈k(zi−zj)2\hat\gamma(h_k) = \frac{1}{2N_k}\sum_{(i,j) \in k}(z_i - z_j)^2. It is fitted with a logarithmic search over the range and golden section; for each range, nugget and sill are a linear least-squares fit weighted with NkN_k, both non-negative.

Boolean operations

Regions consist of rings (outer and holes). All edges of both regions are split at all contacts (also on overlap), each piece is classified by the position of its midpoint – inside, outside, shared in the same or opposite direction – and kept according to the operation; the kept pieces are chained into rings, at crossings with the sharpest left turn. Collinear points are removed; the snapping tolerance is 10−1010^{-10} of the extent.

OperationPieces of APieces of B
Unionoutside, shared same directionoutside
Intersectioninside, shared same directioninside
Difference A − Boutside, shared opposite directioninside, reversed

Why the midpoint suffices: after splitting, no piece crosses an edge of the other region any more; it lies entirely inside, entirely outside or entirely on an edge. A shared piece is taken only once (from A); same direction means both regions lie on the same side – boundary for union and intersection, not for the difference. For the difference the pieces of B inside A are reversed so that the new boundary again has the result on its left. Area and orientation come from the shoelace formula

A=12∑i(xiyi+1−xi+1yi),A = \tfrac12\sum_{i}\big(x_i\,y_{i+1} - x_{i+1}\,y_i\big),

positive counter-clockwise; holes run the other way and count negative.

Convex hull in space

Incremental: for each new point remove the triangles visible from it, connect the horizon (border of the visible set) with the point to new triangles. More than 2n2n triangles indicate contradictory visibility and end with a message.

A triangle (a,b,c)(a, b, c) is visible if its outer normal n=(b−a)×(c−a)n = (b - a)\times(c - a) points to the new point, n⋅(q−a)>ε|n|n\cdot(q - a) > \varepsilon\,|n|. The bound follows from Euler's formula: a triangulated convex hull with VV vertices has F=2V−4F = 2V - 4 triangles, never more than 2n2n. The volume comes from the divergence theorem, tetrahedra from the origin to each triangle:

V=16∑trianglesa⋅(b×c).V = \frac16\sum_{\text{triangles}} a\cdot(b\times c).

The cost is O(nF)O(n\,F) – each point checks all triangles –, O(n2)O(n^2) in the worst case; enough for a few thousand points.

How it is checked

  • Empty circumcircle against all 400 points; Euler; area equal to the hull; grid without slivers, duplicate point detected; Voronoi cells fill the rectangle.
  • Sibson non-negative, sum 1, linearly exact at 300 points; Franke function with order about 2; kriging with linear variogram in 1D equal to piecewise linear interpolation with the variance of the Brownian bridge.
  • Polygons: overlapping squares, adjacent rectangles, hole and plug, ten non-convex star pairs with inclusion-exclusion to 1e-10; hull of cube and sphere.

Limits

Floating point without exact predicates; Sibson only within the convex hull; kriging dense (a few thousand data points), ordinary (no trend, no anisotropy); convex hull O(n2)O(n^2) in the worst case.
