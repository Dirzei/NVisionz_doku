Initial value problems and DAEs – background

The computation behind DormandPrince, Rodas and the common driver: embedded Runge-Kutta methods, error norm and step size control, stiffness, the linearly implicit Rosenbrock method RODAS4, mass matrix and index-1 DAEs – with pseudocode following the source, recomputed examples, checks and limits.

What this is about

An initial value problem asks: how does a state y(t)y(t) (a vector with nn components) evolve from a known start when its rate of change is known?

y'=f(t,y),y(t0)=y0y' = f(t, y), \qquad y(t_0) = y_0

Examples are flight paths, the run-up of a shaft, reaction kinetics or a control loop. A numerical method replaces the continuous solution by values yk≈y(tk)y_k \approx y(t_k) on a grid of time points, and it chooses the step sizes hk=tk+1−tkh_k = t_{k+1} - t_k itself so that the error per step respects a requested tolerance.

The package offers two methods with a common framework:

Embedded Runge-Kutta methods

A Runge-Kutta method with ss stages evaluates ff at intermediate points within the step and combines the slopes:

ki=f(t+cih,y+h∑j<iaijkj),ynew=y+h∑ibiki.k_i = f\Big(t + c_i h,\ y + h \sum_{j<i} a_{ij} k_j\Big), \qquad y_{\text{new}} = y + h \sum_i b_i k_i.

An embedded pair has a second set of weights bi*b_i^* for the same stages, giving a solution of lower order. The difference of the two is an estimate of the local error – almost for free, without further evaluations:

err⁡=h∑i(bi−bi*)ki=h∑ieiki.\mathrm{err} = h \sum_i (b_i - b_i^*)\, k_i = h \sum_i e_i\, k_i.

The computation continues with the higher-order solution (“local extrapolation”). Dormand and Prince (1980) chose their coefficients so that the error of the fifth-order solution is small. The last stage is evaluated at the new point and is the first stage of the next step (FSAL, first same as last) – seven stages thus cost only six evaluations per accepted step.

Error norm and step size control

The error of each component is weighted with a mixed tolerance and averaged quadratically over all components:

ski⁡=absTol⁡+relTol⁡⋅max⁡(|yi|,|ynew,i|),‖err⁡‖=1n∑i(erri⁡ski⁡)2.\mathrm{sk}_i = \mathrm{absTol} + \mathrm{relTol} \cdot \max(|y_i|, |y_{\text{new},i}|), \qquad \|\mathrm{err}\| = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_i \Big(\frac{\mathrm{err}_i}{\mathrm{sk}_i}\Big)^2}.

A step is accepted if ‖err⁡‖≤1\|\mathrm{err}\| \le 1. Because the local error of a method with embedded order qq grows like hq+1h^{q+1}, the step size

hnew=h⋅min⁡(5,max⁡(0.2,0.9‖err⁡‖−1/(q+1)))h_{\text{new}} = h \cdot \min\!\Big(5,\ \max\!\big(0.2,\ 0.9\,\|\mathrm{err}\|^{-1/(q+1)}\big)\Big)

just meets the tolerance, with the safety factor 0.9 and the limits 0.2 and 5 against jumps. q+1=5q + 1 = 5 for Dormand-Prince, 4 for RODAS. After a rejected step the next accepted one may not grow (factor≤1\text{factor} \le 1).

Initial step

Without a given value the first step is estimated after Hairer, Nørsett and Wanner (volume I, section II.4): d0=‖y0‖d_0 = \|y_0\|, d1=‖f(t0,y0)‖d_1 = \|f(t_0, y_0)\| in the weighted norm; h0=0.01d0/d1h_0 = 0.01\,d_0/d_1 (or 10−610^{-6} if one of the two is below 10−510^{-5}), limited by hmax⁡h_{\max} and the distance to the first output time. An Euler step with h0h_0 gives d2=‖f(t0+h0,y1)−f0‖/h0d_2 = \|f(t_0 + h_0, y_1) - f_0\|/h_0, an estimate of the second derivative, and hence

h1=(0.01max⁡(d1,d2))1/q',h=min⁡(100h0,h1).h_1 = \Big(\frac{0.01}{\max(d_1, d_2)}\Big)^{1/q'}, \qquad h = \min(100\,h_0,\ h_1).

This costs two evaluations of ff.

Convention in the framework: defaults relTol 10−610^{-6}, absTol 10−910^{-9}, initial step automatic, no largest step size, at most 100 000 step trials (accepted and rejected together). Output times must be finite, strictly increasing and ≥t0\ge t_0 – integration only goes forward. The initial value and the settings are not modified.

Worked example

y'=−yy' = -y, y(0)=1y(0) = 1, to t=1t = 1 with the defaults. Dormand-Prince needs 6 steps without rejection and 39 evaluations: 2 for the initial step, 1 for the first stage, 6×66 \times 6 for the steps. The error against e−1e^{-1} is 7.3⋅10−87.3 \cdot 10^{-8} – below the relative tolerance 10−610^{-6} because the tolerance applies per step and the control is cautious. RODAS needs 11 steps and 90 evaluations for the same problem (per step 1+n+1+5=81 + n + 1 + 5 = 8 with n=1n = 1, plus 2 for the initial step), error 3.8⋅10−83.8 \cdot 10^{-8}.

Stiffness

A problem is called stiff if it contains processes of very different time scales and the fast ones have long decayed while the slow ones are still of interest. The simplest example:

y'=−1000(y−cos⁡t),y(0)=0.y' = -1000\,(y - \cos t), \qquad y(0) = 0.

After a few thousandths yy follows the slow function cos⁡t\cos t almost exactly. An explicit method must nevertheless take steps of size h≈c/1000h \approx c/1000, because otherwise it becomes unstable: for the test equation y'=λyy' = \lambda y every explicit Runge-Kutta method gives yk+1=R(hλ)yky_{k+1} = R(h\lambda)\, y_k with a polynomial RR, and |R(hλ)|≤1|R(h\lambda)| \le 1 holds only in a bounded region. The step size is then limited by stability, not by accuracy.

RODAS4: linearly implicit

Implicit methods have stability regions containing the whole left half-plane. Their price is a non-linear system of equations per step. Rosenbrock methods replace it by linear systems with the Jacobian J=∂f/∂yJ = \partial f/\partial y – no Newton iteration, no convergence question. RODAS4 (Hairer and Wanner) has six stages, order 4 with embedded order 3, is L-stable (arbitrarily stiff components are damped, R(∞)=0R(\infty) = 0) and stiffly accurate (the solution is the last stage, hence also suited to DAEs). In the form with mass matrix MM and increments kik_i, each stage reads

(Mhγ−J)ki=f(t+cih,y+∑j<iaijkj)+hdi∂f∂t+M∑j<icijhkj,γ=14.\Big(\frac{M}{h\gamma} - J\Big)\, k_i = f\Big(t + c_i h,\ y + \sum_{j<i} a_{ij} k_j\Big) + h\, d_i\, \frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + M \sum_{j<i} \frac{c_{ij}}{h}\, k_j, \qquad \gamma = \tfrac14.

All six stages share the same matrix W=M/(hγ)−JW = M/(h\gamma) - J – it is prepared once per step trial and applied six times. The fifth and sixth stages are evaluated at t+ht + h; the sum up to the fifth stage is the embedded third-order solution, the sixth stage its correction:

y^=y+∑j=14a5jkj+k5,ynew=y^+k6,err⁡=k6.\hat y = y + \sum_{j=1}^{4} a_{5j} k_j + k_5, \qquad y_{\text{new}} = \hat y + k_6, \qquad \mathrm{err} = k_6.

Jacobian and linear systems

  • Jacobian: analytic via a JacobiMatrix, otherwise per step by forward differences, column cc with the step δc=ε⋅max⁡(10−5,|yc|)\delta_c = \sqrt{\varepsilon \cdot \max(10^{-5}, |y_c|)} – this costs nn evaluations. The time derivative ∂f/∂t\partial f/\partial t is always formed by a difference, δt=εmax⁡(10−5,|t|)\delta_t = \sqrt\varepsilon\,\max(10^{-5}, |t|), one evaluation.
  • Linear systems: a LinearerLoeser prepares WW and then solves. The default is GMRES from envisionz.math.krylov without restart on the dense matrix, relative tolerance 10−1310^{-13}, at most 4n+204n + 20 iterations. If the recomputed relative residual then exceeds 10−910^{-9}, the solver throws an ArithmeticException; the step is rejected and repeated with a quarter of the step size. Anyone with an LU factorisation (LinAlg, GSPAR) plugs it in here.

Mass matrix and index-1 DAEs

Many models mix differential equations with algebraic conditions – mass and energy balances that must hold at every instant:

My'=f(t,y),M=diag⁡(m1,…,mn).M y' = f(t, y), \qquad M = \operatorname{diag}(m_1, \dots, m_n).

Each mim_i is meant to be 0 or 1. A row with mi=0m_i = 0 is the algebraic equation 0=fi(t,y)0 = f_i(t, y). Rodas takes the diagonal as the array masse. Since for these rows W=M/(hγ)−JW = M/(h\gamma) - J contains only −J-J, the stage equations enforce the algebraic condition in every stage. This works for index 1: the algebraic equations can be solved (locally) for the algebraic variables, i.e. the corresponding block of JJ is non-singular.

Example (Robertson, case NM6-RO-03): the third equation of the Robertson kinetics is replaced by the conservation law 0=y1+y2+y3−10 = y_1 + y_2 + y_3 - 1, masse = {1, 1, 0}. The result at t=40t = 40 agrees with the literature value to 1.7⋅10−91.7 \cdot 10^{-9}, the algebraic equation holds to 2.2⋅10−162.2 \cdot 10^{-16}. Without the mass matrix the same right-hand side gives something entirely different – the computation only reaches t=4.07t = 4.07 after 3000 step trials.

Convention in the framework: the mass matrix is diagonal; arbitrary entries are allowed, intended are 1 (differential) and 0 (algebraic). The initial values must satisfy the algebraic equations (be consistent) – this is not checked.

Limits

  • No BDF method of variable order (open as NM13) – for very large stiff systems with an expensive Jacobian it would be more economical than RODAS, which forms JJ anew in every step.
  • No dense output and no events (roots of a switching function): values only at the given output times; requesting many times also shortens the steps.
  • Only forward in time; output times strictly increasing.
  • RODAS works with a dense n×nn \times n matrix and by default solves with GMRES without restart (memory O(n2)O(n^2)); for large systems plug in your own LinearerLoeser.
  • DAEs only with a diagonal mass matrix and index 1; consistent initial values are the caller's job.
  • The tolerance bounds the local error per step; the global error can be much larger for unstable problems (e.g. near a pole). Singularities end with “step size underflow”.
