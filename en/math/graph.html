Paths, flows and networks – background

The computation behind Wege, Fluss and Netzwerk: shortest paths after Dijkstra, Bellman-Ford and Floyd-Warshall, maximum flow after Dinic with minimum cut, and non-linear network equations with the global gradient approach.

What it is about

Directed, weighted graphs carry many engineering tasks: the cheapest path through a network, the capacity of a distribution system, the flow distribution in pipe and air networks. The first two are combinatorial, the third is a non-linear system of equations on the graph.

Shortest paths

Dijkstra (weights ≥ 0) always takes the node with the smallest tentative distance from a priority queue and relaxes its edges. Bellman-Ford allows negative weights: n−1n - 1 rounds over all edges; a further improvement shows a negative cycle (result null). Floyd-Warshall gives all pairs in O(n3)O(n^3). The path itself is read back from the predecessors.

Dijkstra – why it is correct. All three methods rest on the same relaxation of an edge (u,v)(u, v):

d(v)←min⁡(d(v),d(u)+w(u,v)).d(v) \leftarrow \min\big(d(v),\ d(u) + w(u, v)\big).

Dijkstra keeps the invariant: when uu is taken as the smallest tentative distance, d(u)d(u) is final – any other path to uu would have to leave the set of finished nodes through a node with at least as large a distance, and since no weight is negative it cannot become shorter afterwards. A negative weight breaks exactly this argument; that is why dijkstra rejects negative weights. The queue is a binary heap without “decrease key”: an improved node is inserted again, stale entries are skipped when taken out. Cost O((n+m)log⁡m)O((n + m)\log m).

Bellman-Ford. After kk rounds over all edges, d(v)d(v) is at most the length of every path with at most kk edges. A shortest path without a negative cycle is simple and so has at most n−1n - 1 edges; if something still changes in round nn, there is a negative cycle reachable from the source. Cost O(nm)O(n\,m), stopping as soon as a round changes nothing.

Floyd-Warshall lets the allowed intermediate nodes grow: dij(k)d^{(k)}_{ij} is the shortest path that only passes through nodes 0,…,k0, \dots, k,

dij(k)=min⁡(dij(k−1),dik(k−1)+dkj(k−1)).d^{(k)}_{ij} = \min\big(d^{(k-1)}_{ij},\ d^{(k-1)}_{ik} + d^{(k-1)}_{kj}\big).

It is computed in place in one table; a negative entry on the diagonal at the end indicates a negative cycle (result null). Cost O(n3)O(n^3), memory O(n2)O(n^2).

Maximum flow after Dinic

In the residual graph (residual capacities and reverse edges) a level graph is built by breadth-first search; in it a depth-first search pushes blocking flows from ss to tt until none remain; then new levels. At the end the nodes reachable from ss in the residual graph are the source side of the minimum cut, whose capacity equals the maximum flow.

Why the end is right. Every feasible flow satisfies, for every cut (S,T)(S, T) with s∈Ss \in S, t∈Tt \in T,

|f|=∑u∈S,v∈Tf(u,v)−∑u∈T,v∈Sf(u,v)≤∑u∈S,v∈Tc(u,v)=c(S,T),|f| = \sum_{u \in S,\ v \in T} f(u, v) - \sum_{u \in T,\ v \in S} f(u, v) \le \sum_{u \in S,\ v \in T} c(u, v) = c(S, T),

so every flow is at most every cut. When the breadth-first search no longer finds tt, SS is the set of nodes reachable in the residual graph: every edge from SS to TT is full, every edge from TT to SS empty – equality, and so flow and cut are both optimal (max-flow min-cut theorem, Ford and Fulkerson). Cost: in each phase the distance from ss to tt in the level graph grows, so at most n−1n - 1 phases; with the pointer per node (a blocked edge is not tried again) one phase costs O(nm)O(n\,m), together O(n2m)O(n^2 m). Residual capacities below 10−1410^{-14} of the largest capacity count as zero so that rounding remainders do not create endless phases.

Network equations

Nodes have a fixed potential (reservoir, ambient) or a given inflow/outflow; branches obey hfrom−hto=rQ|Q|n−1h_{from} - h_{to} = r\,Q|Q|^{n-1}. Newton solves for branch flows and free potentials together, as in the global gradient approach of Todini and Pilati: the Jacobian has the block form of derivatives of the branch laws and the incidence matrix; solved densely with LU.

The equations in detail, for every branch e=(a,b)e = (a, b) and every free node ii with inflow QiQ_i:

Fe=reqe|qe|ne−1−(ha−hb)=0,Gi=Qi+∑e→iqe−∑e←iqe=0.F_e = r_e\,q_e|q_e|^{n_e - 1} - (h_a - h_b) = 0, \qquad G_i = Q_i + \sum_{e\,\to\,i} q_e - \sum_{e\,\leftarrow\,i} q_e = 0 .

With the incidence matrix AA (branch by free node, −1-1 at the start, +1+1 at the end) and D=diag⁡(nere|qe|ne−1)D = \operatorname{diag}(n_e r_e |q_e|^{n_e - 1}) the Jacobian is

J=(DAAT0).J = \begin{pmatrix} D & A \\ A^T & 0 \end{pmatrix}.

At qe=0q_e = 0 with ne>1n_e > 1, DD would be singular; the diagonal entry is therefore bounded below by 10−8re10^{-8} r_e. This changes only the path, not the solution, because at the end F=0F = 0 and G=0G = 0 must hold to the tolerance. Starting with all branch flows 1, Newton then converges quadratically; for n=1n = 1 (Ohm) the system is linear and solved after one step. Without a fixed node the potential is determined only up to a constant and JJ is singular – this is reported.

Worked example. Two parallel pipes with quadratic law, r1=2r_1 = 2, r2=8r_2 = 8, withdrawal 3: equal head loss means r1Q12=r2Q22r_1 Q_1^2 = r_2 Q_2^2, so Q1/Q2=r2/r1=2Q_1/Q_2 = \sqrt{r_2/r_1} = 2, Q1=2Q_1 = 2, Q2=1Q_2 = 1, head loss 2⋅22=82 \cdot 2^2 = 8. With Ohmic branches (n=1n = 1) the same approach becomes nodal analysis of electrical networks; for the cube of twelve unit resistors it gives the known equivalent resistances 5/6, 3/4 and 7/12.

How it is checked

  • Grid with unit weights (Manhattan distance); 20 random graphs with three equal methods and recomputed paths; negative cycle detected.
  • Textbook network after Cormen et al. (flow 23 = cut 23); 30 random networks with flow = cut; bipartite matching equal to enumeration.
  • Resistor cube 5/6, 3/4, 7/12 Ω; parallel pipes Q1/Q2=r2/r1Q_1/Q_2 = \sqrt{r_2/r_1}; loop with Hazen-Williams.

Limits

Floyd-Warshall needs n2n^2 memory; Dinic with floating-point capacities works with a threshold for empty edges (taken from the finite capacities only; infinite capacities are allowed, a path made solely of infinite edges is rejected as unbounded flow); network equations dense (a few hundred nodes); branches without pump and valve characteristics (those are supplied by users such as the secondary air system or water hammer).
