Quasi-Monte Carlo, polynomial chaos and intervals – background

The computation behind Sobol, Halton, Normal, Polynomchaos, Intervall, IntervallNewton and Genau: low-discrepancy sequences, polynomial chaos with Sobol indices, outward-rounded interval arithmetic, roots with proof and numbers of arbitrary precision.

What it is about

Uncertainties propagate through models. Random samples (Monte Carlo) converge slowly like 1/N1/\sqrt N; more evenly distributed points (quasi-Monte Carlo) or a surrogate of orthogonal polynomials (polynomial chaos) reach the same accuracy with far fewer evaluations. Interval arithmetic goes the other way: it tracks not probabilities but guaranteed bounds – also for the rounding errors of the computation itself.

Sobol and Halton

Sobol points are generated after Bratley and Fox with Gray code: the next point differs from the previous one by an XOR with a direction number; the direction numbers come from the table of Joe and Kuo (2008) for the first 64 dimensions. The first 2m2^m points occupy every interval 2−m2^{-m} exactly once in each dimension. Halton points are the radical inverse of the indices in prime bases, optionally randomly shifted (Cranley-Patterson). The normal distribution with erfc⁡\operatorname{erfc} without cancellation and the quantile after Acklam with two Halley steps provide the transformation to normal inputs.

Why even points are faster. The Koksma-Hlawka inequality (from the literature, Niederreiter 1992) bounds the error of a mean over NN points by the product of the variation V(f)V(f) of the integrand and the star discrepancy of the points,

|1N∑n=1Nf(xn)−∫[0,1]dfdx|≤V(f)DN*,DN*=O((log⁡N)dN) for Sobol and Halton.\Big|\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^{N} f(x_n) - \int_{[0,1]^d} f\,dx\Big| \le V(f)\,D_N^*, \qquad D_N^* = O\Big(\frac{(\log N)^d}{N}\Big) \text{ for Sobol and Halton}.

Against 1/N1/\sqrt N for random points this is a large gain for moderate dd and smooth integrands; the factor (log⁡N)d(\log N)^d also shows why it fades in very high dimensions. The bound is not an error estimate – V(f)V(f) is rarely known.

Sobol in detail. Each dimension has 32 direction numbers vkv_k (as 32-bit fractions). The first ss are in the table, the rest follow from the primitive polynomial of degree ss with coefficients a1,…,as−1a_1, \dots, a_{s-1}:

vk=vk−s⊕vk−s2s⊕⨁q=1s−1aqvk−q.v_k = v_{k-s} \oplus \frac{v_{k-s}}{2^s} \oplus \bigoplus_{q=1}^{s-1} a_q\, v_{k-q} .

The nn-th point arises from the previous one with a single XOR (Antonov and Saleev): xn=xn−1⊕vcx_n = x_{n-1} \oplus v_c, cc the number of trailing zeros of nn in binary. This is the Gray-code order – the same points as in natural order, only reordered within each block of 2m2^m; in the first dimension the first 16 points are exactly {i/16}\{i/16\}, starting with 0,12,34,140, \tfrac12, \tfrac34, \tfrac14.

Halton takes one prime bb per dimension and mirrors the digits of the index at the point, n=∑kakbk↦φb(n)=∑kakb−k−1n = \sum_k a_k b^k \mapsto \varphi_b(n) = \sum_k a_k b^{-k-1}. Example: 6=1102↦0.0112=3/86 = 110_2 \mapsto 0.011_2 = 3/8, 5=123↦0.213=7/95 = 12_3 \mapsto 0.21_3 = 7/9, 11=215↦0.125=7/2511 = 21_5 \mapsto 0.12_5 = 7/25. For large bases the first points of two dimensions are strongly correlated; the shift helps only partly.

Normal distribution. Φ(x)=12erfc⁡(−x/2)\Phi(x) = \tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(-x/\sqrt2). For x≤2.5x \le 2.5 the series erf⁡x=2πe−x2∑n≥02nx2n+11⋅3⋯(2n+1)\operatorname{erf} x = \frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}e^{-x^2}\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{2^n x^{2n+1}}{1\cdot3\cdots(2n+1)} (positive terms only, no cancellation), above it the continued fraction

erfc⁡x=e−x2π⋅1x+1/2x+1x+3/2x+⋯,\operatorname{erfc} x = \frac{e^{-x^2}}{\sqrt\pi}\cdot\cfrac{1}{x + \cfrac{1/2}{x + \cfrac{1}{x + \cfrac{3/2}{x + \cdots}}}},

evaluated after Lentz. So erfc⁡\operatorname{erfc} stays relatively accurate far out in the tail, where 1−erf⁡1 - \operatorname{erf} would be nothing but rounding error.

The quantile starts from Acklam's rational approximation and is improved with two Halley steps on Φ(x)−p=0\Phi(x) - p = 0; with u=(Φ(x)−p)/φ(x)u = (\Phi(x) - p)/\varphi(x) and φ'=−xφ\varphi' = -x\varphi the step is

x←x−u1+xu/2.x \leftarrow x - \frac{u}{1 + x u/2}.

For p>12p > \tfrac12 the difference is formed as (1−p)−Q(x)(1 - p) - Q(x) so that it does not cancel in the upper tail.

Polynomial chaos and Sobol indices

A model Y=f(ξ)Y = f(\xi) with independent inputs is expanded in orthogonal polynomials – Hermite for normal inputs (norm k!k!), Legendre for uniform ones (norm 1/(2k+1)1/(2k+1)) – up to total degree pp:

Y≈∑αcαΨα(ξ),E[Y]=c0,Var⁡Y=∑α≠0cα2‖Ψα‖2.Y \approx \sum_\alpha c_\alpha\,\Psi_\alpha(\xi), \qquad E[Y] = c_0, \qquad \operatorname{Var}Y = \sum_{\alpha\ne0}c_\alpha^2\|\Psi_\alpha\|^2.

The polynomials come from their three-term recurrences,

Hek+1(x)=xHek(x)−kHek−1(x),(k+1)Pk+1(x)=(2k+1)xPk(x)−kPk−1(x),He_{k+1}(x) = x\,He_k(x) - k\,He_{k-1}(x), \qquad (k + 1)\,P_{k+1}(x) = (2k + 1)\,x\,P_k(x) - k\,P_{k-1}(x),

with E[Hek2]=k!E[He_k^2] = k! under the standard normal distribution and E[Pk2]=1/(2k+1)E[P_k^2] = 1/(2k + 1) under the uniform distribution on [−1,1][-1, 1]. The basis of all products up to total degree pp in dd variables has (d+pp)\binom{d + p}{p} terms – for d=3d = 3, p=12p = 12 that is 455.

The coefficients come from a regression on samples (QR). The first-order Sobol indices SiS_i sum the variance contributions of terms containing only ξi\xi_i, the total ones ST,iS_{T,i} all terms with ξi\xi_i (Sudret 2008) – without further model evaluations.

Si=∑α∈Aicα2‖Ψα‖2Var⁡Y,Ai={α:αi>0,αj=0(j≠i)},ST,i=∑αi>0cα2‖Ψα‖2Var⁡Y.S_i = \frac{\sum_{\alpha \in A_i} c_\alpha^2\|\Psi_\alpha\|^2}{\operatorname{Var}Y}, \quad A_i = \{\alpha:\ \alpha_i > 0,\ \alpha_j = 0\ (j \ne i)\}, \qquad S_{T,i} = \frac{\sum_{\alpha_i > 0} c_\alpha^2\|\Psi_\alpha\|^2}{\operatorname{Var}Y}.

Why this holds: the basis is orthogonal, so the variance splits into the sum of the terms, and each term belongs to exactly the set of variables whose exponents are non-zero – this is the Hoeffding-Sobol decomposition, counted instead of integrated.

V1=12(1+bπ45)2,V2=a28,V13=b2π8(118−150),V_1 = \tfrac12\Big(1 + \frac{b\pi^4}{5}\Big)^2, \quad V_2 = \frac{a^2}{8}, \quad V_{13} = b^2\pi^8\Big(\frac{1}{18} - \frac{1}{50}\Big),

so S1=0.3139S_1 = 0.3139, S2=0.4424S_2 = 0.4424, S3=0S_3 = 0, but ST,3=0.2437S_{T,3} = 0.2437: ξ3\xi_3 acts only through its interaction with ξ1\xi_1. The expansion in Legendre polynomials up to degree 12 from 3000 Sobol points hits exactly these numbers to 10−410^{-4}.

Interval arithmetic and interval Newton

Each basic operation is carried out on the bounds and the result is rounded outwards by one ulp (nextDown/nextUp; two steps for library functions), so that the true value stays enclosed; division by an interval containing 0 gives the whole line, sin and cos take interior extrema into account.

[a,b]+[c,d]=[down⁡(a+c),up⁡(b+d)],[a,b]⋅[c,d]=[down⁡(min⁡P),up⁡(max⁡P)],P={ac,ad,bc,bd},[a, b] + [c, d] = [\operatorname{down}(a + c),\ \operatorname{up}(b + d)], \qquad [a, b]\cdot[c, d] = [\operatorname{down}(\min P),\ \operatorname{up}(\max P)], \quad P = \{ac, ad, bc, bd\},

with down⁡\operatorname{down}, up⁡\operatorname{up} one ulp down and up (nextDown, nextUp). This suffices because Java rounds the basic operations correctly (error at most half an ulp); for StrictMath functions with at most one ulp of error it takes two steps. This gives the inclusion property: from x∈Xx \in X, y∈Yy \in Y follows x∘y∈X∘Yx \circ y \in X \circ Y – for every computation made only of such steps. The price is overestimation with dependencies: X−XX - X is not [0,0][0, 0] but twice as wide as XX.

An input like 0.1 must be enclosed as well: von("0.1") compares the nearest double with the decimal value and widens by one ulp on the correct side.

Rump's example shows what this is good for: f=333.75b6+a2(11a2b2−b6−121b4−2)+5.5b8+a/(2b)f = 333.75\,b^6 + a^2(11a^2b^2 - b^6 - 121b^4 - 2) + 5.5\,b^8 + a/(2b) with a=77617a = 77617, b=33096b = 33096. The polynomial part is exactly −2-2, so f=−2+a/(2b)=−0.8273960599…f = -2 + a/(2b) = -0.8273960599\ldots; in double precision summands of size 103610^{36} cancel and the result is off by more than 1 without any sign of it. The interval becomes huge in the process – and thereby shows that the result is not to be trusted.

The interval Newton operator

N(X)=m−f(m)F'(X),m=mid⁡(X),N(X) = m - \frac{f(m)}{F'(X)}, \qquad m = \operatorname{mid}(X),

proves: if N(X)N(X) lies in the interior of XX, ff has exactly one root there; if N(X)∩XN(X)\cap X is empty, none. Where F'(X)F'(X) contains 0, the interval is bisected. Derivation from the mean value theorem: if x*∈Xx^* \in X is a root, then 0=f(m)+f'(ξ)(x*−m)0 = f(m) + f'(\xi)(x^* - m) for some ξ∈X\xi \in X, so x*=m−f(m)/f'(ξ)∈N(X)x^* = m - f(m)/f'(\xi) \in N(X) – every root in XX also lies in N(X)N(X), and X∩N(X)X \cap N(X) loses none. If N(X)N(X) lies even in the interior of XX, existence and uniqueness follow (Moore et al.). Because f(m)f(m) itself is computed as an interval, the proof is safe against rounding too. Step by step XX shrinks quadratically as long as F'(X)F'(X) does not contain 0 (ibid.).

Genau computes with BigDecimal and 15 guard digits: π\pi after Machin and after Gauss-Legendre (AGM),

π4=4arctan⁡15−arctan⁡1239,π≈(ak+bk)24tk,\frac\pi4 = 4\arctan\frac15 - \arctan\frac1{239}, \qquad \pi \approx \frac{(a_k + b_k)^2}{4\,t_k},

the first with the arctangent series (about 1.4 digits per term at 1/51/5), the second with quadratic convergence (the number of correct digits doubles per step). Computing both independently and comparing them to 300 digits is the probe. The logarithm is reduced to r∈[1,2]r \in [1, 2], ln⁡x=2artanh⁡r−1r+1+kln⁡2\ln x = 2\operatorname{artanh}\frac{r-1}{r+1} + k\ln 2, with ln⁡2=2artanh⁡13\ln 2 = 2\operatorname{artanh}\frac13; exp halves the argument until |r|≤12|r| \le \tfrac12 and squares back; sin and cos reduce modulo 2π2\pi with extra digits for the digits before the point.

How it is checked

  • Sobol: the first points by hand, in all 64 dimensions each interval 1/40961/4096 exactly once; integral in 8 dimensions with an error below 1/20 of the Monte Carlo error.
  • Polynomial chaos on the Ishigami function: mean, variance and S=(0.3139;0.4424;0)S = (0.3139;\ 0.4424;\ 0), ST=(0.5576;0.4424;0.2437)S_T = (0.5576;\ 0.4424;\ 0.2437) to 1e-4; lognormal with Hermite coefficients e1/2/k!e^{1/2}/k!.
  • Intervals: 5000 pairs against exact BigDecimal computation; Rump's example – double is off by more than 1, the interval encloses the true value; √2 proven, three roots of cos on [0, 10]; π after Machin equal to AGM to 300 digits.

Limits

Sobol up to 64 dimensions, at most 2322^{32} points; the Koksma-Hlawka bound is not an error estimate; polynomial chaos for independent inputs, the number of terms grows like (d+pp)\binom{d + p}{p}; intervals overestimate with dependencies (the “dependency problem”), interval Newton one-dimensional only.
