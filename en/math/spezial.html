Special functions – background

The computation behind Gamma, Bessel, Elliptisch, LambertW and Orthogonal: the Lanczos approximation and the series of 1/Γ1/\Gamma, Bessel functions after Temme and Steed with Hankel's expansion and zeros, elliptic integrals after Carlson, the Lambert W function at the branch point and the classical orthogonal polynomials – with the checks that hold them against closed forms and tables, and their limits.

What it is about

Bessel functions appear in every calculation with cylindrical symmetry: vibrations of a circular plate, heat conduction in a pipe or shaft, the Kaiser window of signal processing. Elliptic integrals give the period of a large pendulum, the length of an ellipse and the inductance of a circular coil. The Lambert W function solves x=wewx = w\,e^w and turns up in diode equations, delay equations and combustion models. Orthogonal polynomials are the building blocks of Gauss quadrature, spectral methods and polynomial chaos.

All five classes live in envisionz.math.spezial and compute in double only, without any third-party library. Before, the gamma function lived in framework-stats and there were four separate versions of the Bessel function I0I_0 for Kaiser windows (regelung, restore, audiometrik, audio); they now call these classes and compute bit-identically to before.

Gamma function

The gamma function extends the factorial, Γ(n)=(n−1)!\Gamma(n) = (n-1)!. For x≥1/2x \ge 1/2 the class computes the Lanczos approximation with g=607/128g = 607/128 and 15 terms:

ln⁡Γ(x)=12ln⁡(2π)+(x−12)ln⁡t−t+ln⁡(c0+∑k=114ckx+k−1),t=x+g−12.\ln\Gamma(x) = \tfrac12\ln(2\pi) + (x - \tfrac12)\ln t - t + \ln\Big(c_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{14}\frac{c_k}{x + k - 1}\Big), \qquad t = x + g - \tfrac12.

For x<1/2x < 1/2 the reflection Γ(x)Γ(1−x)=π/sin⁡(πx)\Gamma(x)\,\Gamma(1-x) = \pi/\sin(\pi x) applies; at the poles 0,−1,−2,…0, -1, -2, \dots the result is NaN. The code moved character by character from envisionz.stats.core.SpecialFunctions, because math now needs the gamma function itself and stats depends on math, not the other way round; SpecialFunctions now calls it here.

The series of 1/Γ1/\Gamma. The Bessel functions need 1/Γ(1±μ)1/\Gamma(1 \pm \mu) for |μ|≤1/2|\mu| \le 1/2, and above all the difference of the two, which would cancel for small μ\mu. With 1/Γ(z)=∑k≥1ckzk1/\Gamma(z) = \sum_{k\ge1} c_k z^k (Abramowitz and Stegun 6.1.34, 26 coefficients), 1/Γ(1+μ)=∑ckμk−11/\Gamma(1+\mu) = \sum c_k \mu^{k-1}, and Temme's auxiliary quantities

γ1=12μ(1Γ(1−μ)−1Γ(1+μ)),γ2=12(1Γ(1−μ)+1Γ(1+μ))\gamma_1 = \frac{1}{2\mu}\Big(\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\mu)} - \frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\mu)}\Big), \qquad \gamma_2 = \frac12\Big(\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\mu)} + \frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\mu)}\Big)

consist exactly of the odd and even powers respectively – the code sums them separately with Horner in μ2\mu^2, never forming a difference. In particular γ1(0)=−c2=−γE\gamma_1(0) = -c_2 = -\gamma_E (Euler's constant).

Bessel functions

JνJ_\nu and YνY_\nu solve x2y''+xy'+(x2−ν2)y=0x^2 y'' + x y' + (x^2 - \nu^2) y = 0, IνI_\nu and KνK_\nu the modified equation with −x2-x^2. For real order ν≥0\nu \ge 0 and x>0x > 0 the code follows Temme and Steed (Numerical Recipes, bessjy and bessik) in four steps:

  1. Continued fraction CF1 for the ratio fν=Jν'/Jνf_\nu = J_\nu'/J_\nu, evaluated with the modified Lentz method; it converges after about xx terms.
  2. Backward recurrence from an unnormalised start value at ν\nu down to μ=ν−n\mu = \nu - n, |μ|≤1/2|\mu| \le 1/2: Jν−1=νxJν+Jν'J_{\nu-1} = \frac{\nu}{x} J_\nu + J_\nu', Jν−1'=ν−1xJν−1−JνJ_{\nu-1}' = \frac{\nu-1}{x}J_{\nu-1} - J_\nu – stable in this direction.
  3. At μ\mu: for x<2x < 2 Temme's series gives YμY_\mu and Yμ+1Y_{\mu+1} (with γ1,γ2\gamma_1, \gamma_2 from above), for x≥2x \ge 2 Steed's complex continued fraction CF2 gives the ratio p+iq=(Jμ'+iYμ')/(Jμ+iYμ)p + iq = (J_\mu' + iY_\mu')/(J_\mu + iY_\mu). The Wronskian JμYμ'−Jμ'Yμ=2πxJ_\mu Y_\mu' - J_\mu' Y_\mu = \frac{2}{\pi x} then fixes the normalisation: Jμ=±W/((p−f)γ+q)J_\mu = \pm\sqrt{W/((p - f)\gamma + q)} with γ=(p−f)/q\gamma = (p - f)/q, W=2/(πx)W = 2/(\pi x).
  4. Forward recurrence for YY from μ\mu back to ν\nu (forward is the stable direction for YY); JJ and J'J' follow from the normalisation factor.

For II and KK the pattern is the same: CF1 for I'/II'/I, Temme's series for Kμ,Kμ+1K_\mu, K_{\mu+1} when x<2x < 2, otherwise Steed's continued fraction for KK; the normalisation uses IνKν'−Iν'Kν=−1/xI_\nu K_\nu' - I_\nu' K_\nu = -1/x. II overflows where exe^x does (xx around 700), KK underflows there.

Hankel's expansion for large xx. CF1 needs about xx steps and loses digits on the way (already 10−1310^{-13} at xx around 1000). Above x>max⁡(25,ν2/2)x > \max(25, \nu^2/2) the class therefore computes asymptotically (Abramowitz and Stegun 9.2.5 ff.):

Jν=2πx(Pcos⁡χ−Qsin⁡χ),Yν=2πx(Psin⁡χ+Qcos⁡χ),χ=x−(ν2+14)π,J_\nu = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\,(P\cos\chi - Q\sin\chi), \quad Y_\nu = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\,(P\sin\chi + Q\cos\chi), \quad \chi = x - \Big(\frac{\nu}{2} + \frac14\Big)\pi,

with P=∑(−1)ka2k/x2kP = \sum (-1)^k a_{2k}/x^{2k}, Q=∑(−1)ka2k+1/x2k+1Q = \sum (-1)^k a_{2k+1}/x^{2k+1}, ak=∏j=1k(4ν2−(2j−1)2)/(k!8k)a_k = \prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(4\nu^2 - (2j-1)^2\big)/(k!\,8^k). Above the boundary the terms fall at least like 1/k!1/k! down to the smallest, which lies below e−2xe^{-2x}. cos⁡χ\cos\chi and sin⁡χ\sin\chi come from the addition theorems, so that xx itself is not rounded by a subtraction. The derivative follows from Jν'=νxJν−Jν+1J_\nu' = \frac{\nu}{x}J_\nu - J_{\nu+1}.

I0I_0 for Kaiser windows. i0(x) is the power series I0(x)=∑k((x/2)k/k!)2I_0(x) = \sum_k \big((x/2)^k/k!\big)^2 in exactly the form in which the windows of the workspace compute: t←t(x/(2k))2t \leftarrow t\,(x/(2k))^2, stopping when the term falls below 10−1710^{-17} of the sum. That is why regelung (Filterentwurf), restore (Vbm3d), audiometrik (Fenster) and audio (AacFilterbank via Fenster) could drop their copies bit-identically. Fensterform in audiocodec deliberately keeps its own: its series is part of the EVAS file format, and a change in the last digit would decode existing files differently.

Zeros of JνJ_\nu and Jν'J_\nu'

The positive zeros jν,kj_{\nu,k} are separated, almost π\pi apart, and interlace with those of the derivative: ν≤j'ν,1<jν,1<j'ν,2<jν,2<…\nu \le j'_{\nu,1} < j_{\nu,1} < j'_{\nu,2} < j_{\nu,2} < \dots The code scans from the start of the range in steps of 1/41/4 for sign changes and refines each bracketed zero with a Newton iteration that never leaves the bracket (bisection otherwise). For J0'J_0' the zero at x=0x = 0 is not counted, as usual, so that j'0,1=j1,1j'_{0,1} = j_{1,1}.

Elliptic integrals after Carlson

Carlson replaces the Legendre forms by symmetric integrals, for example

RF(x,y,z)=12∫0∞dt(t+x)(t+y)(t+z),RD(x,y,z)=32∫0∞dt(t+z)(t+x)(t+y)(t+z),R_F(x,y,z) = \frac12\int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{\sqrt{(t+x)(t+y)(t+z)}}, \qquad R_D(x,y,z) = \frac32\int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{(t+z)\sqrt{(t+x)(t+y)(t+z)}},

plus RJR_J with a fourth argument pp and RC(x,y)=RF(x,y,y)R_C(x,y) = R_F(x,y,y). Their symmetry allows duplication: with λ=xy+yz+zx\lambda = \sqrt{xy} + \sqrt{yz} + \sqrt{zx}

RF(x,y,z)=RF(x+λ4,y+λ4,z+λ4).R_F(x,y,z) = R_F\Big(\frac{x+\lambda}{4}, \frac{y+\lambda}{4}, \frac{z+\lambda}{4}\Big).

Each step quarters the distances of the arguments from their mean AA. Once they are below 4−nQ4^{-n}Q, a Taylor series in the elementary symmetric functions E2,E3E_2, E_3 of the relative deviations replaces the rest, for RFR_F for instance

RF≈A−1/2(1−E210+E314+E2224−3E2E344).R_F \approx A^{-1/2}\Big(1 - \frac{E_2}{10} + \frac{E_3}{14} + \frac{E_2^2}{24} - \frac{3E_2E_3}{44}\Big).

For RDR_D and RJR_J each step also accumulates a term 4−m/(z(z+λ))4^{-m}/(\sqrt z\,(z + \lambda)) or an RCR_C term respectively. The error of the series is of order Q6Q^6 times the stopping bound r=10−16r = 10^{-16} (Carlson 1995).

Legendre forms. With modulus kk, s=sin⁡φs = \sin\varphi, c=cos⁡φc = \cos\varphi, d2=1−k2s2d^2 = 1 - k^2 s^2 and the characteristic nn in the form of Abramowitz and Stegun:

F(φ,k)=sRF(c2,d2,1),E(φ,k)=F(φ,k)−k2s33RD(c2,d2,1),F(\varphi,k) = s\,R_F(c^2, d^2, 1), \qquad E(\varphi,k) = F(\varphi,k) - \frac{k^2 s^3}{3}\,R_D(c^2, d^2, 1),

Π(n,φ,k)=F(φ,k)+ns33RJ(c2,d2,1,1−ns2).\Pi(n,\varphi,k) = F(\varphi,k) + \frac{n\,s^3}{3}\,R_J(c^2, d^2, 1, 1 - n s^2).

The complete integrals are the case φ=π/2\varphi = \pi/2: K=RF(0,1−k2,1)K = R_F(0, 1-k^2, 1), E=K−k23RD(0,1−k2,1)E = K - \frac{k^2}{3}R_D(0, 1-k^2, 1). For |φ|>π/2|\varphi| > \pi/2 the amplitude is reduced to [−π/2,π/2][-\pi/2, \pi/2]: F(φ+jπ)=F(φ)+2jKF(\varphi + j\pi) = F(\varphi) + 2jK, likewise for EE and Π\Pi. RC(1,1+e)R_C(1, 1+e) has its own series 1−e/3+e2/5−…1 - e/3 + e^2/5 - \dots for |e|<10−3|e| < 10^{-3}, because duplication would suffer there from cancellation in the deviations.

Independently of Carlson, the check tests the Legendre relation EK'+E'K−KK'=π/2EK' + E'K - KK' = \pi/2, the arithmetic-geometric mean K=π/(2AGM⁡(1,k'))K = \pi/(2\,\mathrm{AGM}(1, k')) and the incomplete forms against Gauss-Legendre.

Lambert W function

WW is the inverse of weww\,e^w. On the reals it has two branches: W0≥−1W_0 \ge -1 for x≥−1/ex \ge -1/e and W−1≤−1W_{-1} \le -1 for −1/e≤x<0-1/e \le x < 0; they meet at the branch point x=−1/ex = -1/e, W=−1W = -1. There WW has a square-root singularity, and Newton-type iterations converge poorly. Corless et al. (1996) therefore expand in p=±2(ex+1)p = \pm\sqrt{2(ex + 1)} (plus for W0W_0, minus for W−1W_{-1}):

W=−1+p−p23+1172p3−43540p4+76917280p5−…W = -1 + p - \frac{p^2}{3} + \frac{11}{72}p^3 - \frac{43}{540}p^4 + \frac{769}{17280}p^5 - \dots

The class takes ten terms (up to p9p^9); below p=10−3p = 10^{-3} the series is already accurate to rounding and is used without iteration. Otherwise it – or ln⁡x−ln⁡ln⁡x\ln x - \ln\ln x for large xx, ln⁡(−x)−ln⁡(−ln⁡(−x))\ln(-x) - \ln(-\ln(-x)) for W−1W_{-1} near 0, ln⁡(1+x)\ln(1+x) in between – serves as the start value for Halley's method:

w←w−wew−xew(w+1)−(w+2)(wew−x)2w+2,w \leftarrow w - \frac{w e^w - x}{e^w(w+1) - \dfrac{(w+2)(w e^w - x)}{2w + 2}},

stopped as soon as ww changes by at most 4 ulp.

1/e1/e as the sum of two doubles. The quantity ex+1ex + 1 arises from cancellation: near the branch point x+1/ex + 1/e is tiny, and the rounding error of 1/e1/e alone (1.2⋅10−171.2 \cdot 10^{-17}) costs half the digits of pp. The code holds 1/e1/e as a main part plus a remainder and forms (x+1/emain)+1/erest(x + 1/e_\text{main}) + 1/e_\text{rest}. The double −1/e-1/e itself lies 1.2⋅10−171.2 \cdot 10^{-17} below the true value; it stands for the branch point and gives W=−1W = -1 on both branches.

The derivative follows from the inverse rule: W'(x)=W/(x(1+W))W'(x) = W/\big(x(1 + W)\big).

  • Elliptischprobe (17 checks, LV82-01 … 09): Carlson's own test values; homogeneity, equal-argument cases and the RDR_D sum formula at 300 random points; K(1/2)=Γ(1/4)2/(4π)K(1/\sqrt2) = \Gamma(1/4)^2/(4\sqrt\pi); Legendre relation; AGM; incomplete forms against Gauss-Legendre up to φ=4\varphi = 4, k=0.999k = 0.999; periodicity; the limiting cases k=0k = 0, k=1k = 1, n=0n = 0.
  • Orthogonalprobe (18 checks, LV83-01 … 09): WW at closed-form values (omega constant, W(e)=1W(e) = 1, W(−ln⁡2/2)W(-\ln 2/2) on both branches); W(tet)=tW(te^t) = t within the condition bound from −0.9999-0.9999 to 700 and from −1.0001-1.0001 to −700-700; 3000 ulp around −1/e-1/e against Newton with 50 digits; polynomials in closed form, orthogonality and norms with Gauss quadratures; derivatives against independent identities.
  • Bit-identical: the outputs of the checks of stats, regelung, restore, audiometrik and audio, and a hash over window values and polynomial chaos before and after the switch.
  • 43 mutation tests lv81-*, lv82-*, lv83-* (15 of them on WW and the polynomials) and lv7-legendre, all red. One mutation test was at first an equivalent mutant (Newton instead of Halley changes nothing with a 4 ulp stop) and was replaced by an effective one.

Limits

  • Bessel: only real order 0≤ν≤10000 \le \nu \le 1000 and x>0x > 0; no complex arguments, no Hankel functions H(1,2)H^{(1,2)} as a quantity of their own, no spherical Bessel functions (reachable via ν+1/2\nu + 1/2). Underflow and overflow give 0 and infinity.
  • Elliptic: Π\Pi only for 1−nsin2⁡φ>01 - n\sin^2\varphi > 0; the Cauchy principal value for n>1n > 1 is missing. No Jacobi elliptic functions sn⁡,cn⁡,dn⁡\mathrm{sn}, \mathrm{cn}, \mathrm{dn}.
  • Lambert W: only the two real branches.
  • Orthogonal polynomials: degree up to 10510^5; outside the orthogonality interval the values grow fast and overflow. No nodes and weights of the corresponding Gauss rules (Gauss-Legendre is in quadratur).
  • No incomplete gamma function here – it stays in framework-stats.
