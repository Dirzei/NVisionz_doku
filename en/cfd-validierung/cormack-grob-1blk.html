Shock reflection in a channel with a wedge

Inviscid supersonic flow (Mach 2) in a channel with a 3° wedge at the upper wall; its oblique shock is reflected regularly at the lower wall. Coarse mesh.

What the case shows

Purpose

The case checks the regular reflection of an oblique shock at a plane wall at Mach 2.000. The incident shock starts at a 3° wedge at the upper wall (wedge corner at x = 0.30, y = 0.9472, measured on the mesh). The incident shock meets the lower wall at xr=1.786x_r = 1.786 and is reflected there. The check is whether the solver captures both shocks and gets the wall pressure behind the reflection.

Theory of the reference

The incident shock turns the flow by θ=3.000∘\theta = 3.000^\circ, its shock angle is β=32.51∘\beta = 32.51^\circ. The wall turns the flow back by the same angle; this needs a second, reflected shock. It obeys the same shock relations as the first (see wedge), but with the Mach number M2M_2 behind the first shock as its approach flow. With Mn=Msin⁡βM_n = M \sin\beta, for each of the two shocks

pbehind⁡pahead⁡=1+2γγ+1(Mn2−1),Mbehind⁡=Mn,behind⁡sin⁡(β−θ)\frac{p_\mathrm{behind}}{p_\mathrm{ahead}} = 1 + \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(M_n^2 - 1\right), \qquad M_\mathrm{behind} = \frac{M_{n,\mathrm{behind}}}{\sin(\beta - \theta)}

with the respective shock angle from the θ\theta-β\beta-MM relation. In sequence this gives p2/p1=1.181p_2/p_1 = 1.181 and M2=1.892M_2 = 1.892 behind the incident shock, p3/p2=1.174p_3/p_2 = 1.174 and M3=1.787M_3 = 1.787 behind the reflected one, and for the wall pressure behind the reflection

p3p1=p2p1⋅p3p2=1.387\frac{p_3}{p_1} = \frac{p_2}{p_1}\cdot\frac{p_3}{p_2} = 1.387

The incident shock starts at (x0,y0)(x_0, y_0) and meets the wall at

xr=x0+y0tan⁡βx_r = x_0 + \frac{y_0}{\tan\beta}

The reference for the wall pressure is therefore a step: p/p∞=1p/p_\infty = 1 for \(x < x_r\) and \(p_3/p_1\) behind it.

What to look for in the picture

The result view shows the pressure: the incident shock runs obliquely to the wall, the reflected one obliquely away from it; together they form a V. Behind the reflected shock the pressure plateau stands. In the plot the wall pressure is 1 ahead of xr=1.786x_r = 1.786 and rises behind it to p3/p1=1.387p_3/p_1 = 1.387; the dots are the step of the theory. The table compares the plateau of the computation (median behind the reflection) with the theory: 1.387 against 1.387.

Limits of the comparison

  • In the scheme the step is smeared over a few cells. The dots of the theory therefore leave out the two neighbours of the jump; the largest deviation 0.1570.157 is at x=1.82x = 1.82, that is at the reflection (xr=1.786x_r = 1.786), and contributes substantially to the RMS deviation of 0.0298.
  • The comparison runs up to x = 3.00: from the wedge corner at x = 3.1 expansion waves enter, which the reference does not know.
  • Only the pressure at the wall is compared, not the field. The gas is ideal with γ=1.40\gamma = 1.40, the flow inviscid.

Result view

Validation

The reference is the theory of regular reflection (analytical, R84): wall pressure p/p∞ equal to 1 ahead of the reflection and p₃/p₁ behind it (dots; the neighbours of the jump are left out). The wall pressure of the lower wall is compared.
