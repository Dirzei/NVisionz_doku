Shock reflection, mesh 321 × 81

Regular reflection of an oblique shock (Mach 2.9, shock angle 29°) at a plane wall, a test case for shock reflection, finer mesh.

Case summary

Result of the runconverged after 58 steps, relative residual 1.2·10−9

What the case shows

Purpose

The case checks the regular reflection of an oblique shock at a plane wall at Mach 2.900. The upper boundary carries the state behind the incident shock (Mach 2.378); the shock starts at x₀ = 0, y₀ = 1. The incident shock meets the lower wall at xr=1.804x_r = 1.804 and is reflected there. The check is whether the solver captures both shocks and gets the wall pressure behind the reflection.

Theory of the reference

The incident shock turns the flow by θ=10.94∘\theta = 10.94^\circ, its shock angle is β=29.00∘\beta = 29.00^\circ. The wall turns the flow back by the same angle; this needs a second, reflected shock. It obeys the same shock relations as the first (see wedge), but with the Mach number M2M_2 behind the first shock as its approach flow. With Mn=Msin⁡βM_n = M \sin\beta, for each of the two shocks

pbehind⁡pahead⁡=1+2γγ+1(Mn2−1),Mbehind⁡=Mn,behind⁡sin⁡(β−θ)\frac{p_\mathrm{behind}}{p_\mathrm{ahead}} = 1 + \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(M_n^2 - 1\right), \qquad M_\mathrm{behind} = \frac{M_{n,\mathrm{behind}}}{\sin(\beta - \theta)}

with the respective shock angle from the θ\theta-β\beta-MM relation. In sequence this gives p2/p1=2.139p_2/p_1 = 2.139 and M2=2.378M_2 = 2.378 behind the incident shock, p3/p2=1.920p_3/p_2 = 1.920 and M3=1.942M_3 = 1.942 behind the reflected one, and for the wall pressure behind the reflection

p3p1=p2p1⋅p3p2=4.108\frac{p_3}{p_1} = \frac{p_2}{p_1}\cdot\frac{p_3}{p_2} = 4.108

The incident shock starts at (x0,y0)(x_0, y_0) and meets the wall at

xr=x0+y0tan⁡βx_r = x_0 + \frac{y_0}{\tan\beta}

The reference for the wall pressure is therefore a step: p/p∞=1p/p_\infty = 1 for \(x < x_r\) and \(p_3/p_1\) behind it.

What to look for in the picture

The result view shows the pressure: the incident shock runs obliquely to the wall, the reflected one obliquely away from it; together they form a V. Behind the reflected shock the pressure plateau stands. In the plot the wall pressure is 1 ahead of xr=1.804x_r = 1.804 and rises behind it to p3/p1=4.108p_3/p_1 = 4.108; the dots are the step of the theory. The table compares the plateau of the computation (median behind the reflection) with the theory: 4.108 against 4.108.

Limits of the comparison

  • In the scheme the step is smeared over a few cells. The dots of the theory therefore leave out the two neighbours of the jump; the largest deviation 1.681.68 is at x=1.81x = 1.81, that is at the reflection (xr=1.804x_r = 1.804), and contributes substantially to the RMS deviation of 0.130.
  • The whole wall is compared.
  • Only the pressure at the wall is compared, not the field. The gas is ideal with γ=1.40\gamma = 1.40, the flow inviscid.
