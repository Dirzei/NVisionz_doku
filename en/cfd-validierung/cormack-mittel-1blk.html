Shock meets a laminar boundary layer

Supersonic flow (Mach 2) in a channel with a 3° wedge at the upper wall; its oblique shock meets the laminar boundary layer of the lower wall. The wall pressure is compared with a measurement.

Case summary

Result of the runconverged after 48 steps, relative residual 6.9·10−9

What the case shows

Purpose

The case tests the viscous computation on a flow in which an oblique shock meets a laminar boundary layer (Mach 2.000, mesh with 16384 cells). The wall pressure along the plate is compared with a wind-tunnel measurement (R92).

Theory of the reference

The reference is a measurement, not a computation: the pressure at taps in the plate, referred to the stagnation pressure p0p_0 of the inflow. Ahead of the shock the undisturbed flow obeys the isentropic relation

pp0=(1+γ−12M2)−γ/(γ−1),\frac{p}{p_0} = \left(1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2}\,M^2\right)^{-\gamma/(\gamma-1)},

which gives 0.1278 at Mach 2 and γ=1.4\gamma = 1.4. In inviscid flow the wall pressure would jump at the impingement point to the value behind the reflected shock. With a boundary layer this is different: near the wall the flow is slower than sound, and the pressure rise acts upstream there. The rise at the wall is therefore spread over a distance ahead of and behind the impingement point; a laminar boundary layer may separate in the process.

The measurement gives the running length in inches from the leading edge of the plate; it is converted to the length unit of the mesh with the Reynolds number per length. The shock meets the plate at a Reynolds number of 2.96⋅1052.96\cdot 10^5, and the final pressure is 1.40 times the pressure ahead of it.

What the picture should show

The result view shows the pressure: the shock runs from the wedge at the upper wall obliquely to the plate and is reflected there. In the diagram the wall pressure does not rise in a jump but over a distance. Line (computation) and dots (measurement) differ by an RMS of 0.00992, with the largest deviation 0.0236 at x=3.49x = 3.49.

Limits of the comparison

  • Already ahead of the shock the measurement lies below the isentropic value for Mach 2 (0.1235 against 0.1278); the computation is therefore higher by about 0.005 over the whole length.
  • The measured points are read from the printed figure; the reading error is about 0.001 in the pressure ratio.
  • At the channel outlet the back pressure is prescribed. In the boundary layer the flow is subsonic there; the setting acts back on the rear part of the plate.
  • The tolerance of 0.0150 is 1.5 times the measured RMS deviation (a guard against regressions), not a measure of accuracy.
