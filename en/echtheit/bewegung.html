Geometry and time – background

The computation behind Epipolarspur and Zeitspur: tracks from optical flow, fundamental matrix or homography, a robust outlier threshold, a clustering test per tile, the rigidity check, plus jitter, break and flicker against the recording's normal level – with derivations, pseudocode following the source, a worked example, testing and limits.

What this is about

What a camera records of a rigid scene is geometrically constrained: two views of the same scene are linked by a single matrix, and what happens from frame to frame changes only smoothly. An inserted object, or one deformed from frame to frame, does not obey this. So both traces measure not the object but the departure from the model – and only where it clusters.

Correspondences from optical flow

From each frame ii, up to 4000 corners are found and carried forward frame by frame with the pyramidal flow after Lucas and Kanade:

xs+1=xs+ds(xs),s=0,…,a−1,a=3.\mathbf{x}_{s+1} = \mathbf{x}_s + \mathbf{d}_s(\mathbf{x}_s), \qquad s = 0, \dots, a - 1, \quad a = 3.

A track ends when it leaves the image or the reliability of the flow at the rounded location falls below 0.2. The flow is computed in blocks the size of the number of cores and released after use, so that long films fit in memory. Matching deliberately uses flow rather than descriptors: during development a deformation of 2.5 pixels cost 90 % of the descriptor matches – exactly at the location to be measured. A pair with fewer than 40 correspondences is not usable; if fewer than half the pairs are usable, the trace cannot be evaluated.

The model of camera geometry

For a rigid scene, corresponding points in two views satisfy the epipolar constraint x2⊤Fx1=0\mathbf{x}_2^{\top} F\, \mathbf{x}_1 = 0 with the 3×33 \times 3 fundamental matrix FF of rank 2. The measure is the Sampson distance, the first-order solution to the problem of moving the four coordinates as little as possible so that the constraint holds:

dS2=(x2⊤Fx1)2(Fx1)12+(Fx1)22+(F⊤x2)12+(F⊤x2)22.d_S^2 = \frac{(\mathbf{x}_2^{\top} F \mathbf{x}_1)^2}{(F\mathbf{x}_1)_1^2 + (F\mathbf{x}_1)_2^2 + (F^{\top}\mathbf{x}_2)_1^2 + (F^{\top}\mathbf{x}_2)_2^2}.

Derivation (derived). Let c(X)=x2⊤Fx1c(\mathbf{X}) = \mathbf{x}_2^{\top} F \mathbf{x}_1 with X=(x1,y1,x2,y2)\mathbf{X} = (x_1, y_1, x_2, y_2). We seek the smallest e\mathbf{e} with c(X+e)=0c(\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{e}) = 0. Linearised: c+Je=0c + J\mathbf{e} = 0 with J=∂c/∂XJ = \partial c / \partial \mathbf{X}. The smallest e\mathbf{e} is −J⊤(JJ⊤)−1c-J^{\top}(JJ^{\top})^{-1} c, its squared length c2/(JJ⊤)c^2 / (JJ^{\top}). The four entries of JJ are exactly the terms of the denominator. So the distance measures how far all four coordinates together would have to move – in pixels.

If the camera is static, only pans, or the scene is planar, parallax is missing and FF is undetermined. A homography HH then describes the mapping, measured by the transfer error ‖Hx1−x2‖2\| H \mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_2 \|^2 after normalising the homogeneous coordinate. Both are estimated with the same threshold of 1 pixel, and the choice is

H if nH≥0.9nF,otherwise F,H \text{ if } n_H \ge 0.9\, n_F, \quad \text{otherwise } F,

with nn the number of inliers. A homography fixes the location in the second image for every point, a fundamental matrix only a line. With real parallax nHn_H is therefore clearly smaller; without parallax both fit equally well, and HH is the more binding model. The limit 0.9 is empirical (caveat).

Robust estimation follows RANSAC with MSAC costs ∑imin⁡(ei2,s2)\sum_i \min(e_i^2, s^2) and re-estimation on all inliers; the number of samples follows from the current inlier ratio ww, sample size mm (8 points for FF, 4 for HH) and confidence p=0.999p = 0.999, at most 2000:

N=⌈ln⁡(1−p)ln⁡(1−wm)⌉.N = \left\lceil \frac{\ln(1 - p)}{\ln(1 - w^{m})} \right\rceil.

The outlier threshold

The residuals ri=dSr_i = d_S or dH2\sqrt{d_H^2} of all correspondences of a pair give a robust spread; a fixed measure in pixels would know nothing of the blur and lens distortion of the material at hand:

σ^=1.4826⋅mediani⁡ri,τ=max⁡(0.8;5σ^),outlier: ri>τ.\hat\sigma = 1.4826 \cdot \operatorname{median}_i\, r_i, \qquad \tau = \max(0.8;\ 5\,\hat\sigma), \qquad \text{outlier: } r_i > \tau.

Why 1.4826. For normally distributed errors with spread σ\sigma, the median of |e||e| is Φ−1(0.75)σ=0.6745σ\Phi^{-1}(0.75)\,\sigma = 0.6745\,\sigma; the inverse is 1.4826 (from the literature, Rousseeuw and Leroy). The lower limit of 0.8 pixels stands for what the model does not represent (lens distortion). Because the threshold also depends on the median, an inserted location does not affect it as long as the majority of correspondences fit.

Clustering per tile

On a grid of 32×3232 \times 32 pixels, the correspondences ntn_t and outliers ktk_t per tile are counted over all usable pairs. The reference r0r_0, the mismatch rate, is the median of the tile rates; tiles with fewer than 6 correspondences do not count. Assuming that outliers are roughly Poisson distributed with mean λ=r0nt\lambda = r_0 n_t without tampering, the clustering is standardised:

r0=max⁡(0.005;mediant⁡ktnt),zt=kt−r0ntr0nt+1,vt=zt6.r_0 = \max\big(0.005;\ \operatorname{median}_t \tfrac{k_t}{n_t}\big), \qquad z_t = \frac{k_t - r_0 n_t}{\sqrt{r_0 n_t + 1}}, \qquad v_t = \frac{z_t}{6}.

vt≥1v_t \ge 1 means conspicuous, but only if at the same time at least 25 % of the tile's correspondences are outliers (otherwise vtv_t is capped at 0.99). The +1+1 in the denominator keeps the value bounded for tiny λ\lambda. Together the two conditions are a one-sided test against the distribution of the other tiles, not a probability statement: the 6 standard deviations and the 25 % are chosen so that unaltered test material stays unremarkable (caveat).

Neighbouring conspicuous tiles (eight neighbours) form regions; a region with fewer than 2 tiles does not count.

Rigid or not

A car driving through the frame violates the camera model without anything having been edited. So each region gets its own model: for every pair with at least 16 outliers in the region, a separate fundamental matrix is estimated with a threshold of 0.7 pixels; the pair counts as rigid if it explains at least 70 % of those outliers. With gg tested and zz rigid pairs, the region counts as self-moving if

g≥3andz≥0.6g.g \ge 3 \quad\text{and}\quad z \ge 0.6\, g.

A rigid region is named in the text and reduced to 0.99 on the map; a region that fails this, or has too few correspondences for the check, is conspicuous. Caveat: a fundamental matrix has seven degrees of freedom; on a small, nearly planar area it can also explain a slightly deformed motion. That is why the 0.7 pixel threshold counts, not the model alone.

Over time, pair ii counts the sum over the conspicuous tiles: zi/4z_i / 4 if at least 25 % outliers lie there. A pair of frames ii and i+3i + 3 also fires if only one of the two is affected; a frame therefore counts only if both its pairs fire (see time axis).

Consistency over time

For each frame pair (i,i+1)(i, i+1) the forward flow is estimated and summarised per tile, where the reliability is at least 0.3 and at least 20 % of the points are reliable. With f\mathbf{f} the flow and II in grey values 0 to 255, the compensated difference per point is

d(x)=Ii+1(x+f(x))−Ii(x)255,d(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{I_{i+1}(\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})) - I_i(\mathbf{x})}{255},

sampled at every second point. This gives per tile and pair the mean magnitude Rt,iR_{t,i} and the signed mean St,iS_{t,i}. From the flow per tile ft,i\mathbf{f}_{t,i} (median of the reliable points) the frame's median gi\mathbf{g}_i is subtracted; three quantities remain:

Ut,i=‖(ft,i−gi)−(ft,i−1−gi−1)‖,Bt,i=max⁡(0;Rt,i−medianj⁡Rt,j),Ft,i=|(St,i−si)−(St,i−1−si−1)|.U_{t,i} = \big\|(\mathbf{f}_{t,i} - \mathbf{g}_i) - (\mathbf{f}_{t,i-1} - \mathbf{g}_{i-1})\big\|, \quad B_{t,i} = \max\big(0;\ R_{t,i} - \operatorname{median}_j R_{t,j}\big), \quad F_{t,i} = \big|(S_{t,i} - s_i) - (S_{t,i-1} - s_{i-1})\big|.

  • Jitter UU: change of the flow from pair to pair; an object that deforms from frame to frame shakes.
  • Break BB: residual after motion compensation against the tile's own normal level; an object that suddenly appears or vanishes gives a single spike.
  • Flicker FF: brightness jump after motion compensation, from pair to pair.

Per tile these are condensed over time: U^t=rmsi⁡Ut,i\hat U_t = \mathrm{rms}_i U_{t,i}, B^t=maxi⁡Bt,i\hat B_t = \max_i B_{t,i}, F^t=rmsi⁡Ft,i\hat F_t = \mathrm{rms}_i F_{t,i}. The normal level of each quantity is its median over the tiles, bounded from below so that in calm recordings the estimation accuracy itself does not become the threshold:

vt=max⁡(U^t6max⁡(0.08;U˜),B^t8max⁡(0.006;B˜),F^t6max⁡(0.004;F˜)),U˜=mediant⁡U^t etc.v_t = \max\Big(\frac{\hat U_t}{6 \max(0.08;\ \tilde U)},\ \frac{\hat B_t}{8 \max(0.006;\ \tilde B)},\ \frac{\hat F_t}{6 \max(0.004;\ \tilde F)}\Big), \qquad \tilde U = \operatorname{median}_t \hat U_t \text{ etc.}

The border tiles (first and last row and column) are not evaluated: at the image edge the flow window reaches beyond the image, and these tiles shook at seven times the normal level during a calm camera move. Below 10 % measured tiles the trace cannot be evaluated; likewise with fewer than 5 frames. Single tiles stay visible as 0.99 but do not count.

The factors 6, 8, 6 and the base values are settings (caveat): chosen so that unaltered test material stays unremarkable, not derived from a distribution. The median instead of the mean makes the normal level insensitive to the location being sought.

How it is tested

  • Tampering (Eingriffsprobe): an object deformed from frame to frame in a rendered room corner, deformation 2.5 and 1.2 pixels, frames 4 to 11. Geometry and time report it (EC-14, EC-18), mark more than half its area and nothing beside it (EC-15, EC-16, EC-19), and the frames are correct (EC-17, EC-20).
  • Unaltered material (Unveraendertprobe, Unveraendert2probe): the same scene with a genuine object, three seeds, and the empty scene: unremarkable (EC-11, EC-12).
  • Rigid (Grenzprobe): a rigid object moving on its own is no finding but is named (EC-26), and the map does not mark it (EC-27).
  • Nothing to measure (Echtheitsprobe): uniform images and too few frames give "cannot be evaluated" with a reason (EC-31, EC-32, EC-34, EC-35).

The material is generated, the truth known. Counter-tests turn the epipolar map and the verdict of the time trace red; the other thresholds on this page are only covered indirectly by the acceptance tests.

Limits

  • An inserted object moving exactly like the scene, for instance because it was rendered correctly in 3D along the camera path, is not seen by the epipolar trace.
  • The raw figures depend on the quality of the flow: correspondences are missing in structureless areas, and heavily compressed material lowers the reliability.
  • The thresholds are settings measured on generated material. On other material the traces can fire without anything having been edited, for instance with a rolling shutter or digital image stabilisation, which violate the assumption of a single rigid mapping per frame.
  • The time trace computes forward from frame to frame only; occlusions and border tiles are not specifically modelled.
  • A region that barely has a majority of rigid pairs counts as self-moving even if it was edited; the trace then names it but does not judge it.
