Light and shadow – background

The computation behind Lichtspur: lines through shadow and object point in homogeneous coordinates, the majority over all intersections, the common point as a smallest eigenvector and the deviation as an angle – with derivations, pseudocode following the source, a worked example, testing and limits.

What this is about

For a point light source a simple relation holds that survives projection into the image: the line from a shadow point through its object point passes through the image of the light source. If all such lines meet in one point, the shadows fit one light; if one does not, it is the candidate.

Unlike the other traces this one measures no image values. It needs point pairs that someone has placed in the image. The result is thus a computation over markings, and it cannot know how reliable a marking is.

Lines and intersections

Two points a,b\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b} are divided by the largest coordinate ss and get the homogeneous form (x,y,1)(x, y, 1). The line through them is the cross product, brought to a normal of length 1:

l=(y1−y2,x2−x1,x1y2−x2y1)(y1−y2)2+(x2−x1)2.\mathbf{l} = \frac{(y_1 - y_2,\ x_2 - x_1,\ x_1 y_2 - x_2 y_1)}{\sqrt{(y_1 - y_2)^2 + (x_2 - x_1)^2}}.

The intersection of two lines is again the cross product q=li×lj\mathbf{q} = \mathbf{l}_i \times \mathbf{l}_j; it exists for parallel lines too, then q3=0q_3 = 0, a point at infinity. So the special case of a very distant light source, the sun, resolves itself.

The majority

A single wrong marking must not drag the common point along. Therefore, from all pairs of lines, the intersection with the most pairs within tolerance is taken (on a tie the smallest sum of angles); intersections with a cross product below 10−1210^{-12} are dropped. Only this majority determines the point. The procedure is exhaustive, not randomised: for nn pairs there are n(n−1)/2n(n-1)/2 candidates.

The common point

If q\mathbf{q} lies on all lines, li⊤q=0\mathbf{l}_i^{\top}\mathbf{q} = 0. With measurement errors the sum of squares is minimised, subject to ‖q‖=1\|\mathbf{q}\| = 1:

q^=arg⁡min‖q‖=1⁡∑i(li⊤q)2=eigenvector of the smallest eigenvalue of M=∑ilili⊤.\hat{\mathbf{q}} = \arg\min_{\|\mathbf{q}\| = 1} \sum_i (\mathbf{l}_i^{\top} \mathbf{q})^2 = \text{eigenvector of the smallest eigenvalue of } M = \sum_i \mathbf{l}_i \mathbf{l}_i^{\top}.

Derivation (derived). The cost is q⊤Mq\mathbf{q}^{\top} M \mathbf{q}. Under ‖q‖=1\|\mathbf{q}\| = 1 the Lagrange approach leads to Mq=λqM \mathbf{q} = \lambda \mathbf{q}, and the cost is λ\lambda. So the minimum is the smallest eigenvalue. The eigendecomposition of the symmetric 3×33 \times 3 matrix is computed with the Jacobi method.

Caveat: the quantity l⊤q\mathbf{l}^{\top}\mathbf{q} is algebraic. It does not weight by angle; with very differently long pairs or very distant light, the result is not the solution minimising the angular error. For rating, therefore, this quantity is not used but the angle.

The deviation

For the pair (a,b)(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) with a=(x1,y1)\mathbf{a} = (x_1, y_1), u=b−a\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}, and the direction to the point is, without dividing by the possibly vanishing coordinate:

v=(q1−x1q3,q2−y1q3),δ=arctan⁡2(|u×v|,|u⋅v|).\mathbf{v} = \big(q_1 - x_1 q_3,\ q_2 - y_1 q_3\big), \qquad \delta = \arctan2\big(|\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v}|,\ |\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}|\big).

The magnitudes in numerator and denominator compare lines, not directions: the angle lies between 0° and 90°, and the light may lie behind the viewer. The quantity stays well defined for q3=0q_3 = 0, hence the homogeneous notation.

The verdict

With count⁡\mathrm{count}, the number of pairs in the majority:

no majority⟺count⁡<max⁡(3,n/2).\text{no majority} \iff \mathrm{count} < \max(3,\ n/2).

The trace is conspicuous if a majority is missing or at least one pair deviates by more than the tolerance of 4° from the common point. The pairs are then named. With fewer than 4 pairs, a pair of two identical points, or all points at the origin, there is nothing to evaluate. The tolerance is configurable: 4° at a distance of 50 pixels corresponds to 3.5 pixels sideways.

Pseudocode – Lichtspur.pruefe(pairs, tolerance)

if fewer than 4 pairs or a pair degenerate or all at the origin: cannot be evaluated
s ← largest magnitude of a coordinate
for all line pairs (i, j):
    q ← l[i] × l[j];  if norm < 1e-12: continue
    count pairs with deviation(p, q) ≤ tolerance
in ← pairs of the best intersection            most pairs, then smallest sum of angles
q ← smallest eigenvector of Σ l lᵀ over the majority
out ← pairs with deviation(p, q) > tolerance
none ← number of pairs in in < max(3, n/2)
if out not empty or none: conspicuous, name pairs
else: unremarkable

Source: envisionz-framework-echtheit/src/envisionz/echtheit/licht/Lichtspur.java. The tolerance is set through Echtheit.Einstellung.

Worked example

Light at (900,−300)(900, -300). Shadow points on the extension beyond the object point aa: a+0.3(a−L)a + 0.3 (a - L). For (100,200)(100, 200) this gives (−140,350)(-140, 350), for (300,500)(300, 500) it gives (120,740)(120, 740). The intersection of the two lines is exactly (900,−300)(900, -300), the deviation 0°.

A wrong pair with object point (700,650)(700, 650) and shadow (780,720)(780, 720) deviates from the point by 60.7° (test EV-101 builds such a case). Parallel lines give q3=0q_3 = 0 and a deviation of 0° without special handling. A mis-marking of 1 pixel on a pair 50 pixels apart costs arctan⁡(1/50)=1.146°\arctan(1/50) = 1.146°. The numbers were recomputed with docs/theorie/rechnung/echtheit_licht.java.

How it is tested

  • Stufe2probe, constructed pairs with known light: shadows of one light (EV-100), a wrong shadow is named (EV-101), parallel lines (EV-102), outlier among parallel lines (EV-103), too few pairs (EV-104), a pair of two identical points (EV-105), all at the origin (EV-106), the configured tolerance is passed on (EV-114).

Counter-tests turn the tolerance and the passing of the tolerance red; the case "no majority" is not specifically generated in the tests.

Limits

  • The trace computes over markings. A misplaced marking creates a finding or hides one.
  • A point light is assumed. Extended sources give wide penumbras, several lights several intersections; both can give "no majority" without anything having been edited.
  • Lens distortion bends lines and is not compensated.
  • A consistent result does not prove authenticity: it only fits one light.
