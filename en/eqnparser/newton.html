Newton method, Jacobian and damping – background

The loop in AbstractSolver: Newton step, building the Jacobian, Armijo line search, scaling and termination criteria, with pseudocode after the source code, a worked example and the tests.

What this is about

A system of nn equations for nn unknown quantities is to be solved. In thermodynamics and fluid mechanics the equations are nonlinear (equations of state, property functions, characteristic maps), and nobody can solve them explicitly for the unknowns. They are written as a root-finding problem

F(x)=0,F:Rn→Rn\mathbf F(\mathbf x) = \mathbf 0, \qquad \mathbf F:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n

and the solution is approached step by step. The framework has twelve solvers behind one interface; they share a common loop in AbstractSolver and differ in the step (computeStep) and in the damping (armijoStep). This page describes the core they all rely on: the Newton method, the Jacobian matrix, damping, scaling and the termination criteria.

The Newton method

If xk\mathbf x_k is an approximation, F\mathbf F is replaced near it by its tangent plane (first-order Taylor expansion):

F(xk+Δx)≈F(xk)+J(xk)Δx,Jij=∂Fi∂xj\mathbf F(\mathbf x_k + \Delta\mathbf x) \approx \mathbf F(\mathbf x_k) + \mathbf J(\mathbf x_k)\,\Delta\mathbf x, \qquad J_{ij} = \frac{\partial F_i}{\partial x_j}

The root of this linear approximation is the Newton step; it is obtained by solving a linear system, not by forming the inverse:

J(xk)Δx=−F(xk),xk+1=xk+αkΔx\mathbf J(\mathbf x_k)\,\Delta\mathbf x = -\mathbf F(\mathbf x_k), \qquad \mathbf x_{k+1} = \mathbf x_k + \alpha_k\,\Delta\mathbf x

With αk=1\alpha_k = 1 this is the pure Newton method; near the solution it converges quadratically, the error being roughly squared in every step. Far from the solution the full step can overshoot. For that there is the damping factor αk∈(0,1]\alpha_k\in(0,1] (see below).

Why the error is squared

Let x*\mathbf x^* be a solution with a regular Jacobian and ek=xk−x*\mathbf e_k=\mathbf x_k-\mathbf x^* the error. From F(x*)=0\mathbf F(\mathbf x^*)=\mathbf 0 and the full step,

ek+1=ek−J(xk)−1F(xk)=J(xk)−1[J(xk)ek−(F(xk)−F(x*))].\mathbf e_{k+1} = \mathbf e_k - \mathbf J(\mathbf x_k)^{-1}\mathbf F(\mathbf x_k) = \mathbf J(\mathbf x_k)^{-1}\Bigl[\mathbf J(\mathbf x_k)\,\mathbf e_k - \bigl(\mathbf F(\mathbf x_k)-\mathbf F(\mathbf x^*)\bigr)\Bigr].

The bracket is the error of the linear approximation of F\mathbf F between x*\mathbf x^* and xk\mathbf x_k. If J\mathbf J is Lipschitz continuous with constant γ\gamma, it is at most γ2‖ek‖2\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\lVert\mathbf e_k\rVert^2 (integral form of the mean value theorem). With ‖J(xk)−1‖≤β\lVert\mathbf J(\mathbf x_k)^{-1}\rVert\le\beta in a neighbourhood of x*\mathbf x^* this gives

‖ek+1‖≤βγ2‖ek‖2.\lVert\mathbf e_{k+1}\rVert \;\le\; \frac{\beta\gamma}{2}\,\lVert\mathbf e_k\rVert^2 .

Starting close enough, so that βγ2‖e0‖<1\tfrac{\beta\gamma}{2}\lVert\mathbf e_0\rVert<1, the error falls and the number of correct digits roughly doubles per step. In one dimension the constant is exact: ek+1/ek2→F''(x*)/(2F'(x*))e_{k+1}/e_k^2\to F''(x^*)/\bigl(2F'(x^*)\bigr). The statement is local; it says nothing about where this region is. Hence damping and continuation.

Convention in the framework: NewtonArmijoSolver.computeStep solves JΔx=−F\mathbf J\,\Delta\mathbf x=-\mathbf F directly with column pivoting (MatrixSimple.solveLinear). Formerly the inverse was formed and multiplied with the vector; that cost twice as much and lost additional digits on ill-conditioned systems. Columns without a pivot (a variable with no influence on any active residual) would silently give Δx=0\Delta x=0; they are therefore mapped to variable names and reported.

The Jacobian matrix

The quality of the Jacobian decides the behaviour of the method. AbstractSolver.buildJacobian builds it row by row in parallel (each thread writes its own row, evaluations are stateless), and only for the free variables; fixed variables have no column. For each equation the first applicable route is used:

  1. Forward automatic differentiation: if the application supplies a row provider (JacobianRowProvider), a single pass with dual numbers yields the whole row exactly.
  2. Symbolic derivative: from the pre-built DerivativeMatrix only the non-zero positions are evaluated. The derivatives come from the evaluation tree (section Expressions and derivatives).
  3. Derivative of CALL equations: an equation F=output⁡−pCALL⁡(inputs⁡)F = \mathrm{output} - p_{\mathrm{CALL}}(\mathrm{inputs}) uses the derivative of the call (fourth order, fd5); the coefficient of the output variable is +1+1.
  4. Finite differences as fallback with a forward difference: ∂Fi/∂xj≈(Fi(xj+h)−Fi(xj))/h\partial F_i/\partial x_j\approx\bigl(F_i(x_j+h)-F_i(x_j)\bigr)/h with h=max⁡(10−6|xj|,10−8)h=\max(10^{-6}\,|x_j|,\,10^{-8}).

Forward differentiation with dual numbers

Forward AD computes with numbers of the form a+a'εa + a'\varepsilon, where ε≠0\varepsilon\ne0 but ε2=0\varepsilon^2=0. Inserting x+εx+\varepsilon into a smooth function, the Taylor series stops after the linear term: f(x+ε)=f(x)+f'(x)εf(x+\varepsilon)=f(x)+f'(x)\,\varepsilon. The arithmetic rules follow from ε2=0\varepsilon^2=0:

(a+a'ε)(b+b'ε)=ab+(a'b+ab')ε,a+a'εb+b'ε=ab+a'b−ab'b2ε,f(a+a'ε)=f(a)+f'(a)a'ε.(a+a'\varepsilon)(b+b'\varepsilon)=ab+(a'b+ab')\,\varepsilon,\qquad \frac{a+a'\varepsilon}{b+b'\varepsilon}=\frac ab+\frac{a'b-ab'}{b^2}\,\varepsilon,\qquad f(a+a'\varepsilon)=f(a)+f'(a)\,a'\,\varepsilon .

In the solver each number carries, instead of a single a'a', a whole vector a'∈Rm\mathbf a'\in\mathbb R^m for the mm variables of the equation; variable xjx_j starts with the unit vector ej\mathbf e_j. One pass through the expression tree thus gives the value of the equation and its whole row of the Jacobian. For powers, (ab)'=bab−1a'(a^b)'=b\,a^{b-1}a' if the exponent is fixed, and (ab)'=ab(b'ln⁡a+ba'/a)(a^b)'=a^b\bigl(b'\ln a+b\,a'/a\bigr) if it depends on the variables itself.

What follows: the derivative is exact up to rounding, there is no step size hh, and the cost per row is a fixed multiple of the evaluation itself (a factor of about mm for the vectors). Differences, by contrast, have two errors that work against each other: the truncation error h2|F''|\tfrac h2|F''| and cancellation ≈εmach⁡|F|/h\approx\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}|F|/h. Example from the probe: F=XY+sin⁡X/Y−eX−Y+X2+1F=XY+\sin X/Y-e^{X-Y}+\sqrt{X^2+1} at X=1.5,Y=−0.7X=1.5,\;Y=-0.7 gives in one pass ∂F/∂X=−8.994016\partial F/\partial X=-8.994016 and ∂F/∂Y=8.489309\partial F/\partial Y=8.489309, equal to the hand-derived values to 10−1410^{-14}.

Damping: the Armijo line search

For the measure φ(x)=12‖F(x)‖2\varphi(\mathbf x)=\tfrac12\lVert\mathbf F(\mathbf x)\rVert^2 the Newton step always points downhill: the directional derivative is \(\varphi'(0)=-\lVert\mathbf F\rVert^2<0\). The Armijo condition ("sufficient decrease") accepts a step length \(\alpha\) if

φ(x+αΔx)≤φ(x)+c1αφ'(0)\varphi(\mathbf x + \alpha\,\Delta\mathbf x) \;\le\; \varphi(\mathbf x) + c_1\,\alpha\,\varphi'(0)

One starts with α=1\alpha=1 and halves until the condition holds (backtracking). If an evaluation gives NaN or infinity, the trial is treated as a failure and α\alpha is reduced as well.

Why such an α\alpha exists: by Taylor, φ(x+αΔx)=φ(x)+αφ'(0)+o(α)\varphi(\mathbf x+\alpha\Delta\mathbf x)=\varphi(\mathbf x)+\alpha\,\varphi'(0)+o(\alpha). Since φ'(0)<0\varphi'(0)<0 and c1<1c_1<1, the right-hand side exceeds the left for small α\alpha; halving therefore ends after finitely many steps, provided F\mathbf F is continuously differentiable near x\mathbf x. The decrease is guaranteed, not merely non-increase: per step φ\varphi falls by at least c1α‖F‖2c_1\alpha\lVert\mathbf F\rVert^2. Near the solution α=1\alpha=1 is accepted and quadratic convergence is kept.

In the code the condition is written in the squared norm, without the factor 12\tfrac12:

‖F(x+αΔx)‖2≤(1−c1α)‖F(x)‖2.\lVert\mathbf F(\mathbf x+\alpha\,\Delta\mathbf x)\rVert^2 \;\le\; (1-c_1\,\alpha)\,\lVert\mathbf F(\mathbf x)\rVert^2 .

In the form with φ=12‖F‖2\varphi=\tfrac12\lVert\mathbf F\rVert^2 and φ'(0)=−‖F‖2\varphi'(0)=-\lVert\mathbf F\rVert^2 this reads φnew⁡≤φ(1−2c1α)\varphi_{\mathrm{new}}\le\varphi\,(1-2c_1\alpha). The code therefore asks for the decrease with half the c1c_1. With c1=10−4c_1=10^{-4} this is of no consequence; whoever chooses a large c1c_1 should know it. The probe shows it with c1=0.4c_1=0.4 on F=arctan⁡XF=\arctan X from X=1X=1: the full step to 1−π/21-\pi/2 lowers ‖F‖2\lVert F\rVert^2 to 0.436 times its value. That is below 1−c1=0.61-c_1=0.6 and is accepted, but it would be above 1−2c1=0.21-2c_1=0.2.

Example of the cut-back: F=−10−3arctan⁡XF=-10^{-3}\arctan X from X=1.5X=1.5. The full Newton step ΔX=−arctan⁡(1.5)(1+1.52)=−3.194\Delta X=-\arctan(1.5)\,(1+1.5^2)=-3.194 lands at X=−1.694X=-1.694, where |F||F| rises from 0.983⋅10−30.983\cdot10^{-3} to 1.037⋅10−31.037\cdot10^{-3}. With α=12\alpha=\tfrac12, X=−0.0970X=-0.0970 and the decrease suffices. From there the method converges quadratically; without damping it swings up.

Convention in the framework: defaults from SolverConfig: α0=1\alpha_0=1, reduction ρ=0.5\rho=0.5, c1=10−4c_1=10^{-4}, lower limit αmin⁡=10−4\alpha_{\min}=10^{-4}. The line search runs in the original space of the variables, after the step has been scaled back. The merit is evaluated in the physical norm ‖F‖\lVert\mathbf F\rVert, even when scaling is active (cmd.equilibratedMerit switches the old behaviour back on): the scaled norm spans several orders of magnitude, so stiff rows become practically invisible and steps without real progress were accepted. Measured on 178 recomputation cases of an engine model: scaled merit 175 of 178, physical merit 178 of 178. Optionally (default off) there is a non-monotone variant that tests against the maximum of the last five merit values instead of φ(0)\varphi(0), so that the residual may rise temporarily at regime boundaries.

‖F(xk+αΔx)‖2≤max0≤j<min⁡(5,k)⁡‖F(xk−j)‖2−c1α‖F(xk)‖2\lVert\mathbf F(\mathbf x_k+\alpha\Delta\mathbf x)\rVert^2\;\le\;\max_{0\le j<\min(5,k)}\lVert\mathbf F(\mathbf x_{k-j})\rVert^2-c_1\,\alpha\,\lVert\mathbf F(\mathbf x_k)\rVert^2

The probe shows the window on F=XF=X: a step that lifts |F||F| from 0.5 to 0.8 is accepted as long as the value 1 from an earlier step is still among the last five. One step later the 1 drops out and the same step is rejected; the monotone search rejects it at once.

Scaling the Jacobian

The quantities of an engineering system differ by many powers of ten: pressures in Pa next to Mach numbers. This makes the Jacobian ill-conditioned. Before solving, rows and columns are therefore scaled, J˜=RJC\tilde{\mathbf J}=\mathbf R\,\mathbf J\,\mathbf C; the step is determined in the scaled space and transformed back with C\mathbf C; the values themselves always stay in physical space. The choices are diagonal and unit-interval scaling (columns only), Sinkhorn-Knopp equilibration (factors at the start, then every 5 iterations or when the estimated condition number exceeds 101010^{10}), STATIC_NORM (factors frozen at the start point) and DYNAMIC_NORM (row and column scales renewed every iteration). The condition number is estimated by ConditionEstimator in the infinity norm; the message "ill-conditioned" recommends switching to the Levenberg-Marquardt solver.

The Newton step does not depend on the scaling. With regular diagonal matrices R\mathbf R and C\mathbf C one solves J˜Δx˜=−RF\tilde{\mathbf J}\,\Delta\tilde{\mathbf x}=-\mathbf R\mathbf F and transforms back:

Δx=CΔx˜=C(RJC)−1R(−F)=CC−1J−1R−1R(−F)=−J−1F.\Delta\mathbf x=\mathbf C\,\Delta\tilde{\mathbf x}=\mathbf C\,(\mathbf R\mathbf J\mathbf C)^{-1}\mathbf R\,(-\mathbf F)=\mathbf C\,\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf J^{-1}\mathbf R^{-1}\mathbf R\,(-\mathbf F)=-\mathbf J^{-1}\mathbf F .

What follows: for the Newton solver scaling does not change the step at all in exact arithmetic. It acts on rounding and on the pivot choice of the factorisation, on the condition estimate and – only with cmd.equilibratedMerit – on the merit of the line search. The probe shows it on the test model with one row times 10610^6 and XX in thousandths (X=1000YX=1000\,Y): without scaling, with equilibration and with STATIC_NORM the same first step X1=7000/3X_1=7000/3. For Broyden and Levenberg-Marquardt this does not hold: the update and the damping term μD\mu\mathbf D are not scale-invariant.

Termination and robustness

The measure is the sum of absolute values ‖F‖1=∑i|Fi|\lVert\mathbf F\rVert_1=\sum_i|F_i|, optionally with residual normalisation. The iteration stops when

  • ‖F‖1<tol⁡\lVert\mathbf F\rVert_1 < \mathrm{tol} (absolute; tested at the start of every iteration from the second on, i.e. after the step), or
  • ‖F‖1/‖F0‖1<tolrel⁡\lVert\mathbf F\rVert_1/\lVert\mathbf F_0\rVert_1 < \mathrm{tol}_{rel} (relative, only if tolrel⁡>0\mathrm{tol}_{rel}>0), or
  • the maximum number of iterations is reached, or the run is interrupted.

∑i=1n|Fi(xk)|<tol⁡\sum_{i=1}^{n}|F_i(\mathbf x_k)| \;<\; \mathrm{tol}

The sum norm is strict: it is never smaller than the Euclidean norm, and it depends on the units of the equations. A pressure equation in Pa with |F|=10−3|F|=10^{-3} prevents termination, while a dimensionless equation with the same value would long be accurate. On the test model with tol⁡=10−3\mathrm{tol}=10^{-3} the run ends after the third step (∑|F|=3.8⋅10−4\sum|F|=3.8\cdot10^{-4}).

A NaN in the residual counts as divergence (residual = maximum). Stagnation is detected when progress stays below one percent for eight iterations in a row; this gives a warning (hint towards equilibration) but does not stop the run. With the restart ladder switched on, however, the run stops early if the best value of the last 8 iterations improves on the best of the 8 before by less than 0.5 % (plateau, from 16 iterations on) or if the residual oscillates with period 2; the next rung of the ladder then starts immediately.

Convention in the framework: defaults of SolverConfig.Builder: at most 100 iterations, tolerance 10−710^{-7}, relative tolerance 0 (off). After every step PhysicalProjector projects the values onto the variable bounds.

Pseudocode – AbstractSolver.solve

F ← F(x);  fsum0 ← Σ|F|                         at the start (xx = 0)
while xx < maxIter:
    if xx > 0 and (fsum < tol or fsum/fsum0 < tolRel): stop
    J ← Jacobian(x)                              rows in parallel; AD, symbolic, CALL or differences
    if scaled: update R, C if needed;  J ← R·J·C;  F′ ← R·F
    κ ← estimate condition number(J);  warn on poor conditioning
    dx ← computeStep(J, F′)                      Newton: solve J·dx = −F′ directly
    dx ← C·dx                                    back to the original space
    α ← armijoStep(x, dx, F)                     halve α until ‖F(x+α·dx)‖² ≤ (1 − c1·α)·‖F‖²
    x ← x + α·dx;  project x onto the bounds
    F ← F(x)                                     NaN: fsum ← maximum, divergence
    fsum ← Σ|F|  (or normalised)
    count stagnation (8 iterations under 1 % progress: warning)
    if restart ladder and (plateau or period-2 oscillation): stop
    xx ← xx + 1

Condition number: by default a fast heuristic from the row and column sizes of the Jacobian (cost n²). It does not see linear dependence: two almost identical equations give κ ≈ 1. Optionally the solver computes the true estimate of κ∞ by Hager and Higham (one extra LU factorisation per iteration): switch “Condition number by Hager” on the solver card, -KH on the command line. The levels GOOD to CRITICAL then usually come out stricter.

How the solvers differ

The loop stays, the step changes: NEWTON_SPARSE solves the same system with a sparse LU (from framework-math), NEWTON_GMRES iteratively with GMRES, BROYDEN replaces the Jacobian by rank-1 updates and rebuilds it only rarely, LEVENBERG_MARQUARDT damps the step through a parameter instead of a line search. The overview section Twelve solvers behind one interface lists them all. Difficult starts are handled by continuation, see Continuation methods.

Broyden: a secant instead of the Jacobian

After a step sk=xk+1−xk\mathbf s_k=\mathbf x_{k+1}-\mathbf x_k the change of the residual yk=F(xk+1)−F(xk)\mathbf y_k=\mathbf F(\mathbf x_{k+1})-\mathbf F(\mathbf x_k) is known. An approximation Jk+1\mathbf J_{k+1} should reproduce this one direction correctly (secant condition) and otherwise change as little as possible. The smallest change in the Frobenius norm satisfying Jk+1sk=yk\mathbf J_{k+1}\mathbf s_k=\mathbf y_k is the rank-1 update

Jk+1=Jk+(yk−Jksk)skTskTsk.\mathbf J_{k+1}=\mathbf J_k+\frac{(\mathbf y_k-\mathbf J_k\mathbf s_k)\,\mathbf s_k^{\mathsf T}}{\mathbf s_k^{\mathsf T}\mathbf s_k}.

Derivation: every matrix with Bs=y\mathbf B\mathbf s=\mathbf y satisfies (B−Jk)s=y−Jks(\mathbf B-\mathbf J_k)\mathbf s=\mathbf y-\mathbf J_k\mathbf s, hence ‖B−Jk‖F≥‖y−Jks‖/‖s‖\lVert\mathbf B-\mathbf J_k\rVert_F\ge\lVert\mathbf y-\mathbf J_k\mathbf s\rVert/\lVert\mathbf s\rVert. The rank-1 matrix above attains this bound. On vectors orthogonal to sk\mathbf s_k it acts like Jk\mathbf J_k.

Whoever carries the inverse updates it with the Sherman–Morrison formula, (A+usT)−1=A−1−A−1usTA−1/(1+sTA−1u)(\mathbf A+\mathbf u\mathbf s^{\mathsf T})^{-1}=\mathbf A^{-1}-\mathbf A^{-1}\mathbf u\,\mathbf s^{\mathsf T}\mathbf A^{-1}/(1+\mathbf s^{\mathsf T}\mathbf A^{-1}\mathbf u), at O(n2)O(n^2) cost per step. Near the solution Broyden converges superlinearly, not quadratically.

Convention in the framework: BroydenSolver carries no inverse. It keeps Jk=J0+∑iuiviT\mathbf J_k=\mathbf J_0+\sum_i\mathbf u_i\mathbf v_i^{\mathsf T} in product form, builds the matrix from it in every step and solves directly with column pivoting. The update is done in the scaled space (y\mathbf y is multiplied by R\mathbf R for it). It is skipped if sTs<10−14\mathbf s^{\mathsf T}\mathbf s<10^{-14} or |sTy|<10−14sTs|\mathbf s^{\mathsf T}\mathbf y|<10^{-14}\,\mathbf s^{\mathsf T}\mathbf s. It is rebuilt after a self-adjusted interval or on poor conditioning; in addition there is a rollback to a checkpoint when the residual more than doubles, and after three rollbacks in a row plain Newton.

Levenberg-Marquardt: damping as a trust region

Instead of shortening the step afterwards, Levenberg-Marquardt solves a damped system:

(JTJ+μD)Δx=−JTF,D=I(Levenberg)orD=diag⁡(JTJ)(Marquardt).(\mathbf J^{\mathsf T}\mathbf J+\mu\,\mathbf D)\,\Delta\mathbf x=-\mathbf J^{\mathsf T}\mathbf F,\qquad \mathbf D=\mathbf I\ \text{(Levenberg)}\quad\text{or}\quad \mathbf D=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf J^{\mathsf T}\mathbf J)\ \text{(Marquardt)} .

This is the first-order condition of the problem min⁡‖F+JΔx‖2\min\lVert\mathbf F+\mathbf J\Delta\mathbf x\rVert^2 subject to ‖D1/2Δx‖≤Δ\lVert\mathbf D^{1/2}\Delta\mathbf x\rVert\le\Delta: μ\mu is the multiplier of the constraint. Each μ>0\mu>0 has a radius Δ(μ)=‖D1/2Δx(μ)‖\Delta(\mu)=\lVert\mathbf D^{1/2}\Delta\mathbf x(\mu)\rVert that falls as μ\mu grows. The limits: μ→0\mu\to0 gives the Newton step (for a square, regular J\mathbf J Gauss-Newton equals Newton), μ→∞\mu\to\infty gives Δx≈−D−1JTF/μ\Delta\mathbf x\approx-\mathbf D^{-1}\mathbf J^{\mathsf T}\mathbf F/\mu, a short step in the direction of steepest descent of 12‖F‖2\tfrac12\lVert\mathbf F\rVert^2. On the test model at the start point (J=(211−1)\mathbf J=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}, F=(−4,0)\mathbf F=(-4,0)), μ=1\mu=1 gives the step (20/17,16/17)(20/17,\;16/17) with Levenberg and (28/39,32/39)(28/39,\;32/39) with Marquardt; Newton would be (4/3,4/3)(4/3,\;4/3).

μ\mu is controlled by the outcome of the subsequent line search: full step accepted ⇒ μ←μ/ν↓\mu\leftarrow\mu/\nu_{\downarrow}, accepted with damping ⇒ μ←μν↑\mu\leftarrow\mu\,\nu_{\uparrow}, unusable ⇒ μ←μν↑2\mu\leftarrow\mu\,\nu_{\uparrow}^2, with ν↓=5\nu_{\downarrow}=5, ν↑=2\nu_{\uparrow}=2 and 10−16≤μ≤10810^{-16}\le\mu\le10^{8}.

Convention in the framework: with normalisation active (the default for LM is STATIC_NORM) the solver uses D=I\mathbf D=\mathbf I and the start μ0=10−6\mu_0=10^{-6}. Without normalisation it takes D=diag⁡(max⁡(JTJii,10−8))\mathbf D=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\max(J^{\mathsf T}J_{ii},10^{-8})\bigr) and likewise μ0=10−6\mu_0=10^{-6} – relative to the diagonal, since D\mathbf D already carries the scale (until 2026-10 an additional factor 1n∑i(JTJ)ii\tfrac1n\sum_i(\mathbf J^{\mathsf T}\mathbf J)_{ii} stood here; the scale entered twice, and with diagonals around 10610^6 the start was fully damped). If μ\mu is small – below 10−410^{-4} in both forms – it solves JΔx=−F\mathbf J\Delta\mathbf x=-\mathbf F directly instead of the normal equations. The normal equations square the condition number; at κ≈108\kappa\approx10^{8} to 101310^{13} unscaled, κ2\kappa^2 lies beyond double precision. Hence normalisation as the default.

Limits

  • Newton finds only a local solution near the start; multiple roots, regime changes (IF/ELSE) and singularities (sonic limit) cannot be bridged. Start-value aids and continuation methods exist for that.
  • If the Jacobian is singular, no step results or it is unusable; Levenberg-Marquardt is then the better solver.
  • The statement φ'(0)=−‖F‖2\varphi'(0)=-\lVert\mathbf F\rVert^2 holds for the exact Newton step; with Broyden updates the step is only approximately a descent step. The cut-back of both Broyden solvers therefore uses the directional derivative φ'(0)=FTJΔx\varphi'(0)=\mathbf F^{\mathsf T}\mathbf J\,\Delta\mathbf x for the chosen direction, with the Jacobian J\mathbf J evaluated in this step (the common loop builds it anyway). It equals −‖F‖2-\lVert\mathbf F\rVert^2 only when Jk=J\mathbf J_k=\mathbf J, i.e. right after a rebuild. If it is not negative, the direction is no descent: then only ‖F‖\lVert\mathbf F\rVert must not grow, and the next step rebuilds J0\mathbf J_0 cold. On F=−10−3arctan⁡XF=-10^{-3}\arctan X from 1.51.5 Broyden, like Newton-Armijo, rejects the full step, takes α=1/2\alpha=1/2 and converges. (Until 2026-10 the slope was FTΔx\mathbf F^{\mathsf T}\Delta\mathbf x; with it Broyden accepted the full step there and oscillated around ±1.68\pm1.68.)
  • The common loop builds the Jacobian in every step; the Broyden solver uses it only when rebuilding. It therefore saves no evaluations, only factorisations of the true matrix.
  • The line search of Levenberg-Marquardt likewise uses the directional derivative FTJΔx\mathbf F^{\mathsf T}\mathbf J\,\Delta\mathbf x in the norm in which the decrease is measured. For the Newton step this is −‖F‖2-\lVert\mathbf F\rVert^2, for μ>0\mu>0 less (in the example with μ=1\mu=1: JΔx=(56/17,4/17)\mathbf J\Delta\mathbf x=(56/17,\;4/17), hence −224/17=−1417‖F‖2-224/17=-\tfrac{14}{17}\lVert\mathbf F\rVert^2). With the fixed slope −‖F‖2-\lVert\mathbf F\rVert^2 the search was stricter than Armijo and rejected damped steps that decreased enough. If the directional derivative is not negative, only ‖F‖\lVert\mathbf F\rVert must not grow (before, the full step was taken without a check).
  • Forward finite differences are only first-order accurate and lose digits through cancellation at very small step sizes.
