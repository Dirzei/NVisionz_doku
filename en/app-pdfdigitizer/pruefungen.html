Checking read cells – form, decimal separator, column trend, character set

The computation behind CellOcr: how column types are estimated, which gaps in numbers may be closed and which may not, and four mutually independent checks that mark a cell as uncertain – with pseudocode after the source, a worked example, a table of what each check catches, and the honest limit.

What it is about

A recogniser that is wrong rarely says so. Measured on a scanned manual: template matching without training read almost every 6 as 8 while reporting 0 % uncertain. 86.8 became 88.8 – a formally valid number, and the recogniser was certain. With performance data such a value is more dangerous than an empty cell, because it looks plausible.

The app therefore relies not on one number but on several checks that see different kinds of error. Each marks a cell as uncertain and states its reason; the view shades the cell, and the tooltip says which check it was. A marked cell is never changed silently.

Column types

Whether a cell should be a number depends on its column. Counted are the non-empty cells of a column (nn), the plain numbers (zz, form [+-]?[0-9.]+ with at least one digit) and the numbers behind a footnote marker such as “(1) 86.8” (mm):

type={mixed,m>0 and 2(z+m)>n,number,2z>n,textotherwise.\text{type} = \begin{cases} \text{mixed}, & m > 0 \text{ and } 2(z + m) > n,\\ \text{number}, & 2z > n,\\ \text{text} & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases}

With fewer than three cells every column is text. Mixed is a type of its own because both alternatives were wrong: as number, the form check flagged every cell with a marker (35 of 80 rows of a column); as text it checked nothing. The user can change any type; the checks then run again, starting from the state straight after reading – never from the already checked one, or marks from a discarded classification would remain.

Gaps in numbers

Narrow digits – above all the 1 – do not fill their character cell; the white space to their right counts as a word space for the splitter. The example manual had “31 .5” instead of 31.5 twelve times. In number and mixed columns a space is therefore removed when a number character stands on both sides and a point on one side. Without the point condition, “86 12” would become 8612. Likewise a space before a closing bracket goes (“(1 )”), and behind a leading marker exactly one space stands in mixed columns.

Nothing is changed in text columns: they contain real spaces for alignment. Nor on typeset pages whose cells come from the text layer – what is there is in the file.

Form

The form of a cell has to fit its type. Empty is never uncertain – empty means missing, not wrong. A ? (a character below the recogniser's minimum confidence) is uncertain in every column. Otherwise:

Only a marker at the start is stripped; a bracket in the middle is a misread, and that is what the check should find.

Leading zero without a decimal separator. In every number column a number is uncertain that starts with a zero followed by another digit and carries no decimal separator – 0800, -05. It is formally valid, but no table setting writes a measured value like that; it arises when the point after the zero is lost (0.800). In a column with many whole numbers the decimal check does not catch it. 0, 0.8 and 10800 stay certain; text columns are not checked (identifiers like 007).

Decimal separator

A decimal point is a small character, and small characters are the easiest to lose or confuse. But a column writes its numbers consistently. For each number or mixed column (not integer), every cell is classified as carrying a point, a comma or neither; cells with several separators (thousands grouping such as 1.234,5) or an exponent get no verdict. With p,k,op, k, o the numbers of cells with point, comma and neither, and N=p+k+o≥4N = p + k + o \ge 4:

majority={point,p≥0.8N,comma,k≥0.8N,noneotherwise.\text{majority} = \begin{cases} \text{point}, & p \ge 0.8\,N,\\ \text{comma}, & k \ge 0.8\,N,\\ \text{none} & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases}

If there is a majority, every cell with the other separator is uncertain, and so is every cell without one – the point is probably missing there. The threshold 0.8 and the minimum of 4 prevent a mixed column or one with few numbers from getting a verdict.

The other side: a decimal column in which whole values deliberately stand without a point (15 next to 15.5) gets a mark for every such value. That is intended – a missing point is the most frequent misread – but costs review time.

Column trend

The check that catches isolated confusions that form and recogniser let through. In a number column, every cell vrv_r with present neighbours is held against their mean:

er=vr−1+vr+12,uncertain if |vr−er|>max⁡(τ|vr+1−vr−1|,0.02|er|),e_r = \frac{v_{r-1} + v_{r+1}}{2}, \qquad \text{uncertain if } |v_r - e_r| > \max\big(\tau\,|v_{r+1} - v_{r-1}|,\ 0.02\,|e_r|\big),

with the tolerance τ=3\tau = 3 (adjustable). The bound grows with the span of the neighbours: where values rise quickly, more deviation is allowed. With almost equal neighbours the span would be zero and every fluctuation an outlier; that is what the minimum of 2 % of the mean is for. Values behind a footnote marker take part in the trend; a gap next to a cell leaves it without a verdict.

Only columns with a trend. A column whose values have nothing to do with each other from row to row has no trend a value could break; there almost every flag used to be a false alarm. So only a column is checked in which the two steps of each group of three mostly go the same way:

#{r:(vr−vr−1)(vr+1−vr)<0}≤max⁡ig(2,0.4Gig),\#\{r : (v_r - v_{r-1})(v_{r+1} - v_r) < 0\} \;\le\; \maxig(2,\ 0.4\,Gig),

with GG the number of groups of three present values. A rising or falling column has hardly any change of direction; a misread costs two, a block change as well, and two are always free so that in a short column the very error does not hide the trend. With unrelated values the direction changes in two of three groups. Below four groups the column is checked without this verdict. On generated tables with unrelated columns the false alarms of this check fell from 525 to 3; on performance tables 35 of 56 hits remained, the lost ones on pages whose misreads had destroyed the trend itself.

The check knows nothing about blocks. If a table falls back to a smaller value at the start of a new weight block, that looks like a drop and is reported when the drop exceeds three times the neighbour span. In the example manual that was 4 of about 2100 cells; a check that never fires wrongly would also let real errors through.

Character set

A pure digit model knows neither letters nor the degree sign. Reading a text column such as “ISA+20°C”, it confidently outputs digits and characters from its set, and the form check stays silent because a text column has no form. If the model's character set has no letters apart from e and E, every cell of a text column that does not look like a number is therefore uncertain. Numbers stay exempt because heights such as 16,000 with thousands grouping stand in text columns; “like a number” means: at most a marker in front, a sign only at the start, digits with point, comma and space, a percent sign at the end. “+20-0” is not a number.

    cells with the other or no decimal separator:  uncertain

Worked example

  • Trend, one confusion: 25.7, 30.0, 32.1, 84.3, 36.4, 38.6, 42.8 (84.3 instead of 34.3). For 84.3: e=(32.1+36.4)/2=34.25e = (32.1 + 36.4)/2 = 34.25, bound 3⋅4.3=12.93 \cdot 4.3 = 12.9, deviation 50.05 – uncertain. The neighbours stay certain: for 32.1 the span is 84.3−30.0=54.384.3 - 30.0 = 54.3, the bound 162.9.
  • Equal neighbours: 5.0, 5.0, 5.3, 5.0. For 5.3 the span is 0, the bound 2 % of 5.0 = 0.1, the deviation 0.3 – uncertain. 5.05 in the same place would stay certain.
  • Systematic: every 6 as 8 – 88.8, 87.8, 88.8, 89.8 – is a smooth trend. No check fires.
  • Decimal separator: ten numbers, eight with a point (0.8 · 10 = 8): majority point; “13,5” (comma) and “17” (none) become uncertain. With six numbers and four points (0.67) there is no majority and no verdict.

Verification

Every rule is tested on its own numbers: the forms of the types, gaps with and without a point, text columns with real spaces, the decimal check on point and comma columns including thousands grouping and exponent, the column trend with the cases of the worked example, the character set with models with and without letters. Over the whole reading path, the measurement run counts against a ground truth how many wrong cells are marked and how many correct ones are marked anyway (false alarms) – see Measuring and training.

Limits

Systematic confusions are caught by none of the checks. If a model reads every 6 as 8, the column stays smooth, every number has its form, the decimal separator is right, and the recogniser is certain. “0 % uncertain” therefore does not mean “0 % wrong”. The only remedy is a model trained on exactly this typeface – or a look at a few cells against the original.

  • The column trend compares only with the immediate neighbours and knows nothing of blocks. In a column without a trend it checks nothing – a misread there is caught only by form, decimal separator and recogniser.
  • The decimal check marks whole values in decimal columns.
  • The character set check applies only to models without letters.
