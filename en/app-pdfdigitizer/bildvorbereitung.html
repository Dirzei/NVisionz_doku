Preparing the page image – skew, specks, cell crop

The computation behind Schraeglage, Entflecken and GridDetector.zuschnitt: how the angle of a skewed page follows from the sharpness of a projection, why the points are jittered and the page rotated by nearest neighbour, which specks of ink do not count for the grid, and how a cell is cropped without cutting off the baseline with the decimal point – with pseudocode after the source, a worked example, verification and limits.

What it is about

A scan is rarely as clean as a generated page. Three disturbances cost most, and all three act before a single character is read:

DisturbanceEffect without preparationRemedy
skew of a few tenths of a degreethin rules break into two image rows across the width; the table shape is lostestimate the angle, straighten
specks between the rowsa speck is a text row of its own for the horizontal projectionremove small, isolated ink areas for the grid
fixed cell marginat low resolution the baseline lies in the margin, and the decimal point with itextend to the cell boundary while text touches the edge

Measured on 60 generated pages with varying disturbances: skewed pages 25.4 % numbers exact before straightening, 71.4 % after; at 120 and 150 dpi the fixed margin cut off the point in 71 of 254 cases.

Resolution. A scan is read at the resolution it has in the file. A page without text whose image lies in a form object is painted and then recognised like a scan – painted at the resolution of its densest image (image pixels per point of the page, the smaller ratio of width and height), at least 144 and at most 400 dpi. Before, 144 dpi applied to every such page; an image at 300 dpi lost half of its pixels, and small decimal points with them. Enlarging an image beyond its own resolution does not help: on generated pages at 120 and 150 dpi, doubling the size lowered the share of exactly read numbers from 84.7 to 77.4 %, because the image gains no information while the thresholds of the grid detection are in pixels.

Skew: the sharpest projection

If the page is skewed by the angle θ\theta, a point (x,y)(x, y) of a text row does not lie at a fixed height but at y=y0+xtan⁡θy = y_0 + x \tan\theta. Projecting all dark points at a trial angle φ\varphi onto the vertical,

uk=yk−xktan⁡φ,u_k = y_k - x_k \tan\varphi,

and counting them into bins of width Δ\Delta, all points of a row fall into few bins at φ=θ\varphi = \theta; at any other angle they smear over a strip of height W|tan⁡θ−tan⁡φ|W\,|\tan\theta - \tan\varphi|. The angle sought is the one with the greatest sharpness

S(φ)=∑bhb(φ)2,S(\varphi) = \sum_b h_b(\varphi)^2,

hbh_b the number of points in bin bb. Why the sum of squares: for a fixed number of points N=∑bhbN = \sum_b h_b it is largest when the points lie in few bins and smallest for an even spread – NN points in one bin give N2N^2, spread over kk bins only N2/kN^2/k (Cauchy–Schwarz). The method is the projection profile, one of the oldest techniques for estimating document skew.

Two details that were measured

  • Bins of a quarter pixel. With whole pixels the resolution is one pixel of offset across the width – 0.03 degrees for a page 2040 pixels wide, more for narrower pages. That much residual still made thin rules break.
  • Jitter. The coordinates are integers. At φ=0\varphi = 0 all points of an image row fall exactly into one bin, at any small angle slightly spread – the sharpness has an artificial peak at 0, and every estimate got stuck there. Each point therefore receives a fixed offset in [0,1)[0, 1) across the row, computed from its index (repeatable, no external randomness).

The search is coarse in steps of 0.1 degrees over ±3\pm 3 degrees, then fine in 0.01 degrees around the best value. The page is rotated only if the angle is at least 0.1 degrees and the sharpness gains at least 2 % over 0 degrees; otherwise the page stays the same object, pixel for pixel. Straight pages and all earlier results thus remain unchanged.

The rotation is about the image centre by nearest neighbour, not bilinear. Bilinear rotation faded thin rules after JPEG below the ink threshold of 128, and the rules route found only a 1 × 1 grid instead of 13 × 7 at 0.4 degrees.

Pseudocode – Schraeglage.schaetze

collect dark points (x, y), thinned out for large images (step s)
if fewer than 200:  return 0
z[k] ← fixed offset in [0, s) per point                      jitter
S(φ) = Σ_b h_b²  with  b = ⌊(y + z − x·tan φ) / (s/4)⌋
S₀ ← S(0)
φ* ← argmax S(φ) over φ = −3.0 … 3.0 in 0.1
φ* ← argmax S(φ) over φ* ± 0.1 in 0.01
if (S(φ*) − S₀)/S₀ < 2 %:  return 0
return φ* rounded to 0.01

Specks

A speck between two text rows is a row for the horizontal projection. On fax pages with specks the reader found 24 to 27 rows instead of 21. So what is small and stands alone is removed – only for grid detection and reading, never in the original on screen:

  • The ink is split into connected parts (8-neighbourhood).
  • The character height hzh_z is the median height of all parts from 5 pixels up to a tenth of the page. With fewer than 5 such parts nothing happens – a page without text offers no scale.
  • A part is small if its height and width are at most 0.35hz0.35\,h_z.
  • It stands alone if within 1hz1\,h_z of horizontal distance there is no character (a part 0.5hz0.5\,h_z to 3hz3\,h_z tall) overlapping its height by at least ±0.2hz\pm 0.2\,h_z.

The decimal point is small but stands next to digits at their height and stays. A tighter distance of 0.5hz0.5\,h_z was also measured: it removed more and cost 0.7 points – a speck beside a character looks like a point and is therefore kept. If nothing is to be removed, the same object comes back.

Cropping the cell

Each cell is read on its own. A fixed margin of 3 pixels keeps the rules out of the crop – and cuts off the baseline in small rows when the text reaches the rule. The decimal point disappears with the baseline, and 86.8 becomes 868.

The crop therefore starts with the fixed margin and extends row by row at the top and bottom up to the cell boundary while

  • the edge row of the crop carries text (at least one pixel with luminance below 160, the same threshold as for splitting characters), and
  • the next row is not a rule: less than half of the width dark.

If the text does not touch the edge, the crop is the same as with the fixed margin.

What happens to the crop afterwards – splitting into characters, detaching a point at a grey fringe, choosing by position in the row – is described on the page Character recognition.

Worked example

  • Angular resolution: a page 2040 pixels wide, 0.1 degrees: 2040tan⁡0.1°=3.562040 \tan 0.1° = 3.56 pixels of offset across the width – enough to split a rule 2 pixels thin into two image rows. A residual error of 0.03 degrees gives 1.07 pixels.
  • Sharpness: two horizontal rows of 1000 points each, 2 pixels tall, rotated by 0.4 degrees. In the script: S(0)=258700S(0) = 258\,700, S(0.2°)=462380S(0.2°) = 462\,380, S(0.4°)=1000008S(0.4°) = 1\,000\,008 – greatest sharpness at the true angle, a gain of 287 % over 0 degrees.
  • Search: 61 coarse angles, 21 fine – 82 projections instead of the 601 of a uniformly fine search.
  • Specks: at hz=30h_z = 30 a part up to 10 pixels is small; characters as neighbours are 15 to 90 pixels tall and may stand up to 30 pixels away.
  • Crop: a cell between rules in rows 10 and 30, text in rows 11 to 29. With the fixed margin rows 13 to 27 remain (15 rows) – ascender and baseline are missing. The crop extends to 11 and 29 (19 rows); 10 and 30 are rules and stay out.

Verification

The estimate is measured on 14 generated pages with known angles (0.06 degrees as the bound), straight pages must remain the same object under all kinds of disturbance, and a page at 0.4 degrees with JPEG must regain its table shape on the reading path. Speck removal must change nothing on clean pages, must not take a single pixel of a character (points included), and must correct the row count of a speckled page that is wrong without it. The crop is tested on a cell drawn without fonts, also over the whole reading path.

Limits

  • The estimate searches only within ±3\pm 3 degrees; pages lying sideways are turned by the view in quarters.
  • A page with very little text (under 200 dark pixels) is not estimated.
  • A speck standing next to a character at its height stays – it cannot be told from a point.
  • A decimal point touching the digit pixel to pixel (fax without grey levels) is separated neither here nor when splitting characters.
