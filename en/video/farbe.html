Colour and geometry – Background

The conversions behind Farbraum, Pegel, Chromalage and Seitenverhaeltnis: why video material is neither RGB, nor full range, nor square pixels, and how the framework converts it back.

Four assumptions that do not hold for video

A picture from an MPEG stream is not an RGB picture with values from 0 to 255 on a square grid. It consists of a luma plane Y'Y' and two colour-difference planes CbC_b, CrC_r; it uses only part of the number range; the colour planes are, at 4:2:0, only half as wide and half as high, and their sample points lie at a particular place; and the pixels are not square. Whoever overlooks one of these four gets errors that hardly show in a single picture and are obvious when two pictures are compared: colour cast, washed-out picture, colour fringes at vertical edges, faces too narrow.

Y'CbCr and RGB

The luma Y'Y' is a weighted mean of the (gamma-corrected) primaries; the colour differences are the distances of blue and red from this luma, normalised to half the range. The weights Kr,Kg,KbK_r, K_g, K_b depend on the standard: BT.601 (standard definition, PAL and NTSC) with 0.299/0.587/0.1140.299 / 0.587 / 0.114, BT.709 (high definition) with 0.2126/0.7152/0.07220.2126 / 0.7152 / 0.0722. With R,G,BR, G, B in the range 0 to 255:

Y=KrR+KgG+KbB,Cb=B−Y2(1−Kb),Cr=R−Y2(1−Kr)Y = K_r R + K_g G + K_b B, \qquad C_b = \frac{B - Y}{2(1-K_b)}, \qquad C_r = \frac{R - Y}{2(1-K_r)}

Dividing by 2(1−K)2(1-K) brings the difference into half the range. In addition comes the level mapping to the video ranges: Y'=Y⋅219255+16Y' = Y \cdot \tfrac{219}{255} + 16, C'=C⋅224255+128C' = C \cdot \tfrac{224}{255} + 128. The reverse direction stretches first, then with y=(Y'−16)255219y = (Y'-16)\tfrac{255}{219}, u=(Cb'−128)255224u = (C_b'-128)\tfrac{255}{224}, v=(Cr'−128)255224v = (C_r'-128)\tfrac{255}{224}:

R=y+2(1−Kr)v,B=y+2(1−Kb)u,G=y−KbKg2(1−Kb)u−KrKg2(1−Kr)vR = y + 2(1-K_r)\,v, \qquad B = y + 2(1-K_b)\,u, \qquad G = y - \frac{K_b}{K_g}\,2(1-K_b)\,u - \frac{K_r}{K_g}\,2(1-K_r)\,v

The result is clamped to 0 to 255. Applying BT.709 to a PAL tape (or vice versa) gives a visible colour cast, particularly in skin tones.

Pseudocode – Farbraum.nachRgb(y, cb, cr, norm)

yy ← (y − 16) · 255/219                        the level stretch is part of it
u  ← (cb − 128) · 255/224;   v ← (cr − 128) · 255/224
r  ← yy + 2·(1 − kr)·v
b  ← yy + 2·(1 − kb)·u
g  ← yy − (kb/kg)·2·(1 − kb)·u − (kr/kg)·2·(1 − kr)·v
return (clamp(r, 0, 255), clamp(g, 0, 255), clamp(b, 0, 255))

// nachYcbcr(r, g, b, norm):
y  ← kr·r + kg·g + kb·b
cb ← (b − y) / (2·(1 − kb));   cr ← (r − y) / (2·(1 − kr))
return (y·219/255 + 16,  cb·224/255 + 128,  cr·224/255 + 128)

Source: envisionz-framework-video/src/envisionz/video/farbe/Farbraum.java. ebenenNachRgb applies the same conversion pixel by pixel to whole planes.

Decision in the framework: The conversion includes the level mapping: Y' 16 becomes RGB 0, Y' 235 becomes RGB 255. Applying it once more separately stretches twice. The factor two in the colour differences belongs to the reverse direction, because the forward direction divided by 2(1−K)2(1-K); without it the forward direction still matched its target values, and only the round trip over 4096 colours showed the error (113 steps deviation, as noted in the source).

Worked example. Pure red (255,0,0)(255, 0, 0) in BT.601: Y=0.299⋅255=76.245Y = 0.299 \cdot 255 = 76.245, Cb=−76.245/1.772=−43.03C_b = -76.245 / 1.772 = -43.03, Cr=178.755/1.402=127.5C_r = 178.755 / 1.402 = 127.5. With the level mapping Y'=81.48Y' = 81.48, Cb'=90.20C_b' = 90.20, Cr'=240.00C_r' = 240.00 – CrC_r sits exactly at the upper limit, because red deflects the colour difference to the maximum. These are the values given in the class comment.

Signal range

In BT.601 black is at 16 and white at 235, not at 0 and 255; the room below and above absorbs the overshoot and undershoot of analogue transmission. The colour channels have different limits: 16 to 240 with 128 as the neutral point. A picture shown without stretching is washed out; a full-range picture interpreted as video range is clipped at top and bottom.

Yfull=(Y'−16)⋅255219,Cfull=(C'−128)⋅255224+128Y_\text{full} = (Y' - 16)\cdot\frac{255}{219}, \qquad C_\text{full} = (C' - 128)\cdot\frac{255}{224} + 128

Pegel stretches and compresses the planes with the same factors and clamps to 0 to 255. Since the indication in a file can be missing or wrong, sichtVideopegel estimates from the values: if the share of points below 15 or above 236 is under one per mille, the plane looks like video range. This is a hint, not proof – a recording without true black and true white looks the same.

Decision in the framework: Level stretch and aspect ratio belong at the end of the chain. All intermediate steps – merging, deinterlacing, deconvolution – work with the values as they stand in the file; stretching beforehand amplifies noise and loses the headroom for the overshoots. The limits are fixed as constants: HELL_SCHWARZ 16, HELL_WEISS 235, FARB_UNTEN 16, FARB_OBEN 240.

Worked example. Luma 16 becomes 0, 235 becomes 255, 125.5 (the middle) becomes 127.5; compressing, 0 becomes 16, 255 becomes 235, 127.5 becomes 125.5. The test checks the same numbers.

Position of the colour samples

At 4:2:0 there is only one colour value for every four luma values, and the two standards place it differently: MPEG-1 in the centre of the square (offset by half a luma pixel horizontally and vertically), MPEG-2 on the left luma column, vertically in the middle. Whoever makes the wrong assumption when upsampling shifts the colour by half a luma pixel against the luma – visible as a colour fringe at vertical edges that always lies on the same side and looks like a tape fault. For everything from video DVD on, MPEG-2 is the right assumption.

Chromalage.hochrechnen upsamples a colour plane bilinearly to the size of the luma plane, with the position as offset (vx,vy)(v_x, v_y) in luma pixels (MITTE 0.5/0.5; LINKS 0/0.5; OBEN_LINKS 0/0). With the ratios sx=fw/widths_x = \text{fw}/\text{width}, sy=fh/heights_y = \text{fh}/\text{height} the coordinate of the target point (x,z)(x, z) in the colour plane is

fx=(x+12−vx)sx−12,fy=(z+12−vy)sy−12f_x = (x + \tfrac12 - v_x)\, s_x - \tfrac12, \qquad f_y = (z + \tfrac12 - v_y)\, s_y - \tfrac12

and the value is mixed bilinearly from the four surrounding colour values; coordinates outside the plane are set to the edge.

Pseudocode – Chromalage.hochrechnen(farbe, fw, fh, breite, hoehe, lage)

sx ← fw / width;  sy ← fh / height
for z ← 0 to height−1:
    fy ← (z + 0.5 − position.vy) · sy − 0.5
    for x ← 0 to width−1:
        fx ← (x + 0.5 − position.vx) · sx − 0.5
        out[z·width + x] ← bilinear(colour, fw, fh, fx, fy)

// bilinear: clamp fx, fy to [0, fw−1] and [0, fh−1]; x0, y0 = integer part; x1, y1 = min(edge, +1);
// dx, dy = remainder; mix of the four neighbours with weights (1−dx)(1−dy), dx(1−dy), (1−dx)dy, dx·dy

Source: envisionz-framework-video/src/envisionz/video/farbe/Chromalage.java.

Worked example. Factor 2 horizontally, sx=0.5s_x = 0.5. The luma pixel x=1x = 1 gets, for MPEG-1 (vx=0.5v_x = 0.5), fx=(1+0.5−0.5)⋅0.5−0.5=0f_x = (1 + 0.5 - 0.5) \cdot 0.5 - 0.5 = 0, i.e. exactly the first colour value; for MPEG-2 (vx=0v_x = 0) fx=0.25f_x = 0.25, i.e. 75%75\,\% of the first and 25%25\,\% of the second. Non-square pixels

PAL 720 × 576 is not 5:4, although the numbers suggest it. The pixel is narrower than high: at 4:3 in the ratio 16:15, at 16:9 in the ratio 64:45. Only the conversion turns 720 × 576 into a picture of 768 × 576 square pixels. Whoever omits it gets faces that are too narrow, and every shape analysis, edge direction or motion estimate works with distorted distances. The target width is the height times the aspect ratio:

Wtarget=round⁡(H⋅zn),576⋅43=768W_\text{target} = \operatorname{round}\!\left(H \cdot \frac{z}{n}\right), \qquad 576 \cdot \tfrac{4}{3} = 768

Conversion is horizontal only, with a Lanczos kernel of order 3: L(t)=3sin⁡(πt)sin⁡(πt/3)/(πt)2L(t) = 3\,\sin(\pi t)\,\sin(\pi t/3) / (\pi t)^2 for |t|≤3|t| \le 3, otherwise 0. It keeps fine structure better than a bilinear conversion and produces less overshoot than a pure sinc kernel; when shrinking, the kernel grows with the factor to avoid aliasing. The weights are divided by their sum.

Pseudocode – Seitenverhaeltnis.waagerechtUmrechnen(ebene, breite, hoehe, neueBreite)

s ← width / newWidth
reach ← max(1, s) · 3
for z ← 0 to height−1, x ← 0 to newWidth−1:
    centre ← (x + 0.5)·s − 0.5
    sum ← 0;  weight ← 0
    for q ← ⌊centre − reach⌋ to ⌈centre + reach⌉:
        qq ← clamp(q, 0, width−1)
        g  ← lanczos((q − centre) / max(1, s), 3)
        sum ← sum + g · plane[z, qq];  weight ← weight + g
    out[z, x] ← if weight ≠ 0 then sum / weight else 0

Source: envisionz-framework-video/src/envisionz/video/farbe/Seitenverhaeltnis.java (zielbreite, waagerechtUmrechnen).

Decision in the framework: Conversion happens only at the end of the chain. Enlarging beforehand would only increase the amount of data without adding information, and would defeat the purpose of merging. The kernel is meant for changes of a few percent, not for large changes of scale.

How it is tested

Dekoderprobe secures the colour space at the eight primary colours of both standards with hand-calculated target values and the level limits 16 to 235 and 16 to 240. The level calculation is checked to 10−910^{-9}, the round trip RGB → Y'CbCr → RGB over all colours to 10−910^{-9}, and the conversion of whole planes against the single conversion to 10−1210^{-12}. Colour differences outside the limits stay within 0 to 255. The chroma position is tested with a ramp: a uniform plane stays uniform at every position, and upsampled, MPEG-1 and MPEG-2 lie exactly half a luma pixel apart in the interior (difference −0.25-0.25 in units of the colour plane, tolerance 10−910^{-9}). The aspect ratio is tested with 576 lines: 768 columns at 4:3, 1024 at 16:9; converted to the same width, nothing changes. According to the overview, the classes of package farbe have no caller outside the tests yet.

Limits

  • Only Y'CbCr according to BT.601 and BT.709 is distinguished; other standards and gamma curves are not converted by the package. The decoder reads the transfer characteristic from the display extension but does not use it here.
  • sichtVideopegel only estimates; for recordings without deep black and bright white it can be wrong.
  • Upsampling the colour is bilinear; edge-guided methods are in framework-restore.
  • The aspect-ratio conversion is horizontal and meant for small changes.
