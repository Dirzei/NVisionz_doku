Reconstruction – Background

From stored numbers to the picture: inverse quantisation, inverse cosine transform and motion compensation in Quantisierung, Idct and Bewegungskompensation. Formulas, pseudocode after the source, testing and limits.

What an MPEG decoder reconstructs

MPEG-1 and MPEG-2 do not store a picture as pixels. The picture is cut into blocks of 8×88 \times 8 pixels, each block is turned into 64 frequency components by the cosine transform, and these components are coarsely rounded: little precision for fine structure, more for the broad shape. Only the rounded integers are in the stream. For predicted pictures (P and B) the block itself is not stored either, but the residual against a displaced patch of an earlier or later picture.

The decoder undoes this in three steps: numbers back to frequency components (Quantisierung), frequency components back to pixels (Idct), and, where prediction was used, fetch the patch from the reference picture and add the residual (Bewegungskompensation). What was lost in rounding does not come back – the reverse calculation restores only the order of magnitude. This is why the quantiser step of a region measures how robust that region is.

Inverse quantisation

When encoding, each frequency component was divided by the product of an entry of the matrix WiW_i and the step qq, and rounded. The matrix fixes which frequencies may be rounded more coarsely in general – the default matrix for intra blocks grows from 8 (DC) to 83 (highest frequency); the one for predicted blocks is 16 everywhere. The encoder adjusts the step qq per macroblock through the step code kk (1 to 31).

For MPEG-2, with the value read qfi≠0\text{qf}_i \ne 0 and the result FiF_i:

Fi=2qfiWiq32(intra),Fi=(2qfi+sgn⁡(qfi))Wiq32(predicted)F_i = \frac{2\,\text{qf}_i\,W_i\,q}{32} \quad\text{(intra)}, \qquad F_i = \frac{\bigl(2\,\text{qf}_i + \operatorname{sgn}(\text{qf}_i)\bigr)\,W_i\,q}{32} \quad\text{(predicted)}

The division truncates towards zero. The summand sgn⁡(qfi)\operatorname{sgn}(\text{qf}_i) for predicted blocks compensates that the encoder rounded towards zero: it moves the value to the middle of the interval it can come from. The step follows from the code either linearly, q=2kq = 2k, or non-linearly from a table (q_scale_type) that is finer at the bottom and coarser at the top: k=1…8k = 1 \ldots 8 gives q=1…8q = 1 \ldots 8, k=16k = 16 gives 24, k=31k = 31 gives 112.

Two special treatments: the DC component F0F_0 of intra blocks bypasses the matrix, F0=qf0⋅(8≫d)F_0 = \text{qf}_0 \cdot (8 \gg d) with the DC precision d=0…3d = 0 \ldots 3. And at the end the sum of all 64 values is made odd: if it is even, the lowest bit of F63F_{63} is toggled. This keeps rounding differences of the inverse transform between encoder and decoder from building up over a group of pictures.

MPEG-1 calculates differently, namely with the magnitude: |Fi|=(|qfi|⋅q⋅Wi)≫3|F_i| = (|\text{qf}_i| \cdot q \cdot W_i) \gg 3 with q=kq = k (not doubled), then (|Fi|−1)|1(|F_i| - 1)\,|\,1 for |Fi|≠0|F_i| \ne 0 – every value is made odd –, and the sign is applied last. Shifting with the sign would be wrong: −5≫3-5 \gg 3 gives −1-1, −(5≫3)-(5 \gg 3) gives 0.

Pseudocode – Quantisierung.zurueck(koeffizienten, innerbildlich, kennwert)

m ← if intra then intraMatrix else interMatrix
q ← stufe(code)             MPEG-1: k; MPEG-2: 2k or table 7-6
sum ← 0
for i ← 0 to 63:
    qf ← coefficients[i]
    if i = 0 and intra:            f ← qf · (8 >> dcPrecision)
    else if qf = 0:                 f ← 0
    else if intra:                  f ← (2·qf·m[i]·q) / 32          integer, towards zero
    else:                           f ← ((2·qf + sgn(qf))·m[i]·q) / 32

    if MPEG-1 and not (i = 0 and intra) and qf ≠ 0:
        mag ← (|qf|·q·m[i]) >> 3
        if mag ≠ 0: mag ← (mag − 1) or 1                make odd
        f ← if qf < 0 then −min(mag, 2048) else min(mag, 2047)
        out[i] ← f;  sum ← sum + f;  continue

    out[i] ← clamp(f, −2048, 2047)
    sum ← sum + out[i]

if not MPEG-1 and sum even:                       mismatch control
    out[63] ← if out[63] odd then out[63] − 1 else out[63] + 1
    out[63] ← clamp(out[63], −2048, 2047)
return out

Source: envisionz-framework-video/src/envisionz/video/mpeg/Quantisierung.java. The matrices are held in raster order; matrixLesen reads them from the stream in zigzag order and places them in the raster via Abtastfolge.ZICKZACK. At every sequence header without a custom matrix the default matrix applies again (zuruecksetzen).

Decision in the framework: Matrices, step type, DC precision and the MPEG-1 branch are state of the Quantisierung object, not constants; the matrices are reset at every sequence header, the DC precision is limited to 0 to 3. Values are limited to −2048 to 2047. A factor of two in the step makes a wrongly read picture coarse but not obviously wrong – hence it is explicitly commented in the source.

Inverse cosine transform

The two-dimensional transform of an 8×88 \times 8 block with frequency components F(u,v)F(u,v) to pixels f(x,y)f(x,y):

f(x,y)=14∑u=07∑v=07cucvF(u,v)cos⁡(2x+1)uπ16cos⁡(2y+1)vπ16,c0=12,cu>0=1f(x,y) = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{u=0}^{7} \sum_{v=0}^{7} c_u c_v \, F(u,v) \cos\frac{(2x+1)u\pi}{16} \cos\frac{(2y+1)v\pi}{16}, \qquad c_0 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2},\; c_{u>0} = 1

The standard does not prescribe a bit-exact implementation – cosine values cannot be represented finitely – but, through IEEE 1180, how far an implementation may deviate. Idct calculates in double precision and rounds only at the end; for restoration this is the better trade against computing time, because every rounding enters all following steps.

The sum is separable: transform each row first, then each column. Instead of 4096 products per block that is 2⋅8⋅64=10242 \cdot 8 \cdot 64 = 1024, mathematically the same. The factors 12cucos⁡(2x+1)uπ16\tfrac12 c_u \cos\frac{(2x+1)u\pi}{16} are precomputed once.

Pseudocode – Idct.rueck(f)

// KOS[x][u] = c(u) · cos((2x+1)·u·π/16) / 2, computed once when the class loads
for z ← 0 to 7:                         1. rows
    for x ← 0 to 7:
        tmp[z,x] ← sum over u = 0..7 of KOS[x][u] · f[z,u]
for x ← 0 to 7:                         2. columns
    for z ← 0 to 7:
        s ← sum over v = 0..7 of KOS[z][v] · tmp[v,x]
        out[z,x] ← clamp(round(s), −256, 255)
return out

Source: envisionz-framework-video/src/envisionz/video/mpeg/Idct.java. Idct.hin is the forward direction with the same factors; a decoder does not need it, it serves the test (forward and back).

Decision in the framework: The result is limited to −256 to 255; rounding happens once, at the end of the second axis, not after the first.

Worked example. If only the DC component F(0,0)=1024F(0,0) = 1024 is set, f(x,y)=14⋅12⋅1024=128f(x,y) = \tfrac14 \cdot \tfrac12 \cdot 1024 = 128 for all 64 points: a flat block of value 128. This is also the “DC alone” test and fits DC precision d=0d = 0: a value read of 128 becomes 1024 with 8≫0=88 \gg 0 = 8.

Motion compensation

A predicted macroblock is not in the stream; the stream says where it lies in the reference picture, as a motion vector in half pixels, and the residual against that patch. The vector (vx,vz)(v_x, v_z) splits into an integer part g=v≫1g = v \gg 1 and a half-pixel bit v&1v \,\&\, 1. The shift rounds down; for negative vectors a division towards zero would make the remainder jump across the sign. Four cases follow:

Half pixelValue of the pixel
noneaa
horizontal(a+b+1)≫1(a + b + 1) \gg 1 with the right neighbour bb
vertical(a+c+1)≫1(a + c + 1) \gg 1 with the lower neighbour cc
both(a+b+c+d+2)≫2(a + b + c + d + 2) \gg 2

Rounding is up to the nearest integer (add 1 before halving, 2 before quartering). This is mandatory: an encoder relies on it, and rounding down deviates by one at every second pixel – which adds up over a group of pictures, because P pictures build on each other. With two prediction directions (B pictures) the two predictions are averaged with (a+b+1)≫1(a + b + 1) \gg 1. For the chroma planes the vectors are to be halved, because the planes are half the size. Points outside the plane continue from the edge.

holenAusHalbbild fetches the same prediction from one field (every second line): the vertical vector then counts in field lines, the block is read in the field raster and stored in picture lines.

Pseudocode – Bewegungskompensation.holen(bezug, …, x, z, bw, bh, vx, vz, ziel)

gx ← vx >> 1;  gz ← vz >> 1                      the shift rounds down
halfX ← (vx and 1) ≠ 0;  halfZ ← (vz and 1) ≠ 0
for j ← 0 to bh−1, i ← 0 to bw−1:
    px ← x + i + gx;  pz ← z + j + gz
    if not halfX and not halfZ:  w ← P(px, pz)
    else if halfX and not halfZ:  w ← (P(px,pz) + P(px+1,pz) + 1) >> 1
    else if not halfX:                w ← (P(px,pz) + P(px,pz+1) + 1) >> 1
    else:                             w ← (P(px,pz) + P(px+1,pz) + P(px,pz+1) + P(px+1,pz+1) + 2) >> 2
    target[j·bw + i] ← w

// P(x, z): pixel of the reference plane, coordinates clamped to 0..width−1 and 0..height−1
// mitteln(a, b): target[i] ← (a[i] + b[i] + 1) >> 1

Source: envisionz-framework-video/src/envisionz/video/mpeg/Bewegungskompensation.java (holen, holenAusHalbbild, mitteln). The dual-prime prediction is derived by Zweifach and not described here.

Worked example. With neighbours a=100a = 100 and b=103b = 103 the horizontal half pixel is (100+103+1)≫1=102(100 + 103 + 1) \gg 1 = 102; rounding down would give 101. Four pixels 10, 11, 12, 14 give (47+2)≫2=12(47 + 2) \gg 2 = 12.

How it is tested

Without a reference stream, with anchors that can be calculated in closed form (Dekoderprobe, 605 checks according to the module description):

  • Idct against the double sum written out in the test itself: all 64 basis functions one at a time, DC alone, zero block, clamping. The integer output may deviate by at most 0.5+10−60.5 + 10^{-6} from the unrounded formula, Idct.hin matches the formula to better than 10−910^{-9}, and forward and back reproduces the pixels to better than 10−910^{-9}.
  • Quantisation against tables 7-1 and 7-6 of the standard.
  • Motion compensation with 49 integer and 49 half-pixel vectors, plus the single cases: mean of two neighbours rounded up, far outside gives the corner value (not zero), the top field delivers only even lines, the bottom field only odd ones.

A confirmation of the overall result against the normative reference software (ISO/IEC TR 13818-5) is outstanding; it is not available in the workspace. Measured against ffmpeg the decoder is at 69.7 to 76.4 dB (I, P and B pictures); the remainder is the rounding tolerance of ±1 permitted by IEEE 1180, which accumulates over a group of pictures.

Limits

  • Inverse quantisation restores nothing that was rounded away in encoding.
  • Idct is not the integer transform of other decoders; results may differ within the rounding allowed by IEEE 1180. It is slower than an integer implementation.
  • Vectors count in half pixels; quarter pixels (as in newer methods) do not exist.
  • A B picture without both reference pictures cannot be decoded. Unsuitable slices are rejected and counted, missing macroblocks concealed on request – the picture gets worse, the run does not abort.
