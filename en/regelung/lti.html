Linear time-invariant systems – background

The computation behind Uebertragungsfunktion, Wurzelortskurve and Zustandsraum: combining transfer functions, frequency response with continuous phase, margins, the Nyquist criterion as angle counting, asymptotes and breakaway points of the root locus, canonical form and Faddeev-LeVerrier, the zero-order hold via the matrix exponential and pole placement after Ackermann.

What it is about

A transfer function G(s)=Z(s)/N(s)G(s) = Z(s)/N(s) describes a linear time-invariant single-input single-output system in the Laplace domain. From it follow stability (poles on the left), the behaviour under sinusoidal excitation (frequency response G(iω)G(i\omega)) and – for the open loop L=GRL = G R – the stability of the closed loop. The state space x˙=Ax+Bu\dot x = A x + B u, y=Cx+Duy = C x + D u is the equivalent time-domain form and the basis for discretisation and state controllers.

Combination and frequency response

Polynomials are stored with ascending coefficients; poles and zeros are the eigenvalues of the companion matrix (QR method from framework-math). Combination is done without cancellation:

G1G2=Z1Z2N1N2,G1+G2=Z1N2+Z2N1N1N2,G1+GH=ZNHNNH+ZZH.G_1 G_2 = \frac{Z_1 Z_2}{N_1 N_2}, \qquad G_1 + G_2 = \frac{Z_1 N_2 + Z_2 N_1}{N_1 N_2}, \qquad \frac{G}{1 + G H} = \frac{Z\,N_H}{N\,N_H + Z\,Z_H}.

The feedback formula follows from Y=G(W−HY)Y = G\,(W - H Y): Y(1+GH)=GWY(1 + GH) = GW, with G=Z/NG = Z/N, H=ZH/NHH = Z_H/N_H multiplied through by NNHN N_H. Nothing is cancelled, because a common factor in numerator and denominator only vanishes in exact arithmetic; a numerically almost cancelled pole would be a silent source of error. The poles are the eigenvalues of the companion matrix of the monic denominator sn+cn−1sn−1+…+c0s^n + c_{n-1}s^{n-1} + \dots + c_0:

C=(−cn−1−cn−2⋯−c010⋯0⋱⋱⋮010),det⁡(sI−C)=sn+cn−1sn−1+…+c0.C = \begin{pmatrix} -c_{n-1} & -c_{n-2} & \cdots & -c_0 \\ 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ & \ddots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det(sI - C) = s^n + c_{n-1}s^{n-1} + \dots + c_0 .

This is the route of MATLAB's roots: the QR method on the companion matrix is backward stable for the matrix, not for the coefficients; multiple roots are only accurate to about ε1/m\varepsilon^{1/m} (multiplicity mm).

The magnitude |G(iω)||G(i\omega)| comes from Horner's scheme with complex argument. The phase, however, is formed as the sum of the angles of the linear factors,

arg⁡G(iω)=arg⁡zmnm+∑karg⁡(iω−zk)−∑karg⁡(iω−pk),\arg G(i\omega) = \arg\frac{z_m}{n_m} + \sum_k \arg(i\omega - z_k) - \sum_k \arg(i\omega - p_k),

so it does not jump at ±180°: a third-order lag runs continuously down to −270°. The gain margin is 1/|L|1/|L| at the phase crossover (−180°), the phase margin 180°+arg⁡L180° + \arg L at the gain crossover (|L|=1|L| = 1); both crossovers are found on a logarithmic grid and refined by bisection.

Why the margins mean something. The closed loop becomes unstable at the stability boundary exactly when L(iω)=−1L(i\omega) = -1 for some ω\omega. The gain margin says by which factor the gain may grow until the locus hits −1-1 at −180°-180°; the phase margin, how much extra phase lag (such as a dead time TtT_t with −ωTt-\omega T_t) fits in at magnitude 1. Both hold only if the loop was stable without the change and the locus crosses the critical point only once – the reliable verdict comes from the Nyquist criterion.

Worked example. L=4/(s+1)3L = 4/(s+1)^3: phase crossover at ω=3\omega = \sqrt3, |L|=4/8|L| = 4/8, gain margin 2; gain crossover at ωD=42/3−1=1.2328\omega_D = \sqrt{4^{2/3} - 1} = 1.2328, phase margin 180°−3arctan⁡ωD=27.1°180° - 3\arctan\omega_D = 27.1°.

Nyquist criterion

The closed loop has Z=P−NZ = P - N unstable poles if LL has PP unstable poles and the locus L(iω)L(i\omega), ω\omega from −∞-\infty to ∞\infty, encircles the point −1-1 NN times counter-clockwise. The angle of 1+L(iω)1 + L(i\omega) is counted, with ω=tan⁡t\omega = \tan t and tt uniform in (−π/2,π/2)(-\pi/2, \pi/2), so that the far frequencies are included; each angle increment is wrapped to (−π,π](-\pi, \pi]. Poles on the imaginary axis are rejected (the contour would need a detour there).

Derivation. The closed-loop poles are the zeros of F=1+L=(N+Z)/NF = 1 + L = (N + Z)/N. By the argument principle F(s)F(s) turns once clockwise per zero and once counter-clockwise per pole when ss traverses a curve clockwise. If ss runs along the imaginary axis from −i∞-i\infty to +i∞+i\infty and back over the large right half-circle (for proper LL, FF is constant there), exactly the zeros ZZ and poles PP in the right half-plane are enclosed: counter-clockwise encirclements of 0 by FF are N=P−ZN = P - Z, hence Z=P−NZ = P - N. Encirclements of 0 by FF are encirclements of −1-1 by LL.

Z=P−12π[arg⁡(1+L(iω))]ω=−∞∞.Z = P - \frac{1}{2\pi}\Big[\arg\big(1 + L(i\omega)\big)\Big]_{\omega = -\infty}^{\infty}.

The count needs enough points that the angle changes by less than π\pi between neighbours; there are 200,000 values over tt. If the locus passes very close to −1-1 (margin almost zero), that is no longer guaranteed – then the result itself is on a knife edge. stabil(), in contrast, only asks the poles of GG itself: all with negative real part; poles on the axis (integrator, undamped oscillator) do not count as stable.

Root locus

The poles of the loop N+KZ=0N + K Z = 0 move with KK from the poles to the zeros; d=deg⁡N−deg⁡Zd = \deg N - \deg Z branches go to infinity along asymptotes with centroid and angles

σa=∑pk−∑zkd,θk=(2k+1)180°d.\sigma_a = \frac{\sum p_k - \sum z_k}{d}, \qquad \theta_k = \frac{(2k+1)\,180°}{d}.

Derivation. A point ss lies on the locus if K=−N(s)/Z(s)K = -N(s)/Z(s) is real and positive – magnitude condition |Z/N|=1/K|Z/N| = 1/K, angle condition arg⁡(Z/N)=180°+k360°\arg(Z/N) = 180° + k\,360°. For large |s||s|, N/Z=sd−(∑pk−∑zk)sd−1+…N/Z = s^d - (\sum p_k - \sum z_k)\,s^{d-1} + \dots, which agrees with (s−σa)d(s - \sigma_a)^d in the two highest terms. Far out the locus therefore behaves like (s−σa)d=−K(s - \sigma_a)^d = -K: dd rays from σa\sigma_a at the angles whose dd-fold is 180°180°.

Breakaway points lie where K(s)=−N(s)/Z(s)K(s) = -N(s)/Z(s) is stationary: real roots of NZ'−N'ZN Z' - N' Z with K>0K > 0 (there two branches meet and dK/ds=0dK/ds = 0; from K=−N/ZK = -N/Z, K'=−(N'Z−NZ')/Z2K' = -(N'Z - NZ')/Z^2). The critical gain is the smallest KK at which a pole reaches the imaginary axis. For 1/(s(s+1)(s+2))1/(s(s+1)(s+2)): centroid −1, angles 60°, 180°, 300°, breakaway at −1+1/3-1 + 1/\sqrt3 with K=2/(33)K = 2/(3\sqrt3), critical K=6K = 6 with poles at ±i2\pm i\sqrt2 (Hurwitz: s3+3s2+2s+Ks^3 + 3s^2 + 2s + K is stable as long as 3⋅2>K3 \cdot 2 > K).

The critical gain is searched on a grid of KK up to an upper bound and then by bisection; an integrator in the open loop lies on the axis itself at K=0K = 0, so the search starts just above. If the loop never becomes unstable up to the bound (such as 1/((s+1)(s+2))1/((s+1)(s+2)), whose two branches run parallel to the imaginary axis), the result is NaN.

State space

Canonical form. From GG the controllable canonical form (companion matrix of the denominator) is built; if GG is biproper, D=zn/nnD = z_n/n_n is split off first: with a monic denominator G=D+(Z−DN)/NG = D + (Z - D N)/N, the remainder is strictly proper, and its coefficients are the row CC. Back goes via the Faddeev-LeVerrier method without eigenvalues: with M0=0M_0 = 0, cn=1c_n = 1

Mk=AMk−1+cn−k+1I,cn−k=−tr⁡(AMk)k,adj⁡(sI−A)=∑kMksn−k,M_k = A M_{k-1} + c_{n-k+1} I, \qquad c_{n-k} = -\frac{\operatorname{tr}(A M_k)}{k}, \qquad \operatorname{adj}(sI - A) = \sum_k M_k\, s^{n-k},

the numerator is Cadj⁡(sI−A)B+Ddet⁡(sI−A)C\,\operatorname{adj}(sI - A)\,B + D\,\det(sI - A).

Why Faddeev-LeVerrier is right. From (sI−A)adj⁡(sI−A)=det⁡(sI−A)I(sI - A)\,\operatorname{adj}(sI - A) = \det(sI - A)\,I the recursion for MkM_k follows by comparing coefficients; the trace gives the coefficients because ddsdet⁡(sI−A)=tr⁡adj⁡(sI−A)\frac{d}{ds}\det(sI - A) = \operatorname{tr}\operatorname{adj}(sI - A) (Jacobi's formula). The method is exact in exact arithmetic, but badly conditioned in floating point for high orders – for the usual orders up to about 10 it suffices.

Zero-order hold. With piecewise constant input (sampling time TT) xk+1=Adxk+Bdukx_{k+1} = A_d x_k + B_d u_k with

exp⁡((AB00)T)=(AdBd0I);\exp\!\Big(\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} T\Big) = \begin{pmatrix} A_d & B_d \\ 0 & I \end{pmatrix};

Derivation: between two samples uu is constant, variation of constants gives x(T)=eATx(0)+∫0TeAτdτBux(T) = e^{AT}x(0) + \int_0^T e^{A\tau}\,d\tau\,B\,u. Both blocks appear in the exponential of the augmented matrix – without AA having to be invertible (the formula A−1(eAT−I)BA^{-1}(e^{AT} - I)B already fails for the integrator). The step response at the sampling instants is therefore exact, not approximate.

the matrix exponential comes from framework-math (Padé 13 with scaling and squaring). For the double integrator this gives exactly Ad=[[1,T],[0,1]]A_d = [[1, T], [0, 1]], Bd=[T2/2,T]B_d = [T^2/2, T].

Ackermann. For one input u=−kTxu = -k^T x places the poles at the roots of a desired polynomial pp:

kT=enTQs−1p(A),Qs=[B,AB,…,An−1B].k^T = e_n^T\,Q_s^{-1}\,p(A), \qquad Q_s = [B,\ AB,\ \dots,\ A^{n-1}B].

Derivation. In controllable canonical form A−BkTA - Bk^T is again a companion matrix whose last row carries the closed-loop coefficients; kk is the difference of the coefficients there. For arbitrary AA, the theorem of Cayley-Hamilton (ploop(A−BkT)=0p_\text{loop}(A - Bk^T) = 0) and the controllability matrix give the coordinate-free form above. Example: double integrator, desired poles −1-1 and −2-2, i.e. p=s2+3s+2p = s^2 + 3s + 2: k=[2,3]k = [2,\ 3]. The formula is numerically delicate as soon as QsQ_s is ill-conditioned (nearly uncontrollable); it is meant for small orders.

If QsQ_s is singular (not controllable), the computation ends with a message. Controllability and observability rank are the ranks of QsQ_s and of the corresponding observability matrix (duality: (A,C)(A, C) is observable exactly when (AT,CT)(A^T, C^T) is controllable). The rank is counted from the singular values of QQ (Golub-Kahan from framework-math); a singular value below max⁡(m,n)εσmax\max(m, n)\,\varepsilon\,\sigma_\text{max} counts as zero. Until 2026-10-19 the computation went through the eigenvalues of QQTQ Q^T, which squares the condition; the threshold corresponded to a singular value of about 10−10nσmax10^{-10}\sqrt n\,\sigma_\text{max}, and a controllable but badly scaled system such as A=diag⁡(1,2)A = \operatorname{diag}(1, 2), b=(1,10−12)Tb = (1, 10^{-12})^T counted as not controllable.

How it is checked

  • 1/(s+1)1/(s+1) and 2/(s+3)2/(s+3): series, parallel and feedback by hand, poles −3 and −1, Horner at 1+2i1 + 2i by hand.
  • First-order lag: magnitude 1/1+ω21/\sqrt{1+\omega^2}, phase −arctan⁡ω-\arctan\omega; third-order lag continuous down to −267° at ω=100\omega = 100 (1e-12).
  • Margins of 4/(s+1)34/(s+1)^3 in closed form to 1e-9; Nyquist for 4/(s+1)34/(s+1)^3, 10/(s+1)310/(s+1)^3, 2/(s−1)2/(s-1), 0.5/(s−1)0.5/(s-1) against the closed-loop poles.
  • Root locus as above; with a zero at −3 and four poles centroid −4/3.
  • Canonical form and back equal to GG at three complex points (1e-12); zero-order hold on the double integrator (1e-15); step response of 1/(2s+1)1/(2s+1) exact at the sampling instants; ranks; Ackermann places s2+s−2s^2 + s - 2 at −2±i-2 \pm i. and the double integrator with real desired poles at k=[2,3]k = [2, 3].
  • stabil(): an integrator and an undamped oscillator do not count as stable; critical gain NaN if the loop never becomes unstable; accessors return copies.

Limits

Single-input single-output systems for transfer functions, root locus and Ackermann; no dead time; poles on the imaginary axis not allowed for the Nyquist criterion. Multiple poles and zeros only accurate to about ε1/m\varepsilon^{1/m}; rank decision with the threshold max⁡(m,n)εσmax\max(m, n)\,\varepsilon\,\sigma_\text{max} (see above).
