PID and optimal control – background

The computation behind Pid and Optimal: the discrete PID with filtered derivative on the measurement, actuator limits with back-calculation, tuning rules; the algebraic Riccati equations via the matrix sign function and the doubling method, LQR and steady-state Kalman filter.

What it is about

The PID controller is the standard of practice: proportional, integral and derivative action on the control error e=w−ye = w - y. For multivariable systems with a state model, optimal control gives a feedback u=−Kxu = -K x that minimises a quadratic cost; its counterpart is the Kalman filter, which estimates the state from noisy measurements.

The discrete PID

In continuous time the controller with filtered derivative on the measurement reads

U(s)=Kp(1+1Tis)E(s)−KpTds1+TfsY(s),Tf=Td/N.U(s) = K_p\Big(1 + \frac{1}{T_i s}\Big)E(s) - K_p\,\frac{T_d\,s}{1 + T_f s}\,Y(s), \qquad T_f = T_d/N .

The filter limits the high-frequency gain of the derivative to KpNK_p N – without it, measurement noise would be amplified without bound. Per sampling step TsT_s schritt(w, y) computes:

v=Kpe+I+D,u=sat⁡(v),I←I+KpTsTie+TsTt(u−v).v = K_p e + I + D, \qquad u = \operatorname{sat}(v), \qquad I \leftarrow I + \frac{K_p T_s}{T_i}\,e + \frac{T_s}{T_t}\,(u - v).

This is back-calculation (Åström and Hägglund): the integrator follows I˙=Kpe/Ti+(u−v)/Tt\dot I = K_p e/T_i + (u - v)/T_t. As long as the actuator is not limited, u=vu = v and the second term vanishes; once it saturates, the term pulls the integrator back with time constant TtT_t so that vv stays at the limit. Without it, II keeps growing during saturation (windup), and the controller overshoots until the store has emptied again. Usual choice: TtT_t between TdT_d and TiT_i.

The derivative acts on the measurement, not on the error – a setpoint step thus causes no actuator kick – and is filtered with the time constant Tf=Td/NT_f = T_d/N (implicit Euler):

D←TfTf+TsD−KpTdTf+Ts(yk−yk−1).D \leftarrow \frac{T_f}{T_f + T_s}\,D - \frac{K_p T_d}{T_f + T_s}\,(y_k - y_{k-1}).

Derivation. From (1+Tfs)D=−KpTdsY(1 + T_f s)\,D = -K_p T_d\,s\,Y with the implicit Euler method s≈(1−z−1)/Tss \approx (1 - z^{-1})/T_s: Dk(1+Tf/Ts)−(Tf/Ts)Dk−1=−(KpTd/Ts)(yk−yk−1)D_k(1 + T_f/T_s) - (T_f/T_s)D_{k-1} = -(K_p T_d/T_s)(y_k - y_{k-1}), solved for DkD_k the formula above. The implicit method is stable for every TsT_s (the factor Tf/(Tf+Ts)T_f/(T_f + T_s) lies in (0,1)(0, 1)); on a measurement ramp of slope 1, DD tends to −KpTd-K_p T_d, precisely Dk=−KpTd(1−ak−1)D_k = -K_p T_d\big(1 - a^{k-1}\big) with a=Tf/(Tf+Ts)a = T_f/(T_f + T_s).

Without integral action (Ti=∞T_i = \infty) an error remains: on a first-order lag with gain KK it is 1/(1+KKp)1/(1 + K K_p) of the setpoint – by the final value theorem e(∞)=lims→0⁡1/(1+L(s))e(\infty) = \lim_{s \to 0} 1/(1 + L(s)) with L(0)=KKpL(0) = K K_p. With integral action L(0)=∞L(0) = \infty, and the error vanishes.

Tuning rules. After the Ziegler-Nichols oscillation test (ultimate gain KuK_u, period TuT_u): Kp=0.6KuK_p = 0.6 K_u, Ti=Tu/2T_i = T_u/2, Td=Tu/8T_d = T_u/8. From the step response (gain KK, delay time TuT_u, rise time TgT_g): Ziegler-Nichols Kp=1.2Tg/(KTu)K_p = 1.2\,T_g/(K T_u), Ti=2TuT_i = 2T_u, Td=Tu/2T_d = T_u/2; Chien-Hrones-Reswick (aperiodic, setpoint) Kp=0.6Tg/(KTu)K_p = 0.6\,T_g/(K T_u), Ti=TgT_i = T_g, Td=Tu/2T_d = T_u/2.

The rules are empirical, not derivations: Ziegler and Nichols (1942) aimed at a decay to a quarter per oscillation, which is often too little damped; Chien, Hrones and Reswick (1952) gave sets for aperiodic setpoint response. They provide starting values to be retuned on the plant.

Riccati equations, LQR and Kalman

The LQR minimises ∫(xTQx+uTRu)dt\int (x^T Q x + u^T R u)\,dt for x˙=Ax+Bu\dot x = A x + B u. The solution is u=−Kxu = -K x with K=R−1BTXK = R^{-1} B^T X, where XX is the stabilising solution of the continuous algebraic Riccati equation:

ATX+XA−XBR−1BTX+Q=0.A^T X + X A - X B R^{-1} B^T X + Q = 0.

Derivation. For a quadratic value function V(x)=xTXxV(x) = x^T X x the optimality condition (Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman) requires 0=minu⁡[xTQx+uTRu+2xTX(Ax+Bu)]0 = \min_u \big[x^T Q x + u^T R u + 2x^T X (A x + B u)\big]. The minimum over uu lies at u=−R−1BTXxu = -R^{-1}B^T X x; substituted, a quadratic form in xx remains that vanishes for all xx – exactly the Riccati equation. Of its solutions the one sought makes A−BKA - BK stable; it exists if (A,B)(A, B) is stabilisable and (A,Q1/2)(A, Q^{1/2}) detectable. Scalar (aa, bb, qq, rr): x=(a+a2+b2q/r)r/b2x = \big(a + \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 q/r}\big)\,r/b^2, the positive root.

It is solved via the Hamiltonian matrix H=[[A,−G],[−Q,−AT]]H = [[A, -G], [-Q, -A^T]], G=BR−1BTG = B R^{-1} B^T, and its matrix sign function with Newton's method and determinant scaling:

Z←12(cZ+Z−1/c),c=|det⁡Z|−1/(2n).Z \leftarrow \tfrac12\big(c\,Z + Z^{-1}/c\big), \qquad c = |\det Z|^{-1/(2n)}.

From W=sign⁡(H)W = \operatorname{sign}(H) follows XX as the solution of the overdetermined system [W12;W22+I]X=−[W11+I;W21][W_{12};\ W_{22} + I]\,X = -[W_{11} + I;\ W_{21}] (least squares via QR). Eigenvalues on the imaginary axis make ZZ singular and are reported.

Why the system for XX is right. HH is Hamiltonian: with λ\lambda, −λ-\lambda is also an eigenvalue, so HH has nn eigenvalues on each side (none on the axis). The stabilising solution spans with [I;X][I;\ X] the stable invariant subspace, H[I;X]=[I;X]ΛH[I;\ X] = [I;\ X]\,\Lambda with stable Λ\Lambda. The sign function maps stable eigenvalues to −1-1, so W[I;X]=−[I;X]W[I;\ X] = -[I;\ X], rearranged (W+I)[I;X]=0(W + I)[I;\ X] = 0 – the two block rows are the overdetermined system. Newton's iteration Z←(Z+Z−1)/2Z \leftarrow (Z + Z^{-1})/2 converges quadratically to sign⁡(H)\operatorname{sign}(H); the determinant scaling cc speeds up the first steps, in which eigenvalues lie far from ±1\pm 1 (Byers).

In discrete time (xk+1=Axk+Bukx_{k+1} = A x_k + B u_k) the discrete Riccati equation holds; it is solved by the structure-preserving doubling algorithm (SDA) with A0=AA_0 = A, G0=BR−1BTG_0 = B R^{-1} B^T, H0=QH_0 = Q:

W=I+GkHk,Ak+1=AkW−1Ak,Gk+1=Gk+AkW−1GkAkT,Hk+1=Hk+AkTHkW−1Ak,W = I + G_k H_k, \quad A_{k+1} = A_k W^{-1} A_k, \quad G_{k+1} = G_k + A_k W^{-1} G_k A_k^T, \quad H_{k+1} = H_k + A_k^T H_k W^{-1} A_k,

Hk→XH_k \to X quadratically. The discrete equation and the feedback are

X=ATXA−ATXB(R+BTXB)−1BTXA+Q,K=(R+BTXB)−1BTXA,X = A^T X A - A^T X B\,(R + B^T X B)^{-1} B^T X A + Q, \qquad K = (R + B^T X B)^{-1} B^T X A,

derived as in continuous time, only with V(xk+1)V(x_{k+1}) instead of the derivative. SDA doubles the time horizon per step; after kk steps HkH_k corresponds to the Riccati difference equation over 2k2^k steps.

The steady-state Kalman filter is the dual problem: error covariance PP from the CARE with ATA^T, CTC^T and noise covariances QQ, RR; gain L=PCTR−1L = P C^T R^{-1}. Dual means: the estimation error x˜\tilde x obeys x˜˙=(A−LC)x˜+…\dot{\tilde x} = (A - LC)\tilde x + \dots, and A−LCA - LC has the same eigenvalues as AT−CTLTA^T - C^T L^T – a state feedback of the dual system (AT,CT)(A^T, C^T).

How it is checked

  • First-order lag (K 2, T 1) with P controller Kp=3K_p = 3: steady-state error 1/7 to 1e-9; with PI none; no derivative kick at the setpoint step (also with measurement 0.5), then one integral step KpTse/TiK_p T_s e/T_i by hand.
  • Limited actuator (±1): with back-calculation less overshoot than without; both reach the setpoint.
  • Tuning rules according to the formulas.
  • Derivative on a measurement ramp against Dk=−KpTd(1−ak−1)D_k = -K_p T_d(1 - a^{k-1}) (1e-12); discrete LQR with R=4R = 4 against the Riccati difference equation iterated to standstill (1e-10).
  • Scalar: CARE x=(a+a2+b2q/r)r/b2x = (a + \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 q/r})\,r/b^2 to 1e-12, DARE positive with residual 1e-12, Kalman as the dual CARE.
  • Double integrator with Q=IQ = I, R=1R = 1: K=[1,3]K = [1, \sqrt3] to 1e-12; four-state system with two inputs and non-diagonal RR: residual 1e-10, loops stable.

Limits

One PID per loop, no feedforward and no setpoint weighting of the proportional part. Riccati solvers for dense matrices of moderate size; no time-varying problems.
