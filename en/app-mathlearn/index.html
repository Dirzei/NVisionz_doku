envisionz-app-mathlearn

Understand and practise functions

Overview of the app, as of 15 November 2026. A learning app for upper secondary students: two units of four stations each revisit the concept of a function and build up the derivative from the average rate of change to the h-method. The app checks every answer and explains mistakes instead of just saying “wrong”.

8stations in two learning units
7Java source files
2pages: usage with screenshots and background, German and English
377checks in 8 probes
50counter-probes

What the app is for

Students starting on derivatives and curve sketching often trip not over the new material but over the old: what exactly is a function, how do you read a graph, why is a circle not the graph of a function? The app practises exactly that, with tasks you can take hold of.

Each station poses a task, takes the answer and gives feedback aimed at the actual mistake: for a wrong rule it names the pair of values where the rule fails; for a misplaced point it says whether x and y are swapped or only the sign is wrong. Tasks, assessment and feedback texts live in the framework module framework-mathlearn; the app is its user interface.

What it can do

The stations are cards in a vertical bar on the left, the task itself is shown large in the middle. The steps in detail are on the usage page.

The function machine

Infer a rule from pairs of values.

  • Enter numbers, fractions like 1/2 too; the machine outputs the function value.
  • Enter the suspected rule as f(x) = … and have it checked.
  • A wrong rule is refuted with the pairs already entered.

Arrow diagrams

Decide whether a mapping is a function.

  • Domain and codomain as ovals with arrows.
  • After an answer, the element that decides it is marked: two arrows, no arrow.

From table to graph

Place points, read graphs, do the vertical line test.

  • Table of values: drag the points into place with the mouse.
  • Afterwards: read function values with a slider, show mirror images, zoom out.
  • Vertical line: drag a vertical line across a circle, a lying and a standing parabola and count how often it hits.

Filling graphs

Match the shape of a container with the graph of the fill level.

  • Three containers, three height-time graphs, one time slider fills all of them at once.
  • The feedback explains from the shape why the graph is flat or steep.

What is the derivative?

Four stations from the secant to the tangent, with a Remember box and the same colours in text, picture and formula.

  • Average rate of change on the slope triangle: calculate it, or drag A and B until m hits a target.
  • Drag Q towards P: the secant turns into the tangent, the table shows the slopes.
  • The limit: why m(0) is undefined and which value m(h) gets arbitrarily close to.
  • The h-method step by step, at a point and in general; mistakes are shown at their step.

Practice

New tasks on zeros, linear factors and derivatives.

  • Three levels; three correct in a row raise the level, two wrong ones lower it.
  • Feedback names the step where a typical mistake arises; worked solution on request.
  • A seed gives the whole class the same tasks.

The card Learning units at the top of the bar chooses the unit; the bar then shows only its stations. Below are the feedback on the last answer, next to it the history of all attempts, at the very bottom the status: how many tasks of the station are solved and how many stations are done. The bar also carries a button that clears the progress (with a confirmation inside the window) and the help button; it opens the documentation app on the usage page. The app is reachable with the shortcut ALT A.

How the app calculates

From the difference quotient to the derivative

Average rate of change, secants and tangent, why 0 / 0 is undefined, the limit, the h-method with the power rule and the error patterns the feedback recognises.

Data and formats

  • Work state: the app remembers the progress per learning unit and station, the open unit and station, the task number per station and the practice sequence (topic, levels, seed, task). The same number gives the same task; after a restart you continue at the same place. A damaged or foreign state is not taken over and is reported in the status line.
  • Input: numbers and terms in school notation – comma or point, fractions, 2x instead of 2*x, x² or x^2.
  • Language: interface, tasks and feedback in German and English.

What it builds on

  • framework-mathlearn: learning unit, stations, tasks, assessment and feedback texts, progress, school notation. It knows neither AWT nor Swing.
  • framework-diagramm: the coordinate systems of the graph station and the derivative stations, with handles that can be dragged with the mouse.
  • framework-math: exact arithmetic with fractions behind the function machine.
  • framework-swing: bar with cards, confirmation inside the window, icons.
  • framework-core and framework-api: registration as an app via the ServiceLoader, work state.

The app draws the machine, the arrow diagram and the containers itself, in three small classes.

State and limits

The app was created on 15 November 2026. The units “What is a function?” and “What is the derivative?” and the practice card with tasks from a generator are complete. Further units are planned on zeros and equations and on curve sketching, and an entry test that suggests a learning path.

Testing is headless in eight probes: registration, work state, user interface (every station with right and wrong answers, dragging with the mouse, confirmation when clearing, order of the stations), help button (opens the documentation app), language bundles, card width (also with a scroll bar), practice (seed, answers, worked solution, graph after the answer, level changes, reset) and the derivative together with the choice of unit (slope triangle, colours, table, limit line, revealed steps, work state per unit). The number of checks is 377.

Deliberately not included: marks or scores. The app says whether an answer is right and why not, and counts solved tasks – nothing more.
