From the difference quotient to the derivative

The mathematics behind the unit “What is the derivative?”: average rate of change on the slope triangle, secants that turn into the tangent, why you may not simply set h to 0, and the h-method step by step – with the worked examples of the app, the mistakes it recognises and its limits. Everything is computed exactly with fractions.

What it is about

In upper secondary school the derivative is the first concept that needs a limit. Knowing it only as the rule “x² becomes 2x” lets you apply it but not explain it. The unit therefore builds the concept in four steps, and each step has its own station in the app:

StationQuestionResult
Average rate of changeHow steeply does f rise between two points on average?m=ΔyΔxm = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}, the slope of the secant

Colours are the same in text, diagram and formula: Δx and h blue, Δy ochre, the secant green, the tangent vermilion. The palette is one suited to red-green colour blindness; every colour is always accompanied by its letter.

Average rate of change

Two points A(a∣f(a))A(a \mid f(a)) and B(b∣f(b))B(b \mid f(b)) on the graph span a slope triangle: horizontally from A to below B, then vertically up to B. The horizontal side is Δx=b−a\Delta x = b - a, the vertical one Δy=f(b)−f(a)\Delta y = f(b) - f(a). Their ratio is the average rate of change

m=ΔyΔx=f(b)−f(a)b−am = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}(1)

It is the slope of the line through A and B, the secant. If Δy\Delta y is negative, the secant falls. From everyday life the same calculation is known as average speed: distance divided by time.

Example. f(x)=12x2f(x) = \tfrac12 x^2 from a=−3a = -3 to b=2b = 2: Δx=5\Delta x = 5, Δy=2−4.5=−2.5\Delta y = 2 - 4.5 = -2.5, so m=−2.5/5=−0.5m = -2.5 / 5 = -0.5. On average f falls on this stretch, although it also rises in between.

From secant to tangent

Now the first point stays fixed, P(x0∣f(x0))P(x_0 \mid f(x_0)), and the second is called Q(x0+h∣f(x0+h))Q(x_0 + h \mid f(x_0 + h)). Then Δx=h\Delta x = h, and the average rate of change becomes the difference quotient

m(h)=f(x0+h)−f(x0)hm(h) = \frac{f(x_0 + h) - f(x_0)}{h}(2)

As Q moves closer to P, the secant turns about P. For f(x)=x2f(x) = x^2 and x0=1x_0 = 1 the table of the app gives:

From both sides the slopes approach the same number, here 2. The line through P with this slope is the tangent, and the number is called the derivative of f at x0x_0, written f'(x0)f'(x_0). In the app you drag Q along the graph; the row of the table for the dragged h is highlighted, and when Q lies on P the tangent appears with its slope triangle of width 1: Δy=f'(x0)\Delta y = f'(x_0).

Why both sides matter is shown by the absolute value f(x)=|x|f(x) = |x| at 0: from the right every secant slope is 1, from the left −1. There is no common value and therefore no tangent. The unit itself only uses polynomials, where this cannot happen.

The limit

Simply setting h=0h = 0 in (2) gives f(x0)−f(x0)0=00\frac{f(x_0) - f(x_0)}{0} = \frac{0}{0}. That is undefined: every number c satisfies c⋅0=0c \cdot 0 = 0, so every number would fit as a result. Geometrically P and Q coincide, and through a single point there is no secant. The graph of m(h)m(h) therefore has a hole at h=0h = 0.

Still, the values m(h)m(h) get arbitrarily close to one number as h approaches 0 from the left or from the right. This number is the limit, and it defines the derivative:

f'(x0)=limh→0⁡f(x0+h)−f(x0)hf'(x_0) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x_0 + h) - f(x_0)}{h}(3)

Two answers are tempting and wrong. “0, because the numerator is 0”: 0a=0\frac{0}{a} = 0 only holds for a≠0a \neq 0. “1, because numerator and denominator are equal”: aa=1\frac{a}{a} = 1 also only holds for a≠0a \neq 0. The app therefore first asks for m(0)m(0), with the three buttons 0, 1 and “undefined”, and only then for the limit. Once it is found, it is drawn as a horizontal line through the hole.

At school level the notation with lim is meant intuitively: “m(h) gets arbitrarily close to the number if h is close enough to 0”. The rigorous version with ε and δ is not part of the unit.

The h-method

For a polynomial the limit can be calculated without a table. The trick: after expanding, every term of the numerator contains an h. You factor it out and cancel – which is allowed because h≠0h \neq 0 for a secant. Only then does h approach 0. The app shows the seven steps one at a time:

Stepat 3, f(x)=x2f(x) = x^2
1. Difference quotientf(3+h)−f(3)h\frac{f(3 + h) - f(3)}{h}
2. Substituting(3+h)2−9h\frac{(3 + h)^2 - 9}{h}
3. Expanding9+6h+h2−9h\frac{9 + 6h + h^2 - 9}{h}
4. Simplifying6h+h2h\frac{6h + h^2}{h}
5. Factoring out h and cancellingh(6+h)h=6+h\frac{h(6 + h)}{h} = 6 + h
6. Limit h → 0limh→0⁡(6+h)=6\lim_{h \to 0} (6 + h) = 6
7. Resultf'(3)=6f'(3) = 6

Working with x instead of a number gives the derivative function. For f(x)=x3f(x) = x^3:

(x+h)3−x3h=3x2h+3xh2+h3h=3x2+3xh+h2,limh→0⁡(3x2+3xh+h2)=3x2\frac{(x + h)^3 - x^3}{h} = \frac{3x^2 h + 3x h^2 + h^3}{h} = 3x^2 + 3xh + h^2, \qquad \lim_{h \to 0} (3x^2 + 3xh + h^2) = 3x^2

In general, of (x+h)n−xn(x + h)^n - x^n only the term nxn−1n x^{n-1} without h survives cancelling; this is the power rule (xn)'=nxn−1(x^n)' = n x^{n-1}. A constant summand drops out when subtracting, a factor stays. The app computes its working with the same decomposition and checks it against the derivative of the polynomial; if the two disagree, it stops rather than show a wrong working.

To keep these patterns apart, the app chooses the calculation tasks of the first station such that Δx≥2\Delta x \ge 2, Δy≠0\Delta y \neq 0, |m|≠1|m| \neq 1 and Δy2≠Δx\Delta y^2 \neq \Delta x. Otherwise, for example, “upside down” and “not divided” would give the same number.

Limits

  • Only polynomials with rational coefficients: the tasks go up to degree three, the working of the h-method up to degree four. Product, chain and quotient rule, exe^x and sin⁡x\sin x belong to later units.
  • The limit is introduced intuitively, without the ε-δ definition. Points without a derivative (kink, jump) only appear in the text, not as tasks.
  • Points are dragged on a grid: whole numbers in the first station, tenths when moving Q. So only values arise that can be written down exactly; terminating decimals are shown as decimals, all others as fractions, never as rounded approximations.
