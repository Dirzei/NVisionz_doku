Differentiable spectral model of the formant voice – filter and source in the frequency domain

The computation behind FormantBankSpectrum and SourceSpectrum: the frequency response of a band-pass in closed form, the parallel bank with lip radiation, the logarithmic spectrum, and the source spectrum of Rosenberg pulse, tilt correction and coloured noise – as differentiable functions of the parameters. With derivation, pseudocode following the source, recomputed figures, the comparison against the time domain and the limits.

What this is about

The formant synthesiser of the speech output produces sounds by the source-filter principle: a source (glottal pulses, noise) excites a bank of five resonators whose centre frequencies – the formants F1 to F5 – determine the vowel. If a network is to learn to predict these parameters from a phoneme sequence, it needs target values. Measuring them from real speech with linear prediction (LPC) yields the parameters of a different model: LPC describes an all-pole tract, whereas this synthesiser has five summed band-passes with fixed branch gains and a differentiating pre-emphasis. An F2 measured by LPC does not produce the same spectrum in this bank, and a network trained on it would learn targets the synthesiser cannot realise.

The way out is analysis by synthesis: write the synthesiser's spectrum as a differentiable function of its parameters and fit the parameters so that this spectrum comes as close as possible to the measured one (see Spectral fitting). The targets found are then realisable by construction. This page describes the model itself.

A band-pass in closed form

Each formant is a biquad band-pass following the widely used formulas of the "Audio EQ Cookbook" (RBJ for short in the source), in the variant with constant 0 dB peak gain. With centre frequency FF, bandwidth BB and sampling rate fsf_s:

ω0=2πFfs,Q=FB,α=sin⁡ω02Q=Bsin⁡ω02F,\omega_0 = \frac{2\pi F}{f_s},\qquad Q = \frac{F}{B},\qquad \alpha = \frac{\sin\omega_0}{2Q} = \frac{B\,\sin\omega_0}{2F},

H(z)=b0(1−z−2)1+a1z−1+a2z−2,b0=α1+α,a1=−2cos⁡ω01+α,a2=1−α1+α.H(z) = \frac{b_0\,(1 - z^{-2})}{1 + a_1 z^{-1} + a_2 z^{-2}}, \qquad b_0 = \frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha},\quad a_1 = \frac{-2\cos\omega_0}{1+\alpha},\quad a_2 = \frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}.

Evaluated on the unit circle z=ejωz = e^{j\omega}, numerator and denominator become complex numbers whose real and imaginary parts can be written down directly:

N=b0((1−cos⁡2ω)+jsin⁡2ω),D=(1+a1cos⁡ω+a2cos⁡2ω)−j(a1sin⁡ω+a2sin⁡2ω),N = b_0\big((1 - \cos 2\omega) + j\,\sin 2\omega\big), \qquad D = \big(1 + a_1\cos\omega + a_2\cos 2\omega\big) - j\,\big(a_1\sin\omega + a_2\sin 2\omega\big),

H(ejω)=ND=(NrDr+NiDi)+j(NiDr−NrDi)Dr2+Di2.H(e^{j\omega}) = \frac{N}{D} = \frac{(N_r D_r + N_i D_i) + j\,(N_i D_r - N_r D_i)}{D_r^2 + D_i^2}.

The decisive point for differentiability: on a fixed frequency grid cos⁡ω\cos\omega, sin⁡ω\sin\omega, cos⁡2ω\cos 2\omega and sin⁡2ω\sin 2\omega are constants. Only α\alpha and cos⁡ω0\cos\omega_0 depend on FF and BB. The computation graph is small as a result, and no backward pass through the filter recursion over tens of thousands of samples is needed – that would be expensive and numerically delicate.

The parallel bank with lip radiation

Because the bank is parallel, its frequency response is the weighted sum of the individual band-passes. In front of it lies the lip radiation as a differentiator 1−0.96z−11 - 0.96\,z^{-1}:

Htot⁡(ω)=(1−0.96e−jω)∑i=1ngiHi(ω),P(ω)=|Htot⁡(ω)|2,L(ω)=10log10⁡(P(ω)+ε).H_{\mathrm{tot}}(\omega) = \big(1 - 0.96\,e^{-j\omega}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n} g_i\,H_i(\omega), \qquad P(\omega) = |H_{\mathrm{tot}}(\omega)|^2, \qquad L(\omega) = 10\log_{10}\big(P(\omega) + \varepsilon\big).

The branch gains gig_i are parameters with the synthesiser's default values (1.0, 0.95, 0.7, 0.22, 0.10). With alternating signs (branches 2 and 4 negative) the bank sums the way Klatt proposed for parallel formant synthesisers: the flanks between two formants then do not cancel.

The comparison is made on a logarithmic scale: hearing works that way, and a linear error would be determined solely by the region around F1, where the energy is orders of magnitude higher – the upper formants that matter for intelligibility would have no weight.

Pseudocode – FormantBankSpectrum.biquad(F, B) and response(F, B, g)

// constants per grid point ω_k: cos ω, sin ω, cos 2ω, sin 2ω, pre-emphasis (1 − 0.96 cos ω, 0.96 sin ω)
biquad(F, B):                                  F, B single-element tensors, all in the graph
    ω0 ← F · 2π/fs
    α  ← sin(ω0) · B / (2F)
    a0 ← α + 1;   b0 ← α/a0;   a1 ← −2 cos(ω0)/a0;   a2 ← (1 − α)/a0
    Nr ← (1 − cos 2ω) · b0;        Ni ← sin 2ω · b0
    Dr ← a1 cos ω + a2 cos 2ω + 1; Di ← −(a1 sin ω + a2 sin 2ω)
    d  ← Dr² + Di²
    return ((Nr Dr + Ni Di)/d,  (Ni Dr − Nr Di)/d)

response(F, B, g):
    S ← Σ_i g_i · biquad(F_i, B_i)             complex, summed over the branches
    return (pr·Sr − pi·Si,  pr·Si + pi·Sr)     times pre-emphasis pr + j·pi

logSpectrumDb(F, B, g, ε) = (10/ln 10) · ln(|response|² + ε)

Source: envisionz-framework-autograd/src/envisionz/autograd/dsp/FormantBankSpectrum.java. Every step is an operation from Ops; the derivatives with respect to FF, BB and gg arise in the ordinary backward pass.

Decision in the framework: the coefficients are one to one those of the synthesiser – including its variant with peak gain 1 (b0=αb_0 = \alpha, b2=−αb_2 = -\alpha before normalisation): the bandwidth controls only the width of a resonance, never its height. In a real vocal tract a narrower, lower-loss resonance would also be more prominent; this synthesiser does not represent that, and the branch gains compensate. clampInPlace keeps FF in [20,0.49fs][20,\ 0.49\,f_s] and B≥20B \ge 20 Hz – exactly the limits the synthesiser sets itself; outside them it would silently clip. The frequency grid is free: equally spaced from 0 to fs/2f_s/2, or arbitrary, for example only the harmonics of the fundamental.

The source spectrum

For real speech the source is added. The model is deliberately split into two classes: the filter part alone can be checked exactly against the time domain (section Checks); a source term modelled along with it would obscure that comparison.

Rosenberg pulse. The pulse shape is completely determined by fundamental frequency F0F_0 and open quotient oqo_q: over a period of T=round⁡(fs/F0)T = \mathrm{round}(f_s/F_0) samples the pulse rises as a cosine dome up to tp=oq/2t_p = o_q/2, falls until tn=oqt_n = o_q and then stays closed:

p(t)={12(1−cos⁡(πt/tp))t<tpcos⁡(π2(t−tp)/(tn−tp))tp≤t<tn0otherwise,t=k/T.p(t) = \begin{cases} \tfrac12\big(1 - \cos(\pi t/t_p)\big) & t \lt t_p \\ \cos\!\big(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\,(t - t_p)/(t_n - t_p)\big) & t_p \le t \lt t_n \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}, \qquad t = k/T.

Its spectrum is therefore computed, not fitted: per grid frequency the discrete Fourier sum over one period, normalised by 1/T21/T^2, so that the line spectrum of the pulse train and the noise are in the same unit:

Ppulse⁡(ω)=1T2|∑k=0T−1p(k/T)e−jωk|2.P_{\mathrm{pulse}}(\omega) = \frac{1}{T^2}\,\Big|\sum_{k=0}^{T-1} p(k/T)\,e^{-j\omega k}\Big|^2.

Tilt correction. Deviations of the open quotient and of voice quality are absorbed by a factor in dB per octave, referred to 1000 Hz:

Ttilt⁡(f)=10slog2⁡(f/1000)/10=ec(f)s,c(f)=ln⁡1010log2⁡max⁡(20,f)1000.T_{\mathrm{tilt}}(f) = 10^{\,s\,\log_2(f/1000)/10} = e^{\,c(f)\,s}, \qquad c(f) = \frac{\ln 10}{10}\,\log_2\frac{\max(20, f)}{1000}.

The derivative with respect to ss is thus trivial, and the parameter is in a unit that can be judged at a glance.

Noise. The synthesiser's noise source is a one-pole low-pass yn=cyn−1+(1−c)wny_n = c\,y_{n-1} + (1-c)\,w_n over white noise ww; its squared magnitude is closed-form

Pnoise⁡(ω)=(1−c)21−2ccos⁡ω+c2,P_{\mathrm{noise}}(\omega) = \frac{(1-c)^2}{1 - 2c\cos\omega + c^2},

white noise (breathiness) for c=0c = 0. Voiced and unvoiced parts are uncorrelated and therefore add as powers:

Psource⁡=Ppulse⁡⋅Ttilt⁡(s)+Pnoise⁡(c)⋅gn2.P_{\mathrm{source}} = P_{\mathrm{pulse}}\cdot T_{\mathrm{tilt}}(s) + P_{\mathrm{noise}}(c)\cdot g_n^2.

Pseudocode – SourceSpectrum.rosenbergPower(F0, oq)

T ← round(fs / F0);   tp ← oq/2;   tn ← oq
for k ← 0 to T−1:  p[k] ← pulseShape(k/T)            as in the synthesiser
for each grid point b:
    ω ← 2π f_b / fs;   re ← Σ_k p[k] cos(ωk);   im ← −Σ_k p[k] sin(ωk)
    P[b] ← (re² + im²) / T²
return P as a constant (without gradient)

Source: envisionz-framework-autograd/src/envisionz/autograd/dsp/SourceSpectrum.java, methods rosenbergPower, tiltPower, noisePower, power.

According to the class description the first approach was a rigid tilt of −12-12 dB per octave. Measured, however, the Rosenberg pulse falls by about 15 dB per octave between 500 and 1000 Hz and by only about 5 dB above 4 kHz; with the rigid form the fits ran into their limits. The exactly computed pulse spectrum solves that.

How it is checked

  • SpectralTest, comparison against the time domain. ReferenceBiquad is a one-to-one rebuild of the synthesiser filter (direct form I). Excited with a unit impulse, its output is the impulse response of the whole system; its FFT must match the analytical model. Single filters 700 Hz/80 Hz and 2350 Hz/160 Hz with FFT length 4096 below 10−810^{-8} dB, the narrow limiting case 200 Hz/25 Hz with 16,384 (at 4096 the slowly decaying response is truncated – a property of the measurement, established by varying the length); the complete bank with five branches, sign changes and lip radiation over 2049 grid points below 10−710^{-7} dB (2.7⋅10−112.7\cdot 10^{-11} dB according to the module documentation), the same bank without sign changes likewise. Notches more than 120 dB below the maximum are excluded.
  • SpectralTest, meaning of the parameters (8193 grid points): the maximum lies at the centre frequency (within 2 Hz), the width between the −3 dB points is the bandwidth (within 6 %), the peak gain is 1 (within 0.01) and not QQ, the lip radiation rises by 6±0.56 \pm 0.5 dB from 500 to 1000 Hz. Plus gradients of band-pass, bank and logarithmic spectrum against central differences.
  • SourceTest: the noise model against the generated noise source for c=0c = 0, 0.5 and 0.85 (shape deviation below 0.35 dB RMS between 200 and 9000 Hz, 0.16 dB according to the module documentation); the Rosenberg spectrum against the generated pulse train at F0=110F_0 = 110, 160 and 210 Hz, compared at the harmonics (below 1.5 dB RMS, documented 0.12 to 0.81 dB); gradients of the source model and of the whole chain source times filter.

Limits

  • The model describes this synthesiser – five parallel band-passes of fixed structure –, not a general vocal tract. Nasals, zeros and couplings between formants do not exist in it.
  • The bandwidth changes only the width, not the height of a resonance (see decision).
  • The pulse period is rounded to whole samples; F0F_0 is thus quantised to fs/Tf_s/T (at 22,050 Hz and 120 Hz: 184 samples, i.e. 119.84 Hz).
  • rosenbergPower costs grid points times TT operations per call; with 1025 grid points and T=184T = 184 about 189,000. That is why the fast fit computes only at the harmonics.
  • The breathiness, gated periodically in the synthesiser, is modelled as stationary noise; according to the module documentation this hardly affects the residual.
  • Below 20 Hz the tilt correction is held at its value at 20 Hz.
