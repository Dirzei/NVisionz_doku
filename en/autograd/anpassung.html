Differentiable spectral model of the formant voice – fitting to a measured spectrum

The computation behind SpectralFit, FrameFit and FrameAnalyzer: the loss in decibels with the level removed, separate optimisers for quantities of different units, clamps and ordering, comparison only at the harmonics of the fundamental, the logarithmically carried noise share, multi-start across the vowel space and the fast path – with pseudocode following the source, a vowel recomputed with the module, the probes and the limits.

What this is about

The spectral model (see Filter and source) delivers, for given formants, bandwidths and source quantities, the spectrum the synthesiser would produce – as a differentiable function. Fitting goes the other way: for a measured spectrum it searches for the parameters with which the model comes closest to it. This turns a speech corpus into target values the synthesiser can actually reach.

The task is ambiguous. The error surface over the formant frequencies has several minima; a method can put two branches onto the same resonance and miss a real formant. A gradient method reliably finds the nearby minimum, not the global one. Multi-start and an ordering condition F1<F2<F3F_1 \lt F_2 \lt F_3 therefore belong to it, and the residual must make a failure visible.

The loss

The comparison is logarithmic, at a set of grid points k∈Kk \in \mathcal{K}, after removing the mean on both sides – the recording level is not a property of the vocal tract:

ℓ=1|K|∑k∈K((Lk−L)−(Mk−M))2,RMS⁡=ℓdB.\ell = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{K}|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}} \Big( \big(L_k - \bar L\big) - \big(M_k - \bar M\big) \Big)^2, \qquad \mathrm{RMS} = \sqrt{\ell}\ \text{dB}.

LkL_k is the model spectrum in dB, MkM_k the measured one. The square root is the reported residual in decibels.

Filter only: SpectralFit

SpectralFit fits nn formant frequencies, bandwidths and – optionally – branch gains to a target spectrum on the full grid. It is meant for targets that come from the filter itself (such as the synthesiser's impulse response) and as a building block on which the method can be checked.

  • Three separate Adam optimisers for frequencies, bandwidths and gains: the orders of magnitude differ by about a factor of 1000 (hertz against dimensionless), and Adam takes steps of the size of the learning rate (see Adam). A common learning rate would be too large for some and too small for others. Norm clipping is switched off.
  • Clamping after every step to the range the synthesiser accepts – otherwise the fit would run into values it would silently clip.
  • Ordering: the frequencies are sorted and brought to a minimum separation.
  • Multi-start: the same run from several starting points; the smallest residual wins. spreadStarts(n, fMin, fMax, v) distributes nn frequencies evenly over the band and stretches the fan in vv variants from 0.8 to 1.2.
Decision in the framework: defaults of SpectralFit.Options: 400 steps, learning rates 8 Hz (frequencies), 3 Hz (bandwidths) and 0.01 (gains), gains fitted too, level removed, ordering enforced with 120 Hz minimum separation.

Real speech: comparing only at the harmonics

A voiced sound has no smooth spectrum. The source is a pulse train with fundamental frequency F0F_0; its spectrum consists of lines at hF0h\,F_0, with valleys of 20 to 40 dB in between that say nothing about the vocal tract. A comparison over all grid points would be dominated by the noise in those valleys, and the fit would put formants onto harmonics instead of resonances.

The obvious smoothing – cepstral smoothing of the envelope (FrameAnalyzer.envelopeDb) – systematically damps sharp formant peaks. According to the class comment the smoothed curve of a synthetic vowel lay about 10 dB below the true value at F1; the fit compensated with distorted bandwidths, residual 17 dB, although the parameters were correct. The amplitudes of the harmonics, in contrast, lie on the envelope of the vocal tract by construction. Comparison is therefore done only there; the same test then gave 0.5 to 0.75 dB.

  • FrameAnalyzer.powerSpectrum: periodogram of a Hann-windowed frame (power-of-two length, grid points 0 to fs/2f_s/2); averagePower averages several frames of a stationary section and thus lowers the scatter of the periodogram.
  • harmonicBins(P, F0, lo, hi): for each multiple hF0h F_0 in [lo,hi][lo, hi] the largest grid point within a window of ±0.4F0\pm 0.4\,F_0 around the grid point of hF0h F_0 is taken; a point already chosen is not carried twice.
  • harmonicPeaks, the blind peak search without F0F_0, finds additional maxima in the valleys when there is breathiness. According to the class comment a vowel with breathiness 0.06 gave 52 instead of 45 peaks, and the error in F2 rose to up to 54 %. If F0F_0 is known, harmonicBins is therefore used.

Source times filter: FrameFit

FrameFit fits a complete frame: the model is the product of filter and source,

Pk=|Htot⁡(ωk;F,B,g)|2⋅(Ppulse⁡(ωk;F0,oq)ecks+Pnoise⁡(ωk;c)e2u),Lk=10log10⁡(Pk+10−30).P_k = |H_{\mathrm{tot}}(\omega_k; F, B, g)|^2 \cdot \Big(P_{\mathrm{pulse}}(\omega_k; F_0, o_q)\,e^{c_k s} + P_{\mathrm{noise}}(\omega_k; c)\,e^{2u}\Big), \qquad L_k = 10\log_{10}(P_k + 10^{-30}).

Free are only the first fitCount formants (default 3) with their bandwidths, the tilt correction ss and the noise share; fixed are the upper formants, the branch gains, F0F_0, the open quotient and the noise colour. The spectral tilt of the source across the band is larger than the contribution of individual formants; whoever does not model it along inevitably compensates it with distorted bandwidths – the fit then looks good, and the formants are wrong all the same.

Noise share on a logarithmic scale. The noise share gng_n is carried as gn=eug_n = e^u with free uu, starting at u=ln⁡0.01u = \ln 0.01. Adam takes steps of the size of the learning rate, independent of the gradient. According to the module documentation the share was first carried linearly (start 0.05, learning rate 0.004), but the value sought was 0.0006 – far below one step. The parameter ran into the clamp at zero, the missing noise floor was compensated via the tilt, and that pulled F1 down. On a logarithmic scale the fixed step acts multiplicatively (0.05 corresponds to about 5 % per step), covers several orders of magnitude equally well, and the clamp at zero disappears. That is the general rule: a parameter whose plausible range spans orders of magnitude belongs in the logarithm.

Pseudocode – FrameFit.kern(…) (shared core of fit and fitFast)

check at least 6 grid points
target[i] ← 10·log10(P_meas[point_i] + 1e−30);  target ← target − mean(target)
F_free, B_free ← first m values of the start set (parameters);  rest fixed
s ← 0;  u ← ln 0.01                                   tilt, log noise share
pulse ← rosenbergPower(F0, oq)                         constant
four Adam instances (F, B, s, u), norm clipping off
repeat iterations times:
    reset gradients
    P ← bank.powerSpectrum([F_free, F_fixed], [B_free, B_fixed], g)
         · src.power(pulse, s, c, exp(u))  + 1e−30
    L ← 10·log10(P)
    L_sel ← if slow path: L · selection matrix  else: L       grid = harmonics
    ℓ ← mse(L_sel − mean(L_sel), target);  ℓ.backward()
    all four Adam steps
    F_free in [20, 0.49·fs];  B_free in [bwMin, bwMax]

Source: envisionz-framework-autograd/src/envisionz/autograd/dsp/FrameFit.java, methods kern, clamp, fit, fitFast, fitMultiStart, spreadStarts; FrameAnalyzer.java for periodogram and harmonics.

Decision in the framework: defaults of FrameFit.Options: 900 steps, learning rates 6 Hz (frequencies), 2 Hz (bandwidths), 0.05 (tilt) and 0.05 (noise share, multiplicative), 3 fitted formants, ordering with 150 Hz minimum separation, bandwidths in [30,400][30, 400] Hz, tilt in [−12,12][-12, 12] dB per octave, noise share in [10−5,2][10^{-5}, 2]. The slow path fit computes on the full FFT grid and fetches the harmonics via a constant selection matrix; the fast path fitFast puts the grid directly onto the frequencies of the chosen grid points. Both evaluate at the same frequencies and therefore deliver bit-identical results; according to the module documentation the fast one is 23 times faster at 2049 against 44 grid points.

Multi-start across the vowel space

A fan that couples all formants to one common stretch factor never produces the combination of low F1 and high F2 – exactly where the close front vowels lie (/i/ at about 300 and 2300 Hz). According to the class comment /i/ stayed at 24 % error with a coupled fan. FrameFit.spreadStarts therefore lays the starting points out as a grid over the F1/F2 plane:

F1∈{300,450,650},F2∈{1000,1600,2200},F3=min⁡(fmax⁡,3200,max⁡(2500,F2+500)),F_1 \in \{300, 450, 650\}, \qquad F_2 \in \{1000, 1600, 2200\}, \qquad F_3 = \min\big(f_{\max},\ 3200,\ \max(2500,\ F_2 + 500)\big),

further free formants 700 Hz above the previous one each, with the fixed upper formants appended – nine starting points. The smallest residual wins; according to the comment the residual orders the runs correctly (on the generated /i/: 4.22 dB at 35 % frequency error against 1.85 dB at 4 %).

How it is checked

  • SpectralTest, part 5 (SpectralFit, 257 grid points): from frequencies detuned by ±18 % and uniformly wrong bandwidths, the run finds the frequencies to better than 2 % in 600 steps (documented 0.00 %), the bandwidths to better than 10 % (0.41 %), residual below 0.5 dB (0.005 dB). A hopeless starting point (2000/2200/2400 Hz) must show a residual at least twice as large – a failure must not silently claim a good result (documented 2.19 dB). Multi-start with four fans reaches below 1 dB without prior knowledge.
  • SourceTest, part 4 (FrameFit): formants from fully generated vowels /a/, /e/, /i/ to better than 2 % – documented 0.81 %, 0.96 % and 1.04 % at residuals of 0.30 to 0.45 dB and tilt corrections of +0.13+0.13, −0.24-0.24 and +0.05+0.05 dB per octave.
  • SequenceTest: fast against full grid bit-identical over a whole frame sequence (see Frame sequence).

Limits

  • Local method. Without a suitable starting point the fit stays in a secondary minimum; the residual shows that but does not prevent it.
  • Sorting only the frequencies. The ordering condition sorts the frequencies, not the associated bandwidths (and, in SpectralFit, not the gains). If two formants overtake each other during fitting, frequency and bandwidth afterwards belong to different branches.
  • Only voiced frames with known F0F_0. FrameFit needs at least six harmonics and throws otherwise.
  • Multi-start on the slow path. FrameFit.fitMultiStart calls fit, not fitFast; the sequence fit, in contrast, uses the fast path. In the example above nine starts of 900 steps on the full grid took about 27 s.
  • Fixed number of starting points. FrameFit.spreadStarts always produces the grid of nine; the parameter for the number of variants is not evaluated.
  • The reported residual is the loss of the last step before its update.
