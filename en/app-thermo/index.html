envisionz-app-thermo

Inspect and check property data, and calculate combustion

Overview of the app, as of 29 September 2026. The app shows the framework's thermodynamics for each of the 23 species as a table, a plot and a comparison with literature values. Three combustion tools are added: equilibrium, adiabatic flame temperature over the equivalence ratio, and a reactor with ignition delay.

23species in the tree, in three groups
96embedded reference values from the literature
3tabs per species: data, plots, validation
9source files
295checks in three probes (107, 114, 74)

What the app is for

Property data calculated from molecular physics are traceable but not vivid. The app puts the calculation next to the reference tables of the literature and so shows how well it agrees. Agreement at 300 K says little, because vibration hardly contributes there. Only above about 1500 K does it show whether anharmonicity is captured correctly.

The combustion tools use the same thermodynamics for practical questions: which composition settles in equilibrium, how hot a flame gets and how fast a mixture ignites. The app calculates no thermodynamics of its own. It builds tables, holds reference values and calls the framework.

What it can do

Species tree

A tree of the 23 species on the left, grouped into atoms, diatomic and polyatomic molecules. On the right a card is built per species and then remembered. A toolbar switches between molar and mass-based quantities. A help button shows the documentation of the thermodynamics module inside the app.

Data

The table of properties from 200 to 3000 K in steps of 25 K: heat capacity, enthalpy, entropy and the Giauque function. Exact evaluation from the partition functions is done only at nodes 100 K apart, with cubic interpolation in between. Enthalpy and entropy are consistent with the heat capacity, and exact at the nodes.

Plots

Heat capacity, enthalpy and entropy over temperature, with the literature reference points laid over them.

Validation

The comparison of the calculation with the 96 embedded reference points: heat capacity between 1000 and 2500 K for six gases, anchors at 298.15 K for most species, the exact value 5/2 R for monatomic gases. For the six species from the methane mechanism, the values of a mechanism data set serve as anchors. Known deviations that are justified in the source code are shown as well. They lie in the model or in the reference, not in the app.

Equilibrium

A calculator for combustion in chemical equilibrium with 17 species. Fuels are hydrogen, methane and the nine jet fuels of the NJFCP programme. A button gives the adiabatic flame temperature.

Flame temperature over the equivalence ratio

Curves of the adiabatic flame temperature over the equivalence ratio, with the reference band of 2280 to 2300 K at about 1 atm and 298 K.

Reactor

Ignition delay and time history for methane-air and hydrogen-air in an adiabatic reactor at constant pressure; the mole fractions are plotted over time. The status line points out that, with this mechanism, the temperatures behind the flame overshoot the equilibrium.

Workspace state and help

The chosen species, the unit choice and the tab are saved in the workspace state. Three help pages (overview, species, combustion) exist in German and English, plus an older, more detailed documentation in German.

Data and formats

The app opens no files. The reference values are embedded as constants in the source code, not as a file, and therefore work without a working directory and in the native program. The properties are calculated once per species and remembered.

What it builds on

  • thermo: all the thermodynamics. Partition functions of the 23 species, combustion equilibrium with the jet fuels, the reactor with ignition delay.
  • chart: the plots for properties, flame temperature and reactor history.
  • swing: user interface, background runner with wait notice, icons and help display.
  • api: the app interface, registration through the ServiceLoader and workspace state.

State and limits

  • The species tree covers all 23 species of the thermodynamics module; the tools for equilibrium, flame temperature and reactor are in place.
  • The largest deviation of the calculation from the reference values is 1.6 %, with two justified exceptions. The reference values have to be updated by hand when the reference set in the thermodynamics module changes.
  • For some species, high-temperature heat capacities are still missing (NO2, SO2, H2S), as are further points for NO, OH and SO.
  • The first call of the combustion tools builds calculation tables and takes some tens of seconds. It runs in the background with a wait notice.
  • The inputs of the combustion tools are not saved in the workspace state. Jet fuels as data cards of their own, like the species, are deliberately not there yet.
