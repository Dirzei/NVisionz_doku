Cuts, fields, pulldown – Background

How the material is split into usable units before merging: Entschraenkung (fields), Pulldown (3:2 rhythm) and Schnitterkennung (cut detection). Formulas, pseudocode after the source, testing and limits.

Why split first

The multi-frame methods (merging, stacking, drizzle) rest on successive pictures being different observations of the same subject. Three properties of the source material violate that: an interlaced picture contains two points in time, a 3:2 rhythm contains repeats that are no new observation, and a cut separates two different shots. The three classes on this page clear that up, in this order: pulldown before anything else, cut detection before merging.

Deinterlacing

An interlaced PAL picture consists of two fields of 288 lines each, taken at different times one fiftieth of a second apart and sampled offset by one line against each other. Three ways lead to full pictures, two of which throw half away:

  • Weave (weben): push both fields into each other. Right for a static picture; with motion, comb artefacts arise because the two points in time stand alternately line by line in the same picture. Costs half the temporal resolution.
  • Double (verdoppeln): calculate the missing lines from the existing ones of the same field. Always right, but half the spatial resolution is gone. It is not calculated by repeating a line, but with four support points:

v=−a+9b+9c−d16v = \frac{-a + 9b + 9c - d}{16}

where bb and cc are the field lines directly above and below the missing line, aa and dd the next ones each. This is the usual compensation that smears edges less than merely averaging two neighbours; it is exact for straight lines.

  • Motion-compensated (entschraenken): fetch the missing lines from the neighbouring field – they really stand there. That keeps both, but needs a fallback, because a wrongly fetched line content is worse than an interpolated one: it looks like real picture content.

How does one tell whether fetching is allowed? The obvious idea – compare the fetched value with what the lines above and below suggest – is wrong: at a horizontal edge the interpolation is far off, but the fetched value is right there; the criterion thus discards exactly where fetching brings the greatest gain. In the experiment this cost 34 dB (36 instead of 70). The right criterion is the motion, measured between two fields of the same parity: they sample the same lines, a difference can only come from motion. With the largest deviation BB in a neighbourhood of 3×53 \times 5 pixels (line ±1\pm 1, column ±2\pm 2) and the tolerance τ=24\tau = 24:

t=min⁡(1,max⁡(0,B−τ)τ),line=(1−t)⋅fetched+t⋅doubledt = \min\!\left(1,\; \frac{\max(0,\, B - \tau)}{\tau}\right), \qquad \text{line} = (1 - t)\cdot\text{fetched} + t\cdot\text{doubled}

Up to B≤τB \le \tau the line is taken from the neighbouring field, at B≥2τB \ge 2\tau fully interpolated, in between mixed: a transition instead of a jump.

Pseudocode – Entschraenkung.entschraenken(feld, nachbar, gleicheLage, breite, feldHoehe, oben)

fallback ← double(field, top)
if neighbour = empty: return (fallback, share 0)
d ← GlobaleVerschiebung(levels 4).estimate(field, neighbour)         for information only; no offset is applied
out ← copy(fallback)
for each missing line (from field line z) and each column x:
    fetched ← neighbour[z, x]
    if sameParity = empty:  B ← 0
    else:  B ← max |field[z+dz, x+dx] − sameParity[z+dz, x+dx]|     dz = −1..1, dx = −2..2, clamped at the edge
    if B ≤ tolerance:  out ← fetched;  count as fetched
    else:  t ← min(1, (B − tolerance) / tolerance);  out ← fetched·(1 − t) + fallback·t;  if t < 1: count as fetched

double(field, top):
    take existing lines (top field on lines 0, 2, …; bottom on 1, 3, …)
    missing line: v ← (−a + 9b + 9c − d) / 16, clamped to 0..255   support points for the bottom field shifted up by one line
weave(top, bottom):  take lines alternately

Source: envisionz-framework-restore/src/envisionz/restore/deint/Entschraenkung.java (entschraenken, verdoppeln, weben).

Decision in the framework: The vector estimated between the fields is not applied when fetching: the two fields sample different lines, the vector mixes this fixed line offset with the motion and is useless as a motion measure; applied, it made the result with motion worse than mere doubling (34.8 against 35.9 dB, according to the class comment). Without gleicheLage there is no motion detection, then everything is fetched. The fallback is doubling; without a neighbouring field the result is exactly that. When doubling the bottom field the support points were at first the same as for the top (a fixed error): on a ramp line 2z2z got the value of 2z+22z+2.

Worked example. On a linear ramp 100, 110, 120, 130 the compensation for the missing line between 110 and 120 gives (−100+9⋅110+9⋅120−130)/16=1840/16=115(-100 + 9 \cdot 110 + 9 \cdot 120 - 130)/16 = 1840/16 = 115: exactly the middle. With a largest motion B=36B = 36, t=(36−24)/24=0.5t = (36 - 24)/24 = 0.5: the line is half fetched and half calculated.

The 3:2 rhythm

Film runs at 24 frames per second, NTSC at 60 fields. To make that fit, four film frames are stretched to ten fields: the first supplies two, the second three, the third two, the fourth three. Of the ten fields, two are repeats. A repeated field is no new observation – it carries the same values and the same noise. Whoever averages it in gains nothing and takes the result for better supported than it is; and in motion estimation no motion appears between a picture and its repeat, between the others double: the rhythm moves through the result as judder.

It is recognised by all five fields one pair of the same parity not differing. With the mean squared difference uiu_i between the (3×3-smoothed) fields ii and i−2i-2, for each of the five phases pp the ratio

Sp=ui(imod5≠p)ui(imod5=p)S_p = \frac{\overline{u_i}\;(i \bmod 5 \ne p)}{\overline{u_i}\;(i \bmod 5 = p)}

is computed. The phase with the largest ratio counts as found if Sp≥3S_p \ge 3. With a static picture all differences are small, the ratio is near one; with the rhythm only every fifth is small. Smoothing before the comparison is necessary because noise enters quadratically: without it detection failed from a noise strength of 6 levels on, and VHS material has more.

Pseudocode – Pulldown.erkennen(felder, breite, hoehe) and umkehren

if fewer than 10 fields: return “not found”
smooth[i] ← 3×3 mean of fields[i]
u[i] ← mean squared difference(smooth[i], smooth[i−2])  for i ≥ 2       sample, at most ~40000 points
for p ← 0 to 4:
    on ← mean of the u[i] with i mod 5 = p;   off ← mean of the other u[i]
    strength ← off / max(on, 1e−9)
best phase ← the one with the largest strength
found ← strength ≥ 3.0;    repeats ← all i ≥ 2 with i mod 5 = phase
reverse: return all fields except those in repeats    without finding: a copy of equal length

Source: envisionz-framework-restore/src/envisionz/restore/deint/Pulldown.java (erkennen, umkehren).

Decision in the framework: The threshold for “clear” is 3.0; at least ten fields are needed, below that no statement is made. The phase counts from field 0, the repeats are the fields i≥2i \ge 2 with imod5=pi \bmod 5 = p. Removal happens before any other processing.

Cut detection

Multi-frame merging must never average across a cut: beyond a cut the picture shows another shot, and whoever averages both destroys both sides and gets a double image that looks like motion blur. The IFO names cell boundaries, and cuts often lie there – but not only there: a compilation from several tapes has cuts in the middle of a cell. So the picture is measured, with two measures at once:

  • the picture difference DiD_i – mean absolute difference of two successive pictures; it jumps at a cut, but also with fast motion or a flash;
  • the distribution distance Vi=∑k|pk(i)−pk(i−1)|V_i = \sum_k |p_k^{(i)} - p_k^{(i-1)}| – the L1L_1 distance of the brightness histograms (64 classes of 4 levels each); insensitive to motion, a pan over the same scene leaves it almost unchanged.

A cut between picture i−1i-1 and ii is reported if both reach the threshold multiple of their median:

Dimax⁡(2.0,med⁡D)≥4andVimax⁡(0.02,med⁡V)≥4\frac{D_i}{\max(2.0,\; \operatorname{med} D)} \ge 4 \quad\text{and}\quad \frac{V_i}{\max(0.02,\; \operatorname{med} V)} \ge 4

Motion triggers only the first measure, an exposure change only the second. A flash first triggered both, with two false alarms, at lighting up and at going out: the assumption that the distribution was insensitive to such a thing was the wrong way round, because a brightness change shifts it as a whole. Hence before both measurements the mean is equalised: a flash is a uniform addition to the whole picture and vanishes in that, a change of shot does not.

Pseudocode – Schnitterkennung.finden(folge)

if fewer than 3 pictures: return empty list
for i ← 1 to n−1:
    offset ← mean(Y[i−1]) − mean(Y[i])                       brightness equalisation
    D[i] ← mean absolute value of (Y[i] + offset − Y[i−1]),  sample of at most ~40000 points
    V[i] ← Σ |share_k(Y[i] with equalisation) − share_k(Y[i−1])|,  64 classes of 4 levels
mb ← median(D);  mv ← median(V)
for i ← 1 to n−1:
    qb ← D[i] / max(2.0; mb);   qv ← V[i] / max(0.02; mv)
    if qb ≥ threshold and qv ≥ threshold:  cut after picture i−1       threshold = 4.0, at least 1.5

Source: envisionz-framework-restore/src/envisionz/restore/schnitt/Schnitterkennung.java (finden, bildabstand, verteilungsabstand). The choice of neighbouring pictures that do not cross cuts is up to the caller.

Decision in the framework: The threshold is a multiple of the median of the sequence itself (4.0; smallest settable 1.5): a fixed value would be too loose for calm material and too strict for moving material. The lower bounds of the scales (2.0 brightness levels, 0.02) are a fixed error: in a still sequence the median of the differences is zero, the scale fell to 1e−6, and even noise of ±3 levels triggered by a large multiple – a cut where there is none. Below two levels of mean deviation a difference is noise and no scale.

Worked example. If the median of the picture difference is 3.0 levels, the first measure triggers from Di≥12D_i \ge 12; if the median of the distribution distance is below 0.02, 0.02 counts as the scale and the second measure triggers from Vi≥0.08V_i \ge 0.08. Both must be met.

How it is tested

  • Deintprobe (26 checks): weaving the two fields gives the full picture exactly; doubling leaves existing lines unchanged and hits the missing ones on a ramp exactly (top and bottom field), clamps only calculated lines to 255; a static picture comes back exactly, all missing lines from the neighbouring field; strong motion gives the same as doubling (share 0), medium motion each missing line half fetched, half calculated (deviation below 10−910^{-9}); the 3:2 rhythm is recognised for each of the five phases with phase and positions, and reversing removes exactly the repeats; a static picture and motion without repeats give no rhythm; below ten fields no statement.
  • Szenenwechselprobe: the cut between picture 9 and 10 is found, none otherwise; a flash in picture 8, a fast pan over the same scene and a single motion jump do not count as a cut; a still sequence with noise ±3 is no cut, with a real scene change the cut after picture 9 is found; below three pictures no statement.

Limits

  • Deinterlacing distinguishes only “moving” and “not moving” per point; moving picture parts get the half spatial resolution of doubling. The shift between the fields is estimated but not used for fetching.
  • The 3:2 rhythm is recognised only if it runs through at least ten fields and is not interrupted; material with a broken rhythm (after a cut in the middle of a cycle, say) needs detection section by section. Testing covers the 3:2 rhythm; other ratios (2:2, say) are not covered.
  • Cut detection finds hard cuts; a slow dissolve changes both measures only little from picture to picture; whether it reaches the threshold depends on the material and has not been tested.
