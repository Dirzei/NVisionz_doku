Motion estimation – Background

Block search with a distance penalty, Lucas-Kanade pyramidal and iterative, global shift: the calculation behind Bewegungsfeld, LucasKanade and GlobaleVerschiebung, pseudocode after the source, testing and limits.

What motion estimation delivers – and what not

Whoever wants to merge several pictures of the same scene must know how far they are displaced against each other: the camera shakes, the recording runs, people walk through the picture. What is wanted is, for each part of the picture, a vector (vx,vy)(v_x, v_y) that lays one picture on the other. Two ways are implemented in the module: block search (one integer vector per block of 16 × 16 pixels, optionally with a fractional part) and the differential method of Lucas and Kanade (one vector per pixel, in fractions of a pixel). In addition there is the global shift, a single vector for the whole picture.

Both methods find the most similar patch, not the true motion. In a smooth area almost everything fits equally well; the vector found is then chance, and chance that looks like a measurement. So every result carries a measure of its reliability, and the module prefers to throw vectors away rather than invent them.

Block search with a distance penalty

Bewegungsfeld.suchen divides the picture into blocks of 16 × 16 pixels and for each block looks in the neighbouring picture for the displacement with the smallest sum of absolute differences (SAD):

E(v)=∑(x,z)∈block|I1(x,z)−I2(x+vx,z+vz)|+λ(|vx|+|vz|)E(v) = \sum_{(x,z) \in \text{block}} \bigl| I_1(x, z) - I_2(x + v_x,\, z + v_z) \bigr| \;+\; \lambda\,(|v_x| + |v_z|)

The search is logarithmic: starting points are the zero vector, the vector of the left neighbour block and that of the upper neighbour block (motion is coherent). From the best starting point the eight neighbouring positions at distance ss are tested, then the distance is halved, beginning with the largest power of two within the search range. When comparing, the sum is abandoned as soon as it reaches the best value so far, and only every second line is used (PUNKTE_JE_BLOCK = 128).

Why the penalty λ\lambda? A block search always finds something: even in a completely static recording the deviation of almost every block can be lowered by displacing it – somewhere the noise happens to fit better. On a tripod shot, the search without penalty reported 73.9 % of the blocks as moving, at a mean displacement of 3.32 pixels (class comment). The penalty demands that a vector pays off. It grows with the noise, because the deviation of a static block consists wholly of noise and is on average 1.128σ⋅1281.128\,\sigma \cdot 128:

λ=round⁡(0.5σ⋅128)\lambda = \operatorname{round}\bigl(0.5\,\sigma \cdot 128\bigr)

Whoever draws conclusions from the field uses the block's quality, the remaining deviation per pixel without the distance penalty. The fractional part (subpixel) comes from three squared deviations S−,S0,S+S_-, S_0, S_+ around the integer minimum, to which a parabola is fitted:

δ=12⋅S−−S+S−−2S0+S+,−12≤δ≤12\delta = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{S_- - S_+}{S_- - 2S_0 + S_+}, \qquad -\tfrac12 \le \delta \le \tfrac12

Pseudocode – Bewegungsfeld.suchen(mitte, nachbar, breite, hoehe, suchweite, strafe)

w ← max(2, searchRange)
for each block (x, z), row by row:
    best ← ∞
    test(0, 0, penalty 0);   noPath ← best                     deviation without displacement
    test(left);  test(above)                                   vectors of the neighbour blocks, with penalty
    step ← largest power of two ≤ w
    while step ≥ 1:
        m ← best vector so far
        for each of the 8 directions d:  test(m + d · step)
        step ← step / 2
    vector ← best;  quality ← best − penalty · (|vx| + |vz|)      subtract the penalty again
    if subpixel: fractional part per axis from S−, S0, S+ (squared, all lines, no penalty)

test(v):
    if |vx| > w or |vz| > w or the block would lie outside the picture: return
    s ← penalty · (|vx| + |vz|)                 the penalty is in the sum from the start
    for every second line of the block:
        s ← s + Σ |picture1 − picture2 displaced|
        if s ≥ best: return                      early abandon
    best ← s;  found ← v

Source: envisionz-framework-restore/src/envisionz/restore/bewegung/Bewegungsfeld.java (suchen, pruefe, fein, roh, strafeFuer).

Decision in the framework: The penalty is in the sum from the start, not added at the end: otherwise the early abandon would discard positions that would still have won with the penalty. The quality does not contain the penalty – it is to measure the remaining deviation, and with the penalty in it, it would grow with path length. That was an error found by the automatic test (class comment): with the known displacement (3, −2) and the penalty 192, none of 192 blocks passed the quality limit 6.0 although each carried the right vector; the remaining deviation was 3.4 grey levels, the penalty added 7.5. The fractional part is determined without penalty and without early abandon, because an abandoned sum is not a measurement and the penalty would shift the parabola.

Worked example. For σ=3\sigma = 3 grey levels, λ=round⁡(0.5⋅3⋅128)=192\lambda = \operatorname{round}(0.5 \cdot 3 \cdot 128) = 192. A static block has on average a deviation of 1.128⋅3⋅128≈4331.128 \cdot 3 \cdot 128 \approx 433, consisting wholly of noise. A vector with |vx|+|vz|=3|v_x|+|v_z| = 3 costs 576 and is taken only if it lowers the deviation by more – noise cannot do that, real motion can.

Lucas-Kanade, pyramidal and iterative

Basic idea: the brightness of a pixel stays the same under motion, it only moves. For pictures aa and bb with displacement (u,v)(u,v), to first order Ixu+Iyv=−ItI_x u + I_y v = -I_t holds – one equation with two unknowns. Lucas and Kanade assume that the displacement is the same in a small window and solve the overdetermined system by least squares:

∑gIx2∑gIxIy∑gIxIy∑gIy2uv=−∑gIxIt∑gIyIt\begin{pmatrix} \sum g I_x^2 & \sum g I_x I_y \\ \sum g I_x I_y & \sum g I_y^2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} u \\ v \end{pmatrix} = -\begin{pmatrix} \sum g I_x I_t \\ \sum g I_y I_t \end{pmatrix}

The sums run over a window of 15×1515 \times 15 pixels (half width 7) with Gaussian weight gg (σ=3.5\sigma = 3.5): with equal weight everywhere the solution jumps as soon as a different motion begins at the window edge. The assumption holds only for displacements of about one pixel; hence the work is pyramidal – first on a strongly reduced picture, then refined level by level – and iterative on every level: the second picture is warped back by the current estimate, and the remainder is small again.

The reliability is the smaller eigenvalue λsmall\lambda_\text{small} of the system matrix, divided by the window area. In a uniform area there is nothing whose movement could be followed; at a straight edge only motion across it is measurable. Both show up as a small eigenvalue. If it is below the threshold (1.0) the point is not changed – there is then no invented value.

Pseudocode – LucasKanade.schaetzen(a, b, breite, hoehe)

n ← levels (4), reduced until the coarsest level has at least 16 pixels per side
pa[0], pb[0] ← a, b
for s ← 1 to n−1:  pa[s] ← halve(pa[s−1]);  pb[s] ← halve(pb[s−1])
                           first smooth with the kernel 1 4 6 4 1, then take every second pixel
dx, dy ← 0 on the coarsest level
for s ← n−1 down to 0:
    if s < n−1:  dx, dy ← enlarge to the finer level, values times 2
    solveLevel(pa[s], pb[s], dx, dy)

solveLevel:
    weight ← Gaussian window (half width 7, σ = 3.5)
    repeat 5 times:
        warp ← b warped back by (dx, dy) (bilinear)
        for each point: gradient ix, iy from the UNWARPED b at the displaced position  (x ± 1)/2
        for each point (border 7 excluded):
            a11, a12, a22, b1, b2 ← weighted sums in the window; dt ← warp − a
            small ← smaller eigenvalue of [[a11, a12], [a12, a22]]
            quality ← min(1, small / windowArea / (threshold · 10))
            if |det| < 1e−6 or small / windowArea < threshold: continue
            (ux, uy) ← solution of the 2×2 system, limited to ±1.5
            dx += 0.75 · ux;  dy += 0.75 · uy                 damped
        dx, dy ← median 5×5 over the field

Source: envisionz-framework-restore/src/envisionz/restore/fluss/LucasKanade.java (schaetzen, stufeLoesen, verkleinern, median, abtasten). Defaults: 4 levels, half window width 7, 5 iterations, threshold 1.0, damping 0.75, half median window 2.

Decision in the framework: The class comment records four things without which it did not work: (1) The gradients belong to the warped picture, but they are sampled from the unwarped picture at the displaced position; from the bilinearly sampled, smoother field they came out too small and the steps too large – a systematic overestimate of about 15 percent (0.58 instead of 0.50). (2) A plain 2×2 mean is no good for reduction: it lets fine structure through that turns into false coarse structure on subsampling; hence the kernel 1 4 6 4 1 (Burt and Adelson). (3) Between iterations the median instead of the mean: an outlier is thrown out, and at motion edges the jump is preserved. (4) The field is summarised by the median, not the mean: in the end the mean was at 0.611 and the median at 0.535, with a true value of 0.500. The step is damped because the full step overshoots and the solution then oscillates instead of converging.

Global shift

For a fixed camera on a stage, the shift of the whole picture is the camera shake; for multi-frame merging it is exactly what is needed. GlobaleVerschiebung solves the same equation as Lucas-Kanade, but with one displacement for all points: two unknowns against hundreds of thousands of equations, the system is heavily overdetermined and stable. People who also move are the minority of the area; their points enter only damped after the first iterations, with the weight

w=11+t2,t=|r|3med⁡|r|w = \frac{1}{1 + t^2}, \qquad t = \frac{|r|}{3\,\operatorname{med}|r|}

where rr is the residual of the point and med⁡|r|\operatorname{med}|r| the median of the residual magnitudes in the third iteration. The median is insensitive to part of the picture looking completely different. At the end there is a residual error: the mean deviation after laying back, divided by the mean contrast of the reference picture. Near 0 means the pictures coincide; above 0.3 the shift does not explain the second picture, and the number is not a measurement.

Pseudocode – GlobaleVerschiebung.schaetzen(a, b, breite, hoehe)

pyramid as above, 4 levels, coarsest level at least 32 pixels per side
(dx, dy) ← (0, 0)
for s ← n−1 down to 0:
    if s < n−1:  dx ← 2·dx;  dy ← 2·dy
    scale ← 0
    for it ← 0 to 11:                                     12 iterations per level
        for each point outside the border (12 pixels):
            gx, gy ← gradient of b at (x+dx, z+dy);  res ← b(x+dx, z+dy) − a(x, z)
            w ← if scale > 0 then 1/(1 + (|res|/scale)²) else 1
            a11, a12, a22, b1, b2 ← sum with w
        if it = 2: scale ← 3 · median of the residual magnitudes
        if |det| < 1e−9: leave
        (ux, uy) ← solution;  dx += ux;  dy += uy
        if hypot(ux, uy) < 1e−4: leave
residualError ← Σ|b shifted − a| / Σ|a − mean(a)|

Source: envisionz-framework-restore/src/envisionz/restore/fluss/GlobaleVerschiebung.java (schaetzen, stufeLoesen, restfehlerBei).

Decision in the framework: A dense field is indicated only for several simultaneous motions: in the experiment, left half displaced by 2.0 and right half by −1.5, the dense field finds 2.00 and −1.47, the global estimate gives −0.16 – a value wrong for both. For uniform motion the global estimate is preferable. restfehler() makes a limit of the method visible (see below); the limit itself remains.

How it is tested

The tests work without a recording, on generated picture sequences with known truth (fixed seed, known vectors, noise of known strength) – with a real recording the truth is not known. Flussprobe (25 checks) tests according to the source, among others:

  • bilinear sampling is exact on a plane (50 fractional positions), clamps at the edges and hits the last column exactly;
  • global estimate (10.5 | −8.25) on 256 × 256 with a coarse pattern, only via the pyramid, within 0.05; residual error then below 0.05;
  • with a quarter foreign content (1.4 | −0.6) within 0.1; a uniform area gives (0, 0);
  • dense field: uniform displacement (0.5 | 0.3) within 0.06 in the median; two motions (left +2.0, right −1.5) each within 0.1, while the global estimate hits neither;
  • uniform area: nowhere measurable; aperture case (stripes only): no point counts as measurable.

The sub-pixel accuracy of the block search is measured in the class comment: error below 0.10 pixels against 0.25 when rounding to integers, consistently negative (the estimate pulls to the nearest integer). Part of that lies in the test itself, because the artificial displacement smooths the picture; the value is an upper bound. Squared with parabola gave 0.099 as the largest error, sum of absolute values with triangle 0.112.

Limits

  • The block search finds the most similar patch, not the true motion; in smooth areas and with noise the vector remains chance, which only the penalty and the quality expose.
  • Lucas-Kanade measures nothing in uniform areas and along an edge only the motion across it; there the field stays unchanged (measurability via the reliability).
  • Fine patterns can be aliased into noise on the coarse pyramid levels: for a displacement of a good ten pixels the global estimate on a fine pattern was off by more than five pixels (residual error 0.52). The method warns through restfehler() but does not correct.
  • The global estimate knows only one displacement; with several simultaneous motions it returns a value that corresponds to none.
