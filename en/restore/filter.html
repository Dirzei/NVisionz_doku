Sharpness, tiles and colour – Background

Richardson-Lucy deconvolution, smoothing of the MPEG tile edges and the luma-guided upsampling of colour: the calculation behind Entfaltung, Kachelentstoerung and Farbaufloesung, pseudocode after the source, testing and limits.

Where these steps stand in the chain

All three methods work on a single picture and on what a tape machine and an MPEG encoder made of it: the tape softened the luma and transmitted the colour with less bandwidth, the encoder rounded each block of 8 × 8 pixels coarsely on its own. The order is not arbitrary: tile removal comes before any edge analysis, otherwise compression edges are taken for picture edges; deconvolution comes after multi-frame merging, otherwise noise is amplified and the amplified result is then averaged soft.

Richardson-Lucy deconvolution

A recording is the convolution of the subject with the point spread function hh – the picture a single luminous point leaves, wide for a tape machine because the bandwidth of the recording is limited. What is observed is d=h∗ud = h \ast u; the subject uu is wanted. The Richardson-Lucy method approaches it step by step:

u(k+1)=u(k)⋅(h˜∗dh∗u(k)),u(0)=du^{(k+1)} = u^{(k)} \cdot \left( \tilde h \ast \frac{d}{h \ast u^{(k)}} \right), \qquad u^{(0)} = d

with the mirrored point spread function h˜\tilde h. The ratio of observed to expected picture drives the improvement. Because the point spread function sums to one and all factors are non-negative, the method preserves total brightness and never yields negative values – both follow from its construction and are why it suits pictures better than an ordinary inversion in the frequency domain.

Each step sharpens more and amplifies more noise, because deconvolution raises exactly the fine components in which the noise sits as well. Without stopping, rings appear around every edge; the picture then looks overdrawn, not sharper. The number of steps is therefore the most important setting. Entfaltung.gauss(sigma) provides a Gaussian point spread function with half width r=⌈3σ⌉r = \lceil 3\sigma \rceil (at least 1).

Pseudocode – Entfaltung.anwenden(bild, breite, hoehe, schritte)

// constructor: normalise the kernel to sum 1; sum 0 or NaN → exception
estimate ← copy(picture)
mirrored ← kernel rotated by 180°
repeat steps times:
    expected ← convolve(estimate, kernel)                    edge points continued from the edge
    for each point i:  ratio[i] ← picture[i] / max(0.001; expected[i])
    back ← convolve(ratio, mirrored)
    for each point i:  estimate[i] ← clamp(estimate[i] · back[i], 0, 255)
return estimate

Source: envisionz-framework-restore/src/envisionz/restore/filter/Entfaltung.java (anwenden, gauss, falten, spiegeln).

Decision in the framework: The number of steps has no default; the caller must choose it. The denominator is limited below to 0.001 so that no division by zero occurs in black areas. A kernel with sum zero (also one whose weights cancel to zero) is rejected with an exception: formerly it was divided by one, the back-convolution gave zero, and the picture was completely black after one step – without a message (noted in the source as a fixed error).

Tile removal

MPEG calculates in blocks of 8 × 8 pixels. At a tight bit rate each block is rounded coarsely on its own, and steps arise at the block boundaries that are not in the picture; on a video CD with 1.15 Mbit/s they are hard to overlook. Kachelentstoerung smooths only where a block boundary lies (every eight pixels), and only if the step stands alone: for a compression artefact the jump at the boundary is clearly larger than the changes to its left and right. With ss the jump at the boundary and uu the larger of the two neighbouring jumps, treatment happens if

1≤s≤24ands≥2(u+0.5)1 \le s \le 24 \quad\text{and}\quad s \ge 2\,(u + 0.5)

Then the two points on either side of the boundary are drawn by the fraction aa (strength, default 0.5) towards the common mean m=(x1+x2)/2m = (x_1 + x_2)/2: xi'=xi+a(m−xi)x_i' = x_i + a\,(m - x_i). Steps above 24 count as real edges and stay. First the vertical, then the horizontal boundaries are treated, the horizontal ones on the result of the vertical.

The tile measure measures how pronounced the structure is: the mean jump at the boundaries divided by the mean jump between them. For an uncompressed picture it is 1; the higher, the more visible the tiles; a pure tile picture (steps at the boundaries, flat between) gives infinity.

Pseudocode – Kachelentstoerung.anwenden(ebene, breite, hoehe)

out ← copy(plane)
// vertical boundaries: x = 8, 16, 24, … (point x−1 | point x each)
for each row z, for x ← 8, 16, … while x < width−1:
    jump ← |e[x] − e[x−1]|;  left ← |e[x−1] − e[x−2]|;  right ← |e[x+1] − e[x]|
    if jump < 1 or jump > 24 or jump < ratio · (max(left, right) + 0.5): continue
    mid ← (e[x] + e[x−1]) / 2
    out[x−1] ← e[x−1] + (mid − e[x−1]) · strength;   out[x] ← e[x] + (mid − e[x]) · strength
// horizontal boundaries: likewise, on t = copy(out), rows 8, 16, …
// ratio = 2.0;  strength = 0.5, clamped to 0..1

Source: envisionz-framework-restore/src/envisionz/restore/filter/Kachelentstoerung.java (anwenden, kachelmass).

Decision in the framework: The tile size is fixed at 8. The input plane stays unchanged, the result is a new plane. The case “flat interior” in the tile measure was a fixed error: the denominator was zero, and 1 stood for it – “uncompressed” for exactly the picture that is most tiled; now it gives infinity.

Worked example. Flat on both sides of the boundary, 100 on the left, 110 on the right (step 10, neighbouring jumps 0): 10≥2⋅(0+0.5)=110 \ge 2 \cdot (0 + 0.5) = 1, so it is treated. Mean m=105m = 105; at strength 0.5 each of the two points moves by 2.5, 100 | 110 becomes 102.5 | 107.5, the step is 5 (as in the test and the figure). The tile measure for step 10 at slope 0.5 between the boundaries is 10.5/0.5=2110.5 / 0.5 = 21.

Colour from half resolution

At 4:2:0 there is one colour value per four luma values; from 360 × 288 colour points, 720 × 576 must be made. Writing each colour value four times (wiederholen) produces colour squares of 2 × 2 pixels: at every edge the colour lies up to two pixels beside the luma, in steps. That is the impression of “frayed” – the luma edge is not unclean, the colour just does not fit it. Three ways:

  • wiederholen: quadruple; shows the colour planes as they are, right for judging the source.
  • linear: weight between the colour values, with the half-pixel offset (a colour point sits in the middle of the four luma points it covers); the steps vanish, the colour stays soft and runs across edges.
  • gefuehrt: the luma guides. Of the 3×33 \times 3 surrounding colour values cqc_q, those count most whose location has a luma similar to the spot being filled:

c(x,z)=∑qgqcq∑qgq,gq=exp⁡(−(Y(x,z)−Yq)22h2)c(x,z) = \frac{\sum_q g_q\, c_q}{\sum_q g_q}, \qquad g_q = \exp\left(-\frac{\bigl(Y(x,z) - \bar Y_q\bigr)^2}{2\,h^2}\right)

Yq\bar Y_q is the mean luma that colour value qq covers, hh the “hardness”. At the edge from the red pullover to the bright wall, a point on the wall side takes its colour preferably from colour values on the wall side: the colour edge moves onto the luma edge. This is the same idea as the guided filter (He, Sun and Tang, 2010): a well-resolved quantity guides a poorly resolved one. Nothing is invented – the colour remains what it was, it is only assigned to the right side of the edge.

Pseudocode – Farbaufloesung.gefuehrt(c, fb, fh, y, breite, hoehe, haerte)

h2 ← 2 · max(0.5; hardness)²
table[i] ← exp(−(i − 256)² / h2) for i = 0 .. 511          compute once: luma difference −255..255
for each colour point: yCoarse ← mean of the luma values it covers
for each pixel (x, z):
    here ← y[x, z];  sum ← 0;  weights ← 0
    for the 3 × 3 colour points q around the corresponding one (edge continued):
        g ← table[here − yCoarse[q] + 256]
        sum ← sum + c[q] · g;  weights ← weights + g
    out[x, z] ← if weights ≤ 0 then c[corresponding] else rounded(sum / weights)

Source: envisionz-framework-restore/src/envisionz/restore/farbe/Farbaufloesung.java (wiederholen, linear, gefuehrt).

Decision in the framework: The weights come from a table: the exponential function per pixel and neighbour would be about 3.7 million calls per picture at 720 × 576, enough for a still, not for playback. As a starting point for the hardness the source gives about twice the noise spread. The window is small (3 × 3) because the right colour lies immediately next door, only possibly on the other side of the edge.

For the position of the colour samples (MPEG-1 in the centre, MPEG-2 on the left) see Colour and geometry in the video module.

How it is tested

Faltungsprobe (18 checks) works on generated pictures with known truth:

  • Deconvolution: the unit kernel leaves the picture unchanged; a kernel is normalised to sum one ((2,4,2) acts like (1,2,1), distance below 10−910^{-9}); a uniform picture is a fixed point; with the right kernel the bar picture comes at least 25 % closer to the original; total brightness is kept within 1 %; the result stays in 0 to 255 without NaN; a kernel with sum zero is rejected.
  • Tile removal: tile measure of a uniform ramp exactly 1, with step 10 and slope 0.5 exactly 21, of a pure tile picture infinity. Step 10 on the grid becomes 5 at strength 0.5, strength 0 changes nothing, strength above 1 is clamped to 1 (both sides meet in the middle). A real edge (step 30) on the grid stays, a step beside the grid stays, a step in a busy surrounding stays; the horizontal boundary at row 8 is halved likewise.

Further measurements on sharpness and noise exist in the module as tests of the filter chain (SchaerfeProbe, RauschProbe, FarbProbe, FilterProbe); their numbers are not reproduced here.

Limits

  • Deconvolution needs a point spread function; it is not estimated. If the kernel does not fit the recording, sharpening is wrong. Without stopping, rings appear, and it makes sense only on an already low-noise picture.
  • Tile removal knows only the 8 × 8 grid without offset; a shifted grid (after cropping or scaling, say) is not recognised. Steps above 24 and steps in a busy surrounding count as picture content.
  • Guided colour cannot bring back colour that was never recorded: tape machines transmit colour horizontally with much less bandwidth than luma, what was lost there is lost. It only assigns correctly what is there.
