Multi-frame methods – Background

Merging with a confidence map, lucky imaging with sigma clipping and drizzle: what Verschmelzung, Vertrauen, Glueckstapel and Drizzle calculate, why this is not invention and where it ends.

Collecting information, not inventing it

Successive pictures of the same scene sample the same subject with independent noise and, with camera motion, displaced by fractions of a pixel. If they are aligned and combined, the noise falls with NN observations roughly as 1/N1/\sqrt N: from thirty pictures it becomes about 5.5 times smaller (class comment of Glueckstapel). Fine structures that lay in the noise of a single picture emerge. None of this is invented – it was in the data, only spread over several pictures.

Two duties follow. First, outliers must be discarded: where people move on stage, the neighbouring pictures show something else, and whoever averages them in gets streaks. Second, what was discarded must be counted: a spot where six of seven observations were discarded is no better than a single picture, it just looks the same. The alignment itself comes from motion estimation.

Merging with a confidence map

Verschmelzung.verschmelzen lays each observation of the sequence on a reference picture. The displacement comes from the global estimate (GlobaleVerschiebung), the laying is done bilinearly and not rounded, because the whole gain lies in the fractions. A pixel of the observation enters only if it is close enough to the reference picture, and the scale for that comes from the picture itself: from the median of the deviation magnitudes m=med⁡|b−a|m = \operatorname{med}|b - a|, which is insensitive to part of the picture looking completely different. For normally distributed noise this median is only 0.6745σ0.6745\,\sigma, so it is converted to a standard deviation with 1.48261.4826:

σ^=1.4826m,limit=max⁡(1,2.5σ^)\hat\sigma = 1.4826\; m, \qquad \text{limit} = \max\bigl(1,\; 2.5\,\hat\sigma\bigr)

Points with a larger deviation than the limit are discarded (the reference picture itself always enters), the rest is averaged. For each pixel the number of observations that entered is counted; Vertrauen turns it into a grading:

LevelMeaningCondition (nn observations entered, AA offered)
ERSETZTno own observationn=0n = 0
EINZELNreference picture onlyn=1n = 1
DUENNusable, but thin2≤n<max⁡(3,A/2)2 \le n < \max(3,\, A/2)
BELEGTseveral agreeing observationsn≥max⁡(3,A/2)n \ge \max(3,\, A/2)

Pseudocode – Verschmelzung.verschmelzen(folge, bezug, breite, hoehe)

a ← sequence[reference];  sum, weight, count ← 0 for each point
for k ← 0 to length(sequence)−1:
    b ← sequence[k]
    d ← if k = reference then (0, 0)
        else GlobaleVerschiebung(levels 4).estimate(a, b)
    laid ← b sampled bilinearly at (x + dx, z + dz)
    scale ← 1.4826 · median(|laid − a|)        sample: every (n/20000)-th point, at least 0.5
    limit ← max(1, tolerance · scale)           tolerance = 2.5
    for each point i:
        if k ≠ reference and |laid[i] − a[i]| > limit: continue      outlier, discarded
        sum[i] += laid[i];  weight[i] += 1;  count[i] += 1
result[i] ← if weight[i] > 0 then sum[i] / weight[i] else a[i]
return (result, Vertrauen(count, length(sequence)), displacements)

Source: envisionz-framework-restore/src/envisionz/restore/fuse/Verschmelzung.java and Vertrauen.java (stufe).

Decision in the framework: The tolerance is given in standard deviations of the difference (default 2.5); smaller means stricter and thinner evidence, larger means more noise reduction and more risk of streaks. The median alone as scale was an error: the limit then lay at 1.7 instead of 2.5 standard deviations, and nine of a hundred pure noise values were discarded; on static material the noise stayed at 4.08 against 2.98 for plain averaging, after the conversion at 3.04 (class comment). BELEGT demands at least three observations: with two offered there is no BELEGT even if both agree, because two values can be wrong together, only a third makes a majority. No merging across cuts; the choice of neighbouring pictures is up to the caller.

Worked example. If the median of the deviation magnitudes is 2.0 grey levels, σ^=1.4826⋅2.0=2.97\hat\sigma = 1.4826 \cdot 2.0 = 2.97 and the limit 2.5⋅2.97=7.412.5 \cdot 2.97 = 7.41: a point deviating by 9 (a person in the picture, say) is discarded, one with 5 enters. With seven observations offered, a point with n=3n = 3 is already BELEGT (max⁡(3,3)=3\max(3, 3) = 3), with n=2n = 2 only DUENN; with ten offered the limit is at five.

Lucky imaging with sigma clipping

Astronomy fights atmospheric turbulence, which blurs every picture but not each equally. Instead of exposing for long, since Fried (1978) one takes many short exposures, keeps the sharpest few percent and stacks only those. A tape recording has the same problem in another form: noise, dropouts, head-switching disturbances and compression artefacts differ in every picture. Glueckstapel.stapeln proceeds as follows:

  1. Select by sharpness, measured as mean edge steepness. Whoever selected by noise would get the softest pictures: a blurred picture looks noise-free. The sharpest picture is the reference picture.
  2. Align blockwise (16 × 16) to the reference picture, with fractional part and distance penalty; a block enters only if its quality is below the limit.
  3. Per pixel take the median and discard everything deviating from it by more than kk spreads (sigma clipping); the rest is averaged:

x˜=med⁡(xj),s=1n∑(xj−x˜)2,keep:|xj−x˜|≤k⋅max⁡(0.5,s)\tilde x = \operatorname{med}(x_j), \quad s = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{n}\sum (x_j - \tilde x)^2}, \quad \text{keep:}\; |x_j - \tilde x| \le k \cdot \max(0.5,\, s)

A mean would pull every outlier in; in astronomy these are cosmic rays, here tape dropouts. With clipping a dropout disappears completely instead of showing through faintly across thirty pictures.

Pseudocode – Glueckstapel.stapeln(bilder, …, anteil, suchweite, klippSigma, hoechsteGuete)

sorted ← pictures by sharpness descending
take ← max(2, round(count · fraction));  chosen ← the first take;  reference ← chosen[0]
penalty ← penaltyFor(maxQuality / 4)                GUETE_JE_SIGMA = 4: σ from the quality limit
for i ← 1 to take−1:
    field ← Bewegungsfeld.suchen(reference, chosen[i], range, penalty, subpixel = true)
    per block: laid ← bilinear by (genauX, genauY); valid ← (quality ≤ maxQuality)
for each point:
    values ← the valid observations
    if none: result ← reference;  continue
    if clipSigma ≤ 0: result ← mean(values);  continue
    median ← median(values);  spread ← √(Σ (v − median)² / n)
    limit ← clipSigma · max(0.5, spread)
    result ← mean of the values with |v − median| ≤ limit    if none: median
    count the discarded

Source: envisionz-framework-restore/src/envisionz/restore/bewegung/Glueckstapel.java (stapeln, ausrichten, schaerfe, guetegrenzeFuer).

Decision in the framework: The quality limit follows from the noise, limit=4σ\text{limit} = 4\,\sigma (GUETE_JE_SIGMA). Two pictures of the same static spot differ on average by 1.128σ1.128\,\sigma; a block whose remainder lies at that was found again. Run through on the sample recording (σ=0.76\sigma = 0.76): from limit 3 on the noise stays the same (0.5873 at limit 3, 0.5863 at 12), while sharpness falls with every loosening (11.9959 against 10.4644) – a more generous limit brings nothing and costs sharpness. The reference picture is the sharpest, not the first; a soft reference would pull the whole stack to its softness. The distance penalty in the alignment was a found error: without it, in 96 static pictures 4.8 % of the blocks carried a non-zero vector, and the distance to the clean version stood at 0.90 grey levels where σ/96=0.68\sigma/\sqrt{96} = 0.68 would be expected; with the penalty it reaches the target value with 0.67.

Drizzle

Fruchter and Hook (2002) developed drizzle for the Hubble Space Telescope, whose camera undersampled the sky: a pixel was larger than what the optics resolved. Several exposures were taken, each displaced by fractions of a pixel, and a finer picture was assembled from them. The additional resolution is not invented but collected: two exposures offset by half a pixel together contain samples at twice as many positions as one. These values existed before, they lay in different pictures.

Each input pixel is reduced to a drop of edge length t⋅mt \cdot m target cells (scale mm, drop factor t≤1t \le 1) and dropped onto the target grid. It contributes to every target cell it covers, in proportion to the area covered; at the end the sum is divided by the sum of the weights:

Zc=∑pgpcIp∑pgpc,gpc=overlapx⁡⋅overlapz⁡Z_c = \frac{\sum_p g_{pc}\, I_p}{\sum_p g_{pc}}, \qquad g_{pc} = \operatorname{overlap}_x \cdot \operatorname{overlap}_z

The drop factor is the actual control: at 1.0 each pixel covers its full area, nothing stays empty, the result gets soft; smaller means sharper and holier. Holes are not filled secretly but reported.

Pseudocode – Drizzle.rechnen(bilder, breite, hoehe, massstab, tropfen, suchweite, hoechsteGuete, subpixel)

if fewer than 2 pictures: return null
m ← max(1, scale);  t ← clamp(drop, 0.1 .. 1.0)
reference ← the sharpest picture
for each picture:
    field ← if reference then none else Bewegungsfeld.suchen(reference, picture, range, penalty 0, subpixel)
    for each pixel (x, z) of the picture:
        if field ≠ none and quality(block) > maxQuality: continue
        (dx, dz) ← if field then −exact displacement else 0        reversed sign: picture into the reference grid
        mx ← (x + dx + 0.5)·m;   mz ← (z + dz + 0.5)·m;   half ← t·m/2
        for each target cell (qx, qz) in the square [mx ± half] × [mz ± half]:
            g ← overlap(x) · overlap(z)
            sum[q] += value · g;  weights[q] += g
for each cell:  if weights = 0: hole, value 0, hole mask true   else value ← sum / weights, clamped to 0..255
return (picture, hole fraction, mean coverage, hole mask)

Source: envisionz-framework-restore/src/envisionz/restore/bewegung/Drizzle.java (rechnen, fallenlassen). With subpixel = false the displacements are rounded to whole pixels: the counter-test to the actual claim.

Decision in the framework: A hole appears in the picture as 0 and cannot be told from a black pixel there – only the lochmaske says so (by design: holes are not filled). Without offset all samples land at the same position and the result would be a larger, not a better picture; hence before use it should be measured whether sampling was offset at all. Drizzle beats a plain enlargement simply because it interpolates better – that has nothing to do with super-resolution. What the offset contributes is shown only by the counter-test with rounded displacements.

Worked example. Scale 3, drop 0.1: the drop measures 0.3 target cells and covers, of the nine target cells of an input pixel, only the middle one; in the test the hole fraction is exactly 8/9 (Drizzleprobe).

How it is tested

  • Fuseprobe (22 checks): the confidence levels with 7 offered (0 replaced, 1 single, 2 thin, 3 and 7 supported) and with 10 (4 thin, 5 supported); with two offered even full agreement counts only as thin; share and mean exact (2/52/5, 13/513/5). Merged: the single picture carries the set noise (between 7 and 9), plain averaging of seven follows the square-root law with 3.02 within 10 %, merging is better than the single picture by more than a factor of 1.8 and on static material at most 10 % above plain averaging. At a moving object there is exactly one observation (that of the reference picture, no streak, level EINZELN), away from it all seven. Fractional displacements are recovered within 0.05.
  • Drizzleprobe (11 checks): two equal pictures at scale 2 and full drop give exactly the block enlargement; scale 1 with full drop is the identity; a hole stays 0 and the hole mask marks exactly the holes; pictures displaced by (2, 1) fall onto the reference picture after alignment (deviation below 1), unaligned averaging lies clearly further off.
  • Numbers from the class comments come from measurements on the sample recording and on generated fixtures with fixed seed.

Limits

  • Only what can be aligned is merged: the global displacement of the merging knows no motion of individual picture parts; it is discarded as an outlier and counted in the confidence map, not tracked.
  • A spot with level EINZELN or DUENN looks as sharp as any other but is no better supported than a single picture; the confidence map must be carried along.
  • Drizzle gains resolution only where sampling was really offset; with too few or badly distributed pictures, holes remain.
  • The noise reduction 1/N1/\sqrt N holds for independent noise; what stands the same in every picture (fixed pattern, compression grid) does not average out.

Literature
