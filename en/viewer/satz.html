Blocks, Lines and Floats – Background

How the viewer stacks blocks, collapses margins, breaks text into lines and evaluates float and clear: the path from a block with children to boxes with position and size.

What this is about

An HTML document is a tree; what is laid out is a sequence of rectangles. Two levels interlock: the block flow, in which paragraphs, headings and tables stand one below the other, and the line flow, in which words run side by side until a line is full. In addition there are floats (float) that leave the flow, move to the edge and let the text run around them. The rules come from CSS 2.1, chapters 8 to 10; the parts the viewer sets differently or in simplified form are listed under Limits.

What is described here is the order of decisions in the layout engine. Flexbox, grid and tables have their own procedures and their own checks and are not part of this page; in the end they lay out the boxes of their cells through the same block and line flow.

Block flow and collapsing margins

The children of a block are handled in order, with a running pointer yy for the next free height. Block children are laid out, inline children (text, span, a, code) go into a buffer. When the flow meets a block child, the buffer is first laid out as lines, then the child is laid out. Two adjacent blocks collapse their margins: the larger one counts, not the sum.

gap=mb+mt−min⁡(mb,mt)=max⁡(mb,mt)\text{gap} = m_b + m_t - \min(m_b,\, m_t) = \max(m_b,\, m_t)

Here mbm_b is the bottom margin of the upper block and mtm_t the top margin of the lower one. The engine computes it just as written: it places the lower block with its full top margin and subtracts the overlapping part min⁡(mb,mt)\min(m_b, m_t) again. With mb=20m_b = 20 and mt=30m_t = 30 this leaves 20+30−20=3020 + 30 - 20 = 30 pixels. After laid-out lines, a float, an image or an SVG, mbm_b is set to zero; nothing collapses there.

Percentages for margins and padding refer to the width of the containing block, as in CSS 2.1, 8.3. A fixed width applies in the block flow, max-width and min-width limit it.

Breaking lines

The text of a paragraph is first split into words (character runs with their style, width, ascent and descent). The words are then placed side by side: the next word fits if x+w≤rx + w \le r, with the position xx after the last word, the word width ww and the right edge rr of the free line space. If it does not fit, the line is closed and the word starts the next one. A single word at the start of a line is always placed, even if it is too wide. Under white-space: pre there is no wrapping; forced breaks (<br>, line feeds in pre) close the line at once.

A line gets its height and baseline from all words standing in it. With the largest ascent AA, the largest descent DD and the largest line height LL:

h=max⁡(L,A+D),baseline=max⁡(0,L−(A+D))2+Ah = \max(L,\; A + D), \qquad \text{baseline} = \frac{\max(0,\; L - (A + D))}{2} + A

The excess of the line height over the text height is thus split half above and half below the text. Raised and lowered runs (sub, sup) shift their baseline: sub by one fifth of the parent element's font down, super by one third up; the line grows taller for that.

Alignment (text-align) happens only when the line is finished: for right and end the whole line moves to the right edge of the free space, for center to the middle; trailing spaces do not count. Justified text (justify) stays left-aligned.

Floats and line space

An element with float: left or right is taken out of the flow. The engine lays it out, in the full width of the containing block when width is fixed (so that percentages refer to the block), otherwise as narrow as the content allows (shrink-to-fit). Then it looks for the position:

  1. Start at the height y0y_0 where the float stands in the flow, never higher.
  2. Determine the free space [l,r][l, r] for the height from yy to y+hy + h: ll is the largest right edge of the left floats, rr the smallest left edge of the right floats touching that height.
  3. If the free space is at least as wide as the float, place it at ll (left) or at r−wr - w (right).
  4. Otherwise move yy down to the next bottom edge of a float below and start again at 2.

Every placed float is kept in a list as a rectangle with its side. The line flow asks for the free space [l,r][l, r] of its height at every line; the line starts at x=lx = l and breaks at rr. If not even one word fits next to the floats at the start of a line, the line moves down to the next bottom edge of a float. clear: left|right|both pushes a block below the affected floats and resets the margin.

A block with its own formatting context (flex, grid, table, overflow other than visible, flow-root, inline-block, also a float itself) does not overlap a float; it is laid out narrower beside it, with the free space at its top edge as width, and shifted right by ll.

The floats of a level end with the block they stand in: the block grows at least to the bottom edge of its lowest float (as with display: flow-root).

Pseudocode

function blockChildren(block, width):
    y ← 0;  prevBottomMargin ← 0;  buffer ← []
    for each child in block.children:
        if child is inline: buffer.add(child);  continue
        y ← y + setLines(buffer, y, width);  clear buffer
        if child has float left or right:
            prevBottomMargin ← 0
            placeFloat(child, y, width, right?);  continue
        if child is img or svg: lay out as its own box; continue
        y ← max(y, bottomForClear(child, y))        // only with clear
        top ← child.margin.top
        y ← y - min(prevBottomMargin, top)
        beside ← ownContext(child) ? free(y + top, 1, width) : none
        if beside narrower than width:
            y ← y + setBlock(child, y, beside.r - beside.l);  shift by beside.l
        else: y ← y + setBlock(child, y, width)
        prevBottomMargin ← child.margin.bottom
    y ← y + setLines(buffer, y, width)
    for each float of this level: y ← max(y, float.bottom)
    return y

function setLines(words, y0, width):
    y ← y0;  [l, r] ← free(y, height(words[0]), width);  x ← l;  line ← []
    for each w in words:
        if w.forcedBreak:
            align(line, r);  y ← y + commitLine(line)
            clear line;  [l, r] ← free(y, …, width);  x ← l;  continue
        fits ← (x + w.width ≤ r) or line empty or w.pre
        if not fits:
            align(line, r);  y ← y + commitLine(line)
            clear line;  [l, r] ← free(y, height(w), width);  x ← l
        while line empty and w does not fit in [l, r] and [l, r] is narrower:
            lower ← nextBottomEdge(y, height(w))
            if lower ≤ y: break
            y ← lower;  [l, r] ← free(y, height(w), width);  x ← l
        w.x ← x;  line.add(w);  x ← x + w.width
    if line not empty: align(line, r);  y ← y + commitLine(line)
    return y - y0

function free(y, h, width):                    // free space of height y … y+h
    l ← 0;  r ← width
    for each float s with s.top < y + h and s.bottom > y:
        if s is left: l ← max(l, s.rightEdge)  else: r ← min(r, s.leftEdge)
    return [max(0, l), min(width, r)]

function placeFloat(node, y0, width, right):
    (w, h) ← frame size of the laid-out node including margin
    y ← y0
    repeat:
        [l, r] ← free(y, h, width)
        if r - l ≥ w or (l ≤ 0 and r ≥ width):
            x ← right ? r - w : l;  stop
        lower ← nextBottomEdge(y, h)
        if lower ≤ y: x ← right ? width - w : 0;  stop
        y ← lower
    put node at (x, y);  remember rectangle (x, y, x + w, y + h, side)

function commitLine(line):
    A ← max ascent;  D ← max descent;  L ← max line height
    baseline ← max(0, L - (A + D)) / 2 + A
    put every word on baseline;  return max(L, A + D)

Source: envisionz.viewer.box.LayoutEngine, methods layoutBlockChildren, flushInline, commitLine, richteAus, frei, platziereSchwimmer, unterkanteFuerClear.

Decision in the framework: the parent block encloses its floats, as with display: flow-root, where CSS 2.1 would let them protrude; images in the flow do not make room for floats, they are laid out as blocks. Margins of adjacent blocks collapse to the larger one; they are computed as subtracting the smaller. Justified text stays left-aligned; text-align acts on right, end and center. A fixed width applies in the block flow; a float with fixed width is laid out in the full width of the containing block so that percentages refer to it.

How it is checked

box.Schwimmprobe (18 checks) checks the position of left and right floats, lines making room, clear, the second float beside the first and blocks with their own formatting context. box.Satzprobe (65 checks) recomputes the block flow according to CSS 2.1: width from the parent width minus margins, children in the content rectangle, collapsing vertical margins (8.3.1). ui.Setzerprobe checks, among other things, the shift of sub by one fifth and sup by one third of the parent font. Everything is computed in pixels against CSS 2.1; deliberate deviations are marked as such in the checks.

Limits

  • Margins collapse only between consecutive children of the same block; for this the engine carries only the bottom margin of the previous sibling block.
  • Line breaking splits between words, there is no hyphenation; justified text is not set.
  • Of vertical-align only sub and super apply in running text; lengths, middle and top are ignored (in table cells they are laid out as before).
  • float knows left and right; images in the flow do not run around floats.
