Height field, inflation and selection – background

How the app turns an image into a height field: brightness as height, an inflated silhouette from the distance to the edge, the selection as rectangle, ellipse, polygon or pixel mask, the sampling of an ellipse for the portrait map and storing a mask as run lengths.

What this is about

The app depends only on the application framework; image core, segmentation, mesh and renderer live in the app itself. A pixel holds brightness, not depth: a dark eye would otherwise become a hole, a white blouse a bump. Hence two honest ways – brightness as relief (cameo) and an inflated silhouette with brightness as fine detail on top.

Brightness as height

The colour channels give the luminance according to Rec. 709,

Y=0.2126R+0.7152G+0.0722B255∈[0,1].Y = \frac{0.2126\,R + 0.7152\,G + 0.0722\,B}{255} \in [0, 1].

The selection is sampled on an equidistant grid whose cells are square in the world: the number of rows follows from the number of columns and the aspect ratio of the crop. Processing (smoothing, normalising, gamma, inverting) acts on the grid, not on the whole image; that keeps the sliders fast regardless of the image size. In cameo mode the height is z=Y⋅sz = Y \cdot s with the height scale ss.

Inflating a silhouette

For every grid point inside the selection a chamfer distance transform with the weights 3 and 4 measures the distance dd to the edge. With u=d/dmax⁡u = d/d_{\max} a profile P(u)P(u) gives the inflation height:

Ppillow⁡(u)=u,Pcone⁡(u)=u,Pplateau⁡(u)=x2(3−2x),x=min⁡(1,u/0.35).P_{\mathrm{pillow}}(u) = \sqrt{u}, \qquad P_{\mathrm{cone}}(u) = u, \qquad P_{\mathrm{plateau}}(u) = x^2(3 - 2x),\ x = \min(1, u/0.35).

The plateau rises within an edge zone of 35 % and then stays flat. Brightness is added to the silhouette as fine detail,

z=P(u)V+Ys,z = P(u)\,V + Y\,s,

with the volume VV and the detail scale ss; outside the selection z=0z = 0. Since u≥u\sqrt{u} \ge u for u∈[0,1]u \in [0, 1], the cone lies below the pillow on average.

The selection

Rectangle and ellipse are dragged with the mouse and converted into pixels. If the result is shorter than 4 pixels in one direction, the movement counts as a click and resets the selection to the whole image.

The magic wand grows from the clicked pixel over the four neighbours as long as |Y−Ystart⁡|≤t|Y - Y_{\mathrm{start}}| \le t; the slider is in hundredths, t=slider/100t = \text{slider}/100. With edge stop, growing also ends at pixels whose Sobel gradient exceeds 0.15. Invert takes the complement of the mask; clean-up closes, opens, keeps the largest connected area and fills holes. Both act only on pixel masks and otherwise say so in the status line.

The portrait map

An ellipse is sampled in two parameters: tt runs linearly from 0.06 to 0.97 over the height, φ\varphi from −75∘-75^\circ to +75∘+75^\circ over the width, with the sine mapping

x=cx+rxsin⁡φsin⁡75∘.x = c_x + r_x\,\frac{\sin\varphi}{\sin 75^\circ}.

The sample points thus lie closer together towards the centre, as on a curved surface. The field is shifted by its mean and stored with the chosen amplitude. The output always has 39 rows and 27 columns; if the extension .xml is missing, it is appended.

Saving mesh and mask

The export writes the full mesh as OBJ (one line per vertex and per triangle) or as XML. In the work state a pixel mask is stored as run lengths: alternating runs of “off” and “on”, starting with “off”, separated by spaces. A mask whose run lengths need more than 60,000 characters is not stored; the log notes it. On loading, the runs must add up to exactly w⋅hw \cdot h, otherwise the mask is discarded and the selection falls back to the rectangle.

Limits

  • Brightness is lighting, not depth. Cameo mode shows the image as a relief, not a model of the object.
  • Processing acts on the sampled grid. Fine structures below the grid spacing are lost; double supersampling only reduces flicker.
  • Very fine pixel masks are not stored in the work state.
