envisionz-app-relief

From photo to 3D relief without inventing depth

Overview of the app, as of 29 September 2026. A photo or grayscale image becomes a relief that can be turned on screen. The app keeps brightness and depth apart and therefore offers only methods whose result can be explained.

17source files in 4 packages
2relief modes: brightness as height, inflated silhouette
4selection tools: rectangle, ellipse, polygon, magic wand
3inflation profiles: pillow, cone, plateau
> 190checks in four probes
3help pages, German and English

What the app is for

A photo looks spatial but is flat. Turning it into a relief, for instance as a master for embossing, milling or a 3D view, runs into a basic problem: brightness is lighting, not depth. A dark eye would become a deep hole, a white blouse would stand out further than the nose. A method that works on good photographs and fails on all others is worse than none, because the two cases cannot be told apart by looking at the result.

The app therefore takes the honest route. It offers separate modes, and each says where the height comes from. Operation follows the pattern of the other envisionz apps: the image with its selection on the left, the rotatable 3D view on the right, and between them a vertical tool bar with cards for the settings. There are no pop-up windows.

What it can do

Load an image and choose a region

The image appears as a grayscale preview (luminance after Rec. 709). The selection decides what becomes relief.

  • Rectangle and ellipse are dragged with the mouse; a short click resets the selection to the whole image.
  • Polygon is built by clicking, a double click closes it.
  • Magic wand grows from a seed point over similar brightness, with adjustable tolerance and an optional edge stop. Otsu threshold, clean-up (close, open, largest connected region, fill holes) and invert are available as buttons. The mask is shown as an overlay.

Conditioning the brightness

Processing runs on the sampled grid, not on the full image. The sliders therefore react without delay, independent of the image resolution.

  • Blur, percentile normalisation, gamma and invert.
  • Twofold supersampling against moiré patterns from fine screens in the image.
  • Grid width from 33 to 193 columns. Cells stay square so the relief does not look stretched.

Two relief modes

Both modes can be combined in one image: silhouette and brightness are added in absolute terms.

  • Cameo: the height follows the brightness of the selected region. Useful for originals whose brightness really means the desired height, such as drawings or embossings.
  • Inflate silhouette: the height follows only the distance to the edge of the selection, not the brightness. Three profiles (pillow, cone, plateau) and a volume slider set the shape. A cup in front of a wall becomes a body with a rounded back, however bright or dark the cup is.

View and light

The relief is drawn as a triangle mesh, shaded with averaged vertex normals.

  • Rotate with the mouse, zoom with the wheel. The tilt angle is limited so the surface is never seen from below.
  • While dragging, the app draws a half-resolution mesh and the full one on release.
  • Light direction adjustable by azimuth and elevation.

Portrait field

For face photos the app writes a displacement field that a face mesh in another app of the framework can take up.

  • Registration uses the ellipse selection. An overlay grid of longitude lines every 15 degrees and latitude lines with a highlighted eye line shows how well it fits.
  • The field is sampled in the parameter space of the face mesh and centred on its mean.

Data and formats

  • Reading: raster images the JDK can read.
  • Relief XML: the height grid with a version number, readable for round trips and later bridges.
  • OBJ: vertices, normals and faces, so the relief shows up in other 3D tools.
  • Displacement-field XML from the portrait. The reader also accepts the older root element of earlier files, so existing files stay valid.
  • Working state: all parameters, the selection (including polygon points) and the magic-wand mask are saved with the workspace. The pixel mask is stored run-length encoded.

What it is built on

  • framework-api: registration as an app through ServiceLoader and saving of the working state.
  • framework-swing: user interface, tool bar with cards (OptionsDock) and captions in two languages.

The computing cores core, io and render are deliberately free of framework classes and run without a user interface. The 3D drawing is a drawing routine of its own with depth sorting, without a 3D library. The module needs no third-party library.

Status and limits

The path from image to output is complete: loading, selection with four tools, conditioning, both relief modes, light, rotating view, export as XML and OBJ, portrait field and saving of the working state. Four probes name every production class; among other things they check that the height of the inflated silhouette follows only from the selection and not from the brightness.

A method that estimates depth from shading (“shape from shading”) is missing on purpose. It gives fine results on good photographs and wrong ones on all others, without the difference being visible. Also missing are an association of image files with the app and alignment of the portrait by four grip points; the ellipse has been enough so far.
