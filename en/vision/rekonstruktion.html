From two views to the surface

Two images of the same rigid object fix the motion of the camera up to scale. With further views they become a camera path and a point cloud, adjusted jointly and with their uncertainty. A known distance gives the scale, depth maps make the cloud dense, and the Poisson equation closes it into a surface.

What it is about

The chain has five links: matched features of two images, the relative pose from epipolar geometry, attaching further views through points of known position, the bundle adjustment over everything and finally scale, dense points and surface. Every link is measured on scenes from the test object, whose truth is exact.

Epipolar constraint

With the convention X2=RX1+t\mathbf X_2 = R\,\mathbf X_1 + \mathbf t, normalised image positions x^i\hat{\mathbf x}_i and pixels xi\mathbf x_i satisfy

x^2TEx^1=0,E=[t]×R,x2TFx1=0,F=K2−TEK1−1.\hat{\mathbf x}_2^{\mathsf T} E\,\hat{\mathbf x}_1 = 0, \quad E = [\mathbf t]_\times R, \qquad \mathbf x_2^{\mathsf T} F\,\mathbf x_1 = 0, \quad F = K_2^{-\mathsf T} E\, K_1^{-1} .

The fundamental matrix is estimated by the normalised 8-point method: shift and scale the image positions so that their centroid is at the origin and their mean distance is 2\sqrt 2, solve the linear system by singular value decomposition, and then set the smallest singular value to zero. Without this last step FF has rank 3 with noisy data, and the epipolar lines do not meet in one point.

With a known camera five points suffice. The method after Stewénius writes EE as a combination of the four null-space vectors, imposes the cubic constraints det⁡E=0\det E = 0 and 2EETE−tr⁡(EET)E=02 E E^{\mathsf T} E - \operatorname{tr}(E E^{\mathsf T})\,E = 0 and solves the resulting system via a 10 × 10 action matrix; its real eigenvalues give up to ten solutions.

An essential matrix decomposes into four poses (R,±t)(R, \pm\mathbf t) with two rotations; the one is chosen for which the points lie in front of both cameras. Points behind both cameras give the same image positions as −X-\mathbf X in front with −t-\mathbf t; the choice then consistently yields (R,−t)(R, -\mathbf t).

Outliers and final adjustment

Matches contain errors. RANSAC draws samples of five pairs and scores each solution by MSAC (cost capped at the threshold), with local optimisation and a number of draws that adapts to the best inlier ratio so far. A fixed threshold leaves mismatches close to the epipolar line as inliers. A robust final adjustment therefore follows: the spread from the median of the residuals, inliers up to 3σ3\sigma, Cauchy weights. In case of doubt the checking rule applies: if the cost at the truth is higher than at the result, the data are wrong, not the solver.

A point from two rays is first determined linearly (DLT or midpoint of the shortest connection) and then moved with Gauss-Newton so that the sum of squared distances in the images decreases; that is the maximum-likelihood estimate for equal noise in both images.

Bundle adjustment

A further view is attached through points of known position: P3P after Grunert determines from three rays the distances to the three points as roots of a quartic, selected by MSAC and refined with Levenberg-Marquardt.

Then all poses c\mathbf c, inner parameters and points p\mathbf p are adjusted jointly. The normal equations have the block form

(UWWTV)(ΔcΔp)=(ab),\begin{pmatrix} U & W \\ W^{\mathsf T} & V \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \Delta\mathbf c \\ \Delta\mathbf p \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf a \\ \mathbf b \end{pmatrix},

in which VV consists of 3 × 3 blocks per point and is cheap to invert. The Schur complement S=U−WV−1WTS = U - W V^{-1} W^{\mathsf T} leaves only the cameras; the points follow block by block. The adjustment is made robust with Huber weights through iteratively reweighted least squares.

Position, rotation and scale of the whole scene cannot be determined from images. These seven freedoms are fixed by a gauge: one view stays fixed, plus the largest component of t\mathbf t of a second. Anyone comparing covariances with a truth must bring the truth into exactly this gauge.

The covariance of a point contains the share of the camera uncertainty:

Σp=σ02(V−1+V−1WTS−1WV−1).\Sigma_{\mathbf p} = \sigma_0^2 \left( V^{-1} + V^{-1} W^{\mathsf T} S^{-1} W V^{-1} \right) .

Without the second term the points would be too certain. The acceptance test on a room corner from six rendered views gave a distance to the surface of 0.49 mm (0.08 %) with a normalised error of 0.89.

Scale and alignment

A known distance LL between two reconstructed points at distance dd gives the factor s=L/ds = L/d. Its uncertainty has two parts, the measurement of the distance and the position of the points:

(σss)2=(σLL)2+eT(Σ1+Σ2)ed2,\left(\frac{\sigma_s}{s}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{\sigma_L}{L}\right)^2 + \frac{\mathbf e^{\mathsf T} (\Sigma_1 + \Sigma_2)\, \mathbf e}{d^2},

with e\mathbf e the direction of the distance. Several distances are averaged with weights; a χ2\chi^2 over their deviations reveals a wrong value. For comparison with a truth the similarity after Umeyama is used: rotation from the singular value decomposition of the cross-covariance, with sign correction so that a reflection does not pass as a rotation.

Dense points and surface

Depth maps are made by plane sweeping: for every depth dd of a family, uniform in 1/d1/d, a window of the reference view is mapped into two to four neighbouring views and compared by normalised cross-correlation; a parabola through the best 1/d1/d and its neighbours refines the position. A point only counts once another map sees it at the same depth; its normal comes from a plane in the 7 × 7 window and points towards the camera.

The surface is the iso-surface of an indicator function χ\chi (1 inside, 0 outside). Since the normals point outwards, ∇χ≈−V\nabla\chi \approx -\mathbf V is required, with V\mathbf V the smoothed normal field of the points. Taking the divergence of both sides leads to the Poisson equation

−Δχ=∇⋅V,χ=0 on the boundary,-\Delta \chi = \nabla\cdot\mathbf V, \qquad \chi = 0 \text{ on the boundary},

solved with conjugate gradients on a regular grid. The iso-value is the mean of χ\chi at the points. The iso-surface is built from six tetrahedra per cube; neighbouring cubes share their edge points, so the mesh is closed. The edge parameter stays in [10−6,1−10−6][10^{-6}, 1 - 10^{-6}]: if the iso-value lay exactly on a node, several mesh vertices would otherwise coincide.

Closed means: every edge belongs to exactly two triangles, once in each direction. If a triangle is missing, the mesh is open. Output is STL, PLY or OBJ; in OBJ the vertices count from 1.

The test object

Measurement is against rendered scenes. The ray tracer generates every ray through the back-projection of the camera model, samples every pixel several times and shades after Lambert under directional light. Each image comes with a depth map (depth is ZZ in the camera frame, not the ray length), a label map of the body hit, target points and target dimensions. Scenes can be stored as text without loss and read back; an unknown key is an error with line number, not a silent fallback.

For viewing, the maps are stored as grey images: depth from bright (near) to 0.2 (far), missing depth 0; label kk as 0.25+0.75((37k)mod16)/150.25 + 0.75\,((37 k) \bmod 16)/15, so that neighbouring labels differ clearly, without label 0.

How it is checked

  • Zweiansichtenprobe (39): 8-point exact and with rank 2, 5-point in 200 of 200 cases, also for planar scenes, decomposition, triangulation, RANSAC with 40 % outliers, pure rotation, points behind the cameras, acceptance on seven image pairs.
  • Buendelprobe (28): Umeyama exact and with reflection, P3P, pose with 30 % outliers, tracks, bundle adjustment (cost, σ0\sigma_0, gauge, normalised error, self-calibration), scale, acceptance on the room corner.
  • Dichtprobe (12): box mean, reduced camera, iso-surface of a sphere, Poisson on exact points, open mesh detected; acceptance on a patterned sphere from 24 views with mean distance 0.28 % and volume ratio 0.99.
  • Randfallprobe ([ZD-VI-01] to [ZD-VI-15]): mesh output, scene file, grey-value maps, ray without a hit, edge cases of the features.

Limits

  • Dense reconstruction needs short distances between views: at 60 degrees between neighbours the rigid correlation window distorts, at 30 degrees no longer noticeably.
  • Where several edges compete in the window of a corner, the sub-pixel position is not a fixed spot on the surface. Corners with a large residual size are therefore discarded.
  • Only points with at least three observations are output; outliers above max⁡(5σ0,0.5\max(5\sigma_0, 0.5 px)) are removed.
