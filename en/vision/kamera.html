The camera – projection, distortion, calibration

Every measurement from images begins with the question which line of sight belongs to a pixel. The camera model answers it in both directions: from the point in space to the pixel and from the pixel back to the ray. Calibration determines its parameters from images of a known pattern, vanishing points from edges of an object.

What it is about

The model has ten parameters: focal lengths fx,fyf_x, f_y in pixels, principal point cx,cyc_x, c_y, skew ss, three radial coefficients k1,k2,k3k_1, k_2, k_3 and two tangential ones p1,p2p_1, p_2. The position of the camera is a pose: rotation and translation, Xk=RX+t\mathbf X_k = R\,\mathbf X + \mathbf t. Integer image coordinates (u,v)(u, v) denote the centre of a pixel.

Projection and distortion

A point in the camera frame (X,Y,Z)(X, Y, Z) with Z>0Z > 0 is first normalised, x=X/Zx = X/Z, y=Y/Zy = Y/Z, then distorted. With r2=x2+y2r^2 = x^2 + y^2, Brown's model gives

xd=x(1+k1r2+k2r4+k3r6)+2p1xy+p2(r2+2x2),x_d = x\,(1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4 + k_3 r^6) + 2 p_1 x y + p_2 (r^2 + 2x^2), yd=y(1+k1r2+k2r4+k3r6)+p1(r2+2y2)+2p2xy.y_d = y\,(1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4 + k_3 r^6) + p_1 (r^2 + 2y^2) + 2 p_2 x y .

The calibration matrix maps the distorted position to pixels:

u=fxxd+syd+cx,v=fyyd+cy.u = f_x\,x_d + s\,y_d + c_x, \qquad v = f_y\,y_d + c_y .

Back-projection and its range

For the way back K−1K^{-1} is applied and the distortion inverted. There is no closed formula for this; the module solves F(x,y)=(xd,yd)F(x, y) = (x_d, y_d) with Newton and the analytic Jacobian, starting at (xd,yd)(x_d, y_d), at most 50 steps, stopping at a residual below 10−1510^{-15}. If the method does not converge, the result is NaN.

The radial mapping is only invertible up to its maximum for negative coefficients. With k2=k3=0k_2 = k_3 = 0 it reads r↦r(1+k1r2)r \mapsto r\,(1 + k_1 r^2); for k1<0k_1 < 0 the derivative 1+3k1r21 + 3 k_1 r^2 vanishes at

rmax⁡=1−3k1,rd,max⁡=23rmax⁡.r_{\max} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-3 k_1}}, \qquad r_{d,\max} = \tfrac{2}{3}\, r_{\max} .

Pixels with a larger distorted radius have no line of sight. For k1=k2=−0.5k_1 = k_2 = -0.5 the limit lies so close to the image centre that the image corners are outside; the ray tracer renders no ray there and marks the pixel with depth NaN and label −1. A test image with such distortion therefore has corners without content, not corners with wrong content.

Rotation vector

A rotation is kept as a vector ω=θn\omega = \theta\,\mathbf n: axis times angle, three parameters without constraint. The matrix follows from Rodrigues,

R=I+sin⁡θ[n]×+(1−cos⁡θ)[n]×2,R = I + \sin\theta\,[\mathbf n]_\times + (1 - \cos\theta)\,[\mathbf n]_\times^2 ,

and back from cos⁡θ=(tr⁡R−1)/2\cos\theta = (\operatorname{tr} R - 1)/2 and the skew-symmetric part. At θ=0\theta = 0 and θ=π\theta = \pi the simple inversion fails; both cases are handled separately.

Calibration after Zhang

A planar pattern (Z=0Z = 0) is mapped by a homography H=[h1h2h3]∼K[r1r2t]H = [\mathbf h_1\ \mathbf h_2\ \mathbf h_3] \sim K\,[\mathbf r_1\ \mathbf r_2\ \mathbf t]. Since r1\mathbf r_1 and r2\mathbf r_2 are orthonormal, every view yields two linear equations for the symmetric matrix B=K−TK−1B = K^{-\mathsf T} K^{-1}:

h1TBh2=0,h1TBh1=h2TBh2.\mathbf h_1^{\mathsf T} B\, \mathbf h_2 = 0, \qquad \mathbf h_1^{\mathsf T} B\, \mathbf h_1 = \mathbf h_2^{\mathsf T} B\, \mathbf h_2 .

With b=(B11,B12,B22,B13,B23,B33)\mathbf b = (B_{11}, B_{12}, B_{22}, B_{13}, B_{23}, B_{33}) one writes hiTBhj=vijTb\mathbf h_i^{\mathsf T} B\,\mathbf h_j = \mathbf v_{ij}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf b, where

vij=(h1ih1j,h1ih2j+h2ih1j,h2ih2j,h3ih1j+h1ih3j,h3ih2j+h2ih3j,h3ih3j).\mathbf v_{ij} = \bigl(h_{1i}h_{1j},\; h_{1i}h_{2j} + h_{2i}h_{1j},\; h_{2i}h_{2j},\; h_{3i}h_{1j} + h_{1i}h_{3j},\; h_{3i}h_{2j} + h_{2i}h_{3j},\; h_{3i}h_{3j}\bigr).

From three views on (two with zero skew), b\mathbf b is determined up to a factor; KK follows from BB in closed form. That is only the starting value: afterwards inner parameters, distortion and all poses are adjusted jointly with Levenberg-Marquardt, the objective being the sum of squared reprojection errors.

The chessboard corners are searched as saddle points and placed to fractions of a pixel by the gradient method: at the true corner q\mathbf q every gradient g\mathbf g in the window is perpendicular to p−q\mathbf p - \mathbf q, so q\mathbf q solves ∑ggT(p−q)=0\sum \mathbf g \mathbf g^{\mathsf T} (\mathbf p - \mathbf q) = 0 in the least-squares sense.

Vanishing points

Parallel edges meet in the image at the vanishing point of their direction. For two mutually perpendicular directions with vanishing points v1,v2\mathbf v_1, \mathbf v_2, square pixels and principal point c\mathbf c,

f2=−(v1−c)⋅(v2−c).f^2 = -(\mathbf v_1 - \mathbf c)\cdot(\mathbf v_2 - \mathbf c) .

With three pairwise perpendicular directions the principal point is the orthocentre of the triangle of the three vanishing points; principal point and focal length are thus determined from a single image. If a vanishing point lies at infinity (edges parallel to the image plane), the result is NaN.

How it is checked

  • Kameraprobe (39 checks): projection against an independently written formula, round trips pixel – ray – pixel, rotation vector at 0 and 180 degrees, homography, Levenberg-Marquardt with covariance against Monte Carlo, Zhang and adjustment on exact points to 10−1310^{-13}, calibration from rendered images clean and disturbed, vanishing points.
  • Randfallprobe [ZD-VI-15]: a camera with k1=k2=−0.5k_1 = k_2 = -0.5; the image centre hits the wall, the corner has no ray.

Limits

  • Individual distortion coefficients are poorly determined: k2k_2 and k3k_3 compensate each other, and outside the area covered by corners the distortion is not fixed. What is compared is therefore the mapping ray – pixel in the covered area.
  • The views must cover the image into the corners; otherwise the calibration is poorly conditioned.
  • With very strong distortion the radial mapping is invertible only up to a limit radius; beyond it the mapping falls again, and Newton could converge there on the falling branch. entzerre therefore checks after convergence whether the derivative 1+3k1q+5k2q2+7k3q31 + 3k_1 q + 5k_2 q^2 + 7k_3 q^3 is positive on the whole range [0,r2][0, r^2] and the Jacobian determinant at the point is positive as well, and returns NaN otherwise. Pixels beyond the limit radius thus have no ray instead of a wrong one.
