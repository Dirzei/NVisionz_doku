Contours and colour scales – background

How a field of numbers becomes isolines and colour fills: marching squares on grids, chaining into polylines, colour bands by polygon clipping, the same on triangle meshes, and the mapping from value to colour. Pseudocode following the source, conventions, checks, limits.

What this is about

A performance map, a temperature field or a CFD result is given as a value ff at individual nodes – on a grid or, for measurement clouds, at scattered points. Two displays turn this into a picture: isolines join points of equal value (the efficiency island in a compressor map is such a bundle of lines), colour fills colour every location by its value. Both need an assumption about how ff behaves between the nodes. The framework takes the simplest one: linear along the edges of a cell or triangle, and bilinear inside a grid cell.

The computation lives in ContourLayers.levelSegments (segments), IsoPolyline (chaining), ContourRenderer (colour on the grid), TriIsoLines and TriContourRenderer (triangle mesh) and Colormap and ColormapPaintScale (colour). Drawing is done by JFreeChart; this page covers only what is computed before that.

Marching squares: isolines on the grid

Each grid cell has four corners a,b,c,da, b, c, d (counter-clockwise) with values fa…fdf_a \dots f_d. For a level LL every corner is either "above" (f≥Lf \ge L) or "below". The isoline crosses exactly those edges whose end points lie on different sides. On such an edge from PP to QQ the crossing point, linearly interpolated, is at

t=L−fPfQ−fP,x→=x→P+t(x→Q−x→P)t = \frac{L - f_P}{f_Q - f_P}, \qquad \vec{x} = \vec{x}_P + t\,(\vec{x}_Q - \vec{x}_P)

Two crossed edges give a line segment through the cell. Four crossed edges occur at a saddle (opposite corners above); then two segments result.

Pseudocode – ContourLayers.levelSegments(ds, L)

for each cell c of the grid:
    (x, y, z) ← the four corners a, b, c, d
    n ← 0
    for each edge (p, q) in (a,b), (b,c), (c,d), (d,a):
        if z[p] or z[q] is NaN: continue                 skip edge without value
        above_p ← z[p] ≥ L;  above_q ← z[q] ≥ L           equality counts as "above"
        if above_p ≠ above_q:
            t ← (L − z[p]) / (z[q] − z[p])
            point[n] ← x[p] + t·(x[q] − x[p]),  y[p] + t·(y[q] − y[p]);  n ← n+1
    if n = 2: segment (point[0], point[1])
    if n = 4: segments (point[0], point[1]) and (point[2], point[3])
    // zero-length segments (a corner exactly on the level) are discarded

Source: envisionz-framework-chart/src/envisionz/chart/contour/ContourLayers.java. An identical loop (addLevel) fills the data series that JFreeChart draws.

Convention in the framework: "Equal to the level" belongs to the upper side (>=). An isoline therefore passes exactly through a node lying on the level, and both neighbouring cells compute the same point – the line does not fall apart. At a saddle (four crossings) the decision is not made from the cell mean; the points are paired in edge order into two segments.

Which levels are drawn

IsoLines.levels(lo, hi, absDelta, delta, count) returns the levels between the limits, never the limits themselves – a level exactly on the data boundary would give a line that is not one. With a fixed step (Δ>0\Delta > 0) these are lo+Δ,lo+2Δ,…lo+\Delta,\; lo+2\Delta,\dots as long as the value stays below hi−10−9hi-10^{-9}; otherwise nn equally spaced levels

Lk=lo+(hi−lo)kn+1,k=1,…,n(n=max⁡(1,count⁡))L_k = lo + (hi - lo)\,\frac{k}{n+1}, \qquad k = 1,\dots,n \quad (n = \max(1, \mathrm{count}))

IsoLines.bandLevels(lo, hi, B) gives the B−1B-1 inner band limits lo+(hi−lo)k/Blo + (hi-lo)\,k/B; for B<2B < 2 or hi≤lohi \le lo none. Examples from the check: range 0…10 with four levels gives 2, 4, 6, 8; with step 2.5 it gives 2.5, 5, 7.5 (not the upper limit 10); five bands have the limits 2, 4, 6, 8.

Chaining segments into polylines

Marching squares delivers unordered single segments. For a continuous line, a value label at a sensible place and for dashed patterns they must be joined into polylines. IsoPolyline.chain does this in linear time: every end point is sorted into a tolerance grid of mesh width q=10−7⋅q = 10^{-7}\cdot (largest extent of all segments); end points in the same grid cell count as the same point. Starting from an unused segment the line is extended at both ends as long as an unused segment joins at the end point.

chain(segments):
    q ← 1e−7 · max(extent in x, extent in y)               1.0 if the extent is 0
    for each segment i: enter i in a table under key(start) and key(end)
        key(x,y) = round(x/q) · 1 000 000 007 xor round(y/q)
    for each unused segment i:
        mark i used;  line ← [start(i), end(i)]
        repeat at the tail: take an unused segment from the table at the end point;
                           append its other end point;  mark it used
        repeat at the head: likewise, towards the front
        emit line
snap(f, L):  |f − L| ≤ 1e−12 · max(|f|, |L|)  ?  L  :  f

Source: envisionz-framework-chart/src/envisionz/chart/contour/IsoPolyline.java. The label position labelAnchor is the middle point of the line together with its two neighbours; IsoCurveLabel computes the rotation angle from them at draw time in screen coordinates.

Convention in the framework: einrasten (snap) sets a corner value lying at most 10−1210^{-12} relative to the iso value exactly onto the iso value. Without it a value such as 0.30000000000000004 at level 0.3 is "above", its neighbour "below", and an isoline running exactly along grid edges breaks into pieces, each with its own value label. The check confirms that 0.3001 does not snap and that at level 0 only 0 itself does.

Colour fills on the grid

ContourRenderer knows two ways to fill a cell.

Continuous: the cell is split into (s+1)(s+1)-by-(s+1)(s+1) sub-cells (ss = subdivisions, default 6, clamped to 1…16). The sub-cell corners come from bilinear interpolation

f(u,v)=(1−u)(1−v)fa+u(1−v)fb+uvfc+(1−u)vfd,u,v∈[0,1]f(u,v) = (1-u)(1-v)\,f_a + u(1-v)\,f_b + uv\,f_c + (1-u)v\,f_d, \qquad u, v \in [0,1]

and each sub-cell gets the colour of the mean of its four corner values.

In bands: the value range of the scale is divided into BB equally wide bands (default 16, clamped to 2…64). For every band touched by the value range of the cell, the cell quadrilateral is clipped in turn at the lower and at the upper limit (Sutherland-Hodgman, one half-plane at a time). What remains is a polygon that carries exactly one band colour.

clipHalfPlane(polygon, threshold, keepAbove):
    for each corner cur with predecessor prev (cyclic):
        in_cur ← keepAbove ? cur.z ≥ threshold : cur.z ≤ threshold     likewise in_prev
        if in_cur ≠ in_prev:                                 the edge crosses the threshold
            (a, e) ← (prev, cur), ordered by (x, y)             direction-independent: neighbouring cells agree bit for bit
            t ← (threshold − a.z) / (e.z − a.z)                     skipped if |denominator| ≤ 1e−300
            append intersection a + t·(e − a), z = threshold
        if in_cur: append cur

drawIsoBands(cell):
    zMin, zMax ← smallest, largest corner value;  if NaN: draw nothing
    b0 ← ⌊(zMin − lo)/(hi − lo) · B⌋,  b1 ← ⌈(zMax − lo)/(hi − lo) · B⌉   clamped to 0…B−1 and b0+1…B
    for b ← b0 to b1−1:
        bandLo ← lo + b·(hi−lo)/B;  bandHi ← bandLo + (hi−lo)/B
        P ← clipHalfPlane(cell, bandLo, above);  P ← clipHalfPlane(P, bandHi, below)
        if P has at least three corners: add P (in image coordinates) to band b

drawBands():                                            second pass, once per chart
    for each band b with partial polygons:
        U ← union(partial polygons of b)                     shared edges cancel
        fill U in colour(b) and trace its edge 0.7 px wide in the same colour

Source: envisionz-framework-chart/src/envisionz/chart/contour/ContourRenderer.java. The grid refinement (ContourDataset.refine) uses the same bilinear formula and returns the whole point as NaN if one corner value is NaN.

Convention in the framework: The band colour follows the lower band limit – as in the 2020 predecessor program. Fill and edge are drawn together in the same colour so that neighbouring cell polygons overlap slightly and no light seams appear. Cells with a NaN corner value are not filled.

Union per band. Partial polygons are no longer drawn one by one. In the first pass the renderer collects the partial polygons per band, in the second Baendervereinigung merges them into a few outlines and draws one path per band. The method removes shared edges: every partial polygon is oriented the same way and entered as directed edges; if an edge u→vu \to v meets its counter-edge v→uv \to u, it is an inner edge between two partial cells of the same band, and both are dropped. The remaining edges close into loops – outer boundaries positive, holes negative –, so that the nonzero fill rule fills exactly the band area; collinear intermediate points are removed. For the edges to cancel, neighbouring cells must compute their shared intersection points bit for bit identically; that is why clipHalfPlane always interpolates from the endpoint that is smaller in (x,y)(x, y). If an edge still remains unpaired (T-junction, folded grid), it stays in the outline without any error.

In SVG export this makes the difference: instead of two elements per partial cell the file holds one path per band; for a 21 × 21 grid it shrinks from a good 7 MB to about 200 kB. SvgExport also rounds the coordinates to 1/100 pixel (where the JFreeSVG version allows it, from 4.x); otherwise the full float length made up about two thirds of the file.

Triangle meshes

For scattered points framework-data supplies a triangulation; TriIsoLines and TriContourRenderer compute the same on it, only simpler: a triangle has three edges, and an isoline crosses either two of them or none – there is no saddle, the assignment is unambiguous. The band fill clips every triangle with the same two half-planes as above and in addition at the visible window, so that nothing outside is computed when zooming.

march(triangle a, b, c; L):
    if a corner value is NaN: nothing
    fa, fb, fc ← snap(f, L)                                as on the grid, since 2026-09-25
    crossings ← crossing points of edges ab, bc, ca         rule f ≥ L = "above", as on the grid
    if exactly two crossings and not identical: segment

Source: envisionz-framework-chart/src/envisionz/chart/tri/TriIsoLines.java, fill in TriContourRenderer.java.

Convention in the framework: The value range of the scale is formed only from points that occur in at least one triangle (TriChartFactory.bereichBenutzt); NaN does not count. Two detached points used by no triangle would otherwise stretch the colour scale (91 % of the scale in the case that prompted this). If the span is zero, hi = lo + 1e-12 is set. Each band appears in the colour of its lower limit.

Value → colour

A Colormap is a table of anchor colours at positions t∈[0,1]t \in [0,1]; in between, RGB is interpolated linearly and rounded to integers. There are five: Jet (6 anchors), Viridis (5), Turbo (9), grayscale (2), cool-warm (3). ColormapPaintScale maps a value vv in three steps:

t=v−lohi−lo→t∈[0,1](clamped)→t'=⌊tB⌋+12B(bands only, ⌊tB⌋≤B−1)→optionally 1−t't = \frac{v - lo}{hi - lo} \;\to\; t \in [0,1]\ \text{(clamped)} \;\to\; t' = \frac{\lfloor tB \rfloor + \tfrac12}{B}\ \text{(bands only, } \lfloor tB \rfloor \le B-1) \;\to\; \text{optionally } 1 - t'

A value just outside [lo,hi][lo, hi] therefore gets the edge colour – no colour that is missing from the legend. With bands the colour of the band centre applies. With hi≤lohi \le lo, t=0t = 0.

Colormap.at(t):
    if t is NaN: t ← 0
    if t ≤ 0: first anchor colour;  if t ≥ 1: last
    i ← first index with t ≤ pos[i]                         i ≥ 1
    f ← (t − pos[i−1]) / (pos[i] − pos[i−1])
    return per channel  round(c[i−1] + f·(c[i] − c[i−1]))  clamped to 0…255

Source: envisionz-framework-chart/src/envisionz/chart/color/Colormap.java, ColormapPaintScale.java.

Worked example (by hand). Viridis with twelve bands, vv at the middle of the scale: t=0.5t = 0.5, band ⌊6⌋=6\lfloor 6 \rfloor = 6, t'=6.5/12=0.5417t' = 6.5/12 = 0.5417. That lies between the anchors 0.5 (33, 145, 140) and 0.75 (94, 201, 98) with f=0.1667f = 0.1667: red 33+0.1667⋅61=43.2→4333 + 0.1667 \cdot 61 = 43.2 \to 43, green 145+0.1667⋅56=154.3→154145 + 0.1667\cdot 56 = 154.3 \to 154, blue 140−0.1667⋅42=133140 - 0.1667\cdot 42 = 133. Colour: (43, 154, 133).

How it is checked

  • contour.Konturprobe: grid index ↔ cell index (a swapped i/j would give a mirrored but plausible picture), refine against hand-computed bilinear values, levels/bandLevels, IsoPolyline.chain (a tolerance too coarse or too fine breaks lines), Colormap.at including clamping just below 0 and just above 1. Tolerance of the number comparisons 10−910^{-9}.
  • contour.Isolinienprobe: the level examples above, layers and slot numbers of the isolines, clean-up with remove.
  • contour.Isoeinrastprobe: einrasten with the limiting cases named, then on grid and triangle mesh the number of line pieces against the number of lines.
  • contour.Baenderexportprobe: SVG export of a band picture above 1 MiB before, at most a twentieth and below 300 KiB after; coordinates with at most two decimals; one path per band; the drawn picture differs from the non-merged one only at edges (anti-aliasing) and on less than 2 % of the area; a ring of eight squares gives two loops of four corners each, and the hole stays empty; shared edges give bit-identical intersection points in both directions.
  • tri.Dreiecksprobe and tri.Isofarbprobe: used range (0…10, the unused point 1000 does not count), five bands with the grey values 50, 80, 110, 140, 170, each in the colour of the lower limit, behaviour when zoomed and with a scale without span.

Limits

  • The linear edge assumption is an approximation: between the nodes the field has no turning points that the grid does not resolve. A grid that is too coarse produces angular lines; refine does not smooth them, it only interpolates bilinearly.
  • The saddle is not resolved (see above); in cells with four crossings the pairing of segments may differ from the "correct" one.
  • The band clip cuts the cell quadrilateral in the picture plane at the corner values; in a strongly distorted cell the band boundary therefore deviates slightly from the exact isoline.
  • Cells and triangles with NaN are skipped; the area stays empty there.
  • The colour scales are fixed built-in tables; there are no user-defined tables.
