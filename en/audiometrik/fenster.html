Power spectrum, cross-correlation and windows – background

The computation behind Spektren and Fenster: what a short-time power spectrum is, why an excerpt has to be weighted, how the Hann, Hamming, sine and KBD windows are built, how the Bessel function I0I_0 is obtained as a series, pseudocode following the source, how it is tested and where the limits are.

What this is about

Two of the six measures of the measurement chain – the spectral distance and the noise-to-mask ratio – do not compare signals sample by sample but frequency by frequency. For this the signal is split into short, overlapping frames (default 1024 samples, hop 512), each frame is weighted with a window and turned into a power spectrum with the FFT. The alignment additionally needs the cross-correlation of two whole signals, which is also computed via the FFT.

The FFT itself lives in framework-math (Fourier and cosine transform); since 27.09.2026 this module no longer has its own copy. Spektren is the thin layer on top: squared magnitudes and correlation. Fenster provides the weights, including the two windows a later MDCT in the encoder needs.

The power spectrum

For a real frame x0,…,xN−1x_0, \dots, x_{N-1} with NN a power of two the discrete Fourier transform is

Xk=∑n=0N−1xne−2πikn/N,k=0,…,N−1X_k = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n \, e^{-2\pi i k n / N}, \qquad k = 0, \dots, N-1

Because xx is real, XN−k=XkX_{N-k} = \overline{X_k}; the upper half mirrors the lower one. Spektren.leistungsspektrum therefore returns only the N/2+1N/2+1 values

Pk=|Xk|2=(Re⁡Xk)2+(Im⁡Xk)2,k=0,…,N/2P_k = |X_k|^2 = (\operatorname{Re} X_k)^2 + (\operatorname{Im} X_k)^2, \qquad k = 0, \dots, N/2

The value PkP_k belongs to the frequency fk=k⋅fs/Nf_k = k \cdot f_s / N. At fs=48f_s = 48 kHz and N=1024N = 1024 the lines are 46.875 Hz apart; P0P_0 is the DC component, PN/2P_{N/2} the Nyquist frequency 24 kHz.

Convention in the framework: the power spectrum is unscaled – divided neither by NN nor by N2N^2, and lines 1…N/2−11 \dots N/2-1 are not doubled. A sine of amplitude AA with an integer number of periods gives Pk=(AN/2)2P_k = (A N/2)^2 without a window. For the measures this does not matter: the spectral distance compares levels of two spectra treated the same way, and in the NMR error and threshold are on the same scale. Anyone who needs absolute levels has to normalise themselves.

Pseudocode – Spektren.leistungsspektrum(x)

n ← length(x)
check n is a power of two                       otherwise IllegalArgumentException
re ← copy of x;  im ← n zeros                      x stays unchanged
Fft.transformiere(re, im, forward)                 framework-math, no scaling
for k ← 0 to n/2:
    P[k] ← re[k]² + im[k]²
return P                                           length n/2 + 1

Source: envisionz-framework-audiometrik/src/envisionz/audiometrik/Spektren.java. The full complex FFT with zeros in the imaginary part is used, not RealFft.

Cross-correlation via the frequency domain

The cross-correlation of two sequences aa and bb measures how well aa, shifted by kk positions, matches bb:

rk=∑nan+kbnr_k = \sum_{n} a_{n+k} \, b_n

Computed directly, every shift costs a whole sum. Via the FFT it becomes a product: the cyclic correlation has the transform AkBkA_k \overline{B_k}. Hence

r=IDFT⁡(A⋅B),A=DFT⁡(a),B=DFT⁡(b)r = \mathrm{IDFT}\left(A \cdot \overline{B}\right), \qquad A = \mathrm{DFT}(a),\ B = \mathrm{DFT}(b)

The correlation is cyclic: shifts running past the end come back in at the start, the index kk stands for offset kk and at the same time for k−nk - n. So that the two directions do not overlap, the transform length nn must be at least the sum of the signal lengths; kreuzkorrelation pads the rest with zeros. How large nn is chosen is up to the caller – in the alignment it is 2(max⁡(La,Lb)+vmax⁡+1)2\,(\max(L_a, L_b) + v_{\max} + 1), rounded up to a power of two.

Pseudocode – Spektren.kreuzkorrelation(a, b, n)

check n is a power of two
aRe ← a, cut to n or padded with zeros;  aIm ← 0
bRe ← b, likewise;                        bIm ← 0
Fft.transformiere(aRe, aIm, forward)
Fft.transformiere(bRe, bIm, forward)
for i ← 0 to n−1:                                 A · conj(B)
    (aRe[i], aIm[i]) ← (aRe·bRe + aIm·bIm,  aIm·bRe − aRe·bIm)
Fft.transformiere(aRe, aIm, inverse)
Fft.skaliere(aRe, aIm)                                 division by n
return aRe                                            index 0 = offset 0

Source: envisionz-framework-audiometrik/src/envisionz/audiometrik/Spektren.java. The inverse transform in framework-math does not divide by nn; Fft.skaliere makes up for it. According to the class comment the results are bit-identical to the former own copy, which computed x/nx/n, because x/nx/n and x⋅(1/n)x \cdot (1/n) are the same for a power of two.

Why a window is needed

The DFT pretends that the frame repeats periodically. If an oscillation does not fit into the frame with an integer number of periods, a jump arises at the seam, and the energy of that one frequency spreads over the whole spectrum – spectral leakage. A window wnw_n weights the frame so that it goes smoothly towards zero at both ends; the jump disappears, and the energy stays near the true frequency. The price is a wider main lobe: two closely adjacent frequencies are harder to separate.

How strong the effect is shows a sine with 10.5 periods in a frame of 1024 values – exactly between two lines, the worst case. Levels relative to line k=10k = 10, computed with Spektren and Fenster:

Without a window there is still energy of the order of −30 dB ten lines away from the tone; with a codec error of −40 dB the leakage would be louder than the error one wants to measure. That is why both spectral measures use a Hann window.

The four windows

Hann and Hamming

Both are a raised cosine over exactly one period:

wiHann=0.5−0.5cos⁡2πin,wiHamming=0.54−0.46cos⁡2πin,i=0,…,n−1w^{\text{Hann}}_i = 0.5 - 0.5 \cos\frac{2\pi i}{n}, \qquad w^{\text{Hamming}}_i = 0.54 - 0.46 \cos\frac{2\pi i}{n}, \qquad i = 0, \dots, n-1

Hann goes to zero at the edges; its side lobes fall off quickly (see the table above). Hamming leaves a pedestal of 0.08 and in return lowers the first side lobe further, but then falls off more slowly. The sum of both over one period is known in closed form: the cosines cancel, leaving n/2n/2 for Hann and 0.54n0.54\,n for Hamming – this is the gain with which a DC component passes through the window.

Convention in the framework: Hann and Hamming are built in the periodic form – division by nn, not by n−1n-1. The window is then mirror-symmetric about i=n/2i = n/2 (wi=wn−iw_i = w_{n-i}), not about the centre of the array, and w0w_0 has no partner. This form is the right one for spectral analysis with overlap: Hann windows with hop n/2n/2 add up seamlessly to a constant. The symmetric form with n−1n-1 is meant for filter design and is not offered here.

Sine window and the Princen-Bradley condition

wisine=sin⁡(πn(i+12))w^{\text{sine}}_i = \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\left(i + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\right)

The sine window is intended for the MDCT used by a later lossy encoder. There two windows are applied – one before the transform, one after the inverse transform – and consecutive frames overlap by half. For the overlap-add to reconstruct the signal without error, the following must hold:

wi2+wi+n/22=1,i=0,…,n/2−1w_i^2 + w_{i+n/2}^2 = 1, \qquad i = 0, \dots, n/2 - 1

This is the Princen-Bradley condition. For the sine window it follows from sin2⁡+cos2⁡=1\sin^2 + \cos^2 = 1, because wi+n/2=sin⁡(πn(i+12)+π2)=cos⁡(πn(i+12))w_{i+n/2} = \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{n}(i+\frac12) + \frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n}(i+\frac12)\right). The half-sample offset is required: without it w0=0w_0 = 0, and the condition would be violated at i=0i = 0. So the window starts at sin⁡π2n\sin\frac{\pi}{2n}.

Kaiser-Bessel-derived (KBD)

The sine window satisfies the condition but has no knob for the trade-off between main-lobe width and side-lobe attenuation. The KBD window has one: the shape parameter α\alpha. It is built in three steps.

First a Kaiser window over h+1h+1 points, h=n/2h = n/2:

vj=I0(πα1−(2jh−1)2),j=0,…,hv_j = I_0\left(\pi \alpha \sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{2j}{h} - 1\right)^2}\right), \qquad j = 0, \dots, h

Second its running sum, normalised to the total sum; third its square root as the first half, mirrored as the second:

wi=wn−1−i=∑j=0ivj∑j=0hvj,i=0,…,h−1w_i = w_{n-1-i} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{i} v_j}{\sum_{j=0}^{h} v_j}}, \qquad i = 0, \dots, h-1

Why this satisfies the Princen-Bradley condition: the Kaiser window is symmetric, vj=vh−jv_j = v_{h-j}. For ii in the first half, wi+hw_{i+h} belongs to the mirrored second half, wi+h=wh−1−iw_{i+h} = w_{h-1-i}, and its square is the partial sum up to h−1−ih-1-i, which by symmetry equals the sum from i+1i+1 to hh. Both squares together give the total sum divided by itself – that is 1.

A large α\alpha makes the Kaiser window narrow, its running sum rises steeply in the middle; the KBD window becomes flatter inside and steeper at the edges. Fensterprobe checks exactly this direction: kbd(64, 8) has smaller values at index 4 than kbd(64, 1). The source names 4 and 6 as the values usual in AAC.

Pseudocode – Fenster.kbd(n, alpha)

check n even                                   otherwise IllegalArgumentException
h ← n/2;  sum ← 0
for j ← 0 to h:                                  h+1 points
    x ← 2j/h − 1
    v[j] ← besselI0(π · alpha · √max(0, 1 − x²))
    sum ← sum + v[j]
run ← 0
for i ← 0 to h−1:
    run ← run + v[i]
    w[i] ← √(run / sum);  w[n−1−i] ← w[i]       mirrored
return w

Source: envisionz-framework-audiometrik/src/envisionz/audiometrik/Fenster.java. The max⁡(0,⋅)\max(0, \cdot) catches rounding errors that could push 1−x21 - x^2 slightly below zero at the ends.

The Bessel function I0I_0

The modified Bessel function of the first kind of order zero has the everywhere convergent series

I0(x)=∑k=0∞((x/2)kk!)2I_0(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \left(\frac{(x/2)^k}{k!}\right)^2

Consecutive terms differ by the factor (x/2k)2\left(\frac{x/2}{k}\right)^2. So each term is not computed afresh but the previous one is updated – no factorials, no powers, no overflow for the arguments occurring here. All terms are positive; there is no cancellation.

Pseudocode – Fenster.besselI0(x)

sum ← 1;  term ← 1;  half ← x/2
for k ← 1 to 59:
    term ← term · (half/k)²
    sum ← sum + term
    if term < sum · 10⁻¹⁶: stop
return sum

Source: envisionz-framework-audiometrik/src/envisionz/audiometrik/Fenster.java.

Convention in the framework: the series stops as soon as a term falls below 10−1610^{-16} times the sum so far – below the resolution of a double – but after 59 terms at the latest. With α=4\alpha = 4 the largest argument is 4π≈12.64\pi \approx 12.6 with I0(4π)≈32,608I_0(4\pi) \approx 32{,}608; the terms peak around k≈x/2≈6k \approx x/2 \approx 6 and are negligible long before the 59th term.

Applying a window

Fenster.anwenden(quelle, versatz, fenster) takes as many values starting at versatz as the window is long and multiplies them by the weights: zi=xv+iwiz_i = x_{v+i}\, w_i. An index before the start or beyond the end of the source counts as 0. A frame may therefore extend beyond the edge – a signal ends, and continued with its last value it would be a tone that was never there. The source stays unchanged; the result is a new copy.

How it is tested

Fftprobe (5 checks) tests the spectra against cases known in closed form:

  • A sine with 8 periods and amplitude 0.5 in 256 values gives the power (0.5⋅256/2)2=4096(0.5 \cdot 256/2)^2 = 4096 at line 8, relatively exact to 10−910^{-9}; all lines more than one position away lie below 10−1810^{-18} times that.
  • Forward and inverse transform with Fft.skaliere give the input back to 10−1210^{-12}.
  • At 64 points the FFT agrees with the directly computed DFT to 10−910^{-9}.
  • The autocorrelation of a noise signal of length 512 at n=2048n = 2048 has its maximum at index 0.

Fensterprobe tests windows and Bessel function (and the CSV report) with 217 individual checks:

  • I0I_0 against published table values (Abramowitz/Stegun, table 9.8) at 0, 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10, with bounds from 10−1510^{-15} to 10−710^{-7}; in addition evenness I0(−x)=I0(x)I_0(-x) = I_0(x) and strict growth on [0,12][0, 12].
  • Hann and Hamming for nn = 2, 4, 8, 16, 64, 1024 against their formula (10−1210^{-12}), start value 0 and 0.08 respectively, for Hann the symmetry wi=wn−iw_i = w_{n-i}, sum n/2n/2 and 0.54n0.54\,n respectively, range 0…1.
  • Sine window for nn = 2 to 256: formula, start value sin⁡π2n\sin\frac{\pi}{2n}, full mirror symmetry, Princen-Bradley to 10−1210^{-12}.
  • KBD for nn = 4 to 256 and α\alpha = 2, 4, 6: symmetry, range, rise up to the middle, Princen-Bradley to 10−910^{-9}; odd length is rejected; larger α\alpha gives smaller values at the edge; α=0\alpha = 0 gives a valid window.
  • anwenden: excerpts at the start, with offset, beyond the end, before the start and completely outside; source unchanged.

Limits

  • Power spectrum and cross-correlation require a power of two as length; everything else is rejected with an exception. The frame length of the measurement chain must therefore be a power of two.
  • The power spectrum is unscaled; absolute levels in dBFS can only be obtained from it with an own normalisation (window gain, factor 2 for the inner lines).
  • The cross-correlation is cyclic. If nn is chosen too small, positive and negative shifts overlap; the choice of nn lies with the caller.
  • Hann and Hamming exist only in the periodic form. Sine and KBD can be used as analysis windows, but in the module they are intended for the MDCT of an encoder; the measures use Hann exclusively.
  • besselI0 is meant for the arguments of window construction. Because the terms only peak around k≈x/2k \approx x/2, 59 terms are not enough for large arguments: against the series summed to the end, the result deviates by 6⋅10−126 \cdot 10^{-12} at x=60x = 60, by 2⋅10−52 \cdot 10^{-5} at x=80x = 80 and by 2.8 % at x=100x = 100 (recomputed with the module). In the KBD window x≤παx \le \pi\alpha; up to α≈19\alpha \approx 19 this is irrelevant.
