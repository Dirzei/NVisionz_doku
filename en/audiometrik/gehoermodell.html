Bark scale and masking threshold – background

The computation behind Barkskala: critical bands of hearing, the conversion from hertz to Bark, the assignment of FFT lines to bands, the spreading function after Schroeder and the masking threshold formed from it. With pseudocode following the source, a worked example, the probe and an open list of what this model cannot do.

What this is about

A lossy audio encoder saves bits by allowing errors – but only where they cannot be heard. Hearing helps here: a loud tone makes quieter components in its frequency neighbourhood inaudible. It is said to mask them. The limit below which an additional noise next to a given signal is no longer perceived is called the masking threshold.

For the measurement chain this means: a signal-to-noise ratio of 30 dB can sound good or bad depending on where in the spectrum the noise lies. The noise-to-mask ratio (NMR) therefore relates the error to an estimated masking threshold. Barkskala provides this threshold – deliberately in a simple model that is suitable for comparing two methods, not as a statement about audibility.

Critical bands and the Bark scale

The inner ear does not split sound into arbitrarily fine frequencies but acts like a series of overlapping band-pass filters. The width of such a band is called a critical band. Below about 500 Hz it is roughly a constant 100 Hz wide, above that it grows roughly in proportion to frequency. Zwicker divided the hearing range into 24 adjoining critical bands; counting them gives the Bark scale: a frequency axis on which every critical band has the same width, namely 1 Bark.

Barkskala uses the classical upper edges of these 24 bands in hertz:

The formula is strictly increasing and bounded above by 26.81−0.53=26.2826.81 - 0.53 = 26.28 Bark. At 1 kHz it gives 8.53 Bark; critical band 9 of the table extends from 920 to 1080 Hz – the two descriptions fit together.

From FFT lines to bands

The power spectrum of a frame of length NN has N/2+1N/2+1 lines spaced Δf=fNy/(N/2)\Delta f = f_{\text{Ny}} / (N/2) apart, with fNy=fs/2f_{\text{Ny}} = f_s/2. The constructor Barkskala(binzahl, abtastrate) assigns these lines to the critical bands: all band edges below Nyquist are taken over, plus a last band up to Nyquist. The upper edge of a band is rounded to the nearest line; the next band starts one line further on.

The band energy is the sum of the lines of a band:

Eb=∑k=abebPkE_b = \sum_{k = a_b}^{e_b} P_k

with aba_b and ebe_b as first and last line (both inclusive). Each band also gets a centre frequency, computed from the lines, not from the table edges: fb=ab+eb2Δff_b = \frac{a_b + e_b}{2}\,\Delta f.

Pseudocode – constructor Barkskala(binzahl, abtastrate)

ny ← sampleRate / 2;  df ← ny / (binCount − 1)
count ← number of table edges < ny, plus 1            last band up to Nyquist
bin ← 0
for b ← 0 to count−1:
    upper ← edge[b], if b < 24 and edge[b] < ny, else ny
    upperBin ← min(binCount−1, round(upper / df))
    if upperBin < bin: upperBin ← bin               at least one line per band
    start[b] ← bin;  end[b] ← upperBin
    centreHz[b] ← (bin + upperBin)/2 · df
    bin ← min(upperBin + 1, binCount − 1)

The lowest bands are resolved very coarsely with two or three lines: the critical band there is 100 Hz wide, the lines are 47 Hz apart. Towards the top a band comprises dozens of lines.

The spreading function

A masker acts not only in its own critical band but also in neighbouring ones, weakly downwards and more strongly upwards. This spread is described by a spreading function S(Δz)S(\Delta z): it states in dB how strongly the energy of a masker at zqz_q still acts at zb=zq+Δzz_b = z_q + \Delta z. Barkskala uses the form of Schroeder, Atal and Hall:

S(Δz)=15.81+7.5(Δz+0.474)−17.51+(Δz+0.474)2dBS(\Delta z) = 15.81 + 7.5\,(\Delta z + 0.474) - 17.5\sqrt{1 + (\Delta z + 0.474)^2} \quad \text{dB}

with Δz=z(band)−z(masker)\Delta z = z(\text{band}) - z(\text{masker}) in Bark. For large |Δz||\Delta z| the square root becomes |Δz+0.474||\Delta z + 0.474|, so the function becomes two straight lines: upwards (Δz>0\Delta z > 0) with 7.5−17.5=−107.5 - 17.5 = -10 dB per Bark, downwards with 7.5+17.5=257.5 + 17.5 = 25 dB per Bark. Low frequencies therefore mask high ones far more strongly than the other way round.

The constants 15.81 and 0.474 are chosen so that the maximum at Δz=0\Delta z = 0 is practically exactly 0 dB: a band acts on itself with its full energy.

The masking threshold

The spread energy of a band is the sum of the contributions of all bands, added linearly in power:

Gb=∑q=0B−1Eq⋅10S(zb−zq)/10G_b = \sum_{q=0}^{B-1} E_q \cdot 10^{S(z_b - z_q)/10}

with zb=z(fb)z_b = z(f_b) after Traunmüller from the centre frequency of the band. The masking threshold lies a fixed offset ΔdB\Delta_{\text{dB}} below it:

Tb=Gb⋅10−ΔdB/10T_b = G_b \cdot 10^{-\Delta_{\text{dB}}/10}

TbT_b has the same unit as the band energies – unscaled squared FFT magnitudes – and can therefore be compared directly with the band energy of an error signal that has been windowed and transformed in the same way.

Pseudocode – Barkskala.verdeckungsschwelle(energie, abzugDb)

B ← bandCount
for b ← 0 to B−1:  z[b] ← barkAusHz(centreHz[b])
for b ← 0 to B−1:                         target band
    sum ← 0
    for q ← 0 to B−1:                     masking band
        d ← z[b] − z[q] + 0.474
        s ← 15.81 + 7.5·d − 17.5·√(1 + d²)       dB
        sum ← sum + energy[q] · 10^(s/10)
    G[b] ← sum
factor ← 10^(−offsetDb/10)
for b ← 0 to B−1:  T[b] ← G[b] · factor
return T

Source: envisionz-framework-audiometrik/src/envisionz/audiometrik/Barkskala.java. The effort is B2B^2 evaluations per frame, 625 for 25 bands; the Bark values of the band centres are recomputed on every call.

Convention in the framework: the model is deliberately coarse. There is a single, fixed offset for all bands and all signals – 10 dB in the measurement chain, adjustable via nmrAbzugDb –, no distinction between tonal and noise-like maskers and no threshold in quiet. The band edges come from the critical-band table, the Bark values for the spreading from the Traunmüller formula at the band centres. According to the module documentation this is the basic model of the first stage; a refined psychoacoustic model is to replace it in stage 5.

Worked example

A single masker: band energy 1 (i.e. 0 dB) in band 8 (lines 21–23, centre 1031 Hz, 8.71 Bark), all other bands empty; 1024 values at 48 kHz, offset 10 dB. The threshold per band, computed with verdeckungsschwelle:

In the masker's own band the threshold sits exactly at the offset, −10 dB. One band lower (1.02 Bark apart) it lies at −18.2 dB, one band higher (0.91 Bark apart) at −13.7 dB. Four bands lower the masking has fallen to −81 dB, four bands higher only to −40 dB. According to this model an encoder could hide much more noise above a loud tone than below it.

If, in contrast, equal energies are placed in all bands (flat spectrum), the spread energy lies between 1.03 and 1.94 times the band's own energy depending on the band – at most just under 3 dB above it. For broadband signals the offset alone almost entirely determines where the threshold lies.

How it is tested

Barkprobe checks band partition and spreading for consistency – 7 checks at 1024 values and 48 kHz:

  • At least 20 bands.
  • The bands adjoin without gaps (the first starts at line 0, each following one directly after the previous one or – with collapsed bands – on the same line), and the last one ends at Nyquist.
  • The sum of the band energies of a noise frame equals the sum of the spectrum, relatively to 10−910^{-9}.
  • The Bark conversion is strictly increasing (100 Hz to 20 kHz in 100 Hz steps) and gives a value near 8.5 at 1 kHz (bound 0.6; measured 8.527).
  • The masking threshold is positive everywhere and nowhere NaN.

There is no anchor for the value of the threshold: the probe checks that the model is internally consistent, not that it agrees with listening tests – nor does it claim to.

Limits

  • No threshold in quiet. Without it, the threshold in a band that is almost empty in the reference can fall arbitrarily low – in the example above to about −151 dB in band 24. Every error signal counts as far above the threshold there. How strongly this shapes the NMR is shown on the NMR page.
  • No tonality estimate. A pure tone masks less than an equally loud noise band; real models therefore apply different offsets depending on tonality. Here one offset applies to everything.
  • Small frames. If the frame length becomes small, several critical bands fall onto the same line. At 256 values and 48 kHz (line spacing 187.5 Hz) each of bands 1 to 14 contains exactly one line, and the lines slide to higher frequencies: band 1 (critical band 100–200 Hz) lies at 375 Hz. At 32 values there are not enough lines, bands 16 to 24 all lie on the Nyquist line 16, and bandenergien counts this line nine times. With the default of 1024 values neither occurs.
  • Low sample rates. At 16 kHz 22 bands result (21 edges below 8 kHz plus one up to Nyquist); the assignment remains correct, the model just has fewer bands at the top.
  • The threshold is formed per frame from that frame alone; temporal masking (pre- and post-masking) is missing.
