The measures – bit-exactness, deviation, SNR, segmental SNR and spectral distance

Five of the six error measures in Masse: definition, what each measure sees and what it misses, pseudocode following the source, worked examples on generated signals, the probe and the limits. The sixth measure, the noise-to-mask ratio, has its own page (NMR).

What this is about

An encoder delivers a version pp of the original bb. How good is it? A single number is not enough: a lossless encoder must hit every value, a lossy one may make errors but should keep them small, spread them evenly and put them where they disturb little. The measures in Masse answer these questions one by one:

MeasureQuestionDirection
Bit-exactIs every value hit within a tolerance?yes is better
Largest deviationHow large is the worst single error?small is better
SNRHow large is the error energy against the signal energy, overall?large is better
Segmental SNRThe same per segment, each segment weighted equallylarge is better
Spectral distanceHow far apart are the spectra in level?small is better
NMRDoes the error stay below the masking threshold?small is better

All of them assume that bb and pp are already aligned and of equal length. The measurement chain takes care of this; whoever calls the measures directly has to do it themselves. Without alignment they produce nonsense.

Notation: bc,ib_{c,i} and pc,ip_{c,i} are the values of reference and test signal in channel cc at position ii, ec,i=bc,i−pc,ie_{c,i} = b_{c,i} - p_{c,i} is the error. All values lie in the range −1…+1 (full scale = 1).

Bit-exact and largest deviation

Bit-exact is true if both signals have the same channel count and length and every error is at most a tolerance ss:

bit-exact⟺maxc,i⁡|ec,i|≤s\text{bit-exact} \iff \max_{c,i} |e_{c,i}| \le s

The largest deviation is exactly this maximum, the maximum norm of the error:

emax⁡=maxc,i⁡|bc,i−pc,i|e_{\max} = \max_{c,i} |b_{c,i} - p_{c,i}|

Multiplied by 2w−12^{w-1} (ww = word length) it tells by how many quantisation steps the worst value is off. At 16 bit one step is 1/32768≈3.05⋅10−51/32768 \approx 3.05 \cdot 10^{-5}.

Convention in the framework: the measurement chain checks bit-exactness with the tolerance s=10−9s = 10^{-9}. This is far below one quantisation step at 8, 16 and 24 bit (24 bit: 1.19⋅10−71.19 \cdot 10^{-7}), but above one step at 32 bit (2−31≈4.66⋅10−102^{-31} \approx 4.66 \cdot 10^{-10}). With 32-bit PCM, deviations of one or two steps therefore still count as “bit-exact” – recomputed: two steps at one position pass, three do not. For a strict proof at 32 bit, Ganzsignal.gleicht is the right way, comparing without a tolerance (see Signals and WAV).

The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)

The signal-to-noise ratio relates the energy of the reference to the energy of the error, summed over all channels and all values:

SNR⁡=10log10⁡∑c,ibc,i2∑c,iec,i2dB\mathrm{SNR} = 10 \log_{10} \frac{\sum_{c,i} b_{c,i}^2}{\sum_{c,i} e_{c,i}^2} \quad \text{dB}

Every halving of the error amplitude gains 6.02 dB. For a sine of amplitude AA and an uncorrelated error of amplitude BB (integer numbers of periods, so that the means of sin2⁡\sin^2 are exactly 1/21/2), SNR⁡=20log10⁡(A/B)\mathrm{SNR} = 20\log_{10}(A/B). For uniformly distributed noise of amplitude aa the noise power is a2/3a^2/3.

Pseudocode – Masse.snrDb(bezug, pruef)

signal ← 0;  error ← 0
for each channel c:
    for i ← 0 to min(length b, length p) − 1:
        signal ← signal + b[c][i]²
        error ← error + (b[c][i] − p[c][i])²
if error ≤ 10⁻²⁰: return +∞
return 10 · log10(signal / error)

Source: envisionz-framework-audiometrik/src/envisionz/audiometrik/Masse.java.

The SNR is simple and unambiguous, but it is dominated by the loud passages. A piece with a loud part and a quiet part: an encoder that completely destroys the quiet part loses hardly anything in the SNR, because its energy is lost in the overall denominator.

The segmental SNR

The segmental SNR splits the signal into segments of length RR (without overlap), forms the SNR per segment and averages the decibel values:

SNRseg⁡=1|M|∑m∈Mclip[−10,35]⁡(10log10⁡∑i∈mbi2+ε∑i∈mei2+ε)\mathrm{SNR}_{\text{seg}} = \frac{1}{|M|} \sum_{m \in M} \operatorname{clip}_{[-10,\ 35]}\left(10\log_{10}\frac{\sum_{i \in m} b_i^2 + \varepsilon}{\sum_{i \in m} e_i^2 + \varepsilon}\right)

By averaging in dB every segment counts equally, however loud it is – a destroyed quiet part now noticeably pulls the mean down. Two precautions prevent individual segments from dominating the mean:

  • Silence is skipped. A segment whose mean power 1R∑bi2\frac{1}{R}\sum b_i^2 lies more than 40 dB below the square of the peak value of the whole reference does not count (the set MM contains only the others). In silence even the tiniest error would be a very poor segment SNR.
  • Clamping to −10…+35 dB. An error-free segment would have an infinite value, a completely wrong one a very negative value. Both are clamped so that neither dominates the mean.

Pseudocode – Masse.segmentSnrDb(bezug, pruef, rahmen)

peak ← ref.spitzenwert()
silence ← peak² · 10^(−40/10)
sum ← 0;  counted ← 0
for each channel c:
    n ← min(length b, length p)
    for start ← 0, frame, 2·frame, … while start + frame ≤ n:
        s ← Σ b[c][i]²;  f ← Σ (b[c][i] − p[c][i])²     i in the segment
        if s / frame < silence: continue           silent segment
        w ← 10 · log10((s + 10⁻²⁰) / (f + 10⁻²⁰))
        w ← min(35, max(−10, w))
        sum ← sum + w;  counted ← counted + 1
if counted = 0: return NaN
return sum / counted

Source: envisionz-framework-audiometrik/src/envisionz/audiometrik/Masse.java. The measurement chain passes its frame length (default 1024) as the segment length; the hop plays no role here.

The spectral distance

The time-domain measures also punish errors that cannot be heard – a phase rotation, for instance, that leaves the spectrum unchanged. The log-spectral distance compares the levels of the spectra instead. For each frame mm, reference and test signal are weighted with a Hann window, turned into power spectra PkbP^b_k and PkpP^p_k, and the level differences are averaged in the root-mean-square sense:

dm=1K∑k=0K−1(10log10⁡(Pkb+ε)−10log10⁡(Pkp+ε))2,K=R2+1d_m = \sqrt{\frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \left(10\log_{10}(P^b_k + \varepsilon) - 10\log_{10}(P^p_k + \varepsilon)\right)^2}, \qquad K = \frac{R}{2} + 1

The result is the mean of all dmd_m over frames and channels, in dB. Zero means the same power spectrum. The frames overlap (default: 1024 values, hop 512).

Pseudocode – Masse.spektralabstandDb(bezug, pruef, rahmen, vorschub)

w ← Fenster.hann(frame)
sum ← 0;  counted ← 0
for each channel c:
    n ← min(length b, length p)
    for start ← 0, hop, 2·hop, … while start + frame ≤ n:
        Pb ← leistungsspektrum(anwenden(b[c], start, w))
        Pp ← leistungsspektrum(anwenden(p[c], start, w))
        q ← Σ_k (10·log10(Pb[k] + 10⁻²⁰) − 10·log10(Pp[k] + 10⁻²⁰))²
        sum ← sum + √(q / length(Pb));  counted ← counted + 1
if counted = 0: return NaN
return sum / counted

Source: envisionz-framework-audiometrik/src/envisionz/audiometrik/Masse.java. All R/2+1R/2+1 lines are counted, including DC and Nyquist; silent frames are not skipped here.

Convention in the framework: the floor ε=10−20\varepsilon = 10^{-20} in all logarithms prevents log⁡0\log 0. It is absolute, relative to unscaled squared FFT magnitudes, and therefore very small: a line that is empty in the reference ends up at −200 dB. For the segmental SNR (and the NMR) silence is relative to the peak value of the whole reference: mean segment power more than 40 dB below peak². The spectral distance has no silence rule. All three measures evaluate only complete frames; a remainder at the end shorter than one frame is dropped.

Worked examples

The probe case: exactly 40 dB

8192 values, reference bi=0.5sin⁡(2π⋅40i/8192)b_i = 0.5 \sin(2\pi \cdot 40\, i/8192), error ei=0.005cos⁡(2π⋅97i/8192)e_i = 0.005 \cos(2\pi \cdot 97\, i/8192), test signal p=b+ep = b + e. Both have integer numbers of periods, so the energies are exactly 8192⋅0.52/28192 \cdot 0.5^2/2 and 8192⋅0.0052/28192 \cdot 0.005^2/2, and

SNR⁡=10log10⁡0.520.0052=20log10⁡100=40 dB\mathrm{SNR} = 10\log_{10}\frac{0.5^2}{0.005^2} = 20\log_{10} 100 = 40 \text{ dB}

Masseprobe measures 40.000000 dB (bound 0.01 dB).

Tonal and noise-like material

SNR and segmental SNR behave the same for both references: 20 dB per tenfold reduction of the noise amplitude, until the segmental SNR stops at 35 dB. The SNR of the chord with noise amplitude 0.01 hits the closed-form value 33.80 dB.

The spectral distance, in contrast, separates the two references by almost two orders of magnitude. With the noise-like reference every line is well occupied, and an error 40 dB below changes the level per line by fractions of a dB. With the chord almost all lines are empty – after the Hann window only leakage of −100-100 dB and less remains there – and the noise fills them. The level difference in these lines is huge and determines the measure. The spectral distance then mainly measures how empty the spectrum of the reference was.

How it is tested

Masseprobe contains anchors for these measures whose expectation is known in closed form:

  • SNR at a known error level: 40 dB, bound 0.01 dB (case above).
  • A signal against itself is bit-exact (tolerance 10−1210^{-12}), has an infinite SNR and a spectral distance of 0 (bound 10−910^{-9}).
  • After aligning a delayed signal, the SNR in the valid range is infinite (see Alignment).
  • The measurement chain delivers at least seven named values.

Without an anchor of their own are the segmental SNR (neither the clamp nor the silence rule is checked), the largest deviation and the spectral distance for unequal signals; the “not bit-exact” case is not checked separately either.

Limits

  • Spectral distance without a floor. Because ε\varepsilon is absolute and tiny, the measure is dominated by empty lines for references that have them (tones, band-limited material, silence); see the example with 94 dB at 33.8 dB SNR. Silent frames are not skipped: silence against residual noise of 10−610^{-6} gives 18.5 dB (recomputed). For comparing two encoders on the same material the direction remains usable; as an absolute number the value is of little use.
  • Bit-exact at 32 bit. The tolerance 10−910^{-9} of the measurement chain lets deviations of up to two quantisation steps pass at 32-bit PCM (see above).
  • Segmental SNR is capped. Beyond 35 dB per segment it no longer distinguishes – two encoders with 40 and 70 dB SNR both get 35 dB. The silence threshold refers to the peak value of the whole reference; in a piece with a very large dynamic range, quiet but audible passages fall below the threshold and do not count.
  • Channels. All measures loop over the channel count of the reference; a test signal with fewer channels leads to an index-out-of-bounds error. The alignment rejects different channel counts beforehand; with a direct call this check is missing. SNR and largest deviation compare each channel up to the shorter length, bit-exact requires equal length.
  • No auditory weighting. Apart from the NMR none of these measures is perceptually weighted. They say how large the error is, not how it sounds.
