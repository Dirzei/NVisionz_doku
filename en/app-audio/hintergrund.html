Edit, gain and output – background

How the editor cuts without touching the file: the edit list of segments, gains and fades as factors, click protection at the seams, normalising from the peak values, and the four output paths, among them the lossless MP3 cut.

What it is about

The editor never changes the loaded file. It keeps a list of which pieces of the source are to sound, in which order and at which gain. Listening, the waveform and saving all read the same chain built from this list. A new file is created only when saving; the source itself is never overwritten.

The edit list

The timeline is a sequence of segments. Each one refers to a range of the source (start, length in frames) and carries a start and an end gain; in between the gain runs linearly. Cut, copy and paste split segments and rearrange them without reading a single sample. Every change keeps the previous state, hence undo and redo to any depth.

Gain and fades

Volume, normalising and fades act as factors on the gains of the segments concerned. A volume change of dd dB multiplies both gains by 10d/2010^{d/20}. A fade over [a,b)[a, b) multiplies by a ramp r(t)r(t) from 0 to 1 (fade in) or from 1 to 0 (fade out).

If a segment already carries a varying gain g(t)g(t), the product g(t)r(t)g(t)\,r(t) is no longer linear but quadratic. Multiplying only the end points would turn a fade in followed by a fade out over the same range into (0⋅1,1⋅0)=(0,0)(0\cdot 1,\ 1 \cdot 0) = (0, 0): the range would be silent instead of t(1−t)t(1-t). Such a segment is therefore first split into up to 32 pieces of equal length, and only these are multiplied. The error of the piecewise linear approximation stays below

|gr−approximation|≤|Δg||Δr|4⋅322≤14096≈−72dB.\big|\,g\,r - \text{approximation}\,\big| \;\le\; \frac{|\Delta g|\,|\Delta r|}{4\cdot 32^2} \;\le\; \frac{1}{4096} \;\approx\; -72\ \text{dB}.

The bound follows this way: the product of two linear courses has the second derivative 2ΔgΔr2\,\Delta g\,\Delta r, and the chord over a piece of length 1/321/32 deviates from it by at most one eighth of that times (1/32)2(1/32)^2. A segment with constant gain is not split.

Click protection at the seams

Where two segments meet that do not follow each other in the source, the signal jumps; a jump clicks. Click protection fades out 128 frames before every such seam and back in afterwards, with a cosine ramp. At 44.1 kHz that is twice just under three milliseconds, which cannot be heard. Where the sound continues seamlessly across the seam, it is left untouched.

Normalising

Normalising brings the largest peak of the selection (without a selection: of the whole) to a target in dBFS. The peak comes from the waveform overview, i.e. from the peak values per block that are already computed for the display. The file is not read again for this. The factor is

f=10Ltarget/20smax,change=20log10⁡fdB.f = \frac{10^{L_\text{target}/20}}{s_\text{max}}, \qquad \text{change} = 20 \log_{10} f\ \text{dB}.

For silence (\(s_\text{max} < 10^{-6}\)) there is no meaningful factor; the editor then changes nothing and reports NaN.

Output

All four formats read the same chain: the edit list with gains and click protection, optionally followed by the vocal filter and the equaliser with the settings of the app.

  • WAV, 16 bit, and EVAS, our own lossless coder: every 16-bit value comes back unchanged.
  • MP3, re-encoded with our own encoder, at a fixed bit rate or VBR. MPEG-1 Layer III only knows 32, 44.1 and 48 kHz; other sample rates are rejected.
  • MP3, cut losslessly: where an output frame yields exactly one original frame (entirely within one segment, gain 1, on the grid of 1152 values), it is copied byte for byte. Only at seams, in fades and where the gain changed does our own encoder code a bridge. At the end of the bridge it keeps free the space the next copied frame expects in the bit reservoir and puts the original bytes there. The copied frames thus decode bit-identically.

If the source is not an MP3 or effects are to be included, the lossless cut is not possible. Then everything is re-encoded, and the report names the reason. If everything has been cut, nothing is written: a file without sound (for WAV only the header) would not be a result but a mistake.

Limits

  • Edits are saved only as a new file, not as a project; the edit list lives as long as the session.
  • The lossless cut needs MPEG-1 Layer III with the same sample rate and channel count as the timeline.
  • Fades are linear in gain, not in dB; a fade that sounds even over several seconds would be logarithmic.
