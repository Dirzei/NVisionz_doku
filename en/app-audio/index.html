envisionz-app-audio

Play, equalise and visualise audio

Overview of the app, as of 04.10.2026. A player for MP3, MP2 and WAV with a playlist, a ten-band equaliser, six switchable displays and two themes. Everything uses in-house decoders and filters, no third-party libraries. The editor for cutting follows.

What the app is for

The app plays music files and shows what is sounding: as a spectrum, as a needle meter, as a waveform, as a scrolling spectrogram or as a stereo image. It is also the showcase of framework-audio: decoders, filters and analysis come from there; the app only adds operation and display.

How to open files, set the equaliser and switch displays and themes is shown on the Operation page.

What it can do

Playback and playlist

Open several files, play them in order or by double-click.

  • Formats: MP3 (Layer III), MP2 (Layer II), WAV.
  • Title, artist, album, year and cover from ID3 tags (versions 1 and 2).
  • Timeline for seeking, volume, previous and next track. The list is kept across sessions.

Equaliser

Ten bands from 31 Hz to 16 kHz an octave apart, ±12 dB each, plus a preamp.

  • Presets (Linear, Rock, Pop, Classical, Speech, Loudness) and a warning when raised bands might clip.
  • A/B comparison with the unchanged signal; switching is smooth, without clicks.
  • The setting belongs to the app, not the file: it applies to every following track.

Remove or isolate vocals

Vocals sit exactly in the centre of almost every mix. A simple filter uses that.

  • Remove (karaoke): only the side S=(L−R)/2S = (L-R)/2 remains, plus bass and treble of the mid M=(L+R)/2M = (L+R)/2. The voice cancels completely in the side.
  • Isolate: only the mid within the voice band from 120 Hz to 8 kHz.
  • Rough: anything else in the centre (snare, solo instruments) goes along; an instrument panned hard left or right stays at −3 dB. Finer separation with per-frequency masks is planned.

Displays

What is shown is what is sounding now, not what is being read: the display follows the sound and does not run ahead by the device buffer.

  • Spectrum: 31 third-octave bands with peak hold. Meters: VU instruments (0 VU = −18 dBFS, 300 ms).
  • Oscilloscope: both channels, triggered on the rising zero crossing.
  • Waterfall: the spectrum in 160 slices from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, scrolling down for a good eight seconds.
  • Goniometer: mid vertical, side horizontal, with afterglow; below it the correlation from −1 (out of phase) to +1 (mono).
  • Cover: the picture large, the background from its average colour, a narrow spectrum below.

Themes and sliders

Two themes are built in: modern dark and a hardware look with cream scales.

  • A theme sets colours and font of all displays and of the custom-drawn sliders.
  • Further themes are a class plus one line in Java's services (ServiceLoader), also from another module.

Data and formats

  • Reading: .mp3, .mp2, .mpa, .wav.
  • Session: playlist, display, theme, equaliser and vocal filter.
  • Writing: not yet in the app. framework-audio already has the MP3 encoder for it (constant bit rate 32 to 320 kbit/s, psychoacoustic model, bit reservoir, short blocks); it comes into the app with the editor.

What it builds on

  • framework-audio: decoders, playback, equaliser, vocal filter, ID3, tap of what is sounding.
  • framework-swing: sidebar with cards, icons, background runner.
  • framework-api: registration as an app and session state.

Status and limits

Done: playback, list, equaliser, vocal filter, all six displays, both themes and the operation page. Testing is headless: analysis and displays with generated tones, the interface with a generated WAV file, the session state in a round trip.

Without a sound card a file stays loaded and measured; the status line says why nothing is heard.

Limits: the editor (waveform, cutting, output as MP3 or WAV) and a library on H2 are the next stages. Ogg Vorbis and AAC are not read.
