Time domain, unbalance and buckling – background

The computation behind Newmark, Unwucht and Knicken: time stepping after Newmark and Hilber-Hughes-Taylor, modal superposition and harmonic response, unbalance response of a rotor with gyroscopic effect and balancing with influence coefficients, linear buckling loads of plane frames with the geometric stiffness – with pseudocode, test cases and limits.

What it is about

The natural vibrations of a structure or rotor tell where resonances lie; they do not tell how large the response to a given load is. Three routes answer that, all built on the matrices of Rahmen and Rotor: integrate the equation of motion Mu¨+Cu˙+Ku=f(t)M\ddot u + C\dot u + K u = f(t) step by step, solve the steady-state response to a harmonic load directly, or – for the rotor – take the unbalance as a rotating load. Buckling is the static side of the same question: at which load does the stiffness lose its positive definiteness?

Newmark and HHT

Newmark (1959) writes displacement and velocity at the end of the step from a weighted acceleration:

un+1=un+hu˙n+h2[(12−β)an+βan+1],u˙n+1=u˙n+h[(1−γ)an+γan+1].u_{n+1} = u_n + h\,\dot u_n + h^2\big[(\tfrac12 - \beta)\,a_n + \beta\,a_{n+1}\big], \qquad \dot u_{n+1} = \dot u_n + h\big[(1 - \gamma)\,a_n + \gamma\,a_{n+1}\big].

With β=14\beta = \tfrac14, γ=12\gamma = \tfrac12 (average acceleration) the scheme is unconditionally stable, second order and conserves energy for C=0C = 0: the step is a rotation in the phase plane by 2arctan⁡(ωh/2)2\arctan(\omega h/2) instead of ωh\omega h – the period lengthens, the amplitude stays. β=16\beta = \tfrac16 (linear acceleration) is only conditionally stable.

Hilber, Hughes and Taylor (1977) satisfy equilibrium at tn+(1+α)ht_n + (1 + \alpha)h with −13≤α≤0-\tfrac13 \le \alpha \le 0, β=(1−α)2/4\beta = (1 - \alpha)^2/4, γ=12−α\gamma = \tfrac12 - \alpha:

Man+1+(1+α)(Cu˙n+1+Kun+1)−α(Cu˙n+Kun)=f(tn+(1+α)h).M a_{n+1} + (1 + \alpha)\big(C \dot u_{n+1} + K u_{n+1}\big) - \alpha\big(C\dot u_n + K u_n\big) = f\big(t_n + (1 + \alpha)h\big).

It stays second order and damps the high frequencies that the mesh distorts; the spectral radius tends to (1+α)/(1−α)(1 + \alpha)/(1 - \alpha) for ωh→∞\omega h \to \infty. With α=0\alpha = 0 it is the average acceleration, bit for bit.

E ← M + (1+α) γ h C + (1+α) β h² K;  E⁻¹ once
for n = 0, 1, …:
    ũ ← u + h v + h² (½ − β) a;   ṽ ← v + h (1 − γ) a          predictor

    a' ← E⁻¹ r;   u' ← ũ + β h² a';   v' ← ṽ + γ h a'

Modal superposition and harmonic response

With the mass-normalised mode shapes Φ\Phi from Kϕ=ω2MϕK\phi = \omega^2 M\phi the system decouples into single-degree oscillators q¨j+2ζjωjq˙j+ωj2qj=ϕjTf\ddot q_j + 2\zeta_j\omega_j\dot q_j + \omega_j^2 q_j = \phi_j^\mathsf{T} f. For proportional damping (C=aM+bKC = a M + b K) this is exact; the probe compares modal and direct computation to 2.6·10⁻¹⁵. For a harmonic load FeiΩtF e^{i\Omega t}

(K−Ω2M+iΩC)X=F\big(K - \Omega^2 M + i\,\Omega\,C\big)\,X = F

is solved in a real embedding (2n2n unknowns) or modally as ∑jϕjϕjTF/(ωj2−Ω2+2iζjωjΩ)\sum_j \phi_j \phi_j^\mathsf{T}F/(\omega_j^2 - \Omega^2 + 2i\zeta_j\omega_j\Omega). A tuned absorber at its natural frequency holds the main mass still (X1=0X_1 = 0, X2=−F/k2X_2 = -F/k_2).

Unbalance response and balancing

An unbalance U=meU = m e at node kk with angle φ\varphi produces a force UΩ2ei(Ωt+φ)U\Omega^2 e^{i(\Omega t + \varphi)} rotating with the speed Ω\Omega. In the rotor's complex coordinates r=v+iwr = v + i w (see rotor dynamics) the steady-state response is ReiΩtR e^{i\Omega t} with

(K−Ω2(M−G)+iΩC)R=UΩ2eiφek,\Big(K - \Omega^2\,(M - G) + i\,\Omega\,C\Big)\,R = U\,\Omega^2\,e^{i\varphi}\,e_k,

where GG is the gyroscopic matrix (polar moment of inertia of the discs): in forward whirl it stiffens the tilt. A moment unbalance acts on the tilt degree of freedom. For the Laval rotor R=UΩ2/(k−mΩ2+icΩ)R = U\Omega^2/(k - m\Omega^2 + i c\Omega), lagging by 90°90° at resonance.

Balancing with influence coefficients. The response is linear in the unbalance: with the initial run V0V_0 and, per plane jj, a run with trial weight TjT_j, αij=(Vij−V0,i)/Tj\alpha_{ij} = (V_{ij} - V_{0,i})/T_j (complex). The correction weights solve αW=−V0\alpha\,W = -V_0; in one plane W=−V0T/(V1−V0)W = -V_0 T/(V_1 - V_0). The probe imposes a known unbalance and recovers it with opposite sign to 1.6·10⁻¹⁴.

Linear buckling

An axial force NN in the beam changes its bending stiffness by the geometric stiffness (consistent with the cubic Hermite shapes)

KG=N30L(363L−363L3L4L2−3L−L2−36−3L36−3L3L−L2−3L4L2)K_G = \frac{N}{30L}\begin{pmatrix}36 & 3L & -36 & 3L\\ 3L & 4L^2 & -3L & -L^2\\ -36 & -3L & 36 & -3L\\ 3L & -L^2 & -3L & 4L^2\end{pmatrix}

in the transverse and rotational degrees of freedom. The axial forces come from a static computation with the reference load; the buckling load factors λ\lambda satisfy (K+λKG)ϕ=0(K + \lambda K_G)\phi = 0. The code solves −KGϕ=μKϕ-K_G\phi = \mu K\phi with μ=1/λ\mu = 1/\lambda, since KK is positive definite and KGK_G may be singular (members without axial force); the positive μ\mu give the buckling loads in ascending order. With 40 elements the four Euler cases Pk=π2EI/(βL)2P_k = \pi^2 EI/(\beta L)^2 are hit to 8·10⁻⁷, the second buckling load of the pinned column is 4PE4P_E, and the error falls with order 4.

How it is checked

  • Newmark: rotation by 2arctan⁡(ωh/2)2\arctan(\omega h/2) to 5·10⁻¹⁴, energy of the three-mass chain conserved (1.4·10⁻¹³), order 2.00 on the damped oscillator, HHT spectral radius at ωh=107\omega h = 10^7, modal equal to direct, harmonic against the closed form and the absorber.
  • Unbalance: Laval rotor at five speeds (1.9·10⁻¹⁴), overhung disc against the 2 × 2 system with (Id−Ip)Ω2(I_d - I_p)\Omega^2, balancing in one and two planes.
  • Buckling: four Euler cases, rotated column equal to horizontal, second buckling load and mode sin⁡(πx/L)\sin(\pi x/L), error cases (tension only).

Limits

Linear: no contact, no large deformations, no nonlinear post-buckling; buckling in the plane only, no lateral-torsional buckling. Newmark with a fixed step and dense matrices. Unbalance response with isotropic bearings; balancing needs as many measuring points as planes (no least-squares balancing).
