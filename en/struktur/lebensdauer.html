Fatigue and crack growth – background

The computation behind Rainflow, Ermuedung and Rissfortschritt: cycle counting after ASTM E1049, stress-life and strain-life curves, mean-stress effect, notch after Neuber, damage accumulation after Palmgren-Miner and crack growth after Paris – with pseudocode, worked examples and limits.

What it is about

A component under alternating load fails after a finite number of cycles, even if each single load lies far below the strength. Life computation decomposes a measured or computed load sequence into cycles, rates each cycle with an S-N curve (stress or strain against cycles) and sums the damage. If a crack already exists, fracture mechanics describes its growth per cycle up to the critical length.

Rainflow counting after ASTM E1049

The sequence is first reduced to its reversals (intermediate values and repetitions drop out). Then the three-point method (ASTM E1049-85, section 5.4.4) runs over a stack: XX is the range of the two most recent points, YY that of the two before. If X≥YX \ge Y, YY is a closed cycle. If YY contains the starting point, it counts as a half cycle and only the starting point is removed; otherwise as a full cycle, and both points of YY are removed. What remains at the end counts in half cycles.

u ← reversals(sequence);  stack ← []
for each point v in u:
    stack ← stack + [v]
    while |stack| ≥ 3:
        X ← |s[n] − s[n−1]|;  Y ← |s[n−1] − s[n−2]|
        if X < Y: break
        if |stack| = 3:  half cycle (Y, mean of s[1], s[2]);  remove s[1]   Y contains the start
        else:          full cycle (Y, mean of s[n−1], s[n−2]);  remove both
remainder: each pair of neighbouring points is a half cycle

Source: envisionz-framework-struktur/src/envisionz/struktur/lebensdauer/Rainflow.java. Incidentally, a comparison X<YX < Y against X≤YX \le Y counts the same cycles; a counter-probe that spoiled exactly this was equivalent and was replaced by one that checks the means.

Worked example (ASTM E1049, fig. 6). The sequence −2, 1, −3, 5, −1, 3, −4, 4, −2 gives: range 3 half, 4 one and a half, 6 half, 8 full, 9 half – with the means that the probe recomputes by hand.

Why rainflow. A closed cycle corresponds to a closed hysteresis loop in the stress-strain plane: with memory-type material behaviour (Masing) the material returns to the old curve after an intermediate cycle as if it had not occurred. Rainflow counts exactly these loops. The result therefore does not depend on where the sequence is split – only on the starting point (the residue at the end are half cycles).

Stress-life and strain-life curves

In the finite-life region the stress amplitude follows Basquin's line, the strain amplitude the sum of elastic and plastic parts after Coffin-Manson-Basquin:

σa=σf'(2N)b,εa=σf'E(2N)b+εf'(2N)c.\sigma_a = \sigma_f'\,(2N)^b, \qquad \varepsilon_a = \frac{\sigma_f'}{E}\,(2N)^b + \varepsilon_f'\,(2N)^c.

The strain equation is solved for x=ln⁡2Nx = \ln 2N with Newton's method. Both parts are equal at the transition life 2Nt=(εf'E/σf')1/(b−c)2N_t = (\varepsilon_f' E/\sigma_f')^{1/(b-c)} – the probe checks that.

Derivation. Basquin inverted: N=12(σa/σf')1/bN = \tfrac12(\sigma_a/\sigma_f')^{1/b}. The transition life follows from equality σf'E(2N)b=εf'(2N)c\frac{\sigma_f'}{E}(2N)^b = \varepsilon_f'(2N)^c, i.e. (2N)b−c=εf'E/σf'(2N)^{b-c} = \varepsilon_f' E/\sigma_f'. For the strain equation f(x)=σf'Eebx+εf'ecx−εaf(x) = \frac{\sigma_f'}{E}e^{bx} + \varepsilon_f' e^{cx} - \varepsilon_a with x=ln⁡2Nx = \ln 2N is strictly decreasing and convex (b,c<0b, c < 0); Newton's method from x=ln⁡2000x = \ln 2000 therefore converges monotonically after at most one overshoot. One cycle is two reversals, hence the factor 12\tfrac12 everywhere.

Mean stress

A tensile mean stress σm\sigma_m shortens the life. In the Haigh diagram the allowable amplitude is reduced from the fatigue strength σW\sigma_W:

Goodman: σW(1−σmRm),Gerber: σW(1−(σmRm)2),Soderberg: σW(1−σmRe).\text{Goodman: } \sigma_W\Big(1 - \frac{\sigma_m}{R_m}\Big), \qquad \text{Gerber: } \sigma_W\Big(1 - \Big(\frac{\sigma_m}{R_m}\Big)^2\Big), \qquad \text{Soderberg: } \sigma_W\Big(1 - \frac{\sigma_m}{R_e}\Big).

Gerber applies to tension only; for compressive mean stress σW\sigma_W remains. Above RmR_m the amplitude is zero. In the strain approach Morrow (σf'→σf'−σm\sigma_f' \to \sigma_f' - \sigma_m in the elastic part) and Smith-Watson-Topper shift the influence into the life equation:

σmaxεa=σf'2E(2N)2b+σf'εf'(2N)b+c.\sigma_{max}\,\varepsilon_a = \frac{\sigma_f'^2}{E}\,(2N)^{2b} + \sigma_f'\,\varepsilon_f'\,(2N)^{b+c}.

The SWT form arises when the Coffin-Manson equation is multiplied by the Basquin stress σmax=σf'(2N)b\sigma_{max} = \sigma_f'(2N)^b: σmaxεa=σf'(2N)b[σf'E(2N)b+εf'(2N)c]\sigma_{max}\varepsilon_a = \sigma_f'(2N)^b\,[\frac{\sigma_f'}{E}(2N)^b + \varepsilon_f'(2N)^c]. At σm=0\sigma_m = 0 it is therefore equivalent to Coffin-Manson; with mean stress the latter is contained in the measured σmax\sigma_{max}.

Notch after Neuber, damage sum after Miner

At a notch with stress concentration factor KtK_t the local stress becomes smaller than KtSK_t S under plasticity, the strain larger. Neuber requires that the product is preserved, and the cyclic stress-strain curve after Ramberg-Osgood holds:

σε=(KtS)2E,ε=σE+(σK')1/n';\sigma\,\varepsilon = \frac{(K_t S)^2}{E}, \qquad \varepsilon = \frac{\sigma}{E} + \Big(\frac{\sigma}{K'}\Big)^{1/n'};

solved with Newton in σ\sigma, starting at KtSK_t S. Linear damage accumulation after Palmgren-Miner sums over the rainflow spectrum D=∑ni/NiD = \sum n_i / N_i with NiN_i from Basquin for the amplitude (half the range); D=1D = 1 means computed failure.

Newton for Neuber. With f(σ)=σε(σ)−(KtS)2/Ef(\sigma) = \sigma\,\varepsilon(\sigma) - (K_t S)^2/E, f'(σ)=ε+σ(1E+(σ/K')1/n'n'σ)f'(\sigma) = \varepsilon + \sigma\big(\frac1E + \frac{(\sigma/K')^{1/n'}}{n'\sigma}\big) – as in the code, recomputed. ff is convex and increasing for σ>0\sigma > 0; from the start KtSK_t S (right of the solution, because plasticity lowers the stress) Newton approaches the solution monotonically from above.

Crack growth after Paris

A crack of length aa grows per cycle by

dadN=C(ΔK)m,ΔK=Y(a)Δσπa,\frac{da}{dN} = C\,(\Delta K)^m, \qquad \Delta K = Y(a)\,\Delta\sigma\,\sqrt{\pi a},

and not at all below the threshold ΔKth\Delta K_{th}. For fixed YY and m≠2m \ne 2 the number of cycles is closed-form

N=a01−m/2−a11−m/2C(YΔσπ)m(m/2−1),m=2:N=ln⁡(a1/a0)C(YΔσπ)2.N = \frac{a_0^{1 - m/2} - a_1^{1 - m/2}}{C\,(Y\Delta\sigma\sqrt\pi)^m\,(m/2 - 1)}, \qquad m = 2:\ N = \frac{\ln(a_1/a_0)}{C\,(Y\Delta\sigma\sqrt\pi)^2}.

With variable Y(a)Y(a) (for instance Feddersen's factor sec⁡(πa/W)\sqrt{\sec(\pi a/W)} for a plate of finite width) N=∫da/(CΔKm)N = \int da/(C\,\Delta K^m) is computed with a composite Gauss-Legendre rule, in ln⁡a\ln a, because the integrand is largest at the start. The critical crack length follows from Kmax=KICK_{max} = K_{IC}: ac=(KIC/(Yσmax))2/πa_c = \big(K_{IC}/(Y\sigma_{max})\big)^2/\pi.

Derivation of the closed form. Separation of variables: dN=da/(C(YΔσπ)mam/2)dN = da/\big(C\,(Y\Delta\sigma\sqrt{\pi})^m a^{m/2}\big), integrated from a0a_0 to a1a_1: ∫a−m/2da=a1−m/21−m/2\int a^{-m/2}da = \frac{a^{1-m/2}}{1-m/2}, hence the formula above; for m=2m = 2 the logarithm. Conversely the same equation gives the crack length after NN cycles, a(N)=(a01−m/2−Nk(m/2−1))1/(1−m/2)a(N) = \big(a_0^{1-m/2} - N\,k\,(m/2-1)\big)^{1/(1-m/2)}; for m>2m > 2 the bracket reaches zero after finitely many cycles – the crack then grows without bound in the computation. The substitution u=ln⁡au = \ln a (da=aduda = a\,du) smooths the integrand for the quadrature, because a1−m/2a^{1-m/2} is steep near the start for m>2m > 2.

How it is checked

  • Rainflow on the ASTM E1049 example with means by hand; cycle count of a long sequence equal to (reversals − 1)/2.
  • Transition life, Basquin there and back, Morrow and SWT without plastic part equal to Basquin; Haigh limiting cases (Goodman half, Gerber three quarters at Rm/2R_m/2).
  • Neuber: elastic KtSK_t S, plastic σε=(KtS)2/E\sigma\varepsilon = (K_t S)^2/E to 1e-12 and on the cyclic curve; Miner by hand.
  • Paris in closed form against quadrature to 1e-12 (m=3m = 3, also m=2m = 2); with Feddersen 50 against 100 parts to 1e-12 and against the midpoint rule with Richardson to 1e-8; threshold just above and below ΔK\Delta K at the start.

Limits

Linear-elastic fracture mechanics without crack closure and without sequence effects; Miner without load sequence; no endurance limit with a knee in the S-N curve. Material constants are prescribed.
