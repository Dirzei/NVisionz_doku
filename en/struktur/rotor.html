Rotor dynamics and cyclic symmetry – background

The computation behind Rotor and Zyklisch: equation of motion of a rotor with gyroscopic effects, complex coordinates for forward and backward whirl, the inverse eigenproblem with shifted real embedding, Campbell diagram and critical speed; then cyclically symmetric bladed discs per nodal diameter, aliasing and the SAFE diagram.

What it is about

A rotating shaft with discs vibrates in two planes. The gyroscopic effect of the discs couples the tilting motions: with rising speed the natural frequencies split into a forward whirl (the shaft orbits in the direction of rotation, stiffened) and a backward whirl (against it, softened). The Campbell diagram shows the natural frequencies over speed; where an excitation line ω=hΩ\omega = h\,\Omega crosses a forward mode lies a critical speed.

The equation of motion

The shaft consists of Euler-Bernoulli elements with the degrees of freedom (v,w,α=v',β=w')(v, w, \alpha = v', \beta = w') per node; the discs have mass mm, diametral inertia IdI_d and polar inertia IpI_p. For a rigid disc

Idα¨+IpΩβ˙=Mα,Idβ¨−IpΩα˙=Mβ.I_d\,\ddot\alpha + I_p\,\Omega\,\dot\beta = M_\alpha, \qquad I_d\,\ddot\beta - I_p\,\Omega\,\dot\alpha = M_\beta.

With isotropic bearings both planes have the same matrices MM, KK, CC; with the complex coordinates r=v+iwr = v + i\,w, ϑ=α+iβ\vartheta = \alpha + i\,\beta a single system of half the size results:

Mr¨+(C−iΩG)r˙+Kr=0M\,\ddot r + (C - i\,\Omega\,G)\,\dot r + K\,r = 0(1)

(GG holds the polar inertias at the tilt degrees of freedom). With r=r^eλtr = \hat r\,e^{\lambda t} an eigenvalue with positive imaginary part is a forward whirl, with negative a backward whirl – unambiguously, even where both are equally fast.

Derivation of the gyroscopic terms. The angular momentum of a fast spinning disc is H→≈IpΩe→x+Id(α˙e→y+β˙e→z)\vec H \approx I_p\Omega\,\vec e_x + I_d(\dot\alpha\,\vec e_y + \dot\beta\,\vec e_z) with the small tilt of the spin axis e→x→e→x+αe→y+βe→z\vec e_x \to \vec e_x + \alpha\vec e_y + \beta\vec e_z. The time derivative of the large part IpΩI_p\Omega with the tilting axis gives the moments IpΩβ˙I_p\Omega\dot\beta and −IpΩα˙-I_p\Omega\dot\alpha – perpendicular to the tilt rate, doing no work (GG is skew-symmetric). With ϑ=α+iβ\vartheta = \alpha + i\beta both equations combine to Idϑ¨−iIpΩϑ˙=MI_d\ddot\vartheta - i I_p\Omega\dot\vartheta = M. For a rigid disc on a tilt spring kk, Idλ2−iIpΩλ+k=0I_d\lambda^2 - iI_p\Omega\lambda + k = 0 gives the frequencies

ω±=±IpΩ+Ip2Ω2+4Idk2Id:\omega_{\pm} = \frac{\pm I_p\Omega + \sqrt{I_p^2\Omega^2 + 4 I_d k}}{2 I_d}:

the forward whirl ω+\omega_+ rises with Ω\Omega, the backward whirl ω−\omega_- falls – the splitting of the Campbell diagram in miniature (recomputed).

The inverse eigenproblem

In (1) soft modes (the ones sought) sit next to very stiff degrees of freedom. The computation is therefore inverse with μ=1/λ\mu = 1/\lambda from the state matrix

B=(−K−1(C−iΩG)−K−1MI0);B = \begin{pmatrix} -K^{-1}(C - i\,\Omega\,G) & -K^{-1}M \\ I & 0 \end{pmatrix};

the low frequencies become the large eigenvalues and stay accurate. The complex matrix is embedded in real form ([[Re⁡,−Im⁡],[Im⁡,Re⁡]][[\mathrm{Re}, -\mathrm{Im}], [\mathrm{Im}, \mathrm{Re}]]) and shifted by isi\,s beforehand, ss above the spectral radius: then the eigenvalues of the embedding with positive imaginary part are exactly the eigenvalues of BB plus isi s, and the half belonging to the conjugate matrix drops out. The eigenvalues are computed by the QR method of framework-math.

A degree of freedom without mass gives an infinite eigenvalue (μ=0\mu = 0); it is discarded. A damped degree of freedom without mass would give an aperiodic motion that cannot be cleanly separated from rounding noise – it is rejected (give the journal as a disc with mass).

Why inverse. The QR method finds all eigenvalues with an absolute error of about ε‖B‖\varepsilon\,\|B\|. The low frequencies sought are the smallest eigenvalues λ\lambda and would lose relative accuracy next to the stiff modes; as the largest eigenvalues of μ=1/λ\mu = 1/\lambda they are relatively accurate. The shift by isis with ss above the spectral radius separates the half of the real embedding that belongs to the conjugate matrix: its eigenvalues μ−is\bar\mu - is all have negative imaginary part.

Worked example Laval rotor. A massless, simply supported shaft has the midspan stiffness 48EI/L348EI/L^3 (deflection v=Px(3L2−4x2)/(48EI)v = Px(3L^2 - 4x^2)/(48EI), recomputed); with the disc mass mm, ω=48EI/(mL3)\omega = \sqrt{48EI/(mL^3)}. Because the disc sits at midspan it does not tilt – the gyroscopic effect has no influence, and the critical speed for unbalance (h=1h = 1) equals this frequency.

Cyclically symmetric bladed discs

A bladed wheel of NN equal sectors can be computed with a single sector. If a sector has the mass matrix M0M_0, the stiffness K0K_0 within itself and K1K_1 to the next sector, then for the nodal diameter kk with φ=2πk/N\varphi = 2\pi k/N

K(φ)=K0+K1eiφ+K1Te−iφ,K(φ)x=ω2M0x.K(\varphi) = K_0 + K_1\,e^{i\varphi} + K_1^T\,e^{-i\varphi}, \qquad K(\varphi)\,x = \omega^2 M_0\,x.

K(φ)K(\varphi) is Hermitian; it is solved in real embedding (each eigenvalue appears twice). For k=0k = 0 and k=N/2k = N/2 the modes are single, otherwise double (standing waves in two positions).

Derivation. A mode of a cyclically symmetric wheel maps onto itself when turned by one sector, up to a factor: xj+1=eiφxjx_{j+1} = e^{i\varphi}x_j (Bloch-Floquet), and eiNφ=1e^{iN\varphi} = 1 demands φ=2πk/N\varphi = 2\pi k/N. Inserting this into the equation of sector jj, which contains only xj−1x_{j-1}, xjx_j, xj+1x_{j+1}, leaves K(φ)K(\varphi). Example chain of masses mm, ground springs kgk_g and coupling springs kck_c: K0=kg+2kcK_0 = k_g + 2k_c, K1=−kcK_1 = -k_c, hence ω2=(kg+2kc(1−cos⁡φ))/m\omega^2 = (k_g + 2k_c(1 - \cos\varphi))/m – the frequency rises with the nodal diameter (recomputed). The cost drops from one eigenproblem of size NnNn to N/2+1N/2 + 1 problems of size nn.

Aliasing. An excitation of order hh (for instance hh vanes upstream) only hits the nodal diameters k=hmodNk = h \bmod N and N−kN - k. With 24 blades the orders 5, 19 and 29 all excite nodal diameter 5.

SAFE diagram (Singh): nodal diameter horizontal, frequency vertical. A resonance lies only where the frequency line ω=hΩ\omega = h\,\Omega meets the natural frequency of an excited nodal diameter – with centrifugal stiffening after Southwell ω2=ω02+BΩ2\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + B\,\Omega^2. If h2≤Bh^2 \le B, there is no intersection.

How it is checked

  • Laval rotor (massless shaft, 20 kg at midspan): ω=48EI/(mL3)\omega = \sqrt{48 EI/(m L^3)} in both planes to 1e-6, independent of speed, critical speed equal.
  • Simply supported shaft, 30 elements: (nπ/L)2EI/(ρA)(n\pi/L)^2\sqrt{EI/(\rho A)}, n=1n = 1 to 1e-6, n=2n = 2 to 5e-6.
  • Overhung disc on a cantilever at 1500 rad/s: first forward and backward frequency against the frequency equation to 1e-5; forward stiffer, backward softer; without rotation both equal.
  • Disc on two soft damped bearings with journal mass: ωn1−ζ2\omega_n\sqrt{1-\zeta^2} and ζ=c/2k(m+2mz)\zeta = c/\sqrt{2k(m + 2m_z)} to 1e-5; a damper without mass is reported.
  • Disc-blade chain with 24 sectors: per nodal diameter both frequencies against the 2×2 equation to 1e-12; whole wheel (12 sectors) equal to the sector solutions to 1e-10, also with complex coupling.
  • Campbell diagram equal to the modes per speed; a forward mode that does not exist is reported when searching the critical speed.
  • Aliasing (5, 19, 29 → 5; 24 → 0; 12 → 12) and SAFE: every resonance lies on ω(Ω)=hΩ\omega(\Omega) = h\,\Omega to 1e-14 and only at the excited nodal diameter.

Limits

Isotropic bearings (no cross-coupling, no journal-bearing maps), gyroscopic effect of the discs only, linear. Cyclic symmetry without mistuning.
