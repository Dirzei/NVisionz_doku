Rotating discs and cylindrical shell – background

The computation behind Scheibe and Zylinderschale: the disc equations in plane stress, the closed-form solutions for constant thickness, the boundary-value problem for thickness profile and temperature with particular and homogeneous solution, and the bending theory of the cylindrical shell with its edge disturbance.

What it is about

Compressor and turbine discs are loaded by centrifugal force, by the blade pull at the rim and by temperature differences. The largest hoop stress usually sits at the bore. For design the axisymmetric model in plane stress suffices: radial stress σr\sigma_r, hoop stress σθ\sigma_\theta and radial displacement uu as functions of the radius.

The disc equations

Equilibrium of a ring element, compatibility and Hooke's law with thermal strain give two first-order differential equations in uu and F=hrσrF = h\,r\,\sigma_r:

dFdr=hσθ−ρω2r2h,dudr=1−ν2Eσr−νur+(1+ν)αT,σθ=Eur+νσr−EαT.\frac{dF}{dr} = h\,\sigma_\theta - \rho\,\omega^2 r^2 h, \qquad \frac{du}{dr} = \frac{1 - \nu^2}{E}\,\sigma_r - \nu\,\frac{u}{r} + (1 + \nu)\,\alpha T, \qquad \sigma_\theta = E\,\frac{u}{r} + \nu\,\sigma_r - E\,\alpha T.

Derivation. On the ring element of thickness hh between rr and r+drr + dr act radially d(hrσr)d(h r\sigma_r), the hoop forces pointing inward with σθhdr\sigma_\theta h\,dr, and the centrifugal force ρω2r⋅hrdr\rho\omega^2 r\cdot h r\,dr: ddr(hrσr)−hσθ+ρω2r2h=0\frac{d}{dr}(h r\sigma_r) - h\sigma_\theta + \rho\omega^2 r^2 h = 0. The strains are εr=du/dr\varepsilon_r = du/dr, εθ=u/r\varepsilon_\theta = u/r; Hooke in plane stress with thermal strain εr=(σr−νσθ)/E+αT\varepsilon_r = (\sigma_r - \nu\sigma_\theta)/E + \alpha T, εθ=(σθ−νσr)/E+αT\varepsilon_\theta = (\sigma_\theta - \nu\sigma_r)/E + \alpha T. Solving the second for σθ\sigma_\theta and inserting it into the first yields the two first-order equations above. For constant hh and TT this leads to u=C1r+C2/r−(1−ν2)ρω28Er3u = C_1 r + C_2/r - \frac{(1-\nu^2)\rho\omega^2}{8E}r^3, and the free edges fix C1C_1, C2C_2 – giving the closed-form formulas (recomputed, including the limit of the small bore).

Discs of constant thickness without temperature have closed-form solutions (Timoshenko and Goodier); with bore and free edges

σr=3+ν8ρω2(a2+b2−a2b2r2−r2),σθ=3+ν8ρω2(a2+b2+a2b2r2−1+3ν3+νr2).\sigma_r = \tfrac{3+\nu}{8}\rho\omega^2\Big(a^2 + b^2 - \frac{a^2 b^2}{r^2} - r^2\Big), \qquad \sigma_\theta = \tfrac{3+\nu}{8}\rho\omega^2\Big(a^2 + b^2 + \frac{a^2 b^2}{r^2} - \frac{1+3\nu}{3+\nu}\,r^2\Big).

A small bore doubles the hoop stress at the centre: σθ(a→0)=3+ν4ρω2b2\sigma_\theta(a \to 0) = \frac{3+\nu}{4}\rho\omega^2 b^2 against 3+ν8ρω2b2\frac{3+\nu}{8}\rho\omega^2 b^2 of the solid disc.

Arbitrary profile: boundary-value problem

The system is linear in (u,F)(u, F). Two initial-value problems are integrated with the classical Runge-Kutta method from inside out: a particular solution with all loads (centrifugal, temperature) and the inner boundary value, and a homogeneous solution without loads with a unit initial value. Their superposition y=yp+cyhy = y_p + c\,y_h satisfies, with the right cc, the outer boundary condition σr(b)=σb\sigma_r(b) = \sigma_b.

  • bore: σr(a)\sigma_r(a) prescribed inside (usually 0), u(a)u(a) free – u(a)u(a) is the unknown of the homogeneous solution.
  • solid disc: start at r0=10−9br_0 = 10^{-9} b with u=cru = c\,r and σr=σθ\sigma_r = \sigma_\theta (symmetry at the centre).
r_k ← a … b in n steps (solid disc from 1e−9 b)
y_p ← RK4 from inside with loads, start from the inner condition: σ_r(a) = σ_a, part u = 0
y_h ← RK4 from inside without loads, start with unit displacement (σ_r = 0 at the bore,
       for a solid disc u = r₀ and σ_r = σ_θ)
c ← (h b σ_b − F_p(b)) / F_h(b)
y ← y_p + c y_h;  σ_r ← F/(h r);  σ_θ ← E u/r + ν σ_r − E α T

Why the superposition is exact. The system is linear in (u,F)(u, F); every solution is the particular one plus a multiple of the homogeneous one. The inner condition fixes one component, the outer condition the second – a shooting method with a single shot, without iteration. The error is that of the Runge-Kutta method, O(Δr4)O(\Delta r^4); at the centre of the solid disc the equation is singular (1/r1/r), hence the start just beside it with the limiting behaviour u∼ru \sim r.

The cylindrical shell under internal pressure

Far from the edges the internal pressure pp expands a cylindrical shell (radius RR, wall thickness tt) uniformly: wp=pR2/(Et)w_p = pR^2/(Et), hoop force Nθ=pRN_\theta = pR (boiler formula). At a clamped or simply supported edge the expansion is restrained; an edge disturbance with bending moment arises and decays over the length 1/β1/\beta:

Dw''''+EtR2w=p,D=Et312(1−ν2),β4=3(1−ν2)R2t2.D\,w'''' + \frac{E t}{R^2}\,w = p, \qquad D = \frac{E t^3}{12(1-\nu^2)}, \qquad \beta^4 = \frac{3(1-\nu^2)}{R^2 t^2}.

Derivation. In the axisymmetrically loaded cylindrical shell the hoop strain is w/Rw/R; it produces the hoop force Nθ=Etw/RN_\theta = Et\,w/R, whose curvature part Nθ/R=Etw/R2N_\theta/R = Et\,w/R^2 acts radially like a foundation. Axially the wall bends like a plate strip with DD. Radial equilibrium gives the equation above, and β4=Et/(4R2D)\beta^4 = Et/(4R^2D) equals 3(1−ν2)/(R2t2)3(1-\nu^2)/(R^2t^2). The solution of the long shell with clamping satisfies w(0)=w'(0)=0w(0) = w'(0) = 0 and has the edge moment Mx(0)=−Dw''(0)=−p/(2β2)M_x(0) = -D\,w''(0) = -p/(2\beta^2) (recomputed). The decay length 1/β≈0.78Rt1/\beta \approx 0.78\sqrt{Rt} shows why edge disturbances in thin shells stay local.

This is a beam on an elastic foundation. Zylinderschale solves it with cubic Hermite elements (bending) and a consistent foundation matrix; edges free, simply supported (w=0w = 0) or clamped (w=w'=0w = w' = 0). Output are ww, Mx=−Dw''M_x = -D\,w'' and Nθ=Etw/RN_\theta = E t\,w/R.

Closed-form reference solutions of the long shell (Timoshenko and Woinowsky-Krieger): clamped w=wp(1−e−βx(cos⁡βx+sin⁡βx))w = w_p\big(1 - e^{-\beta x}(\cos\beta x + \sin\beta x)\big) with the clamping moment p/(2β2)p/(2\beta^2); simply supported w=wp(1−e−βxcos⁡βx)w = w_p(1 - e^{-\beta x}\cos\beta x).

General shells of revolution. Rotationsschale takes any meridian as a polyline in the (r,z)(r, z) plane and builds it from conical frustum elements (Grafton and Strome): along the meridian the meridional displacement uu is linear, the normal displacement ww cubic. With the angle α\alpha of the generator against the radial direction, Kirchhoff-Love gives

εs=u',εθ=ucos⁡α+wsin⁡αr,κs=−w'',κθ=−cos⁡αw'r,\varepsilon_s = u', \quad \varepsilon_\theta = \frac{u\cos\alpha + w\sin\alpha}{r}, \quad \kappa_s = -w'', \quad \kappa_\theta = -\frac{\cos\alpha\, w'}{r},

integrated over rdsr\,ds with four Gauss points per element. Nodal degrees of freedom are radial and axial displacement and the rotation; nodes on the axis hold radial displacement and rotation. Sphere, cone, circular plate and composite vessels come from the same class; curved meridians are approximated by chords, the error falls quadratically with the element length. The system is banded and solved by Cholesky.

Asymmetric loads. A load that varies around the circumference is split into Fourier harmonics; for harmonic kk, u,w,β∼cos⁡kθu, w, \beta \sim \cos k\theta and the circumferential displacement v∼sin⁡kθv \sim \sin k\theta, and the solutions are superposed. Shear strain and twist are added:

γ=v'−cos⁡αrv−kru,τ=2krw'−2kcos⁡αr2w+sin⁡αrv'−2sin⁡αcos⁡αr2v+sin⁡α2r(v'+cos⁡αrv+kru),\gamma = v' - \frac{\cos\alpha}{r}v - \frac{k}{r}u, \qquad \tau = \frac{2k}{r}w' - \frac{2k\cos\alpha}{r^2}w + \frac{\sin\alpha}{r}v' - \frac{2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}{r^2}v + \frac{\sin\alpha}{2r}\Big(v' + \frac{\cos\alpha}{r}v + \frac{k}{r}u\Big),

and εθ\varepsilon_\theta, κθ\kappa_\theta gain the terms in kk. The last term of the twist is Sanders': without it the rigid tilt of a tube (k=1k = 1) would produce twist, and a long cantilever tube would come out too stiff (by 4 % at L/R=80L/R = 80). Nodes on the axis are handled automatically: for k=1k = 1 the axial displacement is zero and the lateral displacement uniform (V=−UrV = -U_r); from k=2k = 2 everything is zero.

How it is checked

  • Disc of constant thickness: centre σr=σθ=3+ν8ρω2b2\sigma_r = \sigma_\theta = \frac{3+\nu}{8}\rho\omega^2 b^2; a small bore doubles σθ\sigma_\theta; edges stress-free.
  • Numerical against closed form: with bore to 1e-12, solid to 1e-8; a blade pull of 50 MPa adds 2σbb2/(b2−a2)2\sigma_b b^2/(b^2 - a^2) at the bore.
  • Thermal stress of the solid disc without rotation with T=200K(r/b)2T = 200\ \text{K}\,(r/b)^2 after Timoshenko/Goodier to 1e-9.
  • Tapered disc (6 to 2.1 cm): half the disc is in equilibrium, ∫σθhdr=ρω2∫hr2dr\int \sigma_\theta h\,dr = \rho\omega^2 \int h r^2\,dr to 1e-6.
  • Cylindrical shell: clamped and simply supported against the closed-form solutions to 1e-8 (shell 24/β long), moments to 1e-3; both ends free gives the pure membrane state.
  • Shell of revolution: full sphere under internal pressure Ns=Nθ=pR/2N_s = N_\theta = pR/2 to 1e-4; circular plate clamped pa4/(64D)pa^4/(64D) and simply supported (5+ν)pa4/(64(1+ν)D)(5+\nu)pa^4/(64(1+\nu)D) to 1e-6, moments to 1e-4; hemisphere with edge force and edge moment against Geckeler's approximation to 3e-4 and Maxwell-Betti to 1e-8; cone with a free edge: meridional force from equilibrium, hoop force pr/sin⁡αpr/\sin\alpha.
  • Harmonics: clamped circular plate under p(r/a)cos⁡θp\,(r/a)\cos\theta with w=pr(a2−r2)2/(192aD)w = p\,r(a^2-r^2)^2/(192\,aD) to 1e-6 and under p(r/a)2cos⁡2θp\,(r/a)^2\cos 2\theta with w=pr2(a2−r2)2/(384a2D)w = p\,r^2(a^2-r^2)^2/(384\,a^2D) with order 2; cantilever tube with end shear, end moment and distributed load against the Timoshenko beam (shear coefficient 1/2) to 1e-4, also with a flat end cap reaching the axis; long cylinder under pcos⁡2θp\cos 2\theta against the ring pR4/(9D)pR^4/(9D) with inextensional deformation v=−w/2v = -w/2.

Limits

Plane stress (thin discs), linear elastic, no plasticity and no creep. Shells after Kirchhoff-Love without transverse shear deformation, small deformations; asymmetric loads as a sum of harmonics, one run per harmonic.
