Plane and axisymmetric continuum elements – background

The computation behind Flaechenmodell, Scheibe2d and Achsensymmetrisch: isoparametric three-, four- and eight-node elements, plane stress and plane strain, solids of revolution under axisymmetric load, thermal strain, centrifugal force and edge pressure, stresses at the Barlow points – with pseudocode, test cases and limits.

What it is about

Discs with holes, slots or irregular outlines and solids of revolution with an arbitrary meridian section (wheel discs, flanges, thick-walled vessels) can no longer be computed from an ordinary differential equation in rr. The continuum model divides the section into elements, approximates the displacement in each element with shape functions and assembles a linear system Ku=fK u = f from the principle of virtual work. The mesh comes either by hand (nodes and elements) or as a Delaunay triangulation of a point cloud from framework-math.

Isoparametric elements

Geometry and displacement are described with the same shape functions Ni(ξ,η)N_i(\xi, \eta) over the parent element:

x=∑iNixi,y=∑iNiyi,u=∑iNiui,v=∑iNivi.x = \sum_i N_i x_i, \quad y = \sum_i N_i y_i, \qquad u = \sum_i N_i u_i, \quad v = \sum_i N_i v_i.

  • Three-node triangle: N=(1−ξ−η,ξ,η)N = (1 - \xi - \eta,\ \xi,\ \eta), constant strain; three interior integration points.
  • Four-node quadrilateral: Ni=14(1+ξξi)(1+ηηi)N_i = \tfrac14(1 + \xi\xi_i)(1 + \eta\eta_i), 2 × 2 Gauss points at ±1/3\pm 1/\sqrt3.
  • Eight-node quadrilateral (serendipity): corners Ni=14(1+ξξi)(1+ηηi)(ξξi+ηηi−1)N_i = \tfrac14(1 + \xi\xi_i)(1 + \eta\eta_i)(\xi\xi_i + \eta\eta_i - 1), mid-sides 12(1−ξ2)(1+ηηi)\tfrac12(1 - \xi^2)(1 + \eta\eta_i) or 12(1+ξξi)(1−η2)\tfrac12(1 + \xi\xi_i)(1 - \eta^2); 3 × 3 Gauss points.

Derivatives with respect to x,yx, y follow from the Jacobian J=∂(x,y)/∂(ξ,η)J = \partial(x, y)/\partial(\xi, \eta); an element with det⁡J≤0\det J \le 0 (wrong orientation, twisted) is rejected. The element stiffness is

Ke=∫ΩeBTDBwdΩ≈∑gBgTDBgw(xg,yg)det⁡JgWg,K_e = \int_{\Omega_e} B^\mathsf{T} D\,B \; w\,d\Omega \approx \sum_g B_g^\mathsf{T} D\,B_g\; w(x_g, y_g)\,\det J_g\,W_g,

with the weight ww = thickness (disc) or 2πr2\pi r (solid of revolution).

Plane stress and plane strain

Strains (εx,εy,γxy)(\varepsilon_x, \varepsilon_y, \gamma_{xy}), stresses (σx,σy,τxy)(\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \tau_{xy}). In plane stress (thin disc, σz=0\sigma_z = 0)

D=E1−ν2(1ν0ν1000(1−ν)/2),ε0=αT(1,1,0),D = \frac{E}{1 - \nu^2}\begin{pmatrix}1 & \nu & 0\\ \nu & 1 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & (1 - \nu)/2\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \varepsilon_0 = \alpha T\,(1, 1, 0),

in plane strain (long body, εz=0\varepsilon_z = 0) D=E(1+ν)(1−2ν)[…]D = \frac{E}{(1 + \nu)(1 - 2\nu)}\big[\ldots\big] with 1−ν1 - \nu on the diagonal and ε0=(1+ν)αT(1,1,0)\varepsilon_0 = (1 + \nu)\alpha T\,(1, 1, 0). This is the plane-stress state with the substitutes E/(1−ν2)E/(1 - \nu^2), ν/(1−ν)\nu/(1 - \nu) and (1+ν)α(1 + \nu)\alpha – the probe uses that as a second route.

Solids of revolution under axisymmetric load

In (r,z)(r, z) with displacements uu (radial) and ww (axial) the hoop strain εθ=u/r\varepsilon_\theta = u/r joins the plane strains, giving four components:

ε=(∂u∂r,∂w∂z,ur,∂u∂z+∂w∂r),D=E(1+ν)(1−2ν)(1−ννν0ν1−νν0νν1−ν00001−2ν2).\varepsilon = \Big(\frac{\partial u}{\partial r},\ \frac{\partial w}{\partial z},\ \frac{u}{r},\ \frac{\partial u}{\partial z} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial r}\Big), \qquad D = \frac{E}{(1 + \nu)(1 - 2\nu)}\begin{pmatrix}1-\nu & \nu & \nu & 0\\ \nu & 1-\nu & \nu & 0\\ \nu & \nu & 1-\nu & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \tfrac{1-2\nu}{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Integration is over the ring, i.e. with the weight 2πr2\pi r; nodal forces and pressure loads act on the whole circumference. Since the Gauss points lie inside the elements, u/ru/r is never evaluated at r=0r = 0, even for elements on the axis. Thermal strain αT\alpha T acts in all three normal directions, the centrifugal force is ρΩ2r\rho\,\Omega^2 r radially.

Loads

The right-hand side collects nodal forces, body forces and initial strains:

f=∑e∫NTbwdΩ+∫BTDε0wdΩ+∮NTtwds.f = \sum_e \int N^\mathsf{T} b\; w\,d\Omega + \int B^\mathsf{T} D\,\varepsilon_0\; w\,d\Omega + \oint N^\mathsf{T} t\; w\,ds.

Centrifugal force b=ρΩ2(x,y)b = \rho\,\Omega^2 (x, y) and temperature T(x,y)T(x, y) are evaluated at the Gauss points; an edge pressure pp acts against the outward normal of an element edge and is integrated consistently along it (including the mid-side node of the eight-node element). druck(Ort, p) loads all boundary edges whose nodes lie in a region. Supports are fixings or prescribed displacements per degree of freedom; if they are insufficient (rigid-body motion free), the Cholesky factorisation fails and the model reports the missing support.

Stresses at the Barlow points

The stresses σ=D(Bu−ε0)\sigma = D(B u - \varepsilon_0) are one order less accurate inside an element than the displacements, least accurate at the nodes. At the Gauss points of the reduced rule (Barlow 1976: the 2 × 2 points of the eight-node quadrilateral) they reach the order of the displacements. barlowspannungen returns position and stress there, knotenspannungen averages the nodal values of the adjacent elements (illustrative but less accurate). In the Lamé tube with 8 × 8 eight-node elements the probe hits the stress at the Barlow points to 1.3·10⁻⁴ pp, the averaged nodal values only to 7·10⁻³.

Meshes from a point cloud

delaunay(x, y, w, region) triangulates a point cloud with the Bowyer-Watson method from framework-math and keeps the triangles whose centroid lies in the region. This gives triangle meshes for arbitrary outlines, also with holes. A curved inner boundary is replaced by chords, which costs order of convergence (in the quarter ring with a bore the mean error of the hoop stress drops only from 0.064 to 0.040 when the point density doubles, order about 0.7 instead of 1).

How it is checked

  • Patch test after MacNeal and Harder with distorted four- and eight-node elements and a Delaunay mesh: a linear displacement field prescribed on the boundary, interior nodes and stresses exact (1.9·10⁻¹⁴); axisymmetric with u=cru = c\,r, w=dzw = d\,z.
  • Lamé tube under internal pressure (plane and as a long cylinder), rotating disc with a bore against the closed solution (error converges with order 2 under refinement), thermal stress T∝r2T \propto r^2 with rotation against the module's disc computation (1.4·10⁻³), freely supported disc with linear temperature stress-free.
  • Error cases: clockwise element, missing support, ν=0.5\nu = 0.5, negative radius, wrong node count.

Limits

Linear elastic, small deformations, isotropic material per element. Dense matrices: meshes up to a few thousand degrees of freedom. No incompatible-mode elements against shear locking; slender bending parts better with eight-node elements. Axisymmetric only for axisymmetric loads (harmonics exist for shells in Rotationsschale).
