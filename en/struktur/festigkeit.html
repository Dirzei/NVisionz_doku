Burst speed, creep rupture and crack growth – background

The computation behind Berst, Zeitstand and Riss: burst speed of a disc from the mean hoop stress, creep-rupture parameters after Larson-Miller and Manson-Haferd, Monkman-Grant, life-fraction rule and creep-fatigue diagram, crack growth after Walker, Forman and NASGRO with Newman crack closure, failure assessment diagram Option 1 – with pseudocode, test cases and limits.

What it is about

Three life questions for hot, fast-spinning parts: at which speed does the disc burst? How long does it last at temperature and stress before it breaks by creep? How fast does an existing crack grow, and when is it critical? All three build on computations the module already has: the stress distribution of the rotating disc and Paris from fatigue life.

Burst speed

Equilibrium of the half disc (cut through the axis) requires the hoop stress over the cross-section to carry the centrifugal force of the half and the outer rim loads. This gives the mean hoop stress without knowing the distribution:

σθ=ρΩ2∫abr2hdr+σbhbb−σahaa∫abhdr,constant thickness, free edges: σθ=ρΩ23(a2+ab+b2).\bar\sigma_\theta = \frac{\rho\,\Omega^2 \int_a^b r^2 h\,dr + \sigma_b h_b b - \sigma_a h_a a}{\int_a^b h\,dr}, \qquad \text{constant thickness, free edges: } \bar\sigma_\theta = \frac{\rho\,\Omega^2}{3}\,(a^2 + ab + b^2).

At full plastification the mean stress equals the tensile strength; since it grows with Ω2\Omega^2, the burst speed after Robinson (1944) is

ΩB=ΩηRm/σθ(Ω),\Omega_B = \Omega\,\sqrt{\eta\,R_m / \bar\sigma_\theta(\Omega)},

with a utilisation factor η≤1\eta \le 1 (notches, slots, material scatter; Robinson: η=1\eta = 1). Thermal stress does not change the mean stress – it is self-equilibrated. mittlereUmfangsspannung takes either a computed distribution (Simpson over the cross-section) or computes from equilibrium.

Creep rupture

Creep-rupture tests at different temperatures are collapsed onto a master curve over a parameter linking temperature TT [K] and rupture time trt_r [h]:

Larson-Miller: PLM=T(C+log⁡tr)=∑kck(log⁡σ)k,Manson-Haferd: PMH=T−Talog⁡tr−log⁡ta,1PMH=∑kck(log⁡σ)k.\text{Larson-Miller: } P_{LM} = T\,(C + \log t_r) = \sum_k c_k (\log\sigma)^k, \qquad \text{Manson-Haferd: } P_{MH} = \frac{T - T_a}{\log t_r - \log t_a}, \quad \frac{1}{P_{MH}} = \sum_k c_k (\log\sigma)^k.

Larson-Miller is linear in CC and the ckc_k: Tlog⁡tr=−CT+∑ckT(log⁡σ)kT \log t_r = -C\,T + \sum c_k T(\log\sigma)^k, a least-squares fit. Manson-Haferd is linear in log⁡ta\log t_a and the ckc_k for fixed TaT_a (log⁡tr=log⁡ta+(T−Ta)∑ck(log⁡σ)k\log t_r = \log t_a + (T - T_a)\sum c_k(\log\sigma)^k); TaT_a is found with Brent so that the residual is smallest. The two parameters convert exactly into each other pointwise (for a given TT), but a Larson-Miller curve is in general not a Manson-Haferd curve: fitted to data of the other form a residual remains.

Monkman-Grant. Minimum creep rate and rupture time are linked by ε˙minmtr=CMG\dot\varepsilon_{min}^m\,t_r = C_{MG} (m≈1m \approx 1); the fit is log⁡tr=log⁡CMG−mlog⁡ε˙min\log t_r = \log C_{MG} - m\log\dot\varepsilon_{min}.

Creep-fatigue. Creep damage is the life-fraction rule Dc=∑ti/tr,iD_c = \sum t_i/t_{r,i}, fatigue damage the Miner sum DfD_f. The bilinear diagram (as in ASME III Division 5) joins (1,0)(1, 0), the knee (Df*,Dc*)(D_f^*, D_c^*) and (0,1)(0, 1); the utilisation is the factor by which the ray through (Df,Dc)(D_f, D_c) must be extended to reach the limit line – below 1 means acceptable.

Crack growth with mean stress

Paris does not know the mean stress. Three common extensions, all with R=Kmin/KmaxR = K_{min}/K_{max}:

Walker: dadN=C(ΔK(1−R)1−γ)m,Forman: dadN=CΔKm(1−R)Kc−ΔK,\text{Walker: } \frac{da}{dN} = C\Big(\frac{\Delta K}{(1 - R)^{1 - \gamma}}\Big)^m, \qquad \text{Forman: } \frac{da}{dN} = \frac{C\,\Delta K^m}{(1 - R)K_c - \Delta K},

NASGRO: dadN=C[1−f1−RΔK]n(1−ΔKth/ΔK)p(1−Kmax/Kc)q.\text{NASGRO: } \frac{da}{dN} = C\Big[\frac{1 - f}{1 - R}\,\Delta K\Big]^n\,\frac{(1 - \Delta K_{th}/\Delta K)^p}{(1 - K_{max}/K_c)^q}.

ff is Newman's (1984) opening function: for R≥0R \ge 0 f=max⁡(R,A0+A1R+A2R2+A3R3)f = \max(R, A_0 + A_1R + A_2R^2 + A_3R^3) with A0=(0.825−0.34α+0.05α2)[cos⁡(πSmax/(2σ0))]1/αA_0 = (0.825 - 0.34\alpha + 0.05\alpha^2)\,[\cos(\pi S_{max}/(2\sigma_0))]^{1/\alpha} and A0+A1+A2+A3=1A_0 + A_1 + A_2 + A_3 = 1, for −2≤R<0-2 \le R < 0 f=A0+A1Rf = A_0 + A_1 R. The threshold depends on crack length through the short-crack term a/(a+a0)\sqrt{a/(a + a_0)}. Cycles are counted as for Paris with Gauss-Legendre in ln⁡a\ln a; if the rate is zero anywhere (below the threshold) the count is infinite, and if KmaxK_{max} exceeds the toughness before the target this is reported.

Forman in closed form. For fixed YY, ∫((1−R)Kc−ΔK)/(CΔKm)da=(1−R)KcNP(C,m)−NP(C,m−1)\int\big((1 - R)K_c - \Delta K\big)/(C\Delta K^m)\,da = (1 - R)K_c\,N_P(C, m) - N_P(C, m - 1) with the Paris cycle count NPN_P – this is how the probe checks it.

How it is checked

  • Burst: mean stress from the formula, integration of the closed solution, computed distribution and equilibrium agree (1e-9); profile with blade load; heat does not change it; burst speed independent of the reference speed.
  • Creep: master curves recovered exactly from generated data (CC, TaT_a, log⁡ta\log t_a), pointwise conversion there and back, Monkman-Grant, diagram with knee, allowable stress recovered.
  • Crack: Walker at R=0R = 0 and γ=1\gamma = 1 equals Paris, Forman in closed form, NASGRO without threshold equals Paris with ((1−f)/(1−R))n((1 - f)/(1 - R))^n, Newman A0A_0 and f(1)=1f(1) = 1, threshold; FAD f(0)=1f(0) = 1, f(1)f(1) and the critical length.

Limits

Robinson's burst speed is an estimate via the mean stress; notches and local plastification sit only in the factor η\eta. Creep-rupture parameters extrapolate poorly beyond the data range. Crack growth has no sequence effects (overloads); material data and stress intensities are inputs. FAD Option 1 only, without toughness scatter.
