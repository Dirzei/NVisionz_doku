Retraining from corrections

A recogniser that learned from painted lines reads a print poorly that it has never seen. The remedy is real lines with their truth: cells a person corrected or confirmed, and cells of pages whose values are known from a checked transcript. The model is trained further with them – mixed with painted lines, so that it keeps what it can do.

The store

A sample is the image of one line – exactly the crop the line recogniser sees when reading – and its text. The store is a folder: one image per sample, named after the fingerprint of its pixels, and one line in a list with file name, text and origin. Two rules follow:

  • The same image with the same text goes in only once. A second run over the same page stores nothing twice; the store only grows.
  • The same image with a different text gets another line in the list, and on reading the later one counts: the correction of a correction is the more recent statement.

Only a text made of characters the recogniser can output is accepted as truth; an empty text or a foreign character is rejected before anything is written. The list itself is a file like any other: an entry that points out of the folder is not followed.

Where the samples come from

From operation

On the read card of the table recognition the selected cell shows its image. What the user corrects in the table is a correction; a marked cell that was read correctly is confirmed with a button. On saving, every corrected or confirmed cell whose crop consists of exactly one line goes into the store with its text – an unchanged, unconfirmed cell does not: that nobody objected is not a truth.

From pages with a transcript

If the values of a scanned table exist as a checked transcript, the whole page can be harvested. The transcript usually carries only the data rows and sometimes not all columns; so what is required is not the shape of the table but an alignment: the page is read, and the rows of the transcript are aligned with the rows read, in order (Needleman and Wunsch). A row pair costs the mean distance of its known cells,

k(g,w)=1|B|∑c∈Bmin⁡(1,d(z(gc),z(wc))|z(wc)|),k(g, w) = \frac{1}{|B|} \sum_{c \in B} \min\!\Bigl(1,\; \frac{d\bigl(z(g_c),\, z(w_c)\bigr)}{|z(w_c)|}\Bigr),

with BB the columns the transcript knows in this row, z(⋅)z(\cdot) the digits of a text and dd the Levenshtein distance; a row without a partner costs 0.6. Looking at the digits makes the alignment independent of what the recogniser cannot yet do on this print – the point, the sign. Example: read “012” and “35”, transcript “0.12” and “-3.4” – the first cell costs 0, the second one of two digits, the pair 0.25. A pair above 0.5 does not count.

If the grid has more columns than the transcript (a margin note, a page number beside the table), the fullest columns count. And even in an aligned row every cell is checked on its own: if its digits differ by more than half, something other than the transcript may stand there, and it stays out – a wrongly labelled sample does more harm than a missing one does good. The text of the transcript goes into the store, not the text read.

Learning and checking

Every nn-th sample in the order of the list (default every fifth) is set aside and never trained. On it the training log shows, at every check, the character error – Levenshtein distance over the number of characters of the truth, spaces not counted – and the number of samples read entirely correctly. Samples of the same page resemble each other, though; the harder measurement sets aside whole pages, in a store of their own.

The batch

Training runs as in the first learning (line recogniser), except that every line of a batch is, with probability aa, a real one from the store and otherwise a freshly painted one. With a batch of BB lines, aBaB are real on average. The painted lines are the anchor: without them the net would learn the one print and lose the others. There are only as many real lines as are stored – they recur during the run; half of them therefore get slight noise. With a=0a = 0 the result is bit for bit the same model as without a store.

Training starts from the weights of the existing model with a small learning rate; it rises over the first 300 steps and then falls as in the first learning.

Measured

Material: ten scanned pages of a table of measured values from a dot-matrix printer, about 310 numbers each with four decimals, whose values exist as a checked transcript. The model for tables mostly read the digits correctly there, but rarely the dot-matrix decimal point and the sign. Eight pages went into the store (2409 samples, every fifth set aside), two pages stayed out entirely (618 cells). Retraining ran 1500 steps with batches of 16 lines, a=0.5a = 0.5, learning rate 10−410^{-4} – about eleven minutes on six threads.

After only 250 steps the character error on the fifth set aside was 0.37 %. The price is small but visible: on painted lines in foreign fonts the retrained model reads a trace worse (three instead of two of 72 number lines not entirely correct). A retrained model is therefore a model for this print and belongs beside the general one, not in its place. That is also where it lies in the model folder: in a subfolder druck. The read card of the table recognition offers it there for selection (“For one print”); the general choice of a recogniser does not see the subfolder.

Limits

  • The measurement holds for the same print: two other pages of the same document. How far the learning carries to another dot-matrix printer has not been measured.
  • Only cells of exactly one line are stored; multi-line and merged cells stay out.
  • The harvest from a transcript discards cells whose digits the recogniser misses widely – so precisely the hardest samples are missing. They can be added one by one through operation.
  • The line images are excerpts of other people's documents; the store lies where the user puts it and does not by itself belong to a delivered model.
  • Text from the text layer of a typeset page is not a correction of a reader and is not stored.
