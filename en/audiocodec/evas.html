EVAS stream format – layout, checksums, resynchronisation

What the header of an EVAS stream contains and why, how a frame is built, how the two checksums compute (CRC-16 per frame and header, FNV-1a over all samples), and how the decoder resumes at the right place after a damaged frame. With a byte-by-byte example, pseudocode following the source, probes and limits.

What it is about

A stream format has to do three things that have nothing to do with compression itself. It must say how the content is to be read (sample rate, channels, word length, method), it must say exactly how many samples come out at the end, and it must detect and contain damage, so that one bad byte does not make the whole rest unusable.

The header fields are chosen so that the known gaps of older formats do not arise in the first place. MP3, for instance, has no field for the total number of samples or for the encoder's lead-in and lead-out; gapless playback of consecutive tracks was therefore only possible through later add-on tags. And a format without a method identifier can never carry more than the one codec it was built for. EVAS carries both from the start.

The stream header

All numeric fields are stored most significant byte first (big endian).

The duration follows without a pass as T=total samples/sample rateT = \text{total samples} / \text{sample rate} (dauerSekunden). The lead-in is meant for lossy methods whose filter bank sends samples ahead at the start (filter bank); the lossless encoder writes lead-in and lead-out as zero.

Convention in the framework: Stromkopf.lies checks in this order: remaining length, tag, CRC-16, and only then the version and the fields. The checksum deliberately comes before the interpretation, so that a corrupted byte does not turn into a nonsensical sample rate or sample count. The overall checksum over the samples is only taken over here; it is checked after decoding.

The frame

Every frame is self-contained. It starts with the sync word FF 5A, followed by the length of the body in bytes (32 bits), then the body, and finally a CRC-16 over everything from the sync word on. The body starts, bit-packed, with the frame number (32 bits), the number of samples in this frame (16 bits) and the stereo mode (8 bits); then come the subframes (a frame from the inside).

There is no bit reservoir and no back pointer. In MP3 a frame may put bits into free space of preceding frames; whoever starts in the middle of the stream or loses a frame then also loses parts of the following ones. In EVAS a frame depends on nothing but the header: the word length, the method and the nominal block size.

CRC-16 as frame protection

A CRC reads the data as a polynomial over GF(2) – addition is xor, without carry – and forms the remainder of division by a fixed generator polynomial. Here it is

G(x)=x16+x15+x2+1(0x8005⁡),R(x)=M(x)x16modG(x)G(x) = x^{16} + x^{15} + x^{2} + 1 \quad (\mathrm{0x8005}), \qquad R(x) = M(x)\, x^{16} \bmod G(x)

with initial value zero, no bit reflection and no final xor. Catalogues call this variant CRC-16/BUYPASS; its published check value over the ASCII characters “123456789” is FEE8. Because G(x)=(x+1)(x15+x+1)G(x) = (x+1)(x^{15}+x+1) contains the factor x+1x+1, the CRC detects every odd number of flipped bits, and because GG has degree 16, every error burst up to 16 bits long. The general computation with a worked division example is in the checksum theory of framework-compress.

The computation runs byte by byte over a table of 256 entries: entry ii is the remainder of i(x)⋅x16i(x) \cdot x^{16} modulo GG, i.e. the result of eight shift-and-xor steps on i⋅28i \cdot 2^{8}. Per byte, one whole division step of eight bits is thus done at once.

Pseudocode – Pruefsumme.crc16(data, from, to)

// once when the class is loaded
for i ← 0 to 255:
    w ← i ≪ 8
    8 times:  if bit 15 of w set:  w ← ((w ≪ 1) xor 0x8005) and 0xFFFF
              else:              w ← (w ≪ 1) and 0xFFFF
    TABLE[i] ← w

// per call
crc ← 0
for i ← from to to−1:
    crc ← ((crc ≪ 8) xor TABLE[((crc ≫ 8) xor data[i]) and 0xFF]) and 0xFFFF
return crc

The overall checksum FNV-1a

The CRC says whether a frame was corrupted in transit. Whether the encoder computed correctly it cannot say: a wrongly encoded frame has a correct CRC. For that the header carries a second sum over the samples themselves. The decoder recomputes it over what it reconstructed; if it matches, the round trip is proven. FNV-1a with 64 bits is used:

h0=CBF29CE484222325⁡,hj+1=(hj⊕oj)⋅100000001B3⁡mod264h_0 = \mathrm{CBF29CE484222325}, \qquad h_{j+1} = (h_j \oplus o_j) \cdot \mathrm{100000001B3} \bmod 2^{64}

The bytes ojo_j are the samples, interleaved – all channels one after the other for each time step – and each value with its four bytes starting from the least significant. The sum thus also depends on the channel order: swapped channels give a different sum.

Pseudocode – Pruefsumme.ueberWerte(channels)

h ← 0xCBF29CE484222325
for i ← 0 to length(channels[0]) − 1:       length taken from the first channel
    for each channel:
        w ← channel[i]
        for b ← 0 to 3:
            h ← h xor ((w ≫ 8b) and 0xFF)            unsigned shift
            h ← h · 0x100000001B3                   overflow intended, mod 2^64
return h
Convention in the framework: Deliberately not a cryptographic sum. The point is to detect errors in the codec itself, not to protect against deliberate tampering. Computed over no values at all, the sum is the initial value CBF29CE484222325.

Resynchronisation after damage

The decoder walks through the stream frame by frame. If it finds no sync word at the expected place or an impossible body length, it searches byte by byte for the next FF 5A. If a frame's CRC does not match, it stops in strict mode; in lenient mode it counts the frame as skipped and continues behind the frame's stated end.

Pseudocode – Dekodierer.dekodiere(stream)

header ← Stromkopf.lies(stream, 0)                    checks tag, CRC, version
check header.method ∈ {1, 2}
target ← zeros [channels][total];  reached ← 0;  pos ← 36
while pos + 6 ≤ length(stream) and reached < total:
    if u16(pos) ≠ 0xFF5A:
        pos ← next 0xFF5A from pos+1;  if none: stop;  continue
    L ← u32(pos+2);  end ← pos + 6 + L + 2
    if L ≤ 0 or end > length(stream):
        pos ← next 0xFF5A from pos+1;  if none: stop;  continue
    if crc16(stream, pos, end−2) ≠ u16(end−2):
        if strict:  exception “frame damaged”
        skipped ← skipped + 1;  pos ← end;  continue
    to ← liesRahmen(stream, pos+6, end−2)                 writes from number · nominal block size
    reached ← max(reached, to);  pos ← end
if reached < total and strict:  exception “stream too short”
if FNV-1a(target) ≠ header.checksum and strict:  exception “not lossless”
return target

Source: envisionz-framework-audiocodec/src/envisionz/audiocodec/Dekodierer.java, dekodiere and sucheSynchronwort.

A skipped frame leaves zeros in its place; all following frames land at their correct position, because the position follows from the frame number. An FF 5A that happens to occur inside a body can mislead the search but fails the CRC: for random data a 16-bit checksum matches with probability 2−162^{-16}.

How it is tested

  • Summenprobe (30 checks): CRC-16/BUYPASS over “123456789” is FEE8; over nothing zero; a single zero byte stays zero (that would differ for a reflected variant); A5 gives 03DE. The table computation is compared against a bit-by-bit computation without a table, for lengths 0 to 40 and for sections in the middle of the data – a wrongly built table is self-consistent and only shows up this way. Every single flipped bit of an 8-byte block changes the sum, as do two swapped bytes.
  • The same probe for FNV-1a: the initial value over nothing; one, two and five channels against a straightforwardly written version; swapped channels, interleaved versus concatenated, every single changed value, the highest byte and negative values change the sum.
  • Umlaufprobe: a corrupted byte in the header (byte 8) is detected; a corrupted byte in a frame leads to an abort in strict mode and to skipping in lenient mode, and the values from index 12,000 on are correct again afterwards.

Limits

  • Field widths: channel count and word length one byte each, nominal block size, lead-in and lead-out 16 bits each. The encoder allows block sizes from 16 to 65,535.
  • Total samples are stored with 64 bits in the header, but the decoder computes with int; it does not read more than 231−12^{31}-1 samples per channel.
  • Channels of unequal length (finding of the Summenprobe): ueberWerte takes the length from the first channel. A shorter second channel leads to an out-of-bounds access, a longer one is silently truncated. The encoder always delivers channels of equal length.
  • In lenient mode a non-matching overall checksum is not reported; only uebersprungeneRahmen() or a recomputation with pruefsummePasst tells.
  • A CRC-16 does not detect every corruption: for a random error pattern it still matches with probability 2−162^{-16}. In strict mode the overall checksum still catches such a case.
  • So far only methods 1 and 2 exist. The decoder rejects other identifiers; a lossy method with MDCT coefficients is not yet provided in the format.
