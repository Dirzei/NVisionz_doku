Prediction and stereo – linear prediction (LPC)

The computation behind Lpcpraediktor: the prediction model, the normal equations, autocorrelation with a Welch window, the Levinson-Durbin recursion, the order estimate, quantisation with the rounding error passed on, and the integer prediction that encoder and decoder compute identically bit for bit. With pseudocode following the source, numerical examples, probes and limits.

What it is about

The fixed predictors continue polynomials; their weights are fixed once and for all. A tone with few samples per period, a resonance, a sound with pronounced overtones – none of these is a polynomial. Linear prediction therefore determines the weights from the signal itself, anew for each subframe, so that the prediction error is as small as possible on average. The price: the weights have to go into the stream, and the decoder must compute exactly the same with them as the encoder.

This second condition is the delicate part. The weights are determined in floating point, then quantised to integers, and from then on both sides compute in integers only. If the encoder formed the prediction in floating point and the decoder in integers, they would drift apart and the stream would be lost from that point on.

The model and the normal equations

The predictor of order pp estimates each value as a weighted sum of its pp predecessors:

x^n=∑j=1pajxn−j,en=xn−x^n\hat{x}_n = \sum_{j=1}^{p} a_j\, x_{n-j}, \qquad e_n = x_n - \hat{x}_n

We seek the weights that minimise the error energy E=∑nen2E = \sum_n e_n^2. Setting the derivative with respect to each aia_i to zero gives a linear system. In the autocorrelation form – the sum runs over the whole block, continued with zeros outside – its entries depend only on the distance of the indices:

∑j=1pajr|i−j|=ri,i=1,…,p,rv=∑n=vN−1ynyn−v\sum_{j=1}^{p} a_j\, r_{|i-j|} = r_i, \quad i = 1,\dots,p, \qquad r_v = \sum_{n=v}^{N-1} y_n\, y_{n-v}

The matrix with entries r|i−j|r_{|i-j|} is a symmetric Toeplitz matrix. That is more than a nicety: for such systems there is the Levinson-Durbin recursion, which solves in p2p^2 instead of p3p^3 steps and delivers the solutions of all smaller orders along the way.

Autocorrelation with a Welch window

The autocorrelation is not formed over the raw values but over the windowed signal yn=wnxny_n = w_n x_n. Without a window the block edges would be a jump to zero, and the recursion would find weights that fit this artefact instead of the signal. The Welch window, a downward-open parabola, is used:

wn=1−(n−N−12N+12)2,n=0,…,N−1w_n = 1 - \left(\frac{n - \frac{N-1}{2}}{\frac{N+1}{2}}\right)^2, \qquad n = 0,\dots,N-1

It falls to a small positive value at the edges and is 1 in the middle. The window only affects the determination of the weights; the unwindowed signal is what gets predicted.

The Levinson-Durbin recursion

The recursion builds the solution of order oo from the solution of order o−1o-1. With E0=r0E_0 = r_0 and for o=1,2,…,po = 1, 2, \dots, p:

ko=ro−∑j=1o−1aj(o−1)ro−jEo−1k_o = \frac{r_o - \sum_{j=1}^{o-1} a_j^{(o-1)}\, r_{o-j}}{E_{o-1}}

ao(o)=ko,aj(o)=aj(o−1)−koao−j(o−1),j=1,…,o−1a_o^{(o)} = k_o, \qquad a_j^{(o)} = a_j^{(o-1)} - k_o\, a_{o-j}^{(o-1)}, \quad j = 1,\dots,o-1

Eo=Eo−1(1−ko2)E_o = E_{o-1}\,(1 - k_o^2)

The kok_o are called reflection coefficients. As long as |ko|<1|k_o| < 1, the residual energy EoE_o decreases with every order, and the corresponding synthesis filter 1/(1−∑ajz−j)1/(1 - \sum a_j z^{-j}) is stable. The residual energies themselves are the basis of the order choice: EoE_o tells how much energy is left in the error after oo weights.

Pseudocode – Lpcpraediktor.levinsonDurbin(r, pmax)

coeff[0] ← [];  error[0] ← r[0]
if r[0] ≤ 0:  all coefficients 0, all errors 0;  return
a ← zeros;  e ← r[0]
for o ← 1 to pmax:
    s ← r[o] − Σ_{j=1}^{o−1} a[j]·r[o−j]
    k ← (e = 0) ? 0 : s/e;   if k not finite: k ← 0
    k ← clamp(k, −0.999999, 0.999999)                    stability
    new[o] ← k;  new[j] ← a[j] − k·a[o−j]  for j = 1 … o−1
    a[1..o] ← new[1..o]
    e ← e·(1 − k²);   if e < 1e−12: e ← 1e−12
    error[o] ← e;  coeff[o] ← (a[1], …, a[o])            index 0 = most recent value

Source: envisionz-framework-audiocodec/src/envisionz/audiocodec/Lpcpraediktor.java, autokorrelation and levinsonDurbin.

Convention in the framework: Every reflection coefficient is limited to magnitude 0.999999, the residual energy from below to 10−1210^{-12}. Without these bounds a degenerate signal – such as a pure sine, predictable almost without error by two weights – would produce an unstable predictor or non-finite numbers. If the signal energy r0r_0 is not positive (silence), all weights are zero.

Choosing the order

More weights lower the error but cost space: qq bits per weight (precision) and one warm-up value of bb bits per order. For a residual with mean energy σ2\sigma^2 a good entropy coder needs roughly 12log2⁡σ2\tfrac12 \log_2 \sigma^2 bits per value plus a constant. The constant does not depend on the order and drops out of the comparison. This gives the estimate

K^(o)=(N−o)12log2⁡EoN+oq+ob,p^=arg⁡min1≤o<N⁡K^(o)\hat{K}(o) = (N - o)\,\frac{1}{2}\log_2 \frac{E_o}{N} + o\,q + o\,b, \qquad \hat{p} = \arg\min_{1 \le o < N} \hat{K}(o)

with Eo/NE_o/N bounded below by 10−910^{-9}. It hits the order of magnitude but not always the minimum: the residual energy measures the error of the windowed signal with unrounded weights, while what is coded is the unwindowed residual with rounded ones. The encoder therefore computes p^\hat p, p^±1\hat p \pm 1, p^±2\hat p \pm 2, 8, min⁡(16,pmax)\min(16, p_\text{max}) and pmaxp_\text{max} exactly and takes the order with the truly smallest bit cost (subframe choice).

The figure shows the typical course: up to order 4 the curve falls steeply (from 44,778 bits at order 1 to 29,095 at order 4), then it flattens, and beyond the minimum the cost rises again, because every further weight costs 15 + 17 = 32 bits and hardly brings anything. This agrees with the measurement in the module documentation: on generated material LPC gained about two percent over the fixed predictors, most of it already up to order 8 (table in the overview).

Quantisation with the rounding error passed on

The weights are transmitted as integers cjc_j with a common denominator 2s2^s, aj≈cj/2sa_j \approx c_j / 2^s. The shift ss is chosen so that the largest weight just uses the word length qq:

s=q−2−⌊log2⁡maxj⁡|aj|⌋,0≤s≤31s = q - 2 - \left\lfloor \log_2 \max_j |a_j| \right\rfloor, \qquad 0 \le s \le 31

Then the weights are rounded in order, and the rounding error passes on to the next weight:

cj=round⁡(aj2s+δj−1),δj=aj2s+δj−1−cj,δ0=0c_j = \operatorname{round}\!\left(a_j\, 2^s + \delta_{j-1}\right), \qquad \delta_j = a_j\, 2^s + \delta_{j-1} - c_j, \qquad \delta_0 = 0

Each cjc_j is additionally clamped to the two's-complement range [−2q−1,2q−1−1][-2^{q-1}, 2^{q-1}-1]. Passing the error on prevents many roundings in the same direction from adding up to a systematic offset of the prediction: as long as nothing is clamped, every partial sum ∑j≤icj\sum_{j \le i} c_j deviates from the exact partial sum by at most half a unit, however long the series. Rounded individually, the errors could add up to i/2i/2.

Pseudocode – Lpcpraediktor.quantisiere(a, q)

g ← max |a[j]|
if g ≤ 0 or not finite:  return (zeros, s = 0)
limit ← 2^(q−1) − 1
s ← q − 1 − ⌊log2 g⌋ − 1;   s ← clamp(s, 0, 31)
f ← 0
for j ← 0 to p−1:
    f ← f + a[j]·2^s
    c[j] ← clamp(round(f), −limit−1, limit)
    f ← f − c[j]                                         remainder carries on
return (c, s, q)

The integer prediction

From here on there are no floating-point numbers. The prediction is the sum of the products, arithmetically shifted right by ss places – that is division by 2s2^s with rounding towards the smaller integer, also for negative sums:

x^n=⌊2−s∑j=0p−1cjxn−1−j⌋\hat{x}_n = \left\lfloor 2^{-s} \sum_{j=0}^{p-1} c_j\, x_{n-1-j} \right\rfloor

Pseudocode – residuum and ruecktransformiere

function prediction(w, i, p, c, s):
    sum ← 0                                              long, 64 bits
    for j ← 0 to p−1:  sum ← sum + c[j]·w[i−1−j]
    return sum ≫ s                                      arithmetic

function residuum(values, p, c, s):
    for i ← 0 to N−p−1:  rest[i] ← values[i+p] − prediction(values, i+p, p, c, s)

function ruecktransformiere(values, rest, p, c, s):   values[0..p−1] = warm-up
    for i ← p to N−1:  values[i] ← rest[i−p] + prediction(values, i, p, c, s)

Source: Lpcpraediktor.java, quantisiere, residuum, ruecktransformiere. Both directions live in the same class and use the same private function vorhersage.

Convention in the framework: The sum runs in long. At order 32, 25-bit word length and 15-bit weights it reaches up to 32⋅214⋅224=24332 \cdot 2^{14} \cdot 2^{24} = 2^{43} and clearly exceeds the 32-bit limit. Defaults: highest order 32 (MAX_ORDNUNG), precision 15 bits (VORGABE_PRAEZISION, adjustable 2 to 16), shift 0 to 31 (MAX_VERSCHIEBUNG). The subframe header holds order − 1 (5 bits), precision − 1 (4 bits) and shift (5 bits).

How it is tested

The Lpcprobe (12 checks) with anchors in closed form:

  • Sine (f=0.05f = 0.05, A=20,000A = 20{,}000): a1a_1 hits 2cos⁡ω2\cos\omega and a2a_2 hits −1 within 0.02; quantised to 15 bits the order-2 residual stays below 60 (measured: at most 2); the residual energy decreases monotonically with the order.
  • White noise: after order 16 more than 95 % of the energy remains (measured 0.9984).
  • AR(1) signal with a=0.95a = 0.95: the first-order coefficient hits aa within 0.02 (measured 0.9453), the residual energy hits 1−a2=0.09751 - a^2 = 0.0975 within 0.02 (measured 0.1062).
  • Quantisation: the weights of the example above to within 0.001; all fit the precision; all zeros give all zeros.
  • Reversibility for every order 1 to 32 and the shifts 0, 7, 15 and 31 with random weights uniformly distributed in ±16,000: residual and reconstruction return the signal bit-exactly.
  • Overflow edge case: order 32, values alternating 224−12^{24}-1 and −224-2^{24}, all weights 214−12^{14}-1, shift 14 – the round trip stays bit-exact.

Limits

  • Computing time grows with N⋅pmaxN \cdot p_\text{max} for the autocorrelation and with the number of candidates computed exactly (up to eight residuals and Rice plans per subframe).
  • The sum in long is exact, but the residual is stored as int. With very large weights and values the difference wraps around, identically on both sides – the round trip stays correct, but such a residual is expensive and is not chosen.
  • The order estimate can be off; only the candidates are computed exactly, not all orders.
  • Short subframes with N≤pmax+1N \le p_\text{max} + 1 get no LPC.
  • The gain depends on the material. On the generated material of the module documentation it was about two percent over the fixed predictors; more is expected on real music, which so far is documented only through the batch run.
