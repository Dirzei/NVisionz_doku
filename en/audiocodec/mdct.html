MDCT and filter bank – the transform and its aliasing

The computation behind Mdct: definition of the modified discrete cosine transform, why its inverse alone does not return the signal, how the time-domain aliasing cancels on overlap-add (TDAC), and the fast path via folding, DCT-IV and a complex FFT of half length. With derivation, pseudocode following the source, measurements from the probe and limits. Windows and block switching are on the filter bank page.

What it is about

The lossless encoder predicts values in the time domain. A lossy codec works differently: it splits the signal into frequency components, because hearing resolves frequencies with varying precision and misses quiet components next to loud ones. There, in the frequency domain, coarse quantisation will later take place. The transform for this has to do four things: it must concentrate the energy in few coefficients, it must not produce more coefficients than samples come in, it must not create edges at the block boundaries, and without quantisation the way back must return the signal exactly.

The MDCT does this. Each block covers 2M2M samples and advances by MM; the blocks thus overlap by half, and still only MM coefficients are produced per block. The transform is critically sampled. That is only possible because it loses information in every single block – in a form that the neighbouring block exactly compensates.

Definition

From 2M2M time samples x0,…,x2M−1x_0, \dots, x_{2M-1}, MM coefficients are formed:

Xk=∑n=02M−1xncos⁡[πM(n+12+M2)(k+12)],k=0,…,M−1X_k = \sum_{n=0}^{2M-1} x_n \cos\!\left[\frac{\pi}{M}\left(n + \frac12 + \frac{M}{2}\right)\left(k + \frac12\right)\right], \qquad k = 0, \dots, M-1

The inverse has the same kernel and the factor 1/M1/M:

yn=1M∑k=0M−1Xkcos⁡[πM(n+12+M2)(k+12)],n=0,…,2M−1y_n = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{k=0}^{M-1} X_k \cos\!\left[\frac{\pi}{M}\left(n + \frac12 + \frac{M}{2}\right)\left(k + \frac12\right)\right], \qquad n = 0, \dots, 2M-1

The shifts by 12\tfrac12 in time and frequency make the basis functions symmetric or antisymmetric at both block ends; the additional shift by M/2M/2 in time places the points of symmetry exactly on the quarter boundaries of the block. The coefficient XkX_k belongs to the frequency (k+12)(k + \tfrac12) times half the sample rate divided by MM.

The time-domain aliasing

Dividing the block into four quarters (a,b,c,d)(a, b, c, d) of M/2M/2 values each and writing '' for reversal of the order, the inverse of a single block does not give (a,b,c,d)(a, b, c, d) but

y=12(a−b',b−a',c+d',d+c')y = \frac12\,\big(a - b',\; b - a',\; c + d',\; d + c'\big)

The first half contains the signal minus its mirror image, the second half the signal plus its mirror image. With ss for the second half (c,d)(c, d) as a sequence of MM values and s˜j=sM−1−j\tilde{s}_j = s_{M-1-j} for its mirror image: the second half of the inverse is 12(s+s˜)\tfrac12(s + \tilde{s}), and the first half of the inverse of a block over the same values would be 12(s−s˜)\tfrac12(s - \tilde{s}). That is the time-domain aliasing; the Mdctprobe checks exactly this form.

How it cancels

Two consecutive blocks cover MM common values sjs_j: for block tt they are the second half, for block t+1t+1 the first. Both blocks are multiplied by a window before the transform and once more after the inverse. With the falling slope fjf_j of block tt and the rising slope gjg_j of block t+1t+1, the overlap-add gives

zj=fj(fjsj+fM−1−jsM−1−j)2+gj(gjsj−gM−1−jsM−1−j)2z_j = \frac{f_j\,(f_j s_j + f_{M-1-j}\,s_{M-1-j})}{2} + \frac{g_j\,(g_j s_j - g_{M-1-j}\,s_{M-1-j})}{2}

Ordered by sjs_j and sM−1−js_{M-1-j}:

zj=fj2+gj22sj+fjfM−1−j−gjgM−1−j2sM−1−jz_j = \frac{f_j^2 + g_j^2}{2}\, s_j + \frac{f_j f_{M-1-j} - g_j g_{M-1-j}}{2}\, s_{M-1-j}

If the falling slope is the mirror image of the rising one, fj=gM−1−jf_j = g_{M-1-j}, the second fraction vanishes – the aliasing cancels. If in addition the Princen-Bradley condition

gj2+gM−1−j2=1,g_j^2 + g_{M-1-j}^2 = 1,

holds, then fj2+gj2=1f_j^2 + g_j^2 = 1 and zj=sj/2z_j = s_j / 2. This cancellation is called TDAC (time domain aliasing cancellation). What remains is the factor 12\tfrac12.

Convention in the framework: Mdct is the bare definition with the factor 1/M1/M in the inverse and without a window. The factor 2 that turns s/2s/2 back into ss is carried by Fensterbank.synthese. AAC carries it in the forward transform; here Mdct thus remains the definition without an extra factor.

The fast path

Computed directly, the MDCT costs 2M22M^2 multiplications, about two million per block at M=1024M = 1024. The path in Mdct needs one FFT of length M/2M/2.

1. Folding. The 2M2M values are folded into MM values. With the quarters (a,b,c,d)(a, b, c, d):

u=(−c'−d,a−b')u = \big(-c' - d,\; a - b'\big)

Then the MDCT of xx equals the DCT-IV of uu:

Xk=∑n=0M−1uncos⁡[πM(n+12)(k+12)]X_k = \sum_{n=0}^{M-1} u_n \cos\!\left[\frac{\pi}{M}\left(n + \frac12\right)\left(k + \frac12\right)\right]

2. DCT-IV via a complex FFT of length M/2M/2. Pairs of real values become one complex number, rotated, transformed and rotated again:

zn=(u2n+iuM−1−2n)e−iπ(n+1/8)/M,Z=FFTM/2⁡(z)z_n = \big(u_{2n} + i\,u_{M-1-2n}\big)\, e^{-i\pi(n + 1/8)/M}, \qquad Z = \mathrm{FFT}_{M/2}(z)

wk=Zke−iπ(k+1/8)/M,X2k=Re⁡wk,XM−1−2k=−Im⁡wkw_k = Z_k\, e^{-i\pi(k + 1/8)/M}, \qquad X_{2k} = \mathrm{Re}\,w_k, \qquad X_{M-1-2k} = -\mathrm{Im}\,w_k

3. Inverse. The DCT-IV is its own inverse up to the factor 2/M2/M. The inverse therefore computes the same DCT-IV and unfolds with u1=u0…M/2−1u_1 = u_{0 \dots M/2-1} and u2=uM/2…M−1u_2 = u_{M/2 \dots M-1}:

y=1M(u2,−u2',−u1',−u1)y = \frac{1}{M}\,\big(u_2,\; -u_2',\; -u_1',\; -u_1\big)

The factor 12\tfrac12 of the unfolding and 2/M2/M of the DCT-IV together give 1/M1/M.

Pseudocode – Mdct.vorwaerts and dct4

// once in the constructor: M power of two ≥ 4, h ← M/2
preCos[n], preSin[n] ← cos, sin(π(n + 1/8)/M)    for n = 0 … h−1
fftCos[i], fftSin[i] ← cos, sin(2πi/h)           for i = 0 … h/2−1
reverse[i] ← bit-reversed index of i (log2 h bits)

function vorwaerts(x, from, out, at):
    for n ← 0 to h−1:
        u[n]   ← −x[from+M+h−1−n] − x[from+M+h+n]        −c′ − d
        u[h+n] ←  x[from+n] − x[from+M−1−n]              a − b′
    dct4(u);  out[at … at+M−1] ← u

function dct4(v):                                      in place, without normalisation
    for n ← 0 to h−1:                                   pre-rotation, stored bit-reversed
        (r, i) ← (v[2n], v[M−1−2n])
        re[reverse[n]] ← r·preCos[n] + i·preSin[n]
        im[reverse[n]] ← i·preCos[n] − r·preSin[n]
    fft()                                                radix 2, negative exponent
    for k ← 0 to h−1:                                   post-rotation
        r ← re[k]·preCos[k] + im[k]·preSin[k]
        i ← im[k]·preCos[k] − re[k]·preSin[k]
        v[2k] ← r;  v[M−1−2k] ← −i

function rueckwaerts(coeff, from, y):
    u ← coeff[from … from+M−1];  dct4(u)
    for n ← 0 to h−1:
        y[n] ← u[h+n]/M;       y[h+n] ← −u[M−1−n]/M
        y[M+n] ← −u[h−1−n]/M;  y[M+h+n] ← −u[n]/M

Source: envisionz-framework-audiocodec/src/envisionz/audiocodec/Mdct.java. The FFT is a radix-2 loop of its own in the class with precomputed twiddle factors; the work arrays are reused. For the FFT itself see framework-math.

Numbers

  • Frequency position. A cosine of amplitude 1000 exactly on the centre of band k0=37k_0 = 37 of a filter bank with M=256M = 256, one long frame with sine window: the largest coefficient is X37X_{37}, and 99.1 % of the energy lies in bands 36 to 38.
  • Aliasing. For M=64M = 64 and integer noise in ±1000, the inverse of one block matches the form 12(a−b',b−a',c+d',d+c')\tfrac12(a-b', b-a', c+d', d+c') to 10−910^{-9} but deviates from the signal itself by up to 886.
  • Figure above. M=32M = 32, input signal round⁡(1000sin⁡(2πn/99.2)+300)\mathrm{round}(1000 \sin(2\pi n / 99.2) + 300); shown are the windowed, doubled inverses of the frames on [M,3M)[M, 3M) and [2M,4M)[2M, 4M) and their sum on [2M,3M)[2M, 3M).

How it is tested

The Mdctprobe (90 checks) tests the transform against anchors that do not stem from its own computation path:

  • Against the definition: forward and inverse for M=4,8,16,128,1024M = 4, 8, 16, 128, 1024 against the directly computed sum, relative deviation below 10−1110^{-11} (measured at most 4.1⋅10−134.1 \cdot 10^{-13} at M=1024M = 1024). A size that is not a power of two (12) is rejected.
  • Shape of the aliasing as above, to 10−910^{-9}, and proof that a single block is indeed not the signal (deviation above 10).
  • Frequency position: band 37 and more than 90 % of the energy in k0±1k_0 \pm 1.

The checks of the windows, sequences and the bit-exact round trip are on the filter bank page.

Limits

  • MM must be a power of two from 4 upwards.
  • Mdct is not thread-safe; the work arrays are reused on every call. One instance per thread.
  • Computation is in double. The inverse matches the signal up to rounding errors, not exactly; it becomes bit-exact only by rounding to integers (filter bank).
  • There is no quantiser yet and no method in the stream format that transmits coefficients.
