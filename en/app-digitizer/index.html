envisionz-app-digitizer

Turn families of curves in chart images into numbers

Overview of the app, as of 29 September 2026. The Image Digitizer reads charts from image files, calibrates the axes, filters the image, follows the curves, resamples them onto a common grid, fits them and writes tables of numbers.

83Java source files
9manual pages and 3 help pages, each in German and English
74parameters in the search space of the automatic tuning
887points of the reference against which runs are scored
5framework modules underneath

What the app is for

Older performance maps and curve sheets often exist only as a scan or a graphic, without numbers. Anyone who needs them in a calculation has to read them off. By hand that works for one curve, but not for a sheet with thirty or more closely spaced curves that cross, are interrupted by labels and grid lines, and are noisy in the scan.

The app guides this in stages: load the image, calibrate the axes, clean the image, follow the curves, bring them onto a common grid, fit them, output a table. Each stage shows its result in the image, so it can be checked and adjusted before the next one runs. It opens PNG, JPEG, BMP and GIF files.

What it can do

Calibrating the axes

From pixel to value.

  • Two points per axis with a typed-in value, linear or logarithmic; the position is displayed live while setting.
  • Automatic calibration by character recognition of the axis labels, when the recognition module is available.
  • The image can be rotated; the rotation is saved.
  • Additional axes for cruise charts: a second, nonlinear scale (calibrated airspeed, CAS) on the Mach axis, with pressure altitude and outside temperature; from these every point yields Mach, CAS and true airspeed.

Cleaning the image

A chain of filters with a non-destructive preview.

  • Thresholds (global, by brightness, adaptive), despeckling, median, bilateral filter, Perona-Malik and total variation, morphological operations and top-hat.
  • Removal of grid lines and screen patterns in the frequency domain (notch filter, grid layer, peak layer, MCA decomposition), colourising and colour removal.
  • The parameter fields are generated from the descriptions of the filters, with a help text per parameter.
  • Seeds (starting points) and keep-out zones survive filtering; "Original" resets the filter.

Following the curves

Two routes, depending on the kind of chart; the type is proposed from the colour histogram when the image is loaded.

  • Monochrome scan: a whole family of curves is followed at the same time, without curves jumping over one another. The tracer follows the direction of the stroke structure, protects itself at kinks and crossings, bridges gaps and avoids keep-out zones. Starting points are set by hand or from a start line.
  • Colour chart: the series colours are recognised from the legend, and the points are collected per colour: as column mean (lines), as all pixels (point cloud) or as marker centres (one point per symbol).
  • Tracing and colour extraction run in the background; the interface stays usable.

Resampling, fitting, output

From a pixel trace to a table.

  • Reduction by Douglas-Peucker; resampling onto a common grid by arc length or as a fan of rays around a point defined by helper lines. If the helper lines do not meet in a useful way, the fan falls back to parallel cross lines.
  • Fit: a parametric cubic spline x(t), y(t) or a polar fit r(φ) around the fan centre.
  • Output next to the image: pixel values (_raw.csv), fan values (_fan.csv), physical values via the calibration (_phys.csv).
  • Reading off at cross lines: the intersections of every curve with chosen cross lines (for example lines of best range or best endurance), plus a curve value per curve from a first value and a step (_ablesung.csv).
  • Check view: the extracted points over the original image.

Automatic tuning

Let filter, starting points and tracing be searched together, against a reference.

  • Headless runs score a trace against a verified reference: deviation in pixels, curve end in rays of the fan, jumps onto foreign curves.
  • The evolutionary search of the search module tunes all 74 parameters together; a quality measure learned from pairwise comparisons replaces a hand-designed objective function.
  • Parameter sets that were found can be loaded as presets into the filter and tracing cards; the search workbench app-learn finds the scorer through a service.

Data and formats

  • Reads: PNG, JPEG, BMP, GIF; transparent images are flattened to white so that transparency is not read as ink.
  • Writes: CSV next to the image (pixel, fan, physical, reading) and, per image, a work file <image>.digi.json with calibration, additional axes, keep-out zones, helper lines, chart type and rotation. It saves itself when the image is switched and when the app closes; a damaged file is rejected with the file name and the reason.
  • Reference and parameter sets: a CSV reference, and parameter sets as plain key=value files.
  • Reset: a rail button clears everything, optionally including the work file.

What it builds on

  • framework-api: registration as an app and session state (folder and current image).
  • framework-swing: tool rail with cards, chart options, zoom with reset to the typed limits.
  • framework-chart: line chart and table of the extracted points.
  • framework-math: the fast Fourier transform for grid and screen filters, and the cubic spline.
  • framework-evolve: scorer interface, evolutionary search and preference model.
  • framework-neural: the character recognition for axis labels, optionally switched on.

The core (filters, tracing, fitting, character recognition, headless runs) deliberately has no reference to the framework and can also be started as a program of its own; only the connection to the application, the chart view and the bridge to the search reach into the framework.

State and limits

The route is wired end to end for both chart types, from the image to the tables. Finished are calibration with character recognition and additional axes, the filter chain, tracing and colour extraction, fan and polar fit, all four outputs, presets from the search, and a manual of nine pages next to three help pages, each in German and English. Filtering and search were developed and tested on one scanned curve sheet; whether the settings found carry over to other sheets is not established.

The interface is tested headless in several programs (loading and calibrating, cruise axes, reading at cross lines, fan, corrupted work files). The tracing stage is scored against the reference with figures, not judged by eye.

Limits: Following cross lines and a shortest-path method for special curves exist as building blocks but are not hooked into the interface; there, a cross line is a series like any other. A management of series with manual clicking of single points, naming of the legend entries and further development of the character recognition are still to come.
