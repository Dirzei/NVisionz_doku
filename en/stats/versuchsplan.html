Design of experiments and response surfaces – background

The computation behind Plan and Antwortflaeche: factorial and fractional factorial designs, defining relation, aliasing and resolution, central composite and Box-Behnken designs, Latin hypercube, quadratic response surface with stationary point and canonical form.

What it is about

Whoever wants to know how a response depends on several factors does not vary them one after another but according to a design: few, well-distributed runs from which main effects, interactions and curvature can be estimated. The factors are coded (−1, 0, +1).

Factorial and fractional factorial

A full factorial design 2k2^k contains all combinations of the levels ±1, in standard order (the first factor changes fastest); its columns are pairwise orthogonal. A fractional factorial design 2k−p2^{k-p} runs the full combinations of the first k−pk-p factors and sets the others via generators, e.g. D=ABCD = ABC. The generators form the defining relation (for D=ABCD = ABC: I=ABCDI = ABCD); each of its words multiplied by an effect gives the effects aliased with it (AB=CDAB = CD). The resolution is the length of the shortest word: at resolution III main effects are aliased with two-factor interactions, at IV no longer.

Computing with words

Since every column contains only ±1, A⋅A=IA\cdot A = I: an effect is determined by the set of its letters, and the product of two words is their symmetric difference. The code stores a word as a bit mask and multiplies by exclusive or. From pp generators w1,…,wpw_1, \dots, w_p (for D=ABCD = ABC the word ABCDABCD) all 2p−12^p - 1 non-empty products arise – that is the defining relation; an effect ee is aliased with every e⋅we \cdot w, 2p2^p effects per alias chain in total. The resolution is the smallest word length: a word of length RR aliases an effect of order aa with one of order ≥R−a\ge R - a.

I=w1=w2=w1w2=…,e=ew1=ew2=…,R=minw≠I⁡|w|.I = w_1 = w_2 = w_1w_2 = \dots, \qquad e \;=\; e\,w_1 \;=\; e\,w_2 \;=\; \dots, \qquad R = \min_{w \ne I} |w|.

For the 25−22^{5-2} with D=ABD = AB, E=ACE = AC the words are ABDABD, ACEACE and their product BCDEBCDE; length 3 means resolution III, and AA is aliased with BDBD, CECE and ABCDEABCDE.

Designs for response surfaces

  • Central composite: cube, 2k2k axial points at distance α\alpha and centre points. With α=(2k)1/4\alpha = (2^k)^{1/4} the design is rotatable: the prediction variance depends only on the distance from the centre. Optionally face-centred (α=1\alpha = 1).
  • Box-Behnken: for each pair of factors the four combinations ±1 with the others at 0, plus the centre; all points on a sphere, no corners.
  • Latin hypercube: nn points, per factor exactly one in each of the nn strata; optionally the best of several draws by the smallest minimum distance (maximin).

Why α=(2k)1/4\alpha = (2^k)^{1/4}. A second-order design is rotatable when its moments are invariant under rotations; for a symmetric design this comes down to ∑uxui4=3∑uxui2xuj2\sum_u x_{ui}^4 = 3\sum_u x_{ui}^2x_{uj}^2. The cube with F=2kF = 2^k points contributes FF to both sums, the axial points 2α42\alpha^4 to the left one only. From F+2α4=3FF + 2\alpha^4 = 3F follows α4=F\alpha^4 = F.

Box-Behnken has 4(k2)=2k(k−1)4\binom k2 = 2k(k - 1) edge points plus the centre points, so for k=3k = 3 12 + 3 = 15 runs against 20 in the central composite design; every edge point is at distance 2\sqrt2 from the centre.

Hypercube. For each factor a random permutation π\pi of 0,…,n−10, \dots, n - 1 is drawn and xij=(πj(i)+U)/nx_{ij} = (\pi_j(i) + U)/n is set; so exactly one point lies in each stratum [s/n,(s+1)/n)[s/n, (s + 1)/n). The maximin selection compares all pairs and thus costs O(zn2k)O(z\,n^2k) for zz draws.

Quadratic response surface

y=b0+∑ibixi+∑ibiixi2+∑i<jbijxixj=b0+xTb+xTBxy = b_0 + \sum_i b_i x_i + \sum_i b_{ii} x_i^2 + \sum_{i<j} b_{ij} x_i x_j = b_0 + x^T b + x^T B x

is fitted by least squares (QR). The stationary point is xs=−12B−1bx_s = -\frac12 B^{-1}b; the eigenvalues of BB give the canonical form: all negative – maximum, all positive – minimum, mixed – saddle.

Derivation. In the matrix form biib_{ii} sits on the diagonal of BB and bij/2b_{ij}/2 beside it – that is why the code halves the fitted interaction coefficients. The gradient is b+2Bxb + 2Bx; setting it to zero gives xsx_s. With the eigenvectors PP of BB and x=xs+Pwx = x_s + Pw,

y=ys+∑iλiwi2,y = y_s + \sum_i \lambda_i w_i^2,

the canonical form: a small |λi||\lambda_i| means a flat ridge along which one can move without loss. The model has 1+2k+(k2)1 + 2k + \binom k2 coefficients, and the design needs at least as many runs; a 2k2^k alone is not enough, because the columns xi2x_i^2 are all equal to 1 there. The coefficient of determination is R2=1−SSE⁡/SST⁡R^2 = 1 - \mathrm{SSE}/\mathrm{SST}.

What follows. If xsx_s lies far outside the experimental region, it is an extrapolation; one then moves on along the eigendirection with the largest |λ||\lambda| and plans anew.

How it is checked

  • 232^3 balanced and orthogonal; 24−12^{4-1} with D=ABCD = ABC: I=ABCDI = ABCD, resolution IV, AB=CDAB = CD; 25−22^{5-2} with D=ABD = AB, E=ACE = AC: resolution III, A=BD=CE=ABCDEA = BD = CE = ABCDE (as in Montgomery).
  • Central composite k=3k = 3: α=1.6818\alpha = 1.6818, prediction variance equal on a sphere (1e-12), with α=1.5\alpha = 1.5 not; Box-Behnken k=3k = 3 on the sphere r=2r = \sqrt2.
  • Hypercube 20 × 4: each stratum exactly once; the best of 50 draws has the larger minimum distance.
  • Quadratic function recovered exactly from a central composite design, stationary point and eigenvalues by hand.

Limits

Two-level factors for the factorial designs; Box-Behnken for k=6,7k = 6, 7 in the simple pairwise construction (not the tables of the literature). No D-optimal designs.
