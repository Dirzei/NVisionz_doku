State-space models and ARIMA – background

The computation behind Kalman and Arima: the linear Gaussian state-space model, Kalman filter with likelihood from the innovations and missing values, Rauch-Tung-Striebel smoother; ARIMA in Harvey's state form with exact likelihood and forecast via the ψ weights.

What it is about

A series yty_t is viewed as a noisy observation of a hidden state xtx_t that propagates linearly:

xt+1=Txt+wt,wt∼N(0,Q),yt=Hxt+vt,vt∼N(0,R),x1∼N(a1,P1).x_{t+1} = T x_t + w_t,\ w_t \sim N(0, Q), \qquad y_t = H x_t + v_t,\ v_t \sim N(0, R), \qquad x_1 \sim N(a_1, P_1).

The Kalman filter estimates xtx_t from the values up to tt, the smoother from all values. Because the innovations are independent and normal, the filter delivers the exact likelihood on the side – and with it the estimation of ARIMA models, which can be written as state-space models.

Filter, likelihood, smoother

Per step with ata_t, PtP_t as prediction:

vt=yt−Hat,Ft=HPtHT+R,Kt=PtHT/Ft,at|t=at+Ktvt,Pt|t=Pt−KtFtKtT,v_t = y_t - H a_t,\quad F_t = H P_t H^T + R,\quad K_t = P_t H^T/F_t,\quad a_{t|t} = a_t + K_t v_t,\quad P_{t|t} = P_t - K_t F_t K_t^T,

then at+1=Tat|ta_{t+1} = T a_{t|t}, Pt+1=TPt|tTT+QP_{t+1} = T P_{t|t} T^T + Q. The log-likelihood is −12∑t(log⁡2π+log⁡Ft+vt2/Ft)-\frac12\sum_t(\log 2\pi + \log F_t + v_t^2/F_t). A missing value (NaN) skips the update and contributes nothing to the likelihood. The Rauch-Tung-Striebel smoother runs backwards: Jt=Pt|tTTPt+1−1J_t = P_{t|t}T^T P_{t+1}^{-1}, x^t=at|t+Jt(x^t+1−at+1)\hat x_t = a_{t|t} + J_t(\hat x_{t+1} - a_{t+1}).

Derivation. Given the values up to t−1t-1, state and new observation are jointly normal with Cov⁡(xt,yt)=PtHT\operatorname{Cov}(x_t, y_t) = P_tH^T and Var⁡yt=Ft\operatorname{Var}y_t = F_t. The conditional normal distribution of xtx_t given yty_t is exactly the update above: mean at+PtHTFt−1vta_t + P_tH^TF_t^{-1}v_t, covariance Pt−PtHTFt−1HPtP_t - P_tH^TF_t^{-1}HP_t. The innovations vtv_t are the one-step prediction errors; they are independent with variance FtF_t, and the joint density factors into their product (prediction error decomposition):

log⁡p(y1,…,yn)=∑tlog⁡p(yt∣y1..t−1)=−12∑t(log⁡2π+log⁡Ft+vt2Ft).\log p(y_1, \dots, y_n) = \sum_t \log p(y_t \mid y_{1..t-1}) = -\tfrac12\sum_t\Bigl(\log 2\pi + \log F_t + \frac{v_t^2}{F_t}\Bigr).

The exact likelihood therefore costs O(nr3)O(n\,r^3) instead of O(n3)O(n^3) for the dense normal density. If yty_t is missing, the conditional distribution is the unconditional one – the update is skipped, and the likelihood is that of the remaining values. The smoother follows from the same computation backwards: JtJ_t is the regression coefficient of xtx_t on xt+1x_{t+1} given the values up to tt.

ARIMA in state form

ARIMA(p,d,qp, d, q) with (1−ϕ1B−…)(1−B)dyt=(1+θ1B+…)et(1 - \phi_1 B - \dots)(1 - B)^d y_t = (1 + \theta_1 B + \dots)e_t: the dd-fold differences are taken first, the rest is ARMA in Harvey's form with r=max⁡(p,q+1)r = \max(p, q+1) states. The initial covariance is the stationary solution of the Lyapunov equation P=TPTT+QP = T P T^T + Q; σ2\sigma^2 is concentrated out of the likelihood, and L-BFGS maximises the remaining parameters. The forecast over hh steps has variance σ2∑j<hψj2\sigma^2\sum_{j<h}\psi_j^2 with the ψ weights ψ(B)=θ(B)/(ϕ(B)(1−B)d)\psi(B) = \theta(B)/\bigl(\phi(B)(1-B)^d\bigr).

State form. With r=max⁡(p,q+1)r = \max(p, q+1), ϕi=0\phi_i = 0 for i>pi > p and θj=0\theta_j = 0 for j>qj > q,

T=(ϕ11⋮⋱ϕr−11ϕr0⋯0),Q=σ2ggT,g=(1,θ1,…,θr−1)T,H=(1,0,…,0),R=0.T = \begin{pmatrix} \phi_1 & 1 & & \\ \vdots & & \ddots & \\ \phi_{r-1} & & & 1 \\ \phi_r & 0 & \cdots & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad Q = \sigma^2 gg^T,\ g = (1, \theta_1, \dots, \theta_{r-1})^T, \qquad H = (1, 0, \dots, 0), \quad R = 0 .

The stationary covariance solves P=TPTT+QP = TPT^T + Q; vectorised this is (I−T⊗T)vec⁡P=vec⁡Q(I - T \otimes T)\operatorname{vec}P = \operatorname{vec}Q, a linear system of size r2r^2. For ARMA(1,1) it gives Var⁡y=σ2(1+2ϕθ+θ2)/(1−ϕ2)\operatorname{Var}y = \sigma^2(1 + 2\phi\theta + \theta^2)/(1 - \phi^2), covariance σ2θ\sigma^2\theta and Var⁡=σ2θ2\operatorname{Var} = \sigma^2\theta^2 for the second state.

A unique positive semidefinite solution exists exactly when all eigenvalues of TT lie inside the unit circle; then P=∑j≥0TjQ(TT)jP = \sum_{j\ge0} T^j Q (T^T)^j. The module checks this on the eigenvalues themselves (Francis QR from framework-math) and rejects spectral radii ≥1\ge 1.

Concentrated likelihood. Running the filter with σ2=1\sigma^2 = 1, all FtF_t scale with σ2\sigma^2, the vtv_t do not. The derivative with respect to σ2\sigma^2 vanishes at σ^2=1n∑tvt2/Ft\hat\sigma^2 = \frac1n\sum_t v_t^2/F_t; substituted, there remains

log⁡Lc=−n2(log⁡2πσ^2+1)−12∑tlog⁡Ft.\log L_c = -\tfrac n2\bigl(\log 2\pi\hat\sigma^2 + 1\bigr) - \tfrac12\sum_t\log F_t .

Forecast. The ψj\psi_j follow from ϕ*(B)ψ(B)=θ(B)\phi^*(B)\psi(B) = \theta(B) with ϕ*(B)=ϕ(B)(1−B)d\phi^*(B) = \phi(B)(1 - B)^d by comparing coefficients: ψ0=1\psi_0 = 1, ψj=θj−∑i≥1ϕi*ψj−i\psi_j = \theta_j - \sum_{i\ge1}\phi^*_i\psi_{j-i}. For ARMA(1,1), ψj=(ϕ+θ)ϕj−1\psi_j = (\phi + \theta)\phi^{j-1}; for ARIMA(1,1,0), ψj=(1−ϕj+1)/(1−ϕ)\psi_j = (1 - \phi^{j+1})/(1 - \phi); for ARIMA(0,2,0), ψj=j+1\psi_j = j + 1 and hence the variances 1,5,14σ21, 5, 14\,\sigma^2. The forecast mean comes from the filter for the differenced series and is summed up dd times.

What follows. ARIMA(0,1,1) forecasts exactly like exponential smoothing with α=1+θ\alpha = 1 + \theta; with d≥1d \ge 1 the forecast variance grows without bound, whereas a stationary model tends to Var⁡y\operatorname{Var}y. The ψ variance treats the past disturbances as known; with MA parts and short series the exact filter variance is larger by the uncertainty of the last state. The uncertainty of the estimated parameters is contained in neither.

How it is checked

  • Local level model (30 values): smoother and its variance equal to the dense Gaussian computation C(C+rI)−1yC(C + rI)^{-1}y to 1e-10; likelihood equal to the normal density; with two missing values equal to the density of the others.
  • AR(1) with mean on the series lh (48 values): ϕ=0.574\phi = 0.574, μ=2.413\mu = 2.413, σ2=0.1975\sigma^2 = 0.1975, log L = −29.38 – the values R's arima prints; Kalman likelihood equal to the closed-form AR(1) likelihood.
  • ARIMA(0,1,1): forecast equal to exponential smoothing with α=1+θ\alpha = 1 + \theta (1e-8); AR(1) forecast and variance in closed form (1e-12); ARIMA(0,2,0) with variances 5 and 14 σ2\sigma^2 after 2 and 3 steps.

zustandsraum.TheorieCprobe (4 checks): the Lyapunov solution for ARMA(1,1) against the closed-form covariance, a unit root rejected, stationary models near the unit root (ϕ=0.995\phi = 0.995, −0.999-0.999, ARMA with 0.998) accepted; the forecast variances for ARMA(1,1) and ARIMA(1,1,0) against the closed-form ψ weights; the concentrated likelihood equal to the full one at σ^2\hat\sigma^2 and maximal there. Counter-checks (MA part with the wrong sign, ψ without the MA part, σ^2\hat\sigma^2 with n−1n - 1) are detected.

The recomputation with sympy and numpy (update as a conditional normal distribution, prediction error decomposition against the dense likelihood, stationary variances of AR(1) and ARMA(1,1), ψ weights of (1−B)2(1 - B)^2, ARIMA(0,1,1) equal to exponential smoothing) is in the source tree under docs/theorie/rechnung/stats_zustandsraum.py.

Limits

Scalar observation; with MA parts the prediction covariance is singular and the smoother fails there – ARIMA uses the filter only. At most d=2d = 2; no seasonal models. Stationarity is tested on the eigenvalues of TT (spectral radius below 1); near the unit root Var⁡y\operatorname{Var}y becomes very large (ϕ=0.999\phi = 0.999: 500) and the likelihood is flat there. Until 2026-10-19 the test was ‖T1024‖max⁡<10−3\|T^{1024}\|_{\max} < 10^{-3}; it rejected spectral radii above about 0.993 although they are stationary.
