Statistical process control – background

The computation behind Spc: control charts for mean and range, individual values, proportions and defect counts, the chart constants, CUSUM and EWMA for small shifts, process capability CpC_p, CpkC_{pk}, PpP_p, PpkP_{pk}, the first Western Electric rule and Pareto analysis.

What this is about

Every manufacturing process varies. Statistical process control (SPC) distinguishes two kinds of variation: random variation arising from many small, unavoidable influences, and systematic variation with an assignable cause – a worn tool, a new material batch, a misadjusted stop. A process that varies only randomly is called in control. A control chart shows when it no longer is.

Two confusions are so common that they deserve to come first:

  • Control limits are not specification limits. The control limits of a chart come from the process itself (what it normally does), the specification limits from the drawing (what it should do). A process can be perfectly in control and still lie entirely outside the specification.
  • Capability indices assume a process in control. The CpkC_{pk} of a process that jumps or drifts describes a state that does not exist.

Shewhart control charts

A control chart plots a statistic per sample over time, with a centre line and control limits at three standard deviations of the statistic. For normally distributed values a point of a process in control lies outside with probability 0.27 % – on average one false alarm every 370 points.

The standard deviation is not estimated from all values together but from the spread within the subgroups or between consecutive values. Otherwise a shift of the process would widen the limits and hide itself. The chart constants serve this purpose.

CUSUM and EWMA: small, persistent shifts

A Shewhart chart judges each point on its own. A shift of half a standard deviation stays inconspicuous at every single point; on average about 150 points pass before one crosses the limit. CUSUM and EWMA therefore accumulate information over many points.

CUSUM (tabular)

Two sums run along, one for upward and one for downward shifts:

Ci+=max⁡(0,Ci−1++(xi−T)−kσ),Ci−=max⁡(0,Ci−1−+(T−xi)−kσ)C_i^+ = \max\bigl(0,\ C_{i-1}^+ + (x_i - T) - k\sigma\bigr), \qquad C_i^- = \max\bigl(0,\ C_{i-1}^- + (T - x_i) - k\sigma\bigr)

with target TT and start value 0. The allowance kσk\sigma (usually k=0.5k = 0.5, half the shift to be detected) keeps the sums near zero for a process on target; a persistent shift makes one sum grow steadily. An alarm is raised when a sum exceeds a decision interval hσh\sigma (usually h=4h = 4 or 5).

Framework convention: cusum(x, T, k, h, σ) returns the two sum sequences together with hh and σ\sigma; limit() is the decision interval hσh\sigma, alarms() the indices at which C+C^+ or C−C^- exceeds it. The variant without hh uses h=5h = 5 as R qcc::cusum. In the catalogue TT is the mean of the data, σ\sigma their standard deviation, k=0.5k = 0.5 and h=5h = 5 (adjustable); the result reports the limit and the alarms. Until finding WT8 the result always carried h=0h = 0, and no limit was evaluated.

Process capability

How well does the spread fit into the tolerance from LSL⁡\mathrm{LSL} to USL⁡\mathrm{USL}? With the mean x\bar x:

Cp=USL⁡−LSL⁡6σw,Cpk=min⁡(USL⁡−x3σw,x−LSL⁡3σw)C_p = \frac{\mathrm{USL} - \mathrm{LSL}}{6\sigma_w}, \qquad C_{pk} = \min\left(\frac{\mathrm{USL} - \bar x}{3\sigma_w},\ \frac{\bar x - \mathrm{LSL}}{3\sigma_w}\right)

What the numbers mean: for a normal distribution centred in the tolerance a fraction 2Φ(−3Cp)2\Phi(-3C_p) lies outside, i.e. 0.27 % at Cp=1C_p = 1 and about 63 ppm at Cp=1.33C_p = 1.33; CpkC_{pk} gives the fraction beyond the nearer limit as Φ(−3Cpk)\Phi(-3C_{pk}).

CpC_p compares only widths and says nothing about location; a process with small spread next to the tolerance has a good CpC_p. CpkC_{pk} measures the distance to the nearer limit. PpP_p and PpkP_{pk} are the same formulas with the overall standard deviation ss of all values:

IndexSpreadin the code
CpC_p, CpkC_{pk} (short-term)σw=MR/1.128\sigma_w = \overline{MR}/1.128moving ranges of consecutive individual values
PpP_p, PpkP_{pk} (overall)ss, denominator n−1n-1all values

For a process in control both spreads are about equal. If ss is clearly larger than σw\sigma_w, the process drifts or jumps between measurements.

Western Electric rule 1 and Pareto

westernElectricRule1(x, centre, σ) reports all points with |xi−centre|>3σ|x_i - \text{centre}| > 3\sigma – the first of the four Western Electric rules. The other rules (two of three beyond 2σ2\sigma, four of five beyond 1σ1\sigma, eight in a row on one side) are not implemented.

pareto sorts defect types by frequency in descending order and forms the cumulative fraction in percent. For equal frequencies the order is that of the input.

Limits

  • Up to n=10n = 10 the three-digit table values apply, above that computed values with full precision; the rounding therefore jumps between n=10n = 10 and 11. Subgroups of size 1 and subgroups of unequal size are rejected.
  • No s chart: xbarSMeans only returns the mean chart with A3A_3; B3B_3/B4B_4 are not tabulated.
  • CpC_p/CpkC_{pk} always estimate σw\sigma_w from moving ranges of individual values, even when the data come from subgroups; there is no variant with R/d2\bar R/d_2 or s/c4\bar s/c_4.
  • Capability indices assume normality; for skewed characteristics (runout, roughness) there is no quantile method.
  • p chart only with one limit from n\bar n; inaccurate for strongly varying sample sizes.
  • Only rule 1 of Western Electric.
