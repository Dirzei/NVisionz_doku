Time series – background

The computation behind TimeSeries: moving averages, exponential smoothing, Holt, classical decomposition, differencing, AR model after Yule-Walker, FFT and periodogram, cross-correlation, the Dickey-Fuller statistic and the search for a change point.

What this is about

In a time series the order of the values is part of the information: a value depends on the previous one, there are trends, daily or annual cycles, jumps. Most methods on the other pages assume independent values and are not directly applicable here. The tools on this page answer typical questions about a series of measurements:

  • What does the course look like without the noise? (smoothing)
  • Which part is trend, which part periodic? (decomposition, periodogram)
  • How strongly does a value depend on its predecessors, and what comes next? (autocorrelation, AR model, Holt)
  • Does the series drift away, or does it return to a level? (Dickey-Fuller)
  • When did the level change? (change point)
Framework convention: A time series is a double[] with equal time steps; the time axis itself is not passed. Missing values are not handled.

Smoothing

Moving average

The centred moving average of window width ww replaces each value by the mean of ww neighbours. With h=⌊w/2⌋h = \lfloor w/2\rfloor the window covers the indices i−hi-h to i−h+w−1i-h+w-1: for odd ww it lies symmetrically around ii, for even ww it reaches one value further into the past than into the future. The edges where the window is incomplete are NaN.

Exponential smoothing

s0=x0,st=αxt+(1−α)st−1s_0 = x_0, \qquad s_t = \alpha x_t + (1-\alpha)\,s_{t-1}

Each earlier value enters with the weight α(1−α)j\alpha(1-\alpha)^j, i.e. exponentially decreasing. A small α\alpha smooths strongly and reacts sluggishly. The catalogue uses α=0.3\alpha = 0.3.

Holt: level and trend

Exponential smoothing always lags behind a trend. Holt therefore also smooths the slope:

ℓt=αxt+(1−α)(ℓt−1+bt−1),bt=β(ℓt−ℓt−1)+(1−β)bt−1ℓ_t = \alpha x_t + (1-\alpha)(ℓ_{t-1} + b_{t-1}), \qquad b_t = \beta(ℓ_t - ℓ_{t-1}) + (1-\beta)\,b_{t-1}

Start with ℓ0=x0ℓ_0 = x_0, b0=x1−x0b_0 = x_1 - x_0. The fitted value at time tt is the one-step forecast ℓt−1+bt−1ℓ_{t-1} + b_{t-1}; the forecast hh steps after the end is ℓn+hbnℓ_n + h\,b_n. α\alpha and β\beta are set by the caller; there is no optimisation.

Classical decomposition

The classical decomposition writes a series with period mm as

xt=Tt+St+Rt(additive)orxt=Tt⋅St⋅Rt(multiplicative)x_t = T_t + S_t + R_t \quad\text{(additive)} \qquad\text{or}\qquad x_t = T_t\cdot S_t\cdot R_t \quad\text{(multiplicative)}

  1. Trend TtT_t: centred moving average over one period – for odd mm with window width mm and equal weights, for even mm the 2×m2\times m average with window width m+1m + 1 and weights 12m,1m,…,1m,12m\frac1{2m}, \frac1m, \dots, \frac1m, \frac1{2m}.
  2. Detrended: xt−Ttx_t - T_t or xt/Ttx_t/T_t.
  3. Seasonal figure: mean of the detrended values per phase tmod⁡mt \,\operatorname{mod}\, m, then shifted (additive: sum 0) or scaled (multiplicative: mean 1) so that the season contributes nothing over one period.
  4. Remainder: xt−Tt−Stx_t - T_t - S_t or xt/(TtSt)x_t/(T_t S_t).
Framework convention: The phase is counted from the first value of the series (tmod⁡mt \,\operatorname{mod}\, m with t=0t = 0 at the start). For even mm the module, like R decompose, takes the centred 2×m2\times m average with half weights at both ends, which covers one period exactly once. Until finding WT9 a window of width m+1m+1 with equal weights stood here; it covered one phase twice and left a remnant of the season in the trend (see the worked example).
trend ← m odd ? moving average(x, m)
              : (½x[t−m/2] + x[t−m/2+1] + … + x[t+m/2−1] + ½x[t+m/2]) / m   2×m average
detrended[t] ← additive ? x[t] − trend[t] : x[t]/trend[t]      NaN at the edges
for each phase s: season[s] ← mean of the detrended values with t mod m = s
                     (0 or 1 if none exist)
additive: season ← season − mean(season);  else season ← season / mean(season)
remainder[t] ← additive ? x[t] − trend[t] − season[t mod m] : x[t]/(trend[t]·season[t mod m])

Source: envisionz-framework-stats/src/envisionz/stats/timeseries/TimeSeries.java, methods movingAverage, expSmoothing, holt, decompose. In the catalogue: period 12, additive.

difference(x, lag) forms xt−xt−lagx_t - x_{t-\text{lag}} once; the series becomes lag values shorter. difference(x, lag, d) differences dd times in succession like R diff(x, lag, differences = d); the series becomes d⋅d\cdotlag values shorter.

AR model after Yule-Walker

An autoregressive model of order pp explains each value from its pp predecessors:

xt−μ=ϕ1(xt−1−μ)+…+ϕp(xt−p−μ)+εtx_t - \mu = \phi_1(x_{t-1} - \mu) + \dots + \phi_p(x_{t-p} - \mu) + \varepsilon_t

Multiplying by xt−k−μx_{t-k} - \mu and taking expectations yields the Yule-Walker equations, a Toeplitz system in the autocorrelations:

rk=∑j=1pϕjr|k−j|,k=1,…,pr_k = \sum_{j=1}^{p}\phi_j\,r_{|k-j|}, \qquad k = 1, \dots, p

The module inserts the sample autocorrelations (denominator nn, see Autocorrelation) and solves the system with the Durbin-Levinson recursion in O(p2)O(p^2) instead of Gauss in O(p3)O(p^3). It returns ϕ1,…,ϕp\phi_1, \dots, \phi_p; the residual variance is not returned. With denominator nn the estimated model is always stationary.

FFT and periodogram

The module has its own radix-2 Cooley-Tukey FFT (the same structure as in framework-math: bit-reversed reordering, then log2⁡N\log_2 N rounds of butterflies). Inputs whose length is not a power of two are padded with zeros to the next power of two NN. The inverse transform – unlike framework-math – divides by NN.

The periodogram of the mean-corrected series is

Ik=|Xk|2n,k=0,…,N2−1I_k = \frac{|X_k|^2}{n}, \qquad k = 0, \dots, \tfrac N2 - 1

with the original length nn in the denominator. Bin kk corresponds to the frequency k/Nk/N cycles per sampling step – relative to the padded length NN, not to nn. The Nyquist bin k=N/2k = N/2 is not included.

c ← x − mean(x)
N ← smallest power of two ≥ n;  pad c with zeros to N
(re, im) ← fft(c)                                     radix 2, forward, unscaled
for k ← 0 to N/2 − 1:  I[k] ← (re[k]² + im[k]²)/n

Source: envisionz-framework-stats/src/envisionz/stats/timeseries/TimeSeries.java, methods fft, fftInPlace, periodogram.

Cross-correlation

For two series of equal length and lags −L≤ℓ≤L-L \le ℓ \le L:

rxy(ℓ)=1n∑t(xt−x)(yt+ℓ−y)sxsyr_{xy}(ℓ) = \frac{\frac1n\sum_t (x_t - \bar x)(y_{t+ℓ} - \bar y)}{s_x\,s_y}

with population standard deviations and 1/n1/n throughout, as in R ccf. A maximum at ℓ>0ℓ > 0 means: yy follows xx by ℓℓ steps. The result array starts at ℓ=−Lℓ = -L.

Dickey-Fuller statistic

A series with a unit root (random walk, xt=xt−1+εtx_t = x_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t) has no fixed mean, and many methods produce spurious relations on it. The augmented Dickey-Fuller test estimates the regression

Δxt=α+βxt−1+∑i=1LγiΔxt−i+εt\Delta x_t = \alpha + \beta\,x_{t-1} + \sum_{i=1}^{L}\gamma_i\,\Delta x_{t-i} + \varepsilon_t

with Regression.ols and reports the t value of β\beta. With a unit root β=0\beta = 0; for a stationary series β<0\beta < 0 (the series is pulled back to its level). The lags Δxt−i\Delta x_{t-i} absorb autocorrelation of the differences.

Framework convention: adfStatistic returns only the test statistic, no p-value. Under H0H_0 the t statistic does not follow the t distribution but the Dickey-Fuller distribution. For the model with constant the asymptotic critical values are −3.43 (1 %), −2.86 (5 %) and −2.57 (10 %); only values below them speak against a unit root.

Change point

changepoint looks for the one index kk at which the level changes most clearly: for each k=1…n−1k = 1 \dots n-1 it splits the series into [0,k)[0, k) and [k,n)[k, n), takes the mean of each part and sums the squared deviations. The index with the smallest sum is the change point – the first index of the second section. This is the least-squares solution for exactly one jump in the mean, cost O(n2)O(n^2). Whether the jump is significant, the method does not say.

Worked examples

Decomposition with an even period

A series with linear trend 10+0.5t10 + 0.5t and the season (2,−1,0,−1)(2, -1, 0, -1), period 4, 16 values. The module takes the 2×42\times4 average with weights 18,14,14,14,18\frac18, \frac14, \frac14, \frac14, \frac18:

  • Trend at t=2…5t = 2 \dots 5: 11.0; 11.5; 12.0; 12.5 – exactly the straight line; at t=0,1,14,15t = 0, 1, 14, 15 NaN.
  • Seasonal figure: 2; −1; 0; −1 – exactly the built-in season.
  • Remainder: zero everywhere up to rounding.

The former window of width 5 with equal weights (until finding WT9) gave the trend 11.4; 11.3; 12.0; 12.3 and the seasonal figure 2.0; −0.8; −0.4; −0.8 here: it contains one phase twice; if it is phase 0, +2/5=0.4+2/5 = 0.4 ends up in the trend, if it is phase 1 or 3, −1/5=−0.2-1/5 = -0.2. With an odd period this does not occur.

Holt

Series 10, 12, 13, 15, 16, 18 with α=0.5\alpha = 0.5, β=0.3\beta = 0.3: start ℓ0=10ℓ_0 = 10, b0=2b_0 = 2. One-step forecasts 12; 14; 15.35; 16.97; 18.14. At the end ℓ=18.069ℓ = 18.069, b=1.631b = 1.631; forecast one step ahead 19.70, three steps ahead 22.96.

Periodogram with padding

A sine with exactly 5 periods on 50 values is padded to N=64N = 64. The frequency 5/50=0.15/50 = 0.1 corresponds to bin 0.1⋅64=6.40.1\cdot64 = 6.4 – the maximum lies at bin 6, and the energy spreads to the neighbours. With 64 values and 8 periods (probe) the sine falls exactly on bin 8.

Dickey-Fuller

Generated with Rng(7): a random walk of 200 steps gives −2.51-2.51 (above −2.86, unit root not rejected), an AR(1) process with ϕ=0.5\phi = 0.5 gives −7.12-7.12 (clearly below, stationary). Yule-Walker estimates the coefficients 0.423 and 0.036 for the AR(1) process with order 2. All values recomputed with the module.

How it is checked

TimeSeriesTest (17 checks): moving average with window 3 (middle 3, edges NaN); exponential smoothing with α=0.5\alpha = 0.5 (1.5 and 2.25); Holt on a straight line forecasts 6 to 10−610^{-6}; differences {2,3,4}\{2, 3, 4\}; twofold differences of 1,4,9,16,251, 4, 9, 16, 25 give {2,2,2}\{2, 2, 2\}; the decomposition of the worked example (trend at t=2t = 2 exactly 11.0; trend, season and remainder exact, edges NaN); FFT of {1,1,1,1}\{1,1,1,1\} gives {4,0,0,0}\{4, 0, 0, 0\}; periodogram maximum of a sine with 8 periods at bin 8; AR(1) of an alternating series negative; cross-correlation of a series with itself at ℓ=0ℓ = 0 equal to 1; ADF finite; change point at index 4 for a jump from 1 to 10.

Limits

  • No STL decomposition; the classical decomposition has ⌊m/2⌋\lfloor m/2\rfloor values without trend and remainder at each end.
  • No ARIMA, no estimation of α\alpha and β\beta for Holt, no Holt-Winters with season.
  • ADF without p-value and without automatic choice of the number of lags.
  • The periodogram is unsmoothed and unwindowed; padding shifts the frequency grid.
  • changepoint finds exactly one break and does not say whether it is real.
