Markov chain Monte Carlo – background

The computation behind Metropolis and Ising: random-walk Metropolis-Hastings, the adaptive variant after Haario, effective sample size after Geyer and R-hat after Gelman-Rubin; as an application the two-dimensional Ising model with exact partition function and Onsager's solution.

What it is about

Many distributions are known only up to a constant – a posterior density, a Boltzmann distribution. A Markov chain whose equilibrium is exactly this distribution still delivers samples: one draws a proposal and accepts it with a probability that needs only density ratios. Successive members are correlated; how many independent samples they are worth is told by the effective sample size.

Metropolis-Hastings, adaptive

Proposal x'=x+δx' = x + \delta, δ∼N(0,Σ)\delta \sim N(0, \Sigma) (symmetric), acceptance with min⁡(1,π(x')/π(x))\min(1, \pi(x')/\pi(x)), computed with the log density. In the adaptive version after Haario, Saksman and Tamminen Σ\Sigma is estimated from the chain so far from half the burn-in onwards and scaled by 2.382/d2.38^2/d – the factor that mixes nearly optimally for normal targets in dd dimensions.

Derivation. A chain with transition kernel P(x,x')=q(x'∣x)α(x,x')P(x, x') = q(x' \mid x)\,\alpha(x, x') for x'≠xx' \ne x has π\pi as its equilibrium if it satisfies detailed balance: π(x)P(x,x')=π(x')P(x',x)\pi(x)P(x, x') = \pi(x')P(x', x). With a symmetric proposal q(x'∣x)=q(x∣x')q(x' \mid x) = q(x \mid x') and

α(x,x')=min⁡(1,π(x')π(x))⇒π(x)α(x,x')=min⁡(π(x),π(x'))=π(x')α(x',x)\alpha(x, x') = \min\Bigl(1, \frac{\pi(x')}{\pi(x)}\Bigr) \quad\Rightarrow\quad \pi(x)\,\alpha(x, x') = \min\bigl(\pi(x), \pi(x')\bigr) = \pi(x')\,\alpha(x', x)

this holds, and the normalising constant of π\pi cancels. A rejected proposal leaves xx in place, and this value counts again – otherwise the equilibrium would be shifted. Strictly speaking the adaptive version is no longer a Markov chain; Haario, Saksman and Tamminen show that the averages still converge to the right values because the adaptation changes less and less. For dd-dimensional normal targets the step size 2.38/d2.38/\sqrt d times the target spread maximises efficiency; the acceptance rate is then 0.234 for large dd (Roberts, Gelman and Gilks).

Effective size and R-hat

The effective sample size is n/τn/\tau with the integrated autocorrelation time τ=1+2∑kρk\tau = 1 + 2\sum_k \rho_k. After Geyer the sum stops as soon as a pair sum ρ2m+ρ2m+1\rho_{2m} + \rho_{2m+1} is no longer positive (initial positive sequence). For an AR(1) sequence with ρ\rho, neff=n(1−ρ)/(1+ρ)n_{\text{eff}} = n\,(1-\rho)/(1+\rho).

Derivation. For a stationary sequence with variance σ2\sigma^2

Var⁡x=σ2n(1+2∑k=1n−1(1−kn)ρk)→σ2τn,\operatorname{Var}\bar x = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\Bigl(1 + 2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\bigl(1 - \tfrac kn\bigr)\rho_k\Bigr) \;\to\; \frac{\sigma^2\tau}{n},

so the sequence is worth as much as n/τn/\tau independent values. For AR(1), ρk=ρk\rho_k = \rho^k and τ=1+2ρ/(1−ρ)=(1+ρ)/(1−ρ)\tau = 1 + 2\rho/(1-\rho) = (1+\rho)/(1-\rho), i.e. 4 for ρ=0.6\rho = 0.6. The estimated ρk\rho_k become noise for large kk; Geyer shows that for reversible chains the pair sums Γm=ρ2m+ρ2m+1\Gamma_m = \rho_{2m} + \rho_{2m+1} are positive, and therefore stops at the first non-positive Γm\Gamma_m – this cuts off the noise without a cut-off parameter.

R-hat after Gelman and Rubin compares mm chains of length nn: with the mean within-chain variance WW and the variance between the means B/nB/n

R^=n−1nW+BnW;\hat R = \sqrt{\frac{\frac{n-1}{n}W + \frac{B}{n}}{W}};

values near 1 (below about 1.01) show that the chains see the same distribution.

Derivation. If the chains have not yet reached equilibrium, WW underestimates the variance of the target (each chain has seen only part of it), while V^=n−1nW+Bn\hat V = \frac{n-1}{n}W + \frac Bn overestimates it because the starting points are spread out. Both tend to the same variance; R^=V^/W\hat R = \sqrt{\hat V/W} measures the gap. For the chains 1,…,41, \dots, 4 and 2,…,52, \dots, 5, W=5/3W = 5/3, B=4⋅12=2B = 4\cdot\frac12 = 2 and R^=(34⋅53+12)/53=1.05\hat R = \sqrt{(\frac34\cdot\frac53 + \frac12)/\frac53} = \sqrt{1.05}.

Accuracy and cost. The Monte Carlo error of a mean is στ/n\sigma\sqrt{\tau/n}; one more digit costs a hundred times as many steps. Each step costs one evaluation of the density. The estimate of τ\tau itself is uncertain as long as the chain is shorter than a few hundred τ\tau.

The Ising model

On an L×LL \times L lattice with periodic boundaries, spins si=±1s_i = \pm1 carry the energy H=−J∑⟨ij⟩sisj−h∑siH = -J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}s_i s_j - h\sum s_i. Ising.metropolis flips single spins with acceptance probability min⁡(1,e−βΔE)\min(1, e^{-\beta\Delta E}) (table for the five possible neighbour sums) and averages energy, |M||M|, specific heat β2Var⁡E/N\beta^2\operatorname{Var}E/N and susceptibility per spin. For small lattices (up to 20 spins) exakt enumerates all 2N2^N states – the reference for the chain. The spontaneous magnetisation of the infinite lattice is, after Onsager and Yang,

m=(1−sinh−4⁡(2βJ))1/8(β>βc),βc=12ln⁡(1+2).m = \big(1 - \sinh^{-4}(2\beta J)\big)^{1/8} \quad (\beta > \beta_c), \qquad \beta_c = \tfrac12\ln(1 + \sqrt2).

Derivation of the table. Flipping spin ii changes only its four bonds and its field term:

ΔE=2si(J∑j∼isj+h),∑j∼isj∈{−4,−2,0,2,4}.\Delta E = 2\,s_i\Bigl(J\sum_{j \sim i} s_j + h\Bigr), \qquad \sum_{j\sim i} s_j \in \{-4, -2, 0, 2, 4\}.

For h=0h = 0 there are only five values of ΔE\Delta E, and e−βΔEe^{-\beta\Delta E} can be tabulated once; each step then costs one neighbour sum and one random number. The acceptance min⁡(1,e−βΔE)\min(1, e^{-\beta\Delta E}) is the Metropolis rule with π∝e−βH\pi \propto e^{-\beta H}.

Derivation of the heat. With the partition function Z=∑e−βHZ = \sum e^{-\beta H}, ⟨E⟩=−∂βln⁡Z\langle E\rangle = -\partial_\beta\ln Z and ∂β⟨E⟩=−(⟨E2⟩−⟨E⟩2)\partial_\beta\langle E\rangle = -(\langle E^2\rangle - \langle E\rangle^2). Hence

C=β2NVar⁡E=−β2Nd⟨E⟩dβ,C = \frac{\beta^2}{N}\operatorname{Var}E = -\frac{\beta^2}{N}\,\frac{d\langle E\rangle}{d\beta},

the fluctuation of the energy in the chain is the same quantity as the derivative of the mean energy – this is what the exact enumeration of the 4×4 lattice checks. On the 3×3 lattice the identity is also in the recomputation.

What follows. Below βc≈0.4407\beta_c \approx 0.4407 the infinite lattice is non-magnetic, above it mm rises steeply (m(0.6)=0.9736m(0.6) = 0.9736). A finite lattice shows no true phase transition; |M||M| is of order 1/N1/\sqrt N for small β\beta, not zero. Near βc\beta_c the autocorrelation time of the single-spin chain grows roughly like LzL^{z} with z≈2.17z \approx 2.17 (critical slowing down); long chains or cluster methods are needed there.

How it is checked

  • Effective size of an AR(1) sequence (ρ=0.6\rho = 0.6, 200 000 values) like n(1−ρ)/(1+ρ)n(1-\rho)/(1+\rho) to 3 %; equal to the written-out Geyer sequence to 1e-12.
  • R-hat by hand (two chains 1…4 and 2…5) to 1e-15; identically distributed chains below 1.01, shifted ones above 1.5.
  • Adaptive Metropolis on a correlated normal distribution; Beta(3, 5) via the logit transform: mean 3/8.
  • Ising 4×4 exact: heat equal to −β2dE/dβ-\beta^2\,dE/d\beta, limits for small and large β\beta; Metropolis against exact to 0.01; 32×32 at β=0.6\beta = 0.6: |m|=0.9739|m| = 0.9739 against Onsager 0.9736.

The recomputation with sympy and numpy (detailed balance, τ=4\tau = 4 for ρ=0.6\rho = 0.6 via Geyer's pair sums, R-hat of the two chains, βc\beta_c and m(0.6)m(0.6) after Onsager, heat =β2Var⁡E=−β2dE/dβ= \beta^2\operatorname{Var}E = -\beta^2\,dE/d\beta on the 3×3 lattice) is in the source tree under docs/theorie/rechnung/stats_mcmc.py. Every formula on this page that carries a reference has a counter-check (distorted acceptance probability, premature stop of the pair sums, wrong denominator in R-hat, Haario factor, table and field term of the Ising model, heat, Onsager exponent); all are detected.

Limits

Only random-walk proposals (no Hamiltonian Monte Carlo); Ising only with single-spin Metropolis, which mixes slowly near βc\beta_c.
