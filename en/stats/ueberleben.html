Survival times and reliability – background

The computation behind Ueberleben, Fehlerbaum and Markow: Kaplan-Meier with Greenwood, log-rank, Weibull with censoring, Cox model with Breslow and Efron; fault trees with minimal cut sets and exact probability, continuous-time Markov chains for availability and mean time to failure.

What it is about

Lifetimes are often observed incompletely: a component has not failed by the end of the test, a patient leaves the study. Such censored times carry information (“at least this long”) but must not be counted like failures. At system level reliability engineering asks how component failures lead to failure of the whole – logically in the fault tree, in time with repair in Markov chains.

Kaplan-Meier, log-rank

At each event time tjt_j with njn_j units at risk and djd_j events:

S^(t)=∏tj≤t(1−djnj),Var⁡^S^=S^2∑tj≤tdjnj(nj−dj)(Greenwood);\hat S(t) = \prod_{t_j \le t}\Big(1 - \frac{d_j}{n_j}\Big), \qquad \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}\hat S = \hat S^2\sum_{t_j\le t}\frac{d_j}{n_j(n_j - d_j)} \quad\text{(Greenwood)};

the interval is formed on the log-log scale and so stays within [0, 1]. The log-rank test compares two groups: per event time the observed against the expected events of the first group under equality, with hypergeometric variance; χ2=(O1−E1)2/V\chi^2 = (O_1 - E_1)^2/V with one degree of freedom.

Deriving the product, Greenwood and the band

Nothing changes between two event times; at tjt_j, dj/njd_j/n_j is the estimated conditional failure probability among those still observed. Censored units leave the risk set after their time without counting an event – a unit censored exactly at tjt_j still counts in njn_j there. For Greenwood the factors are treated as independent binomial proportions: Var⁡ln⁡(1−q^j)≈dj/(nj(nj−dj))\operatorname{Var}\ln(1 - \hat q_j) \approx d_j/(n_j(n_j - d_j)), summed over jj, and the delta method Var⁡S^≈S^2Var⁡ln⁡S^\operatorname{Var}\hat S \approx \hat S^2\operatorname{Var}\ln\hat S gives the formula. For the band set g=∑dj/(nj(nj−dj))g = \sum d_j/(n_j(n_j - d_j)) and compute on the scale ln⁡(−ln⁡S)\ln(-\ln S), whose variance is again by the delta method g/(ln⁡S)2g/(\ln S)^2:

[exp⁡(−eln⁡(−ln⁡S^)+zg/|ln⁡S^|),exp⁡(−eln⁡(−ln⁡S^)−zg/|ln⁡S^|)].\Big[\exp\big(-e^{\,\ln(-\ln\hat S) + z\sqrt g/|\ln\hat S|}\big),\ \exp\big(-e^{\,\ln(-\ln\hat S) - z\sqrt g/|\ln\hat S|}\big)\Big].

Log-rank. At each event time the mm events are, under equality, hypergeometrically distributed over the n1n_1 and n−n1n - n_1 units at risk: E=mn1/nE = m n_1/n, V=mn1(n−n1)(n−m)/(n2(n−1))V = m\,n_1(n - n_1)(n - m)/(n^2(n - 1)). The sums over all event times are added independently; the test statistic is asymptotically χ12\chi^2_1.

Weibull with censoring

With S(t)=e−(t/λ)kS(t) = e^{-(t/\lambda)^k} the likelihood of right-censored data has a profile equation for the shape kk alone (sums over events dd or all units):

∑dln⁡tid+1k−∑tikln⁡ti∑tik=0,λ=(∑tikd)1/k.\frac{\sum_{d}\ln t_i}{d} + \frac1k - \frac{\sum t_i^k\ln t_i}{\sum t_i^k} = 0, \qquad \lambda = \Big(\frac{\sum t_i^k}{d}\Big)^{1/k}.

Derivation. The log-likelihood is ℓ=dln⁡k−dkln⁡λ+(k−1)∑dln⁡ti−∑(ti/λ)k\ell = d\ln k - dk\ln\lambda + (k - 1)\sum_d\ln t_i - \sum(t_i/\lambda)^k – events contribute the density, censored units only SS. ∂ℓ/∂λ=0\partial\ell/\partial\lambda = 0 gives λk=∑tik/d\lambda^k = \sum t_i^k/d; substituting and differentiating with respect to kk leaves the profile equation. Its left side decreases strictly in kk (1/k1/k decreases, and ∑tkln⁡t/∑tk\sum t^k\ln t/\sum t^k is the mean of ln⁡t\ln t under increasingly steep weights), so it has exactly one root; the code brackets it from [10−3,1][10^{-3}, 1] by doubling and bisects to a relative 10−1510^{-15}.

Cox model

The model h(t∣x)=h0(t)eβTxh(t \mid x) = h_0(t)\,e^{\beta^T x} leaves the baseline hazard open. β\beta is estimated from the partial likelihood with Newton-Raphson; with equal times (ties) after Breslow or Efron. Tests are Wald, score and likelihood ratio; without ties the score test equals the log-rank test.

Partial likelihood. Among all units of the risk set RjR_j at time tjt_j, the probability that exactly the observed one fails is eηi/∑Rjeηe^{\eta_i}/\sum_{R_j}e^{\eta} with η=βTx\eta = \beta^Tx – h0h_0 cancels. With mjm_j simultaneous events DjD_j, S0=∑RjeηS_0 = \sum_{R_j}e^\eta and F0=∑DjeηF_0 = \sum_{D_j}e^\eta:

ℓBreslow=∑j(∑Djηi−mjln⁡S0),ℓEfron=∑j(∑Djηi−∑r=0mj−1ln⁡(S0−rmjF0)).\ell_{\text{Breslow}} = \sum_j\Big(\sum_{D_j}\eta_i - m_j\ln S_0\Big), \qquad \ell_{\text{Efron}} = \sum_j\Big(\sum_{D_j}\eta_i - \sum_{r=0}^{m_j - 1}\ln\big(S_0 - \tfrac{r}{m_j}F_0\big)\Big).

Efron assumes that the tied units failed one after another in unknown order and removes their share from the risk set on average; Breslow leaves all of them in S0S_0. ℓ\ell is concave in β\beta, so Newton-Raphson with the information I=−ℓ''I = -\ell'' converges from β=0\beta = 0; should a full step still lower ℓ\ell, it is halved up to 30 times (a safeguard that practically never triggers for concave ℓ\ell). Score test U(0)TI(0)−1U(0)U(0)^TI(0)^{-1}U(0), Wald test β^TI(β^)β^\hat\beta^TI(\hat\beta)\hat\beta, LR 2(ℓ(β^)−ℓ(0))2(\ell(\hat\beta) - \ell(0)). Cost per iteration O(n⋅#times⋅p2)O(n\cdot\#\text{times}\cdot p^2), because the risk set is summed anew for each time.

Fault tree

Basic events with probability pip_i are combined with AND, OR and k-out-of-n into a top event. The minimal cut sets (smallest sets of basic events that trigger the top event) arise by expanding from the top (MOCUS) and removing all supersets (absorption). The exact probability for independent events uses the Shannon decomposition P=piP(⋅∣i)+(1−pi)P(⋅∣i)P = p_i P(\cdot \mid i) + (1 - p_i)P(\cdot \mid \bar i) over all basic events (up to 22); as approximations serve the sum over the cut sets (rare events) and 1−∏(1−P(C))1 - \prod(1 - P(C)) after Esary and Proschan – both lie above.

The Shannon decomposition is exact because it sums over all 2n2^n states of the basic events; that costs O(2n)O(2^n) evaluations of the tree – hence the limit of 22. The rare-event approximation is the first term of the inclusion-exclusion formula and thus an upper bound; Esary-Proschan treats the cut sets as independent and also lies above for monotone trees, but closer.

Markov chains

A system with states (all working, one unit failed, …) and transition rates (failure λ\lambda, repair μ\mu) is a continuous-time Markov chain with generator QQ (row sums 0). The stationary distribution solves πQ=0\pi Q = 0, ∑π=1\sum\pi = 1; the course is p(t)=p(0)eQtp(t) = p(0)\,e^{Qt} with the matrix exponential of framework-math; the mean time to the first failure state is τ=(−QAA)−11\tau = (-Q_{AA})^{-1}\mathbf 1 over the working states AA. For two parallel units with one repair crew MTTF⁡=(3λ+μ)/(2λ2)\mathrm{MTTF} = (3\lambda + \mu)/(2\lambda^2).

Deriving the MTTF. Let τi\tau_i be the mean time to failure from state i∈Ai \in A. After the first jump τi=1/qi+∑j∈A,j≠i(qij/qi)τj\tau_i = 1/q_i + \sum_{j\in A, j\ne i} (q_{ij}/q_i)\tau_j with qi=−qiiq_i = -q_{ii}; rearranged this is −QAAτ=1-Q_{AA}\tau = \mathbf 1. For two parallel units (states 2, 1 working) QAA=(−2λ2λμ−(λ+μ))Q_{AA} = \begin{pmatrix}-2\lambda & 2\lambda\\ \mu & -(\lambda + \mu)\end{pmatrix}, and solving gives τ2=(3λ+μ)/(2λ2)\tau_2 = (3\lambda + \mu)/(2\lambda^2).

How it is checked

  • Data set aml (R package survival): Kaplan-Meier and Greenwood by hand; log-rank χ2=3.4\chi^2 = 3.4, p = 0.0653; Cox with Efron β=0.9155\beta = 0.9155, se 0.5119, LR 3.38, score 3.42 – as the reference outputs.
  • Without ties: score test = log-rank to 1e-12, Breslow = Efron; Weibull against Nelder-Mead on the full likelihood (1e-5).
  • Fault tree: 2-out-of-3 equal to 3p2−2p33p^2 - 2p^3; absorption gives {A}, {B, C}; bridge network exact as a polynomial, approximations in the right order.
  • Markov: single unit A(t)=μλ+μ+λλ+μe−(λ+μ)tA(t) = \frac{\mu}{\lambda+\mu} + \frac{\lambda}{\lambda+\mu}e^{-(\lambda+\mu)t} to 1e-13; parallel system MTTF and availability in closed form.

Limits

Right censoring only; Cox without time-dependent covariates. Fault tree with independent basic events, exact up to 22 events. Markov chains dense.
