Multivariate methods – background

The computation behind Multivariate: principal component analysis via the singular value decomposition, k-means after Lloyd with silhouette values, agglomerative hierarchical clustering, classical multidimensional scaling and Fisher's discriminant direction.

What this is about

When each observation consists of many quantities – twenty measuring points of a compressor, ten dimensions of a part – it helps little to look at each quantity on its own. The quantities are related, and the interesting structure lies in these relations. Multivariate methods answer three kinds of question:

  • Condense: can the many quantities be replaced by a few new ones without losing much information? (principal components, MDS)
  • Group: do the observations fall into natural groups? (k-means, hierarchical clustering)
  • Separate: in which direction do two known groups differ most clearly? (discriminant analysis)
Framework convention: The data matrix is x[i][j] = observation ii, quantity jj – rows are observations, as in R. Distances are Euclidean throughout. The linear algebra (SVD, eigendecomposition, equation solver) comes from framework-math.

Principal component analysis

The principal components are new, mutually perpendicular axes in the space of the quantities: the first points in the direction of largest spread, the second in the direction of largest remaining spread perpendicular to it, and so on. Mathematically they are the eigenvectors of the covariance matrix S=1n−1ZTZS = \frac{1}{n-1}Z^TZ of the centred data ZZ; the eigenvalues are the variances along these axes.

The module does not form SS, however. It decomposes the centred data matrix directly with the singular value decomposition

Z=UΣVT⇒ZTZ=VΣ2VTZ = U\,\Sigma\,V^T \quad\Rightarrow\quad Z^TZ = V\,\Sigma^2\,V^T

The columns of VV are therefore the principal axes (loadings), and the eigenvalues are λj=σj2/(n−1)\lambda_j = \sigma_j^2/(n-1). The detour via ZTZZ^TZ would square the condition number – small eigenvalues would be lost in rounding. Until September 2026 LinAlg.svd computed exactly that way; since finding ST3 it works with the Golub-Kahan method directly on ZZ.

With scale = true each quantity is additionally divided by its standard deviation; the basis is then the correlation matrix. That is necessary when the quantities have different units – otherwise the quantity with the largest numbers alone determines the first component.

for each column j:  mean[j] ← mean;  sd[j] ← scale ? sd(column) : 1
Z ← (x − mean)/sd
(U, s, V) ← svd(Z)                                   Golub-Kahan, s descending
q ← min(n, p)                                        as many singular values as the SVD returns
for j < q:  λ[j] ← s[j]²/(n − 1);   fraction[j] ← λ[j]/Σλ
loadings ← V;   scores ← Z·V

Source: envisionz-framework-stats/src/envisionz/stats/multivariate/Multivariate.java, method pca; SVD in envisionz-framework-math/src/envisionz/math/linear/LinAlg.java (svd, via GolubKahan).

Framework convention: The signs of the loading vectors are not fixed: vv and −v-v are equally good principal axes. Since the switch to Golub-Kahan (ST3) individual components may have changed sign relative to earlier runs or to R; eigenvalues and fractions are not affected. There are q=min⁡(n,p)q = \min(n, p) components as in R prcomp: eigenvalues and fractions have length qq, the loadings are p×qp\times q, the scores n×qn\times q. For n≤pn \le p the last variance is zero (up to rounding), because the centred data have rank at most n−1n-1. Until finding WT7 the loop ran over pp and failed with an exception for n<pn < p. In the catalogue scale = true is the default.

k-means

k-means divides nn points into kk groups so that the sum of squared distances to the group centres becomes small:

WSS⁡=∑i=1n‖xi−ca(i)‖2\mathrm{WSS} = \sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_i - c_{a(i)}\|^2

Finding the global minimum is NP-hard. Lloyd's method alternates two steps, neither of which increases WSS: assign (each point to the nearest centre) and update (each centre to the centroid of its points). It ends in a local minimum that depends on the start points.

start centres ← k distinct random data points (rng.sampleWithout)
a ← all 0
for it ← 0 to maxIter−1:
    changed ← false
    for each point i: nearest centre q;  if a[i] ≠ q: a[i] ← q; changed ← true
    for each centre q with points: c[q] ← centroid of its points     empty ones keep their position
    if not changed: break
WSS ← Σ ‖xᵢ − c[a[i]]‖²

Source: envisionz-framework-stats/src/envisionz/stats/multivariate/Multivariate.java, method kMeans. In the catalogue: k=3k = 3 (2 to 12), seed 1.

Silhouette

How well does a point fit its group? With a(i)a(i) = mean distance to the other points of its own group and b(i)b(i) = smallest mean distance to another group:

s(i)=b(i)−a(i)max⁡(a(i),b(i))s(i) = \frac{b(i) - a(i)}{\max(a(i), b(i))}

ss close to 1: well assigned; close to 0: between two groups; negative: probably wrongly assigned. The mean over all points rates the whole partition and helps in choosing kk. Cost O(n2)O(n^2).

Hierarchical clustering

Agglomerative clustering starts with each point as its own group and in each step merges the two nearest groups, until only one is left. The n−1n-1 merges with their distances (“heights”) form the dendrogram; where it is cut determines the number of groups.

What matters is how the distance between a new group A∪BA ∪ B and another group CC is defined. The module updates it from the old distances (Lance-Williams):

singlemin⁡(d(A,C),d(B,C))\min(d(A,C),\ d(B,C))nearest neighbour – tends to chaining
completemax⁡(d(A,C),d(B,C))\max(d(A,C),\ d(B,C))farthest neighbour – compact groups (default in the catalogue)
average|A|d(A,C)+|B|d(B,C)|A|+|B|\frac{|A|\,d(A,C) + |B|\,d(B,C)}{|A| + |B|}mean distance of all pairs (UPGMA) – in between
d ← Euclidean distance matrix;  size ← 1;  active ← true;  id[i] ← i;  next ← n
for step ← 1 to n−1:
    (a, b) ← active pair with smallest d[a][b]       a < b
    record merge(id[a], id[b], d[a][b])
    for each active group m ≠ a, b:  d[a][m] ← linkage rule(d[a][m], d[b][m], sizes)
    size[a] ← size[a] + size[b];  id[a] ← next;  next ← next + 1;  active[b] ← false

Source: envisionz-framework-stats/src/envisionz/stats/multivariate/Multivariate.java, method hclust with the enum Linkage. Cost O(n3)O(n^3) because of the full search for the smallest distance in each step.

Framework convention: The merges (Merge(i, j, height)) number the original points 0 to n−1n-1 and each newly formed group consecutively n,n+1,…n, n+1, \dots This differs from R hclust$merge (negative numbers for points and 1-based step numbers for groups there).

Classical multidimensional scaling

Given only a table of distances dijd_{ij} – for instance dissimilarities between materials – one seeks points in few dimensions whose distances fit as well as possible. For Euclidean distances this works exactly via the double-centred matrix

B=−12JD(2)J,J=I−1n11T,bij=−12(dij2−di⋅2−d⋅j2+d⋅⋅2)B = -\tfrac12\,J\,D^{(2)}\,J, \qquad J = I - \tfrac1n\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^T, \qquad b_{ij} = -\tfrac12\left(d_{ij}^2 - \bar d_{i\cdot}^2 - \bar d_{\cdot j}^2 + \bar d_{\cdot\cdot}^2\right)

BB is the matrix of scalar products of the sought points, centred at the centroid. Its eigendecomposition B=VΛVTB = V\Lambda V^T gives the coordinates X=VmΛm1/2X = V_m\Lambda_m^{1/2} from the mm largest eigenvalues. Negative eigenvalues (for non-Euclidean distances) are set to 0. The eigendecomposition is the cyclic Jacobi method from framework-math, eigenvalues sorted in descending order.

Fisher's discriminant direction

For two groups with mean vectors μ1\mu_1, μ2\mu_2, Fisher seeks the direction ww in which the projections of the groups are best separated – large distance of the projected means relative to the spread within the groups:

maxw⁡(wT(μ1−μ2))2wTSWw⇒w∝SW−1(μ1−μ2)\max_w \frac{(w^T(\mu_1 - \mu_2))^2}{w^TS_Ww} \quad\Rightarrow\quad w \propto S_W^{-1}(\mu_1 - \mu_2)

with the pooled scatter matrix SW=∑g∑i∈g(xi−μg)(xi−μg)TS_W = \sum_{g}\sum_{i ∈ g}(x_i - \mu_g)(x_i - \mu_g)^T. So that SWS_W stays solvable even with constant directions, the module adds a small ridge term 10−9⋅(trace⁡(SW)/p+10−12)10^{-9}\cdot(\operatorname{trace}(S_W)/p + 10^{-12}) to the diagonal. The result is normalised to length 1.

Worked examples

Principal components in two dimensions

Ten points (2.5; 2.4), (0.5; 0.7), (2.2; 2.9), (1.9; 2.2), (3.1; 3.0), (2.3; 2.7), (2.0; 1.6), (1.0; 1.1), (1.5; 1.6), (1.1; 0.9), not scaled:

  • Eigenvalues 1.28403 and 0.04908 – the first component carries 96.3 % of the spread.
  • First loading (0.6779; 0.7352): almost the diagonal, both quantities grow together.
  • Score of the first point: 0.8280 on the first, −0.1751 on the second component.

Clustering six points

(0, 0), (0, 1), (1, 0) and (10, 10), (10, 11), (11, 10): k-means with k=2k = 2 and seed 1 separates the two triples after one pass; centres (1/3; 1/3) and (31/3; 31/3), WSS 2.667; mean silhouette 0.920. The dendrogram first merges points at distance 1 and finally the two groups – with single at height 13.45 (smallest distance between the groups), with complete at height 14.87 (largest), with average at height 14.17. Values recomputed with the module.

How it is checked

MultivariateTest (8 checks): principal components of exactly collinear data (first component 100 %, second eigenvalue 0 to 10−610^{-6}); principal components of 3 observations with 4 quantities (3 components, sum of eigenvalues equal to the trace of the covariance matrix 28/3, third eigenvalue 0); k-means separates the six points above; mean silhouette above 0.7; hierarchical (complete) first merge below 2, last above 10; MDS reconstructs the distances of a 3×4 rectangle to 10−610^{-6}; Fisher direction predominantly in xx when the groups differ in xx.

Limits

  • k-means depends on the start; there is no multi-start and no k-means++ initialisation. Empty groups keep their old centre.
  • The silhouette of a point that is alone in its group becomes 1 here (a=0a = 0); R cluster::silhouette sets it to 0.
  • Hierarchical clustering with O(n3)O(n^3) time and O(n2)O(n^2) memory – a few thousand points are the limit. No Ward linkage.
  • Fisher's direction only for two groups, without threshold or assignment rule.
  • Factor analysis, PAM, quadratic discriminant analysis, MANOVA and correspondence analysis are not implemented.
