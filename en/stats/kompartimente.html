Compartment models: epidemics and pharmacokinetics – background

The computation behind Sir and Pk: SIR and SEIR with final-size equation and fitting; one- and two-compartment models, oral dosing after Bateman, multiple dosing and non-compartmental analysis.

What it is about

Compartment models divide a quantity into compartments and describe the flows between them with ordinary differential equations – in epidemiology susceptible, infected and recovered, in pharmacokinetics blood and tissue with a drug. Both are small, but their key figures (reproduction number, half-life, area under the curve) need careful computation.

SIR and SEIR

S˙=−βSIN,E˙=βSIN−σE,I˙=σE−γI,R˙=γI\dot S = -\beta\frac{S I}{N}, \quad \dot E = \beta\frac{S I}{N} - \sigma E, \quad \dot I = \sigma E - \gamma I, \quad \dot R = \gamma I

(with σ=∞\sigma = \infty EE drops out: SIR). Integration uses Dormand-Prince from framework-math; S+E+I+R=NS + E + I + R = N is conserved. The basic reproduction number is R0=β/γR_0 = \beta/\gamma. The fraction s∞s_\infty still susceptible at the end satisfies the final-size equation

ln⁡s∞s0+R0(s0+i0−s∞)=0,\ln\frac{s_\infty}{s_0} + R_0\,(s_0 + i_0 - s_\infty) = 0,

solved with Brent; for R0=2.5R_0 = 2.5 finally 89.26 % of the population fall ill. In the SIR model the peak of II lies where S=N/R0S = N/R_0, with Imax=S0+I0−NR0(1+ln⁡R0S0N)I_{max} = S_0 + I_0 - \frac{N}{R_0}\big(1 + \ln\frac{R_0 S_0}{N}\big). passe estimates β\beta and γ\gamma from a series of infected with Levenberg-Marquardt (in logarithms).

Deriving the final size and the peak

Dividing S˙\dot S by R˙\dot R eliminates time and with it EE and II:

dSdR=−R0SN⇒S=S0e−R0(R−Rstart⁡)/N.\frac{dS}{dR} = -R_0\,\frac{S}{N} \quad\Rightarrow\quad S = S_0\,e^{-R_0\,(R - R_{\mathrm{start}})/N}.

At the end nobody is infected or latent, so R∞=N−S∞R_\infty = N - S_\infty; in fractions with rstart⁡=1−s0−i0r_{\mathrm{start}} = 1 - s_0 - i_0 this is exactly the final-size equation above – it holds for SIR and SEIR alike, because latency only delays. The function f(s)=ln⁡(s/s0)+R0(s0+i0−s)f(s) = \ln(s/s_0) + R_0(s_0 + i_0 - s) is concave (f''=−1/s2f'' = -1/s^2), f(s0)=R0i0≥0f(s_0) = R_0 i_0 \ge 0 and f→−∞f \to -\infty as s→0s \to 0; below s0s_0 there is therefore exactly one root, which Brent finds safely from this bracket. For the peak, divide I˙\dot I by S˙\dot S in the SIR model: dI/dS=−1+N/(R0S)dI/dS = -1 + N/(R_0 S), integrated I+S−NR0ln⁡S=const⁡I + S - \frac{N}{R_0}\ln S = \mathrm{const}; at S=N/R0S = N/R_0, I˙=0\dot I = 0, and substituting gives ImaxI_{max}. Herd immunity follows as well: an outbreak grows only while S>N/R0S > N/R_0.

One and two compartments

One compartment with volume VV and elimination constant kk: bolus C=DVe−ktC = \frac DV e^{-kt}; infusion of rate R0R_0 over TT rises to R0Vk(1−e−kt)\frac{R_0}{Vk}(1 - e^{-kt}) and falls exponentially afterwards; oral dosing with absorption kak_a and bioavailability FF after Bateman:

C(t)=FDkaV(ka−k)(e−kt−e−kat),tmax=ln⁡(ka/k)ka−k.C(t) = \frac{F D k_a}{V(k_a - k)}\big(e^{-kt} - e^{-k_a t}\big), \qquad t_{max} = \frac{\ln(k_a/k)}{k_a - k}.

Multiple doses superpose linearly; at steady state the trough lies at DVe−kτ1−e−kτ\frac DV\frac{e^{-k\tau}}{1 - e^{-k\tau}}. In the two-compartment model C=Ae−αt+Be−βtC = A e^{-\alpha t} + B e^{-\beta t} with the hybrid constants α,β=12(s±s2−4k10k21)\alpha, \beta = \frac12\big(s \pm \sqrt{s^2 - 4k_{10}k_{21}}\big), s=k10+k12+k21s = k_{10} + k_{12} + k_{21}, A=DV1α−k21α−βA = \frac{D}{V_1}\frac{\alpha - k_{21}}{\alpha - \beta}, B=DV1k21−βα−βB = \frac{D}{V_1}\frac{k_{21} - \beta}{\alpha - \beta}.

Derivations

Bateman. In the absorption compartment A˙=−kaA\dot A = -k_a A, A(0)=FDA(0) = F D; in the blood VC˙=kaA−VkCV\dot C = k_a A - V k C. The solution of this linear equation with C(0)=0C(0) = 0 is the Bateman function; C˙=0\dot C = 0 gives ke−kt=kae−katk e^{-kt} = k_a e^{-k_a t}, hence tmaxt_{max}. As ka→kk_a \to k numerator and denominator vanish; by l'Hôpital C=FDkVte−ktC = \frac{F D k}{V}\,t\,e^{-kt} remains – that is what the code computes when \(|k_a - k| < 10^{-12}k_a\), instead of dividing by almost zero.

Multiple dosing. The equation is linear, so the doses superpose. Just after the (n+1)(n+1)-th dose, with q=e−kτq = e^{-k\tau},

C=DV(1+q+…+qn)→DV11−q(peak),DVq1−q(trough).C = \frac{D}{V}\,(1 + q + \dots + q^n) \;\to\; \frac{D}{V}\frac{1}{1 - q}\ \text{(peak)}, \qquad \frac{D}{V}\frac{q}{1 - q}\ \text{(trough)}.

A dose falling exactly at time tt is already counted (peak level). The peak-to-trough ratio is 1/q=ekτ1/q = e^{k\tau} – exactly 2 for a dosing interval of one half-life.

Two compartments. With x1x_1, x2x_2 the amounts in the central and peripheral compartment,

ddt(x1x2)=(−(k10+k12)k21k12−k21)(x1x2);\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}x_1\\ x_2\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}-(k_{10} + k_{12}) & k_{21}\\ k_{12} & -k_{21}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_1\\ x_2\end{pmatrix};

the eigenvalues −α-\alpha, −β-\beta satisfy λ2+sλ+k10k21=0\lambda^2 + s\lambda + k_{10}k_{21} = 0 (trace −s-s, determinant k10k21k_{10}k_{21}) – hence the hybrid constants and αβ=k10k21\alpha\beta = k_{10}k_{21}. AA and BB follow from C(0)=D/V1C(0) = D/V_1 and C˙(0)=−(k10+k12)D/V1\dot C(0) = -(k_{10} + k_{12})D/V_1. Integrated, AUC⁡=A/α+B/β=D/(V1k10)\mathrm{AUC} = A/\alpha + B/\beta = D/(V_1 k_{10}): elimination happens only from the central compartment.

Infusion. During the dose VC˙=R0−VkCV\dot C = R_0 - VkC with the solution above; after the end TT the level reached falls with e−k(t−T)e^{-k(t - T)}. Both are continuous at t=Tt = T.

Non-compartmental analysis

From measured concentrations without a model: area AUC with the trapezoidal rule, linear for rising, logarithmic for falling values (exact for exponential decay); λz\lambda_z from the log-linear fit to the last points; from that t1/2=ln⁡2/λzt_{1/2} = \ln 2/\lambda_z, AUC∞⁡=AUC⁡+Clast/λz\mathrm{AUC}_\infty = \mathrm{AUC} + C_{last}/\lambda_z, AUMC∞⁡\mathrm{AUMC}_\infty likewise, mean residence time MRT⁡=AUMC∞⁡/AUC∞⁡\mathrm{MRT} = \mathrm{AUMC}_\infty/\mathrm{AUC}_\infty and clearance D/AUC∞⁡D/\mathrm{AUC}_\infty.

Why logarithmic on the decline. Between two measurements with \(c_i < c_{i-1}\) the code assumes the exponential course \(c(t) = c_{i-1}e^{-k_i(t - t_{i-1})}\) with \(k_i = \ln(c_{i-1}/c_i)/\Delta t\). Then

∫ti−1ticdt=ci−1−ciki,∫ti−1titcdt=ci−1ti−1−citiki+ci−1−ciki2,\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_i} c\,dt = \frac{c_{i-1} - c_i}{k_i}, \qquad \int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_i} t\,c\,dt = \frac{c_{i-1}t_{i-1} - c_i t_i}{k_i} + \frac{c_{i-1} - c_i}{k_i^2},

and the tail areas behind the last measurement are Cl/λzC_l/\lambda_z and Cltl/λz+Cl/λz2C_l t_l/\lambda_z + C_l/\lambda_z^2 (integration by parts). For a true exponential decline all parts are exact; the linear trapezoidal rule, by contrast, overestimates a convex decline in every interval. λz\lambda_z is the slope of the least-squares line of ln⁡c\ln c over tt through the last points. For a one-compartment bolus this gives MRT⁡=1/k\mathrm{MRT} = 1/k – the mean residence time of a molecule.

How it is checked

  • SEIR: conservation to 1e-15, final size as SIR; attack rate 0.89264 at R0=2.5R_0 = 2.5; peak in closed form to 1e-8 (found with Brent), S=N/R0S = N/R_0 there; β\beta, γ\gamma recovered from generated data.
  • Bateman: tmaxt_{max} in closed form, differential equation satisfied, limit ka=kk_a = k continuous; infusion continuous, steady state R0/(Vk)R_0/(Vk); trough against the series.
  • Two compartments against Dormand-Prince (1e-9), αβ=k10k21\alpha\beta = k_{10}k_{21}, AUC = D/(V1k10)D/(V_1 k_{10}); NCA on exact exponential data exact.

Limits

Deterministic models with fixed rates; no age structure, no non-linear (saturable) elimination.
