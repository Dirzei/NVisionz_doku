Structural reliability – background

The computation behind Form, Stichprobe and Teilmengen: mapping to standard normal space, reliability index and design point after Hasofer-Lind (FORM), curvature correction after Breitung (SORM), Monte Carlo, importance sampling around the design point and subset simulation after Au and Beck – with pseudocode, test cases and limits.

What it is about

A component fails when the load exceeds the resistance; both scatter. With a limit-state function g(x)g(x) of the basic variables (failure at g≤0g \le 0) the probability of failure is Pf=P[g(X)≤0]P_f = P[g(X) \le 0] – an integral of the joint density over the failure domain, usually in many dimensions and with a very small value (10⁻³ to 10⁻⁷). Plain counting then needs millions of evaluations of gg. The methods on this page use the geometry of the problem in standard normal space.

Standard normal space

Independent basic variables with distribution function FiF_i are obtained from standard normal ziz_i via xi=Fi−1(Φ(zi))x_i = F_i^{-1}(\Phi(z_i)); for normal variables this is μ+σz\mu + \sigma z, for lognormal ones exp⁡(λ+ζz)\exp(\lambda + \zeta z) with ζ2=ln⁡(1+σ2/μ2)\zeta^2 = \ln(1 + \sigma^2/\mu^2), λ=ln⁡μ−ζ2/2\lambda = \ln\mu - \zeta^2/2. Correlations are given as a correlation matrix of the zz: z=Luz = L u with the Cholesky factor and independent uu (for normal marginals that is the correlation of the xx, otherwise the Nataf correlation, supplied by the caller). Thus G(u)=g(x(u))G(u) = g(x(u)) and Pf=∫G≤0φn(u)duP_f = \int_{G \le 0}\varphi_n(u)\,du.

FORM after Hasofer and Lind

The design point is the point of the limit surface closest to the origin,

u*=arg⁡min⁡12|u|2subject toG(u)=0,β=±|u*|,Pf≈Φ(−β),α=u*/β.u^* = \arg\min \tfrac12|u|^2 \quad\text{subject to}\quad G(u) = 0, \qquad \beta = \pm|u^*|, \qquad P_f \approx \Phi(-\beta), \qquad \alpha = u^*/\beta.

It is solved with the SQP method from framework-math (gradient of GG by differences). β\beta is negative if the origin already fails (G(0)≤0G(0) \le 0). The sensitivity factors αi\alpha_i tell how much each variable contributes to the probability of failure (∑αi2=1\sum\alpha_i^2 = 1). For a linear limit state of normal variables FORM is exact: g=R−Sg = R - S gives β=(μR−μS)/σR2+σS2−2ρσRσS\beta = (\mu_R - \mu_S)/\sqrt{\sigma_R^2 + \sigma_S^2 - 2\rho\sigma_R\sigma_S}. Lognormal R−SR - S is a plane in standard normal space as well (ln⁡R−ln⁡S=0\ln R - \ln S = 0), hence also exact.

SORM after Breitung

If the limit surface is curved, Breitung (1984) corrects with the principal curvatures κi\kappa_i at the design point:

Pf≈Φ(−β)∏i=1n−1(1+βκi)−1/2.P_f \approx \Phi(-\beta)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(1 + \beta\kappa_i)^{-1/2}.

The curvatures are the eigenvalues of the Hessian of GG (central differences), rotated into the tangent plane (Gram-Schmidt around the direction −∇G/|∇G|-\nabla G/|\nabla G|) and divided by |∇G||\nabla G| – so they do not depend on the scale of gg. Positive means the failure domain bulges away from the origin and PfP_f gets smaller. The formula is asymptotic for large β\beta; on the paraboloid β=3\beta = 3, κ=0.2\kappa = 0.2 it lies 4.9 % above the exact value ∫0∞Φ(−β−κt)e−tdt\int_0^\infty\Phi(-\beta - \kappa t)\,e^{-t}\,dt. If 1+βκi≤01 + \beta\kappa_i \le 0 the point is not a minimum of the distance, and the method reports it.

Monte Carlo and importance sampling

Plain Monte Carlo counts P^f=1N∑1[G(ui)≤0]\hat P_f = \frac1N\sum 1[G(u_i) \le 0] with standard error P^f(1−P^f)/N\sqrt{\hat P_f(1 - \hat P_f)/N}; a coefficient of variation of 10 % needs about 100/Pf100/P_f samples. Importance sampling around the design point draws from N(u*,I)N(u^*, I) and weights with the density ratio

w(v)=φn(v)φn(v−u*)=exp⁡(−u*Tv+12|u*|2),P^f=1N∑w(vi)1[G(vi)≤0].w(v) = \frac{\varphi_n(v)}{\varphi_n(v - u^*)} = \exp\big(-u^{*\mathsf{T}} v + \tfrac12|u^*|^2\big), \qquad \hat P_f = \frac1N\sum w(v_i)\,1[G(v_i) \le 0].

The estimator is unbiased for any centre; around u*u^* about half of the samples fall into the failure domain and the scatter is small (at β=3.2\beta = 3.2 and 20 000 samples a coefficient of variation of 1.3 %).

Subset simulation

Au and Beck (2001) write the small probability as a product of conditional, moderate ones: with thresholds b1>b2>…>bm=0b_1 > b_2 > \ldots > b_m = 0 and Fj={G≤bj}F_j = \{G \le b_j\}

Pf=P(F1)∏j=2mP(Fj∣Fj−1).P_f = P(F_1)\prod_{j=2}^m P(F_j \mid F_{j-1}).

Each threshold is the p0p_0 quantile (usually p0=0.1p_0 = 0.1) of the limit-state function at that level; the Np0N p_0 best points seed Markov chains of length 1/p01/p_0 that sample the conditional distribution with the modified Metropolis algorithm: per coordinate a uniform step, accepted with min⁡(1,φ(ξ)/φ(uk))\min(1, \varphi(\xi)/\varphi(u_k)), the whole candidate rejected if it is not in Fj−1F_{j-1}.

u₁…u_N ~ N(0, I);  P ← 1
repeat:
    sort by G;  b ← p₀ quantile
    if b ≤ 0:  P ← P · fraction(G ≤ 0);  done
    P ← P · p₀
    for each of the N p₀ seeds: chain of length 1/p₀
        per coordinate ξ = u_k + s·U(−1, 1), accepted with φ(ξ)/φ(u_k)
        keep candidate if G ≤ b, else repeat old point

The reported coefficient of variation assumes independent samples and underestimates the true scatter (the chains are correlated). In the probe one run with 1000 samples per level at β=3.2\beta = 3.2 scatters by 27 %; the mean of 20 runs lies 0.7 standard errors from Φ(−β)\Phi(-\beta).

How it is checked

  • FORM: linear R−SR - S with and without correlation, design point and α\alpha in closed form, origin in failure gives negative β\beta; lognormal exact, also via the quantile function of the distribution class.
  • SORM: paraboloid with two curvatures in closed form, against the exact probability, opposite curvature raises PfP_f, fourfold gg gives the same.
  • Sampling with fixed seed within four standard errors of Φ(−β)\Phi(-\beta), same seed same number; subsets over 20 runs and on the paraboloid.

Limits

FORM and SORM find one design point; with several equivalent points (systems, limit surfaces with several branches) they underestimate PfP_f. Breitung is asymptotic and useless for βκ\beta\kappa near −1. The Nataf correlation of non-normal marginals is not computed by the module. Sampling evaluates gg densely, without parallelisation.
