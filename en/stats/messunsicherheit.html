Measurement uncertainty, confidence bands and calibration – background

The computation behind Gum, Konfidenzband, Kalibrierung and Fuenflochsonde: propagation after the GUM of first and second order, Welch-Satterthwaite, Monte Carlo after Supplement 1; bands of non-linear models with the delta method; inverse prediction on calibration lines with Fieller's interval; calibration polynomials of a five-hole probe.

What it is about

A measurement result is only complete with its uncertainty. If a quantity y=f(x1,…,xn)y = f(x_1, \dots, x_n) depends on inputs with standard uncertainties uiu_i, the GUM requires propagating them. If a model is fitted to measured values, one wants to know how accurate the fitted curve and a new measurement are. And an instrument is calibrated so that one can compute back from the reading to the measurand – with its uncertainty.

GUM: propagation of first and second order

With the sensitivities ci=∂f/∂xic_i = \partial f/\partial x_i (central differences) and the correlations rijr_{ij}:

uc2=∑i,jcicjuiujrij.u_c^2 = \sum_{i,j} c_i c_j\, u_i u_j\, r_{ij}.

Where the formula comes from. The Taylor expansion about the estimates, y−f(x)≈∑ici(xi−xi)y - f(\bar x) \approx \sum_i c_i (x_i - \bar x_i), is a linear sum; its variance is ∑i,jcicjCov⁡(xi,xj)\sum_{i,j} c_i c_j \operatorname{Cov}(x_i, x_j) with Cov⁡=uiujrij\operatorname{Cov} = u_i u_j r_{ij}. The formula therefore holds for any distribution of the inputs, as soon as ff is practically linear over roughly ±2ui\pm 2u_i. The sensitivities are differentiated centrally with step hi=10−3uih_i = 10^{-3}u_i; the error is O(hi2f''')O(h_i^2 f'''), relatively 10−610^{-6} – far below any uncertainty statement.

If ff is markedly non-linear, the second-order terms for uncorrelated normal inputs are added (GUM 5.1.2, note): ∑i,j(12fij2+fifijj)ui2uj2\sum_{i,j}\big(\tfrac12 f_{ij}^2 + f_i f_{ijj}\big)u_i^2 u_j^2. For the product x1x2x_1 x_2 this gives exactly the exact variance. The effective degrees of freedom after Welch-Satterthwaite νeff=uc4/∑i(ciui)4/νi\nu_{\text{eff}} = u_c^4/\sum_i (c_i u_i)^4/\nu_i determine the coverage factor k=t(1+p)/2(νeff)k = t_{(1+p)/2}(\nu_{\text{eff}}).

Second order. Keeping the quadratic Taylor term 12∑fijδiδj\tfrac12\sum f_{ij}\,\delta_i\delta_j and using Eδi4=3ui4E\delta_i^4 = 3u_i^4 for independent normal quantities, multiplying out leaves exactly the terms above; the third derivative fijjf_{ijj} comes from the mixed product of the linear and the cubic term. For the product y=x1x2y = x_1x_2, f12=1f_{12} = 1, all third derivatives vanish, and the result is x22u12+x12u22+u12u22x_2^2u_1^2 + x_1^2u_2^2 + u_1^2u_2^2 – the exact variance.

Coverage factor. Welch-Satterthwaite replaces the distribution of uc2u_c^2, a sum of estimated variances, by a scaled chi-square with the same mean and variance; νeff\nu_{\text{eff}} follows from that. Type B inputs (without degrees of freedom) count with νi=∞\nu_i = \infty and contribute nothing to the denominator. If there is no finite νi\nu_i at all, the code takes the normal quantile, so k=2k = 2 at 95.45 %.

Monte Carlo after Supplement 1. The inputs are drawn from their distributions (normal, rectangular, triangular, t), ff is evaluated at each set; from the sample follow mean, standard deviation, the probabilistically symmetric and the shortest coverage interval. Example 9.2.3 of the Supplement (sum of four rectangular quantities with u=1u = 1): standard deviation 2.00, 95 % interval ±3.88 (the GUF route gives ±3.92).

Drawing with standard deviation uu. Each quantity is drawn as xi+uizx_i + u_i z, zz with mean 0 and variance 1: rectangular z=3(2U−1)z = \sqrt3\,(2U - 1) (the uniform distribution on [−1,1][-1, 1] has variance 1/31/3), triangular z=6(U1+U2−1)z = \sqrt6\,(U_1 + U_2 - 1) (sum of two uniforms, variance 1/61/6, support ±6\pm\sqrt6), t-distributed z=(ν−2)/νN/χν2/νz = \sqrt{(\nu - 2)/\nu}\; N/\sqrt{\chi^2_\nu/\nu}. The chi-square with any, also non-integer, ν\nu is 2Γ(ν/2)2\,\Gamma(\nu/2), drawn after Marsaglia and Tsang. So here uiu_i is the standard deviation of the t quantity; Supplement 1 instead scales the t distribution by uiu_i itself (variance ui2ν/(ν−2)u_i^2\nu/(\nu - 2)) – to follow its convention, enter uiν/(ν−2)u_i\sqrt{\nu/(\nu - 2)}.

Shortest interval. From the sorted draws y(1)≤…≤y(m)y_{(1)} \le \dots \le y_{(m)} and r=round⁡(pm)r = \mathrm{round}(pm), the probabilistically symmetric interval is [y(l),y(l+r−1)][y_{(l)}, y_{(l + r - 1)}] with l=round⁡((1−p)m/2)l = \mathrm{round}((1 - p)m/2), the shortest one the narrowest of all windows [y(i),y(i+r−1)][y_{(i)}, y_{(i + r - 1)}] – one pass in O(m)O(m) after sorting in O(mlog⁡m)O(m\log m). For skewed distributions the two differ clearly: for y=x2y = x^2, xx standard normal, the shortest 95 % interval starts at 0 and ends at 3.84, the symmetric one reaches 5.02.

Bands of non-linear models

A model y=f(x,θ)y = f(x, \theta) is fitted with Levenberg-Marquardt; with the Jacobian JJ (central differences) at the optimum s2=RSS⁡/(n−p)s^2 = \mathrm{RSS}/(n - p) and Cov⁡θ=s2(JTJ)−1\operatorname{Cov}\theta = s^2 (J^T J)^{-1}. At a point x0x_0 with the gradient g=∂f(x0,θ)/∂θg = \partial f(x_0, \theta)/\partial\theta (delta method):

confidence band ±tn−pgTCov⁡g,prediction band ±tn−ps2+gTCov⁡g.\text{confidence band } \pm t_{n-p}\sqrt{g^T\operatorname{Cov}\,g}, \qquad \text{prediction band } \pm t_{n-p}\sqrt{s^2 + g^T\operatorname{Cov}\,g}.

The delta method is the same linearisation as in the GUM, only with θ\theta as inputs: Var⁡f(x0,θ^)≈gTCov⁡θg\operatorname{Var} f(x_0, \hat\theta) \approx g^T\operatorname{Cov}\theta\, g. The prediction band adds the scatter s2s^2 of a single new measurement, which is independent of θ^\hat\theta.

For the straight line these are exactly the well-known formulas ts1/n+(x0−x)2/Sxxt s\sqrt{1/n + (x_0 - \bar x)^2/S_{xx}} and with 1+1 + under the root.

Calibration line and inverse prediction

From the line y=a+bxy = a + b x (n points) and the mean y0y_0 of mm new readings follows x0=(y0−a)/bx_0 = (y_0 - a)/b with

u(x0)=s|b|1m+1n+(y0−y)2b2Sxx.u(x_0) = \frac{s}{|b|}\sqrt{\frac1m + \frac1n + \frac{(y_0 - \bar y)^2}{b^2 S_{xx}}}.

This is again the delta method, now for x0=(y0−a)/bx_0 = (y_0 - a)/b with the three sources y0y_0 (variance s2/ms^2/m), aa and bb; since y\bar y and bb are uncorrelated, one writes a=y−bxa = \bar y - b\bar x and obtains the three terms under the root.

The exact confidence interval after Fieller is the set of xx with (y0−a−bx)2≤t2s2(1m+1n+(x−x)2Sxx)(y_0 - a - b x)^2 \le t^2 s^2\big(\frac1m + \frac1n + \frac{(x - \bar x)^2}{S_{xx}}\big) – a quadratic inequality in xx, whose interval lies almost symmetrically around x0x_0 for a steep line. Multiplied out it reads Ax2+Bx+C≤0A x^2 + Bx + C \le 0 with A=b2−t2s2/SxxA = b^2 - t^2s^2/S_{xx}; only for A>0A > 0, i.e. when the slope differs significantly from zero, is the region a finite interval. For calibration polynomials of higher order Newton computes back.

Five-hole probe

A five-hole probe measures flow angles and pressures with five holes (centre 5, top 1, right 2, bottom 3, left 4). With pm=(p1+p2+p3+p4)/4p_m = (p_1 + p_2 + p_3 + p_4)/4 and D=p5−pmD = p_5 - p_m the coefficients are

Cα=p1−p3D,Cβ=p2−p4D,Ct=pt−p5D,Cs=pm−psD.C_\alpha = \frac{p_1 - p_3}{D}, \quad C_\beta = \frac{p_2 - p_4}{D}, \quad C_t = \frac{p_t - p_5}{D}, \quad C_s = \frac{p_m - p_s}{D}.

Dividing by DD, a pressure of the order of the dynamic pressure, makes the coefficients independent of the approach velocity – as long as Reynolds and Mach number effects are small. For a sphere in potential flow p−ps=q(1−94sin2⁡θ)p - p_s = q\,(1 - \tfrac94\sin^2\theta) at the surface angle θ\theta to the oncoming flow; with holes at 45° this gives D=1.125qD = 1.125\,q and Cs=−1/9C_s = -1/9 on the axis.

In calibration α\alpha, β\beta, CtC_t and CsC_s are fitted as polynomials of total order mm in (Cα,Cβ)(C_\alpha, C_\beta) by least squares; in measurement the five pressures give angles, total and static pressure – reliable only within the calibrated angle range.

How it is checked

  • GUM: linear model by hand without and with correlation (1e-9); product with second order exact (1e-6), Monte Carlo meets the exact standard deviation; Welch-Satterthwaite by hand; Supplement 1 example ±3.88.
  • Bands: data set Misra1a of the NIST reference collection StRD – parameters to 1e-8, standard errors to 1e-6 against the certified values; the straight line as special case to 1e-8.
  • Calibration: inverse prediction and uu by hand (1e-15), Fieller limits satisfy their equation (1e-10); polynomial of order 2 recovered exactly.
  • Five-hole probe: as forward model a spherical probe in potential flow; calibration on 25 × 25 points up to ±30° with a polynomial of order 11, at 200 intermediate points angles to 0.05°, pressures to 0.2 % of the dynamic pressure.

Limits

Second order only for uncorrelated inputs. Welch-Satterthwaite assumes independent variance estimates; with correlated inputs νeff\nu_{\text{eff}} is only an approximation. Monte Carlo sampling knows no correlations, and its accuracy grows only with 1/m1/\sqrt m – two valid digits of the standard deviation need about 10510^5 draws. Bands with linear approximation (delta method) – too optimistic for strongly non-linear models and few points. Five-hole probe without Reynolds and Mach dependence of the calibration.
