Measurement uncertainty, confidence bands and calibration – background

The computation behind Gum, Konfidenzband, Kalibrierung and Fuenflochsonde: propagation after the GUM of first and second order, Welch-Satterthwaite, Monte Carlo after Supplement 1; bands of non-linear models with the delta method; inverse prediction on calibration lines with Fieller's interval; calibration polynomials of a five-hole probe; flow measurement with differential pressure devices after ISO 5167 and critical nozzles after ISO 9300 (Durchfluss); platinum resistance thermometers after IEC 60751 (Widerstandsthermometer); thermocouples after ITS-90 (Thermoelement); corrections of a temperature probe and averaging over a cross-section (Sonde); data reconciliation after the method of VDI 2048 (Abgleich).

What it is about

A measurement result is only complete with its uncertainty. If a quantity y=f(x1,…,xn)y = f(x_1, \dots, x_n) depends on inputs with standard uncertainties uiu_i, the GUM requires propagating them. If a model is fitted to measured values, one wants to know how accurate the fitted curve and a new measurement are. And an instrument is calibrated so that one can compute back from the reading to the measurand – with its uncertainty.

GUM: propagation of first and second order

With the sensitivities ci=∂f/∂xic_i = \partial f/\partial x_i (central differences) and the correlations rijr_{ij}:

uc2=∑i,jcicjuiujrij.u_c^2 = \sum_{i,j} c_i c_j\, u_i u_j\, r_{ij}.

Where the formula comes from. The Taylor expansion about the estimates, y−f(x)≈∑ici(xi−xi)y - f(\bar x) \approx \sum_i c_i (x_i - \bar x_i), is a linear sum; its variance is ∑i,jcicjCov⁡(xi,xj)\sum_{i,j} c_i c_j \operatorname{Cov}(x_i, x_j) with Cov⁡=uiujrij\operatorname{Cov} = u_i u_j r_{ij}. The formula therefore holds for any distribution of the inputs, as soon as ff is practically linear over roughly ±2ui\pm 2u_i. The sensitivities are differentiated centrally with step hi=10−3uih_i = 10^{-3}u_i; the error is O(hi2f''')O(h_i^2 f'''), relatively 10−610^{-6} – far below any uncertainty statement.

If ff is markedly non-linear, the second-order terms for uncorrelated normal inputs are added (GUM 5.1.2, note): ∑i,j(12fij2+fifijj)ui2uj2\sum_{i,j}\big(\tfrac12 f_{ij}^2 + f_i f_{ijj}\big)u_i^2 u_j^2. For the product x1x2x_1 x_2 this gives exactly the exact variance. The effective degrees of freedom after Welch-Satterthwaite νeff=uc4/∑i(ciui)4/νi\nu_{\text{eff}} = u_c^4/\sum_i (c_i u_i)^4/\nu_i determine the coverage factor k=t(1+p)/2(νeff)k = t_{(1+p)/2}(\nu_{\text{eff}}).

Second order. Keeping the quadratic Taylor term 12∑fijδiδj\tfrac12\sum f_{ij}\,\delta_i\delta_j and using Eδi4=3ui4E\delta_i^4 = 3u_i^4 for independent normal quantities, multiplying out leaves exactly the terms above; the third derivative fijjf_{ijj} comes from the mixed product of the linear and the cubic term. For the product y=x1x2y = x_1x_2, f12=1f_{12} = 1, all third derivatives vanish, and the result is x22u12+x12u22+u12u22x_2^2u_1^2 + x_1^2u_2^2 + u_1^2u_2^2 – the exact variance.

Coverage factor. Welch-Satterthwaite replaces the distribution of uc2u_c^2, a sum of estimated variances, by a scaled chi-square with the same mean and variance; νeff\nu_{\text{eff}} follows from that. Type B inputs (without degrees of freedom) count with νi=∞\nu_i = \infty and contribute nothing to the denominator. If there is no finite νi\nu_i at all, the code takes the normal quantile, so k=2k = 2 at 95.45 %.

Monte Carlo after Supplement 1. The inputs are drawn from their distributions (normal, rectangular, triangular, t), ff is evaluated at each set; from the sample follow mean, standard deviation, the probabilistically symmetric and the shortest coverage interval. Example 9.2.3 of the Supplement (sum of four rectangular quantities with u=1u = 1): standard deviation 2.00, 95 % interval ±3.88 (the GUF route gives ±3.92).

Drawing with standard deviation uu. Each quantity is drawn as xi+uizx_i + u_i z, zz with mean 0 and variance 1: rectangular z=3(2U−1)z = \sqrt3\,(2U - 1) (the uniform distribution on [−1,1][-1, 1] has variance 1/31/3), triangular z=6(U1+U2−1)z = \sqrt6\,(U_1 + U_2 - 1) (sum of two uniforms, variance 1/61/6, support ±6\pm\sqrt6), t-distributed z=(ν−2)/νN/χν2/νz = \sqrt{(\nu - 2)/\nu}\; N/\sqrt{\chi^2_\nu/\nu}. The chi-square with any, also non-integer, ν\nu is 2Γ(ν/2)2\,\Gamma(\nu/2), drawn after Marsaglia and Tsang. So here uiu_i is the standard deviation of the t quantity; Supplement 1 instead scales the t distribution by uiu_i itself (variance ui2ν/(ν−2)u_i^2\nu/(\nu - 2)) – to follow its convention, enter uiν/(ν−2)u_i\sqrt{\nu/(\nu - 2)}.

Shortest interval. From the sorted draws y(1)≤…≤y(m)y_{(1)} \le \dots \le y_{(m)} and r=round⁡(pm)r = \mathrm{round}(pm), the probabilistically symmetric interval is [y(l),y(l+r−1)][y_{(l)}, y_{(l + r - 1)}] with l=round⁡((1−p)m/2)l = \mathrm{round}((1 - p)m/2), the shortest one the narrowest of all windows [y(i),y(i+r−1)][y_{(i)}, y_{(i + r - 1)}] – one pass in O(m)O(m) after sorting in O(mlog⁡m)O(m\log m). For skewed distributions the two differ clearly: for y=x2y = x^2, xx standard normal, the shortest 95 % interval starts at 0 and ends at 3.84, the symmetric one reaches 5.02.

Bands of non-linear models

A model y=f(x,θ)y = f(x, \theta) is fitted with Levenberg-Marquardt; with the Jacobian JJ (central differences) at the optimum s2=RSS⁡/(n−p)s^2 = \mathrm{RSS}/(n - p) and Cov⁡θ=s2(JTJ)−1\operatorname{Cov}\theta = s^2 (J^T J)^{-1}. At a point x0x_0 with the gradient g=∂f(x0,θ)/∂θg = \partial f(x_0, \theta)/\partial\theta (delta method):

confidence band ±tn−pgTCov⁡g,prediction band ±tn−ps2+gTCov⁡g.\text{confidence band } \pm t_{n-p}\sqrt{g^T\operatorname{Cov}\,g}, \qquad \text{prediction band } \pm t_{n-p}\sqrt{s^2 + g^T\operatorname{Cov}\,g}.

The delta method is the same linearisation as in the GUM, only with θ\theta as inputs: Var⁡f(x0,θ^)≈gTCov⁡θg\operatorname{Var} f(x_0, \hat\theta) \approx g^T\operatorname{Cov}\theta\, g. The prediction band adds the scatter s2s^2 of a single new measurement, which is independent of θ^\hat\theta.

For the straight line these are exactly the well-known formulas ts1/n+(x0−x)2/Sxxt s\sqrt{1/n + (x_0 - \bar x)^2/S_{xx}} and with 1+1 + under the root.

Calibration line and inverse prediction

From the line y=a+bxy = a + b x (n points) and the mean y0y_0 of mm new readings follows x0=(y0−a)/bx_0 = (y_0 - a)/b with

u(x0)=s|b|1m+1n+(y0−y)2b2Sxx.u(x_0) = \frac{s}{|b|}\sqrt{\frac1m + \frac1n + \frac{(y_0 - \bar y)^2}{b^2 S_{xx}}}.

This is again the delta method, now for x0=(y0−a)/bx_0 = (y_0 - a)/b with the three sources y0y_0 (variance s2/ms^2/m), aa and bb; since y\bar y and bb are uncorrelated, one writes a=y−bxa = \bar y - b\bar x and obtains the three terms under the root.

The exact confidence interval after Fieller is the set of xx with (y0−a−bx)2≤t2s2(1m+1n+(x−x)2Sxx)(y_0 - a - b x)^2 \le t^2 s^2\big(\frac1m + \frac1n + \frac{(x - \bar x)^2}{S_{xx}}\big) – a quadratic inequality in xx, whose interval lies almost symmetrically around x0x_0 for a steep line. Multiplied out it reads Ax2+Bx+C≤0A x^2 + Bx + C \le 0 with A=b2−t2s2/SxxA = b^2 - t^2s^2/S_{xx}; only for A>0A > 0, i.e. when the slope differs significantly from zero, is the region a finite interval. For calibration polynomials of higher order Newton computes back.

Five-hole probe

A five-hole probe measures flow angles and pressures with five holes (centre 5, top 1, right 2, bottom 3, left 4). With pm=(p1+p2+p3+p4)/4p_m = (p_1 + p_2 + p_3 + p_4)/4 and D=p5−pmD = p_5 - p_m the coefficients are

Cα=p1−p3D,Cβ=p2−p4D,Ct=pt−p5D,Cs=pm−psD.C_\alpha = \frac{p_1 - p_3}{D}, \quad C_\beta = \frac{p_2 - p_4}{D}, \quad C_t = \frac{p_t - p_5}{D}, \quad C_s = \frac{p_m - p_s}{D}.

Dividing by DD, a pressure of the order of the dynamic pressure, makes the coefficients independent of the approach velocity – as long as Reynolds and Mach number effects are small. For a sphere in potential flow p−ps=q(1−94sin2⁡θ)p - p_s = q\,(1 - \tfrac94\sin^2\theta) at the surface angle θ\theta to the oncoming flow; with holes at 45° this gives D=1.125qD = 1.125\,q and Cs=−1/9C_s = -1/9 on the axis.

In calibration α\alpha, β\beta, CtC_t and CsC_s are fitted as polynomials of total order mm in (Cα,Cβ)(C_\alpha, C_\beta) by least squares; in measurement the five pressures give angles, total and static pressure – reliable only within the calibrated angle range.

Flow measurement with differential pressure devices

Orifice plates, nozzles and Venturi tubes narrow the cross-section; the differential pressure Δp=p1−p2\Delta p = p_1 - p_2 between inlet and throat measures the mass flow. With β=d/D\beta = d/D all three share the basic equation (ISO 5167-1)

qm=C1−β4επ4d22Δpρ1.q_m = \frac{C}{\sqrt{1 - \beta^4}}\,\varepsilon\,\frac{\pi}{4}d^2\sqrt{2\,\Delta p\,\rho_1}.

Where it comes from. Bernoulli between inlet and throat together with continuity w1=β2w2w_1 = \beta^2 w_2 gives w2=2Δp/ρ/1−β4w_2 = \sqrt{2\Delta p/\rho}\,/\sqrt{1-\beta^4} – the velocity-of-approach factor. The real flow deviates (vena contracta, friction, tapping position); the discharge coefficient CC collects that. For gases the density changes on the way to the throat; the expansibility factor ε≤1\varepsilon \le 1 accounts for it.

For the orifice plate CC is the Reader-Harris/Gallagher equation (ISO 5167-2, 5.3.2.1): a base value 0.5961+0.0261β2−0.216β80.5961 + 0.0261\beta^2 - 0.216\beta^8, terms in 1/ReD1/Re_D, terms depending on the tapping position (L1L_1, L2L_2: corner, flange, D and D/2), and an extra term for pipes below 71.12 mm. The expansibility of the orifice is empirical, ε=1−(0.351+0.256β4+0.93β8)(1−(p2/p1)1/κ)\varepsilon = 1 - (0.351 + 0.256\beta^4 + 0.93\beta^8)\big(1 - (p_2/p_1)^{1/\kappa}\big).

Iteration over the Reynolds number. CC depends on ReD=4qm/(πDμ)Re_D = 4q_m/(\pi D\mu), and qmq_m on CC. Starting from ReD→∞Re_D \to \infty the fixed point is sought; since CC depends only weakly on ReDRe_D, the map is strongly contracting. The result is self-consistent: the Reynolds number of the result gives the same CC again. Outside the limits of the standard (β\beta, DD, ReDRe_D, p2/p1p_2/p_1) the calculation continues, but every violation is reported.

Nozzles and Venturi tubes accelerate without separation; there the expansibility of isentropic flow applies, which follows in closed form from the energy and mass balance (τ=p2/p1\tau = p_2/p_1):

ε2=κτ2/κκ−11−β41−β4τ2/κ1−τ(κ−1)/κ1−τ.\varepsilon^2 = \frac{\kappa\,\tau^{2/\kappa}}{\kappa - 1}\,\frac{1 - \beta^4}{1 - \beta^4\tau^{2/\kappa}}\,\frac{1 - \tau^{(\kappa-1)/\kappa}}{1 - \tau}.

Permanent pressure loss of the orifice (ISO 5167-2, 5.4): Δϖ/Δp=(1−β4(1−C2)−Cβ2)/(1−β4(1−C2)+Cβ2)\Delta\varpi/\Delta p = \big(\sqrt{1 - \beta^4(1 - C^2)} - C\beta^2\big)/\big(\sqrt{1 - \beta^4(1 - C^2)} + C\beta^2\big).

Critical flow nozzle (ISO 9300). Below the critical pressure ratio p*/p0=(2/(κ+1))κ/(κ−1)p^*/p_0 = \big(2/(\kappa+1)\big)^{\kappa/(\kappa-1)} the throat is sonic and the mass flow depends only on the stagnation state:

qm=A*CdC*p0RT0,C*=κ(2κ+1)κ+12(κ−1).q_m = \frac{A^*\,C_d\,C^*\,p_0}{\sqrt{R\,T_0}}, \qquad C^* = \sqrt\kappa\left(\frac{2}{\kappa+1}\right)^{\frac{\kappa+1}{2(\kappa-1)}}.

For κ=1.4\kappa = 1.4, C*=1.4(5/6)3=0.684731C^* = \sqrt{1.4}\,(5/6)^3 = 0.684731 and p*/p0=0.528282p^*/p_0 = 0.528282.

Platinum resistance thermometer

The Callendar-Van Dusen characteristic (IEC 60751) is

R(t)=R0(1+At+Bt2+C(t−100)t3),C=0 for t≥0∘C,R(t) = R_0\big(1 + At + Bt^2 + C\,(t - 100)\,t^3\big), \qquad C = 0 \text{ for } t \ge 0\,^\circ\mathrm{C},

with A=3.9083⋅10−3A = 3.9083\cdot10^{-3}, B=−5.775⋅10−7B = -5.775\cdot10^{-7}, C=−4.183⋅10−12C = -4.183\cdot10^{-12}. The mean temperature coefficient (R(100)−R(0))/(100R0)=0.00385055(R(100) - R(0))/(100R_0) = 0.00385055 is the nominal 0.00385 of the standard. Both branches meet at 0 °C with equal value and slope R0AR_0A, because the CC term vanishes there with t3t^3.

Inversion. Above 0 °C, R/R0=1+At+Bt2R/R_0 = 1 + At + Bt^2 is quadratic; the physical root is t=(−A+A2−4B(1−R/R0))/(2B)t = \big(-A + \sqrt{A^2 - 4B(1 - R/R_0)}\big)/(2B). Below, the equation is quartic and is solved with Brent between −200 °C and 0 °C, where it is strictly monotonic.

Tolerances (IEC 60751:2008, table 2): AA 0.1+0.0017|t|0.1 + 0.0017|t|, A 0.15+0.002|t|0.15 + 0.002|t|, B 0.3+0.005|t|0.3 + 0.005|t|, C 0.6+0.01|t|0.6 + 0.01|t| in K.

Thermocouples after ITS-90

For the eight standard types B, E, J, K, N, R, S and T NIST (Monograph 175, database SRD 60) gives the thermoelectric voltage with the reference junction at 0 °C as a reference function in sub-ranges,

E(t)=∑i=0nciti[+a0ea1(t−a2)2for type K from 0∘C],E(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} c_i\,t^i \;\big[\,+\;a_0\,e^{a_1 (t - a_2)^2}\ \text{for type K from } 0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\big],

with EE in mV and tt in °C. The coefficients are taken verbatim from the file allcoeff.tab (transferred by program, not typed). The tables of the same source round to 1 µV; the reference function must meet every table value at every 10 °C node to half a unit of the last digit. At the sub-range limits (B 630.615 °C; J 760 °C; R and S 1064.18 °C and 1664.5 °C) the pieces join continuously.

Inversion. NIST also gives approximate polynomials t≈∑diEit \approx \sum d_i E^i with error bands of a few hundredths of a kelvin. Here they only serve as a start: Newton on the reference function, bracketed by the range of the approximation, inverts EE as accurately as the reference function itself (measured below 1e-9 K; for type R at 1664.5 °C 1.3e-7 K, because the pieces themselves jump by 2e-9 mV there). The range of the inversion is that of the approximations: type B can only be inverted from 250 °C, below that E(t)E(t) has a minimum near 21 °C and is not unique.

Reference junction. What is measured is the difference between measuring and reference junction, Emeas=E(t)−E(tr)E_{\text{meas}} = E(t) - E(t_r); hence t=E−1(Emeas+E(tr))t = E^{-1}\big(E_{\text{meas}} + E(t_r)\big). The correction is added in voltage, not in temperature – the characteristic is not linear.

Temperature probe in hot gas, and averaging

Recovery factor. A probe in a flow does not take the total temperature but the recovery temperature Tr=T+rv2/(2cp)T_r = T + r\,v^2/(2c_p); the recovery factor rr is about 0.68 for bare wires in cross flow and 0.86 in parallel flow, and is calibrated, not computed. With Tt=T+v2/(2cp)T_t = T + v^2/(2c_p) follows Tt=Tr+(1−r)v2/(2cp)T_t = T_r + (1 - r)\,v^2/(2c_p), and with the Mach number

TtTr=1+κ−12M21+rκ−12M2.\frac{T_t}{T_r} = \frac{1 + \tfrac{\kappa-1}{2}M^2}{1 + r\,\tfrac{\kappa-1}{2}M^2}.

Energy balance of the head. In steady state the heat transfer to the head (area AA, coefficient α\alpha) covers radiation to the wall and conduction into the lead:

αA(Tr−Tk)=εσA(Tk4−Tw4)+λAdm(Tk−Tr)cosh⁡mL−(Tb−Tr)sinh⁡mL,m=αdUλAd.\alpha A\,(T_r - T_k) = \varepsilon\sigma A\,(T_k^4 - T_w^4) + \lambda A_d\,m\,\frac{(T_k - T_r)\cosh mL - (T_b - T_r)}{\sinh mL}, \qquad m = \sqrt{\frac{\alpha_d U}{\lambda A_d}}.

The last term is the heat flow at the tip of a fin of length LL between the base at TbT_b and the head at TkT_k, surrounded by gas at TrT_r – exact for constant properties. The balance is linear in TrT_r and is solved for it in closed form (the correction of the reading); in TkT_k it is strictly decreasing and is solved by Newton between the coldest and the warmest surrounding temperature (the prediction).

Limiting cases. Without radiation Tk−Tr=(Tb−Tr)b/(αA+a)T_k - T_r = (T_b - T_r)\,b/(\alpha A + a) with a=λAdmcoth⁡mLa = \lambda A_d m \coth mL, b=λAdm/sinh⁡mLb = \lambda A_d m/\sinh mL; without head area the familiar conduction error (Tb−Tr)/cosh⁡mL(T_b - T_r)/\cosh mL. Without conduction the radiation error Tr−Tk=εσ(Tk4−Tw4)/αT_r - T_k = \varepsilon\sigma(T_k^4 - T_w^4)/\alpha remains.

Averaging. Over area elements dAdA of a measuring plane there are three averages: the area average ∑φdA/∑dA\sum\varphi\,dA/\sum dA, the mass average ∑ρuφdA/∑ρudA\sum\rho u\varphi\,dA/\sum\rho u\,dA and the mixed-out average – the uniform state of an ideal gas carrying the same fluxes of mass m˙\dot m, axial momentum II, circumferential momentum and total enthalpy HH. With v=∑ρuvdA/m˙\bar v = \sum \rho u v\,dA/\dot m, p=(I−m˙u)/A\bar p = (I - \dot m\bar u)/A and T=p/(ρR)\bar T = \bar p/(\bar\rho R) the energy equation becomes quadratic in u\bar u:

(12−cpR)u2+cpRIm˙u+(12v2−Hm˙)=0.\Big(\tfrac12 - \tfrac{c_p}{R}\Big)\bar u^2 + \frac{c_p}{R}\frac{I}{\dot m}\,\bar u + \Big(\tfrac12\bar v^2 - \frac{H}{\dot m}\Big) = 0.

The smaller root is subsonic, the larger supersonic. The supersonic root is only physical when the subsonic average lies above the normal-shock limit (M*=(κ−1)/(2κ)M^* = \sqrt{(\kappa-1)/(2\kappa)}, 0.378 for air) – below, its pressure becomes negative and the computation reports it. A uniform supersonic field gives as its subsonic root exactly the state behind the normal shock. Mixing preserves the total temperature (it is the mass average) and loses total pressure.

Data reconciliation (VDI 2048)

Redundant measurements xx with covariance SS do not satisfy their balances f(x)=0f(x) = 0 because of measuring errors. Sought are the reconciled values x^\hat x that satisfy the balances and minimise (x^−x)TS−1(x^−x)(\hat x - x)^T S^{-1}(\hat x - x). For linear constraints Ax=bAx = b this has a closed solution (Lagrange):

r=Ax−b,λ=(ASAT)−1r,x^=x−SATλ,Sx^=S−SAT(ASAT)−1AS.r = Ax - b,\quad \lambda = (ASA^T)^{-1}r,\quad \hat x = x - SA^T\lambda,\quad S_{\hat x} = S - SA^T(ASA^T)^{-1}AS.

x^\hat x is the projection of xx onto the admissible set in the inner product S−1S^{-1}; hence ASx^=0A S_{\hat x} = 0 – the reconciled values do not scatter against the balances, and their uncertainty is never larger than that of the measurement.

Test statistics. The global statistic Q=vTS−1v=rT(ASAT)−1rQ = v^TS^{-1}v = r^T(ASA^T)^{-1}r with v=x^−xv = \hat x - x is, for a correct model, χ2\chi^2-distributed with as many degrees of freedom as constraints (the redundancy); it is tested against the quantile at the chosen level. The individual statistic |vi|/(Sv)ii|v_i|/\sqrt{(S_v)_{ii}} with Sv=S−Sx^S_v = S - S_{\hat x} is the absolute value of a standard normal and is tested against the two-sided normal quantile. A quantity in no constraint stays unchanged, its statistic is 0.

Gross errors. For uncorrelated measurements the largest individual statistic points, without noise, reliably to the faulty measurement: an error δ\delta on measurement kk gives ti=|(Sv)ik|δ/(skk(Sv)ii)t_i = |(S_v)_{ik}|\,\delta/(s_{kk}\sqrt{(S_v)_{ii}}), by Cauchy-Schwarz largest at i=ki = k. For correlated measurements this does not hold – if two corrections are strongly correlated, the error of one can give the larger value at the other. The individual statistic then only says that something in this group is wrong.

Non-linear constraints (such as energy balances m˙1T1=m˙2T2+m˙3T3\dot m_1 T_1 = \dot m_2 T_2 + \dot m_3 T_3): the same formula with the Jacobian FF at the last iterate and the linearised residual f(x^k)+F(x−x^k)f(\hat x_k) + F(x - \hat x_k), repeated until the iterate stands still (Britt and Luecke). The covariance comes from the last linearisation. Linear constraints stand after the second step.

How it is checked

  • GUM: linear model by hand without and with correlation (1e-9); product with second order exact (1e-6), Monte Carlo meets the exact standard deviation; Welch-Satterthwaite by hand; Supplement 1 example ±3.88.
  • Bands: data set Misra1a of the NIST reference collection StRD – parameters to 1e-8, standard errors to 1e-6 against the certified values; the straight line as special case to 1e-8.
  • Calibration: inverse prediction and uu by hand (1e-15), Fieller limits satisfy their equation (1e-10); polynomial of order 2 recovered exactly.
  • Five-hole probe: as forward model a spherical probe in potential flow; calibration on 25 × 25 points up to ±30° with a polynomial of order 11, at 200 intermediate points angles to 0.05°, pressures to 0.2 % of the dynamic pressure.
  • Flow: orifice equation in the limit ReD→∞Re_D \to \infty by hand, extra term continuous at 71.12 mm; iteration self-consistent to 1e-13, differential pressure to mass flow and back; nozzle expansibility from energy and mass balance; critical nozzle C*=0.684731C^* = 0.684731; findings outside the limits of the standard.
  • Pt100: R(100)=138.5055ΩR(100) = 138.5055\,\Omega, table values of the standard at −200 and 850 °C, 421 points forth and back to 1e-9 K, tolerances per class.

Limits

Second order only for uncorrelated inputs. Welch-Satterthwaite assumes independent variance estimates; with correlated inputs νeff\nu_{\text{eff}} is only an approximation. Monte Carlo sampling knows no correlations, and its accuracy grows only with 1/m1/\sqrt m – two valid digits of the standard deviation need about 10510^5 draws. Bands with linear approximation (delta method) – too optimistic for strongly non-linear models and few points. Five-hole probe without Reynolds and Mach dependence of the calibration. Thermocouples only after the reference function – class tolerances and drift are not modelled. The probe uses constant properties and a grey head in front of a large wall; heat transfer coefficients are inputs. Reconciliation knows only measured quantities (no unmeasured intermediate quantities) and linearises the covariance.
