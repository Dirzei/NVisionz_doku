Algorithms of the chart surface – background

What the chart surface computes before it draws: round axis step sizes (linear, logarithmic, time in calendar units), colour fields in bands with isolines, Delaunay triangulation of scattered points, histogram classes, box plot statistics, thinning of long polylines per pixel column, and text width from the font's advance widths. Formulas following the source code, worked examples from the checks, verification, limits.

What this is about

A chart only looks calm if many small decisions follow fixed rules: where do the ticks of an axis go, how many digits does a label carry, where does the boundary between two colour bands run inside a cell, which class does a value fall into that lies exactly on a class boundary? This page describes the rules by which Achse, Konturfeld, Zeichner, the renderers Histogramm and Boxplot, and Schriftmass decide. All calculations are independent of the locale and of the fonts installed on the machine; SVG and PDF are therefore character-identical on every machine.

Axis step sizes

Linear axis. About nn steps are wanted (default n=6n = 6, adjustable with striche(n)) over the span SS. The raw step r=S/nr = S/n is rounded up to the next “nice” number, i.e. 1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten:

p=10⌊log10⁡r⌋,s=min⁡{fp:f∈{1,2,5,10},fp≥r(1−10−12)}p = 10^{\lfloor \log_{10} r \rfloor}, \qquad s = \min\,\bigl\{\, f\,p \;:\; f \in \{1, 2, 5, 10\},\ f\,p \ge r\,(1 - 10^{-12}) \,\bigr\}

So at most nn steps fit into the span. An automatic range is then widened to whole multiples of ss: min⁡=⌊lo/s⌋s\min = \lfloor lo/s \rfloor\,s, max⁡=⌈hi/s⌉s\max = \lceil hi/s \rceil\,s (with a slack of 10−910^{-9} steps, so that a value already on a tick does not create another step). If all data share one value, the range is first widened by 10 % of its magnitude (by 1 at zero) on both sides.

Worked example. Data from 0.13 to 9.7, n=6n = 6: r=9.57/6=1.595r = 9.57/6 = 1.595, p=1p = 1, the first fitting factor is 2, so s=2s = 2. The range becomes 0 to 10, with ticks at 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. Further cases from the check: span 1 with six steps gives 0.2; span 7 gives 2; span 1000 with five steps gives 200; span 0.03 with six steps gives 0.005.

Digits of the labels. A label carries as many decimals as the step size needs: first k=⌈−log10⁡s⌉k = \lceil -\log_{10} s \rceil (not negative), then kk is raised until s⋅10ks \cdot 10^k is an integer (at most 12 digits). A fixed step of 2.5 thus gets one decimal (k=0k = 0 was not enough). From a magnitude of 10610^6 or below 10−310^{-3} the number is written with an exponent and three significant digits (1e-5). The decimal separator is always a point, whatever the language setting of the machine.

Logarithmic axis. The automatic range runs from the power of ten below the smallest positive value to the one above the largest: a=⌊log10⁡lo⌋a = \lfloor \log_{10} lo \rfloor, b=⌈log10⁡hi⌉b = \lceil \log_{10} hi \rceil, at least one decade. Major ticks sit at every jj-th power of ten with j=⌈(b−a)/8⌉j = \lceil (b - a)/8 \rceil, so at most eight or nine are labelled; minor ticks at k⋅10ek\cdot 10^e, k=2,…,9k = 2, \dots, 9, exist only up to six decades. The mapping to pixels is linear in the logarithm:

t=log10⁡v−log10⁡min⁡log10⁡max⁡−log10⁡min⁡,xpx⁡=x0+t(x1−x0)t = \frac{\log_{10} v - \log_{10} \min}{\log_{10} \max - \log_{10} \min}, \qquad x_{\mathrm{px}} = x_0 + t\,(x_1 - x_0)

For v≤0v \le 0 there is no pixel (NaN); no renderer draws such values. Worked examples of the check: 3·10⁻⁹ to 0.7 becomes 10−910^{-9} to 1 – nine decades, so j=2j = 2, five major ticks and no minor ones; 2·10⁻³ to 0.5 becomes 10−310^{-3} to 1 with four major ticks starting at 1e-3 and 3⋅8=243 \cdot 8 = 24 minor ticks.

Convention in the framework: A fixed step (schrittFest) replaces ss on linear axes; on logarithmic axes it means “every ss-th decade”, on category axes “every ss-th category labelled”. So that a too small fixed step does not draw without end, the tick list stops after 10 000 ticks.

Time axis in calendar units

On a time axis the values are milliseconds since 1970 (UTC); ticks should nevertheless fall on calendar boundaries in a time zone – full hours, midnight, the first of a month. The axis therefore picks, from a fixed ladder of steps, the smallest one with which at most nn steps fit into the span:

UnitCountsLength for comparison
millisecond1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 5001 ms
second, minute1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 301 s, 60 s
hour1, 2, 3, 6, 123600 s
day, week1, 2 and 186 400 s and 7 days
month1, 2, 3, 630.44 days
year1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000365.25 days

The length serves only for the choice; the ticks themselves come from the calendar of the zone. The first tick is the largest one not after the start (the “floor”): below a day a multiple of the step counted from midnight, for days the day of month 1+km1 + k\,m, for weeks the Monday, for months the month 1+km1 + k\,m, for years a multiple of mm. Each further tick is the floor after adding mm units in the calendar, so months of unequal length and the change to daylight saving time fall correctly.

An automatic range is widened to ticks of the chosen unit. Because widening enlarges the span and may require a coarser unit, the axis repeats choice and widening until the unit stays the same (at most 40 times). The label follows the unit (yyyy, yyyy-MM, yyyy-MM-dd, HH:mm, HH:mm:ss, with milliseconds); a tick below a day step that falls on midnight carries the date.

Worked examples of the check. 00:10 to 05:50 (UTC): 30 minutes would give 11.3 steps, one hour 5.7 – so hourly steps, the range becomes 00:00 to 06:00, the first tick reads 2026-01-01, the others 01:00 to 06:00. 15 January to 10 December: one month would give 10.8 steps, two months 5.4; the range becomes 1 January to 1 January of the following year (365 days, 6.0 steps of two months – the unit stays), ticks 2026-01, 2026-03, …, 2027-01. 1998 to 2026: ten-year steps from 1990 to 2030.

Contour field: bands, isolines, fans

A Konturfeld consists of polygons (the cells of a mesh or a cut) with one value per cell. Without bands each cell is filled in the colour of its value. With NN bands (baender(N), 2 to 64) the value range [lo,hi][lo, hi] is divided into bands; the boundaries are

gk=lo+(hi−lo)kN(linear),gk=lo(hilo)k/N(logarithmic),k=0,…,Ng_k = lo + (hi - lo)\,\frac{k}{N} \quad\text{(linear)}, \qquad g_k = lo \left(\frac{hi}{lo}\right)^{k/N} \quad\text{(logarithmic)}, \qquad k = 0, \dots, N

or freely set (stufen(g…), strictly increasing). A band takes the colour of the scale at its middle: for equal bands at the position (b+12)/N(b + \tfrac12)/N, otherwise at the value 12(gb+gb+1)\tfrac12(g_b + g_{b+1}) or – logarithmically – gbgb+1\sqrt{g_b\,g_{b+1}}. Values below g1g_1 fall into the first band, values from gN−1g_{N-1} into the last, also outside a fixed range. For equal bands the band follows from ⌊(v−g0)/(gN−g0)⋅N⌋\lfloor (v - g_0)/(g_N - g_0)\cdot N \rfloor, for free boundaries from a binary search with gb≤v<gb+1g_b \le v < g_{b+1}.

Logarithmic scale. The position of a value on the colour scale is t=ln⁡(v/lo)/ln⁡(hi/lo)t = \ln(v/lo)/\ln(hi/lo); cells with v≤0v \le 0 stay empty, the colour bar is divided in powers of ten. In the example of the check (range 1 to 1000, three bands) the boundaries are 1, 10, 100, 1000, and the value 10 sits at t=1/3t = 1/3.

From cell value to a smooth field. A band boundary following only the cell values would run as a staircase along the cell edges. The field therefore first gets values at the nodes: corners with equal coordinates (to within 10−910^{-9} of the extent) are the same node KK, and its value is the mean of the finite values of all cells ZZ touching it:

wK=1|MK|∑Z∈MKwZ,MK=cells with a finite value at node K\bar w_K = \frac{1}{|M_K|} \sum_{Z \in M_K} w_Z, \qquad M_K = \text{cells with a finite value at node } K

When drawing, each cell with mm corners P1,…,PmP_1, \dots, P_m is split into a fan of mm triangles (S,Pk,Pk+1)(S, P_k, P_{k+1}) around its centroid S=1m∑PkS = \tfrac1m \sum P_k. The centroid gets the mean of the cell's node values, not the cell value – otherwise the field would again depend on the cells and the boundaries would zigzag with the mesh. Within each triangle the value is linear; a band [gb,gb+1][g_b, g_{b+1}] is cut out by Sutherland-Hodgman against two half-planes v≥gbv \ge g_b and v≤gb+1v \le g_{b+1}, the intersection on an edge PiPjP_iP_j being at

t=g−vivj−vi,Q=Pi+t(Pj−Pi)t = \frac{g - v_i}{v_j - v_i}, \qquad Q = P_i + t\,(P_j - P_i)

The first band is open downwards, the last upwards. All pieces of a band go to the canvas as one area, so that anti-aliasing does not draw hairline gaps between neighbouring pieces.

Isolines (isolinien(true)) lie on the inner band boundaries g1,…,gN−1g_1, \dots, g_{N-1}: in every fan triangle crossing a boundary, one segment between the two edge intersections from the same formula. Line and colour change therefore always coincide. The same decomposition yields isostrecken() in data coordinates for the renderer Isolinien, which lays lines without fill over another field.

Worked example of the check. In a grid with value 0 everywhere and 1 in one cell, the four nodes of that cell have the value 14\tfrac14 (one of four adjacent cells). The centroid gets 14\tfrac14 and, with two bands and the boundary 0.5, lies in the lower band; with the cell value it would be 1 and the cell would be a coloured square.

Convention in the framework: The mesh is neither triangulated nor resampled to a raster; each cell remains a polygon in data coordinates, and the fan is created only when drawing. For non-convex cells the centroid may lie outside; meshes from flow simulation have convex cells.

Scattered points: Delaunay by Bowyer-Watson

Konturfeld.ausPunkten turns individual measurement points (xi,yi,wi)(x_i, y_i, w_i) into a field: the points are joined into triangles, each triangle is a cell with the mean of its three values, and the corners carry exactly the values of the points (they replace the node means). The joining follows Delaunay: no point lies inside the circumcircle of a triangle.

dreiecke(points):
    drop points with NaN; map to the square [0,1]² (both directions with s = max(Δx, Δy))
    duplicate points (equal after rounding to 10⁻¹²) only once
    auxiliary triangle (−10⁵, −10⁵), (10⁵, −10⁵), (0.5, 10⁵) to start with
    for each point q:
        bad ← all triangles whose circumcircle contains q
        boundary ← edges belonging to exactly one bad triangle
        remove bad triangles; add one triangle (edge, q) per boundary edge
    drop triangles with a corner of the auxiliary triangle and degenerate ones (|area| < 10⁻¹⁴)
    orient the rest counter-clockwise

The circumcircle test is the determinant with the corners relative to the test point qq, a→=A−q\vec a = A - q etc.:

d=(ax2+ay2)(bxcy−cxby)−(bx2+by2)(axcy−cxay)+(cx2+cy2)(axby−bxay)d = (a_x^2 + a_y^2)(b_x c_y - c_x b_y) - (b_x^2 + b_y^2)(a_x c_y - c_x a_y) + (c_x^2 + c_y^2)(a_x b_y - b_x a_y)

For a counter-clockwise triangle qq is inside if d>τd > \tau, otherwise if d<−τd < -\tau. The tolerance is relative: τ=10−12max⁡(10−12,|s1|+|s2|+|s3|)\tau = 10^{-12}\max\bigl(10^{-12}, |s_1| + |s_2| + |s_3|\bigr) with the three summands sis_i. A fixed tolerance would not suit triangles in the unit square and those with a corner of the auxiliary triangle, whose summands are of the order 101010^{10}, at the same time. Four points exactly on a circle (of a grid, say) thus count as “not inside” and do not tear open a cavity with a hole.

Why the auxiliary triangle is so large. With corners only 20 units away from the unit square, the auxiliary triangle lay inside the circumcircle of genuine triangles at the edge of the convex hull; when the auxiliary triangles were removed, a genuine triangle went missing. For nn points with hh points on the hull, a triangulation has, by Euler's formula,

T=2n−2−hT = 2n - 2 - h

triangles. In the case of the check – four corners of an 8 × 6 rectangle and 150 random points inside, n=154n = 154, h=4h = 4 – that is 302; the too small auxiliary triangle produced 301. The check also verifies the Delaunay property of every triangle against all points, the orientation and the total area 48.

With ausPunkten(…, maxKante) triangles with an edge longer than maxKante are dropped – the long, thin triangles the convex hull spans across indentations of a non-convex point cloud. Fewer than three usable points, points on a straight line, or no remaining triangle are rejected with an exception.

Histogram: classes

Without a setting, the renderer Histogramm chooses the number of classes by Sturges from the number nn of finite values and divides the range [a,b][a, b] of the values (or a fixed range) evenly:

m=⌈log2⁡n⌉+1,gi=a+(b−a)im,i=0,…,mm = \lceil \log_2 n \rceil + 1, \qquad g_i = a + (b - a)\,\frac{i}{m}, \quad i = 0, \dots, m

The classes are half-open, [gk,gk+1)[g_k, g_{k+1}), only the last one is closed, [gm−1,gm][g_{m-1}, g_m]. So every value counts exactly once, even one lying on an inner boundary, and the maximum is included. Values outside a fixed range are not counted but returned as a number. The height is either the count zkz_k, the fraction zk/Nz_k/N (sum 1) or the density

hk=zkN(gk+1−gk),∑khk(gk+1−gk)=1h_k = \frac{z_k}{N\,(g_{k+1} - g_k)}, \qquad \sum_k h_k\,(g_{k+1} - g_k) = 1

with the number NN of counted values. Instead of a number, a fixed width ww from a start s0s_0 can be set; the boundaries are then s0+iws_0 + i\,w, far enough to cover the values.

Worked example of the check. The values 0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4 (plus a NaN that does not count): n=8n = 8, m=⌈log2⁡8⌉+1=4m = \lceil \log_2 8 \rceil + 1 = 4, boundaries 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, counts 1, 2, 1, 4 – the 4 counts towards the last class. With a fixed width of 1.5 from 0 the boundaries become 0, 1.5, 3, 4.5 and the counts 3, 1, 4. With the fixed range 1 to 3 and two classes: 2 and 4, two values outside.

Box plot: quartiles and whiskers

Boxplot computes the quartiles after Hyndman and Fan, type 7 – the definition many statistics environments use by default. With the finite values sorted ascending, x(0)≤…≤x(n−1)x_{(0)} \le \dots \le x_{(n-1)}:

h=(n−1)p,Q(p)=x(⌊h⌋)+(h−⌊h⌋)(x(⌊h⌋+1)−x(⌊h⌋))h = (n - 1)\,p, \qquad Q(p) = x_{(\lfloor h \rfloor)} + \bigl(h - \lfloor h \rfloor\bigr)\,\bigl(x_{(\lfloor h \rfloor + 1)} - x_{(\lfloor h \rfloor)}\bigr)

The box spans Q1=Q(0.25)Q_1 = Q(0.25) to Q3=Q(0.75)Q_3 = Q(0.75) with the median Q(0.5)Q(0.5). The whiskers follow Tukey: with the interquartile range I=Q3−Q1I = Q_3 - Q_1 and the factor ff (default 1.5) they reach the smallest and largest value within [Q1−fI,Q3+fI][Q_1 - f I,\ Q_3 + f I]; values outside are outliers and are drawn as points. The arithmetic mean over all values appears as a diamond.

Worked example of the check. The values 1 to 9 and 30: n=10n = 10, for Q1Q_1 we have h=2.25h = 2.25, so Q1=3+0.25⋅1=3.25Q_1 = 3 + 0.25 \cdot 1 = 3.25; median 5.5; Q3=7.75Q_3 = 7.75. I=4.5I = 4.5, upper fence 7.75+1.5⋅4.5=14.57.75 + 1.5 \cdot 4.5 = 14.5. The whiskers reach 1 and 9, 30 is an outlier, the mean is 7.5. An infinite factor knows no outliers.

Thinning per pixel column

A series of 100 000 points on a surface 300 pixels wide would put over 300 points into each pixel column – all of them drawn, only their envelope seen. The renderer Linie therefore thins as soon as the number of points exceeds four times the width of the surface in pixels: for each run of consecutive points in the same pixel column ⌊xpx⁡⌋\lfloor x_{\mathrm{px}} \rfloor at most four remain, namely the first, the one with the smallest and the one with the largest yy (in the order in which they occur) and the last.

The vertical extent of every column is thus preserved, including a single spike, and the polyline crosses into the next column at the same place as before. A gap (NaN) stays a gap. In the check the 100 000 points of an oscillation with an inserted spike are thinned to at most 4⋅3014 \cdot 301; smallest and largest value, first and last point are unchanged. Point symbols are not thinned.

Text width from advance widths

To lay out margins, the legend and rotated labels, the width of every text must be known. In the raster image (Java2D) the machine's font measures. For SVG and PDF that would be a dependency on installed fonts: the same file would look different on every machine. Schriftmass therefore holds the advance widths of Helvetica and Helvetica-Bold from their font metric files (AFM) for the characters 32 to 255 of the WinAnsi encoding, in thousandths of the font size gg:

B(text)=g1000∑c∈textw(c),line height=1.25gB(\text{text}) = \frac{g}{1000} \sum_{c \,\in\, \text{text}} w(c), \qquad \text{line height} = 1.25\,g

Examples from the check: W 944, i 222 (bold 278), every digit 556, so an m at 12 points is 12⋅0.833=9.99612 \cdot 0.833 = 9.996. Umlauts advance like their base letter. Characters outside WinAnsi and the euro sign, which is missing from the metric files, count like a digit (556); italic advances like upright, kerning is left out. SVG and PDF measure with the same table; the SVG names two more families with the same advance widths next to Helvetica, the PDF refers to the standard font Helvetica in WinAnsi, so that measured and set width agree.

How it is checked

The checks compare with target values that can be recomputed by hand, not with an earlier run. Each load-bearing place also has a counter-check: a deliberate corruption of the source on which the responsible check must turn red.

  • Diagrammprobe: step sizes (1/6 → 0.2, 7/6 → 2, 1000/5 → 200), linear and logarithmic axis with major and minor ticks, mapping there and back (linear and logarithmic), thinning with preserved envelope, bands (boundary sharp inside the cell, one area per band, isolines on the boundaries, smoothing by nodes).
  • Achsenprobe: time axis in hours, choice of unit by span from milliseconds to decades, midnight in a zone across the change to daylight saving time, weeks from Monday, fixed steps.
  • Farbfeldprobe: free levels with binary search, logarithmic scale, Delaunay triangulation with the number of triangles by Euler, circumcircle condition for every triangle, area and corner values; Isolinienprobe: isoline segments of a linear field exactly on the boundary.
  • Diagrammartenprobe: classes, Sturges, fraction and density, fixed range and fixed width of the histogram; type 7 quartiles and Tukey whiskers on values whose quartiles can be recomputed by hand.
  • Schriftprobe: advance widths of single characters, equal measurement in SVG and PDF.

Limits

  • The Delaunay triangulation tests all triangles for each point; the effort grows quadratically. It is meant for a few thousand points.
  • For very many points almost on a straight line at the edge of the hull, the relative tolerance is not proven safe; random clouds and grids, also strongly stretched, are checked.
  • The fan around the centroid assumes convex or at least star-shaped cells.
  • Values are mixed linearly in the fan triangle; band boundaries are therefore piecewise straight, a curved boundary is resolved by the cell size.
  • The lengths of the time units (month 30.44 days, year 365.25 days) only serve the choice of unit; a month of 28 or 31 days can narrowly exceed the wanted number of steps.
  • The text width in SVG and PDF is that of Helvetica; if a viewer shows the SVG in a font with other advance widths, the margins are only approximately right. Kerning is not computed.
  • In the raster image (Java2D) the machine's font measures; image and SVG are therefore not pixel-identical in the position of texts.
