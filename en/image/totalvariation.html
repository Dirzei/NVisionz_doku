Edge-preserving denoising – total variation after Chambolle

The computation behind TotalVariation: the model of Rudin, Osher and Fatemi, why total variation penalises noise and spares edges, the dual form and Chambolle's projection method, pseudocode following the source, an example that can be checked exactly, testing and limits.

What it is about

The bilateral filter and Perona-Malik are recipes: they say how to get from the noisy image to the smoothed one. Total-variation denoising works the other way round. It first describes which image one wants, and then searches for it.

The desired image uu should satisfy two demands as well as possible at the same time: it should be close to the measured image ff, and it should have as little total variation as possible – the sum of all brightness changes across the image. The second demand is the core. Noise fluctuates a little back and forth everywhere and therefore contributes to the total variation at every pixel. An edge, by contrast, varies only in one place, once by its height.

What matters is how it is measured: by the magnitude of the change, not by its square. A rise from 50 to 150 has the total variation 100 – regardless of whether it happens as a sharp jump or as a gentle ramp over ten points. Total variation therefore has nothing against sharp edges. A quadratic smoothness demand, as behind the ordinary Gaussian mean, would clearly prefer the ramp (10⋅102=100010 \cdot 10^2 = 1000 against 1002=10000100^2 = 10000) and smear edges.

The model of Rudin, Osher and Fatemi

For an image plane with values fif_i the desired image is the solution of

minu⁡12∑i(ui−fi)2+λTV⁡(u),TV⁡(u)=∑i|∇ui|=∑i(∂xu)i2+(∂zu)i2\min_u \; \frac{1}{2} \sum_i (u_i - f_i)^2 \; + \; \lambda \, \mathrm{TV}(u), \qquad \mathrm{TV}(u) = \sum_i |\nabla u_i| = \sum_i \sqrt{(\partial_x u)_i^2 + (\partial_z u)_i^2}

The first part is fidelity to the measured image, the second the total variation. The weight λ\lambda (gewicht) balances the two: for λ→0\lambda \to 0, u=fu = f; for large λ\lambda the image becomes flatter and flatter until only the mean remains. The problem is convex – there is exactly one solution and no local minima in which a method could get stuck. It is, however, not differentiable: |∇u||\nabla u| has a kink at zero, exactly where the result is meant to become flat. That is why the detour via the dual form is taken.

The dual form

The magnitude of a vector can be written as a maximum: |v|=max|p|≤1⁡p⋅v|v| = \max_{|p| \le 1} p \cdot v. Inserting this for each pixel turns the total variation into

TV⁡(u)=max|pi|≤1⁡∑ipi⋅(∇u)i=max|pi|≤1⁡−∑iui(div⁡p)i\mathrm{TV}(u) = \max_{|p_i| \le 1} \sum_i p_i \cdot (\nabla u)_i = \max_{|p_i| \le 1} \; -\sum_i u_i \, (\mathrm{div}\, p)_i

with a vector field p=(px,pz)p = (p^x, p^z) of length at most one at each point. The second step is discrete integration by parts: the divergence is formed so that it is the negative adjoint of the gradient. Exchanging minimum and maximum, the minimum over uu can be computed in closed form, and what remains is

u=f−λdiv⁡p,p=arg⁡min|pi|≤1⁡‖λdiv⁡p−f‖2u = f - \lambda \, \mathrm{div}\, p, \qquad p = \arg\min_{|p_i| \le 1} \big\| \lambda\, \mathrm{div}\, p - f \big\|^2

This is the form given by Chambolle in 2004 (the sign of pp can be chosen freely because the constraint is symmetric). Instead of a non-smooth problem in uu, a smooth problem in pp with a simple constraint has to be solved: every vector pip_i must stay inside the unit disc.

Chambolle's method as implemented

The solution uses a projected gradient method: take a step downhill, then pull every vector that has left the disc back onto its boundary. Up to a positive factor, the gradient of the objective with respect to pp is −∇(div⁡p−f/λ)-\nabla(\mathrm{div}\,p - f/\lambda), and because u=f−λdiv⁡pu = f - \lambda\,\mathrm{div}\,p, ∇(div⁡p−f/λ)=−∇u/λ\nabla(\mathrm{div}\,p - f/\lambda) = -\nabla u / \lambda. A step thus reads

p(n+1)=Π(p(n)−τλ∇u(n)),Π(q)=qmax⁡(1,|q|)p^{(n+1)} = \Pi\!\left(p^{(n)} - \frac{\tau}{\lambda}\, \nabla u^{(n)}\right), \qquad \Pi(q) = \frac{q}{\max(1, |q|)}

Put simply: at every point pp points against the gradient of the provisional result. Where the image is flat, pp stays small and uu follows ff. Where pp reaches length one, the bound is active – there the total variation is "used up", and that is where a real edge stands.

Discretisation

The gradient is formed forwards and set to zero at the last entry of a row or column; the divergence backwards, with zero outside the image:

(∂xu)x,z={ux+1,z−ux,zx<w−10x=w−1,(div⁡p)x,z=px,zx−px−1,zx+px,zz−px,z−1z(\partial_x u)_{x,z} = \begin{cases} u_{x+1,z} - u_{x,z} & x < w-1 \\ 0 & x = w-1 \end{cases}, \qquad (\mathrm{div}\,p)_{x,z} = p^x_{x,z} - p^x_{x-1,z} + p^z_{x,z} - p^z_{x,z-1}

with p−1,zx=px,−1z=0p^x_{-1,z} = p^z_{x,-1} = 0. Because the gradient is zero at the last entry, pw−1,zxp^x_{w-1,z} is never changed and keeps its starting value zero. The sum of the divergence therefore telescopes to zero over every row and every column, and it follows that

∑iui=∑ifi−λ∑i(div⁡p)i=∑ifi\sum_i u_i = \sum_i f_i - \lambda \sum_i (\mathrm{div}\,p)_i = \sum_i f_i

The mean is conserved, in every step, not only in the limit. In addition, both components of pp lie in [−1,1][-1, 1], so the divergence has magnitude at most 4, and the result deviates from the input by at most 4λ4\lambda anywhere.

Convention in the framework: Default new TotalVariation() = weight λ=12\lambda = 12, 30 steps, step size τ=0.125\tau = 0.125. The constructor limits τ\tau to [0.01,0.25][0.01, 0.25], the weight to at least 0.01 and the number of steps to at least 1. Chambolle proved convergence for τ≤1/8\tau \le 1/8 and remarked that in practice step sizes up to 1/41/4 work; the code takes 1/81/8 as default and 1/41/4 as upper bound. There is no stopping criterion – exactly the requested number of steps is computed.
Convention in the framework: The sign in the step is minus: p−(τ/λ)∇up - (\tau/\lambda)\nabla u. The source code states explicitly that with the wrong sign the method runs away from the target and delivers a result worse than the noisy original.
Convention in the framework: Each pass first forms uu from the previous pp and then updates pp; the uu of the last pass is returned. The last update of pp therefore no longer enters the result: with nn steps, n−1n-1 updates take effect, and with schritte = 1 the image stays unchanged.

Pseudocode – TotalVariation.anwenden(f, w, h)

px ← 0;  pz ← 0                                 vector field, one component per direction
repeat steps times:
    // 1. provisional result from the previous vector field
    for each pixel (x, z):
        div ← px[x,z] − (if x > 0: px[x−1,z] else 0)
            + pz[x,z] − (if z > 0: pz[x,z−1] else 0)
        u[x,z] ← f[x,z] − λ · div
    // 2. gradient step on p and projection onto the unit disc
    for each pixel (x, z):
        gx ← if x+1 < w: u[x+1,z] − u[x,z] else 0
        gz ← if z+1 < h: u[x,z+1] − u[x,z] else 0
        nx ← px[x,z] − (τ/λ)·gx                   MINUS: against the gradient of u
        nz ← pz[x,z] − (τ/λ)·gz
        l ← max(1, √(nx² + nz²))
        px[x,z] ← nx / l;  pz[x,z] ← nz / l
return u                                        the u of the last pass

Source: envisionz-framework-image/src/envisionz/image/TotalVariation.java. Memory: four fields of image size (pxp^x, pzp^z, divergence, uu); cost per step linear in the number of pixels.

Worked example

A step, computed exactly

For a row consisting of two plateaus the result can be stated by hand. Suppose the result is flat on each plateau, with values cLc_L on the left and cRc_R on the right, each plateau LL points long. Then the total variation is simply cR−cLc_R - c_L, and the objective reads

12∑i∈left(cL−fi)2+12∑i∈right(cR−fi)2+λ(cR−cL)\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i \in \text{left}} (c_L - f_i)^2 + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i \in \text{right}} (c_R - f_i)^2 + \lambda\,(c_R - c_L)

Differentiating with respect to cLc_L and setting to zero gives L(cL−fL)−λ=0L\,(c_L - \bar f_L) - \lambda = 0, hence

cL=fL+λL,cR=fR−λLc_L = \bar f_L + \frac{\lambda}{L}, \qquad c_R = \bar f_R - \frac{\lambda}{L}

Each plateau becomes flat and moves by λ/L\lambda / L towards the other, as long as the two do not meet. For the row (50,54,47,52,150,146,153,149)(50, 54, 47, 52, 150, 146, 153, 149) the plateau means are 50.75 and 149.5, and with L=4L = 4:

How it is tested

Filterprobe tests what follows from the construction:

  • Fixed point: a constant image stays exactly unchanged after 40 steps – the gradient is zero, pp stays zero.
  • Conservation of the mean: after 40 steps with λ=15\lambda = 15, τ=0.25\tau = 0.25, relative to 10−910^{-9}; likewise for a single row, in which the vertical gradient is zero everywhere.
  • Bound: no point deviates from the input by more than 4λ4\lambda.
  • Small weight: at λ=0.01\lambda = 0.01 every deviation stays below 0.040.04.
  • 1×1 image: both gradients are set to zero, the image stays as it is.
  • Together with all filters: input unchanged, length, shift equivariance (deviation below 10−810^{-8}), constant images in six tiny sizes (1×1, 1×9, 9×1, 2×2, 3×2, 2×7).

Limits

  • Loss of contrast in small objects. According to the example an area moves by λ/L\lambda/L towards its surroundings; in the image it is perimeter over area times λ\lambda. Small objects, thin lines and lettering therefore lose more contrast than large areas and disappear entirely below a certain size.
  • Staircasing. Gentle ramps are broken into flat steps because total variation favours piecewise constant images; on sky or skin tones this looks "poster-like".
  • Texture goes too. Fine, regular detail has a lot of total variation and is treated like noise. The decomposition into smooth and textured parts exists for that.
  • No stopping criterion. The number of steps is fixed; the method does not check whether the solution has been reached. In the image example, 100 instead of 30 steps still gain 0.7 dB. Because the step on pp is scaled by τ/λ\tau/\lambda, a large weight needs more steps – in the row above, λ=200\lambda = 200 had not quite converged after 500 steps.
  • One weight for the whole image. Regions with noise of different strength cannot be treated separately.
  • Direction: forward differences make the discrete total variation slightly direction dependent; diagonal edges are treated somewhat differently from axis-parallel ones.
