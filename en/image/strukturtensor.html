Texture and direction – the orientation field via the structure tensor

The computation behind Orientierungsfeld: why the gradient alone yields no direction, the averaged structure tensor, its eigenvalues and eigenvectors in closed form, the quality as a measure of unambiguity, pseudocode following the source, worked examples, testing and limits.

What it is about

At many places in an image there is a preferred direction: an edge, a stroke, a stripe pattern, the fibres of a fabric. For some methods this direction matters more than the brightness itself. One example is the deinterlacing of interlaced video material: each field contains only every other line, the missing ones must be filled in. Interpolating vertically between the line above and the line below is right for horizontal structures. For an oblique edge it is wrong – the edge becomes jagged. Interpolating along the edge instead keeps it smooth.

For that, two answers are needed per pixel: in which direction does a structure run here? And how unambiguous is that? In a uniform area there is no direction, at a corner there are two, in noise arbitrarily many. There the direction must not be relied upon. Orientierungsfeld (orientation field) delivers both: the direction as a unit vector and angle, and the unambiguity as a quality between zero and one.

From the gradient to the structure tensor

Why the gradient is not enough

The gradient ∇f=(fx,fz)\nabla f = (f_x, f_z) points in the direction of steepest ascent, that is across a structure. A direction along the structure is obtained from it by rotating by 90°. The problem arises when averaging, which is needed to suppress noise: on the two flanks of a bright line the gradients point in opposite directions. Their mean is zero, although the line has a perfectly unambiguous direction. A direction has no sign – 30° and 210° are the same line – but a vector does.

The outer product

The solution is to average, instead of the gradient, its outer product with itself. This is a symmetric 2×2 matrix in which the sign drops out ((−g)(−g)T=ggT(-g)(-g)^\mathsf{T} = g g^\mathsf{T}):

J=∇f∇fT=fx2fxfzfxfzfz2=abbcJ = \overline{\nabla f\, \nabla f^\mathsf{T}} = \begin{pmatrix} \overline{f_x^2} & \overline{f_x f_z} \\ \overline{f_x f_z} & \overline{f_z^2} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ b & c \end{pmatrix}

The bar stands for local averaging. JJ is called the structure tensor. It is positive semidefinite; its two eigenvalues λ1≥λ2≥0\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge 0 are the mean squared derivative in the direction of strongest and weakest ascent. The eigenvector for λ1\lambda_1 lies across the structure, the one for λ2\lambda_2 points along it – in that direction the brightness changes least.

Eigenvalues and eigenvectors in closed form

A symmetric 2×2 matrix needs no iterative method. With the trace a+ca + c,

λ1,2=(a+c)±(a−c)2+4b22\lambda_{1,2} = \frac{(a + c) \pm \sqrt{(a - c)^2 + 4b^2}}{2}

An eigenvector for λ1\lambda_1 follows from the first row of (J−λ1I)v=0(J - \lambda_1 I)\,v = 0, i.e. (a−λ1)vx+bvz=0(a - \lambda_1) v_x + b\, v_z = 0:

v=(b,λ1−a)v = (b,\ \lambda_1 - a)

Rotated by 90° and normalised, this gives the direction of the structure:

r=(−(λ1−a),b)b2+(λ1−a)2r = \frac{(-(\lambda_1 - a),\ b)}{\sqrt{b^2 + (\lambda_1 - a)^2}}

The angle is φ=atan2⁡(rz,rx)\varphi = \operatorname{atan2}(r_z, r_x) in degrees. The quality compares the two eigenvalues:

q=λ1−λ2λ1+λ2∈[0,1]q = \frac{\lambda_1 - \lambda_2}{\lambda_1 + \lambda_2} \in [0, 1]

At q=1q = 1, λ2=0\lambda_2 = 0: the brightness changes in one direction only; a pure stripe or edge pattern is present. At q=0q = 0 both eigenvalues are equal, no direction is preferred – a flat area (both zero), a corner or noise.

The implementation

  • Derivatives: central differences, fx≈(fx+1,z−fx−1,z)/2f_x \approx (f_{x+1,z} - f_{x-1,z})/2, fzf_z accordingly; at the border the index is clamped to the image.
  • Averaging: each of the three components fx2f_x^2, fxfzf_x f_z, fz2f_z^2 separately with a box mean over (2r+1)×(2r+1)(2r+1) \times (2r+1) points, first row-wise, then column-wise; edge value continued. For a radius below 1 no averaging takes place.
  • Eigendecomposition per pixel as above. If the eigenvector is practically zero (length below 10−1210^{-12}), v=(1,0)v = (1, 0) is set; the direction is then vertical (90°).
  • Quality: (λ1−λ2)/(λ1+λ2)(\lambda_1 - \lambda_2)/(\lambda_1 + \lambda_2), zero for a trace below 10−910^{-9}.
Convention in the framework: Rows count downwards (zz grows downwards), the angle is measured from the horizontal. A positive angle therefore turns clockwise on the screen. winkel(i) lies in the range (−180°,180°](-180°, 180°]; because a direction has no sign, φ\varphi and φ±180°\varphi \pm 180° are equivalent and angles are only to be compared modulo 180°. The parameter glaettung is the radius of the box mean, not a Gaussian width. The field is computed completely in the constructor and only queried afterwards.
Convention in the framework: Without averaging (glaettung = 0) the tensor has rank one at every point, and the quality is one wherever there is any gradient at all – even in pure noise. The quality only becomes meaningful with averaging; the source comment notes that otherwise the tensor is degenerate pointwise.

Pseudocode – new Orientierungsfeld(e, w, h, glaettung)

for each pixel (x, z):
    gx ← (e[z, min(x+1, w−1)] − e[z, max(x−1, 0)]) / 2
    gz ← (e[min(z+1, h−1), x] − e[max(z−1, 0), x]) / 2
    jxx[i] ← gx²;  jxy[i] ← gx·gz;  jzz[i] ← gz²
jxx, jxy, jzz ← each averaged(…, glaettung)    box (2r+1)², row-wise, then column-wise
for each pixel i:
    a ← jxx[i];  b ← jxy[i];  c ← jzz[i]
    trace ← a + c;  root ← √max(0, (a − c)² + 4b²)
    large ← (trace + root)/2;  small ← (trace − root)/2
    (vx, vz) ← (b, large − a)                    eigenvector of the large eigenvalue: across
    l ← √(vx² + vz²)
    if l < 1e−12:  (vx, vz) ← (1, 0);  l ← 1
    rx[i] ← −vz/l;  rz[i] ← vx/l                  rotated by 90°: along
    quality[i] ← if trace > 1e−9 then (large − small)/trace else 0

winkel(i) = atan2(rz[i], rx[i]) in degrees

Source: envisionz-framework-image/src/envisionz/image/Orientierungsfeld.java. Memory: three tensor fields and three result fields of image size; cost linear in the image size, the box mean costs 2(2r+1)2(2r+1) additions per point and component.

Worked examples

A linear ramp by hand

f=x+2zf = x + 2z on an image of 25 × 25 points. In the interior the central differences are exact, fx=1f_x = 1, fz=2f_z = 2, equal at every point; averaging changes nothing. So

J=1224,λ1=5,λ2=0,v=(2,4),r=(−4,2)20=(−0.8944,0.4472)J = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \lambda_1 = 5, \ \lambda_2 = 0, \quad v = (2, 4), \quad r = \frac{(-4, 2)}{\sqrt{20}} = (-0.8944,\ 0.4472)

The angle is atan2⁡(0.4472,−0.8944)=153.435°\operatorname{atan2}(0.4472, -0.8944) = 153.435° – that is atan2⁡(2,1)+90°=63.435°+90°\operatorname{atan2}(2, 1) + 90° = 63.435° + 90°: the structure is perpendicular to the gradient (1,2)(1, 2). The quality is (5−0)/(5+0)=1(5 - 0)/(5 + 0) = 1. The class returns exactly these values at the image centre.

Edges, corners, areas

An image of 25 × 25 points with value 50 and a bright quarter (200) at the bottom right from column and row 12, averaging with radius 2:

At the corner the tensor does deliver a direction – the bisector – but with low quality, and that is exactly how it is to be read.

Averaging against noise

A stripe pattern at 30° with a period of 6 points and amplitude 40, on 41 × 41 points, with normally distributed noise of standard deviation 20 on top. Evaluated in the interior (25 × 25 points):

Without averaging the direction is arbitrarily wrong at some points, and the quality nevertheless claims full certainty. With averaging the deviation drops quickly, and the quality gives an honest measure. The deviations are angles modulo 180° against the target direction 30°.

How it is tested

Filterprobe, section orientation field:

  • Six linear ramps px+qzp x + q z with (p,q)=(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,−1),(1,2),(3,−1)(p, q) = (1,0), (0,1), (1,1), (1,-1), (1,2), (3,-1), without averaging: in the interior the angle atan2⁡(q,p)+90°\operatorname{atan2}(q, p) + 90° (modulo 180°) must be met to 10−910^{-9} and the quality must be one to 10−910^{-9} – the central difference is exact for linear functions.
  • Horizontal stripes (period 5, averaging radius 2): angle zero to 10−910^{-9}.
  • Constant image: quality exactly zero everywhere. 1×1 image: quality zero, direction still a unit vector.
  • Promises of the interface: direction a unit vector everywhere (to 10−910^{-9}), quality between zero and one, width and height passed through.

The module documentation reports as a measured value: five known angles to within 0.6°.

Limits

  • Fine patterns: for an oscillation with wave number kk, the central difference does not measure kk but sin⁡k\sin k. For short periods this shifts the direction towards the nearest diagonal: the noise-free 30° pattern with period 6 from the example gives 32.41° instead of 30°, independent of the averaging radius.
  • Image border: clamping turns the central difference at the border into half a one-sided difference; the derivative across the border is halved there, the direction falsified. For the ramp x+2zx + 2z the point (0, 12) on the left border gives 165.96° instead of 153.43°.
  • Box mean: the averaging is not rotationally symmetric; diagonal and axis-parallel structures are averaged over differently shaped neighbourhoods.
  • One scale: derivatives over three points and a fixed averaging radius – structures that are much coarser give weak gradients; structures finer than the averaging that change direction cancel out.
  • Corners and crossings: the tensor cannot represent two directions at one place; it reports the bisector with low quality.
