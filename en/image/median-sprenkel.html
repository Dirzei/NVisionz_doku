Removing defects – median and speckle filter

The computation behind Median and Sprenkel: why impulse defects need a different treatment from noise, the median as a robust order statistic, the search for small connected blobs, pseudocode following the source, worked examples, testing and limits.

What it is about

Noise falsifies every pixel a little. An impulse defect falsifies a few pixels completely: a tape dropout, a speck of dust on the scanner, a scratch in the film. The affected value has nothing to do with the image any more – it is black, white or anything in between.

All averaging methods fail against such values. A mean over nine values, one of which is 150 levels off, is shifted by almost 17 levels; the defect is not gone, but spread over the whole neighbourhood. The edge-preserving denoisers do not help either: to the bilateral filter a strongly deviating point looks like an edge, and it leaves it alone.

What is needed is a method that recognises and discards wrong values instead of including them. The module has two. The median works with a fixed window and discards outliers by sorting. Sprenkel (speckle) does not ask about the window but about the size of the blob a conspicuous point belongs to, and replaces small blobs as a whole. The third tool of this section, the notch filter against periodic patterns, works in the frequency domain and has its own page.

The median

Definition and robustness

If the mm values of a window are sorted in ascending order, v(1)≤v(2)≤…≤v(m)v_{(1)} \le v_{(2)} \le \dots \le v_{(m)}, the median for odd mm is the middle one:

med⁡(v1,…,vm)=v((m+1)/2)\mathrm{med}(v_1, \dots, v_m) = v_{((m+1)/2)}

The window around a pixel is a square of m=(2r+1)2m = (2r+1)^2 points or, with nurWaagerecht (horizontal only), a piece of a row with m=2r+1m = 2r+1 points. mm is odd in both cases; a mean of two middle values is never needed.

The decisive property is robustness: up to (m−1)/2(m-1)/2 of the values can be falsified arbitrarily – arbitrarily large, arbitrarily small – and the median still lies between two unfalsified values. For 3×3 that is four out of nine. For the mean, a single one suffices. In statistics this number is called the breakdown point: 50 % for the median, 0 % for the mean.

Further properties that follow from the definition:

  • Every output value occurs in the input. No new grey values and no values outside the range arise.
  • A straight edge is preserved: on each side the majority of the window lies on its own side.
  • A monotonically rising ramp along the window direction stays unchanged: the middle value of a uniformly rising piece is the point itself.
  • Thin lines disappear. A line of width bb occupies b(2r+1)b\,(2r+1) of the (2r+1)2(2r+1)^2 places in the square window. It has the majority only from b≥r+1b \ge r+1. Lines with a width up to rr are therefore removed completely – at r=1r = 1 every line one point wide, at r=2r = 2 also lines of two points.
  • Corners are rounded: a single protruding point of an outline is an outlier for the median.
Convention in the framework: Default new Median() = radius 1, square (3×3). The radius is set to at least 1. At the image border the edge value is continued; in an image corner several copies of the same point therefore lie in the window – a 3×3 corner contains the corner point four times, but still five other values. Each pixel's window is sorted completely (Arrays.sort), without a running histogram, because the values are double and not 256 levels.

Pseudocode – Median.anwenden(e, w, h)

rz ← if horizontalOnly then 0 else r
buffer ← array of length (2r+1)·(2rz+1)
for each pixel (x, z):
    k ← 0
    for dz ← −rz to rz:
        zz ← clamp(z + dz, 0, h−1)
        for dx ← −r to r:
            buffer[k] ← e[zz, clamp(x + dx, 0, w−1)];  k ← k+1
    sort buffer[0 … k−1]
    out[z, x] ← buffer[k/2]                   k odd: the middle value
return out

Source: envisionz-framework-image/src/envisionz/image/Median.java. Cost per pixel mlog⁡mm \log m comparisons; negligible at 3×3, noticeable at 7×7 (49 values).

Speckle: defects by their size

The idea

A median filter removes a blotch only if it is narrower than the radius – and takes all other fine structures of the image with it. Sprenkel proceeds differently. It does not ask "what does the neighbourhood of this point look like", but "how large is the blob this point belongs to". A defect blotch is small and self-contained; a real structure is connected to the rest of the image. Only what is small enough is touched – the rest of the image stays unchanged, point for point.

The three steps

1. Find conspicuous points. The reference is a local comparison value, the median over the 7×7 window (new Median(3, false)). A point counts as conspicuous if

|fi−med7×7⁡(f)i|>a|f_i - \mathrm{med}_{7\times 7}(f)_i| > a

with the deviation aa (abweichung, default 30). A fixed reference value would not work: in a dark image region everything would then be conspicuous. A mean would not work either: it would be pulled along by the blotch itself. As long as the blotch covers less than half of the window, the median sees only the background.

2. Form connected blobs. Conspicuous points that are connected via their four direct neighbours (left, right, above, below) form a blob. They are collected with a stack: from the first conspicuous point, all not yet visited conspicuous neighbours, until no more are added. Area (number of points) and bounding box are tracked along the way.

3. Replace small blobs. A blob counts as a defect if

area≤Fandextent=max⁡(width,height)≤A\text{area} \le F \quad \text{and} \quad \text{extent} = \max(\text{width}, \text{height}) \le A

with FF = hoechstflaeche (default 40) and AA = hoechstausdehnung (default 12). Each of its points is replaced by the 7×7 median at that location.

Why two limits? A long, thin scratch has little area but a large extent. With the area alone it would be removed as a defect; whether that is wanted depends on the material. The extent separates the two cases: a scratch 30 points long and one point wide stays below F=40F = 40 with its area of 30, but exceeds A=12A = 12 and remains.

Convention in the framework: Default new Sprenkel() = deviation 30, area at most 40, extent at most 12. The constructor sets each of the three quantities to at least 1. Connectivity via four neighbours, not eight: two blotches that touch only at a corner count as two. Collecting a blob stops as soon as it has more than 4F4F points; it is then certainly too large, and the rest need not be searched. gefundeneStoerungen() returns the number of blobs replaced last.
Convention in the framework: On stopping, the stack is cleared. Without that, the not yet processed points of the large blob moved into the next one and falsified its area and extent there – a single speckle was then no longer removed as soon as a blob that was too large lay elsewhere in the image. The probe tests exactly this case.

Pseudocode – Sprenkel.anwenden(e, w, h)

ref ← Median(3, false).anwenden(e)            7×7 median as reference
conspicuous[i] ← |e[i] − ref[i]| > a          for all i
out ← copy of e;  visited ← false;  found ← 0
for start ← 0 to w·h − 1:
    if not conspicuous[start] or visited[start]: continue
    blob ← empty;  stack ← {start};  visited[start] ← true
    bounding box ← empty
    while stack not empty:
        i ← stack.pop();  blob.add(i);  extend bounding box by i
        if |blob| > 4·F:  clear stack;  stop       certainly too large
        for the four neighbours j of i inside the image:
            if conspicuous[j] and not visited[j]:
                visited[j] ← true;  stack.push(j)
    extent ← max(width, height) of the bounding box
    if |blob| > F or extent > A: continue          not a defect
    for each i in blob:  out[i] ← ref[i]
    found ← found + 1
return out

Source: envisionz-framework-image/src/envisionz/image/Sprenkel.java. Every point is pushed onto the stack at most once; the cost of the blob search is linear in the image size. Most of the computing time goes into the 7×7 median.

Worked examples

Single impulses

Test image 128 × 128: ramp 60+0.5x60 + 0.5\,x, with a circle of radius 35 and value 180. On 2 % of the points (347 of them, fixed random seed) the value is replaced by 16 or 235 – the limits of the luminance range, as with a tape dropout. PSNR against the undisturbed image:

MethodPSNRRemark
disturbed23.70 dB
Bilateral()23.70 dBthe impulses remain
Median() – 3×348.54 dB
Sprenkel()40.71 dB339 blobs replaced

For isolated points the small median is the right tool. Sprenkel does replace all 347 disturbed points – they form 329 blobs, because adjacent impulses are connected – but reports 339 finds: ten of them are not impulses. Even on the undisturbed image the method finds eight such spots on the circle boundary (see limits). The 3×3 median does not leave the undisturbed image untouched either: it changes 129 points on the circle boundary, because it rounds off its staircase.

Blotches

On the plain ramp 60+0.5x60 + 0.5\,x (without circle) there are five blotches of 3×3 points each with the value 235:

MethodPSNR
disturbed30.62 dB
Median() – 3×333.17 dB
Sprenkel() – 5 blobs found81.52 dB

The 3×3 median cannot remove a 3×3 blotch: in its window the blotch is the majority. Sprenkel recognises the five blobs completely and replaces them by the 7×7 median; the small remaining error arises because the median of a ramp does not exactly hit the centre value when the blotch occupies part of the window.

How large a blotch may be

With the defaults, square blotches 100 levels high on a flat background:

BlotchAreaResult
3×39removed completely
4×416removed completely
5×525rim removed, a core of 3×3 = 9 points remains
6×636rim removed, a core of 4×4 = 16 points remains

The reason is the reference value: from 5×5 on, the blotch occupies at least 25 of the 49 places of the 7×7 window in its centre, the median there is the blotch itself, and the core points do not count as conspicuous. The area limit of 40 is not even reached in these cases; the effective limit is set by the 7×7 window.

How it is tested

Filterprobe, sections median and speckle, with countable target values:

  • Median: a constant image stays constant; a single outlier (250 in 100) is removed exactly, also from the neighbouring points; likewise an outlier of −1000 in the image corner; a horizontal ramp stays exactly under the horizontal median; every output value occurs in the input; 1×1 image; image narrower than the kernel.
  • Speckle: constant image unchanged and nothing found; a single point is replaced by the background and exactly one defect counted; a 4×4 block stays completely at area limit 9 and is removed at limit 20; a line of seven points stays at extent limit 5 and is removed at 7; a deviation of 20 at limit 30 stays.
  • Independence of blobs: a scratch of 2 × 50 points and a single speckle in the same image – the speckle must be removed, the scratch must remain at the tested location.
  • Together with all filters: input unchanged, shift equivariance, tiny images, extreme values ±106\pm 10^6 within the value range without tolerance.

Limits

  • Fine structures: the median removes lines up to width rr and rounds corners. For lettering and chart lines even r=1r = 1 is too much if the strokes are only one point wide; then Sprenkel is the gentler choice.
  • Corners as speckles: on an undisturbed image with a sharp circle, Sprenkel replaces eight points – the individually protruding steps of the circle's staircase, which deviate by more than 30 levels from their 7×7 median and form a blob of area one. The method knows no difference between a speck of dust and a single genuine pixel of the same size.
  • Large blotches: from about half the window size (5×5 at 7×7) the reference median sees the blotch itself; a core remains (see example).
  • Weak defects: what is less than the deviation aa away from the median does not count as a defect. A fixed threshold in levels assumes that the plane is in the value range of the material.
  • Replacement is by the median, not by interpolation from the rim of the blob. In a ramp or at an edge this is an approximation.
  • Direction: connectivity via four neighbours separates diagonally connected points; an oblique scratch one point wide falls apart into single points and is removed as many small defects.
