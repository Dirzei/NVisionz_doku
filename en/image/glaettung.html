Image stabilisation – background

The computation behind Glaettung.glaetten: how the intended camera path is obtained from the frame-to-frame shifts, locally weighted regression of second degree, its equivalent filter kernel and frequency response, why neither a straight line nor a natural spline is enough, pseudocode following the source, worked example, testing and limits.

What it is about

A hand-held video recording shakes. To calm it, one first estimates for each frame by how many pixels it is shifted relative to the previous one, and sums these shifts. The result is the path of the camera over time, separately for horizontal and vertical. This path contains two things:

  • the intended motion – a pan, a tracking shot, a zoom. It is smooth: the camera is started, moves for a while and is braked;
  • the shake – fast, irregular deflections from frame to frame.

If a smooth curve is laid through the measured path, this curve is the intended motion, and the difference to the measured path is the shake. Each frame is then shifted back by exactly this difference. The core of stabilisation is thus a smoothing problem for a sequence of numbers: the shake should go, but the start, braking and changes of direction of the camera should remain.

Locally weighted regression of second degree

The model

Around each position ii of the sequence y0,…,yn−1y_0, \dots, y_{n-1} a parabola is laid through the neighbourhood. With the distance dj=j−id_j = j - i,

mina,b,c⁡∑j=i−Ri+Rgj(yj−a−bdj−cdj2)2,gj=exp⁡(−dj22σ2)\min_{a, b, c} \sum_{j = i-R}^{i+R} g_j \,\big(y_j - a - b\, d_j - c\, d_j^2\big)^2, \qquad g_j = \exp\!\left(-\frac{d_j^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)

is solved, and the smoothed value at position ii is the value of the parabola at d=0d = 0, that is aa. Near points count a lot, distant ones little; beyond RR not at all. At the beginning and end of the sequence the window is simply cut off.

The weights are linked to the window width FF (fenster):

σ=max⁡(1,F2),R=⌈2.5σ⌉\sigma = \max\!\left(1, \frac{F}{2}\right), \qquad R = \lceil 2.5\, \sigma \rceil

At F=15F = 15, therefore, σ=7.5\sigma = 7.5 and R=19R = 19: up to 39 values enter each parabola.

The normal equations

With pj=(1,dj,dj2)Tp_j = (1, d_j, d_j^2)^\mathsf{T} the minimisation leads to a linear system with three unknowns:

(∑jgjpjpjT)abc=∑jgjpjyj\Big(\sum_j g_j\, p_j p_j^\mathsf{T}\Big) \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \\ c \end{pmatrix} = \sum_j g_j\, p_j\, y_j

The matrix depends only on the weights and distances, not on the values yjy_j. This has two consequences that matter for understanding.

  • The smoothing is linear: the smoothed value is a fixed weighted sum of the input values, y^i=∑jwijyj\hat y_i = \sum_j w_{ij}\, y_j. It can therefore be regarded like an ordinary filter with a kernel ww.
  • Parabolas are reproduced exactly: if all yjy_j lie on a parabola, the minimum is zero and aa is exactly the value. Constants, straight lines and parabolas pass the smoothing unchanged, also at the border, as long as three points still lie in the window there.

The equivalent kernel

Smoothing a sequence that is zero everywhere except for a one at position ii yields the weights ww of the kernel. For F=15F = 15 in the middle of a long sequence, computed with Glaettung.glaetten:

The sum of all weights is one. Remarkable are the negative tails from about 12 steps on: through them the kernel preserves the curvature of a path. A purely positive kernel – an ordinary Gaussian mean – would flatten every curve.

Frequency response

What the kernel does to an oscillation is shown by a cosine sequence with period PP frames: after smoothing, it has amplitude H(P)H(P) in the middle. For F=15F = 15:

Fluctuations with a period up to about 15 frames are removed almost completely; of those from 40 frames on, 90 % or more remain; the transition lies in between. At 25 frames per second this means: shake faster than about 1.7 Hz disappears, motions extending over more than 1.6 seconds are largely preserved. The window width shifts this transition.

Why not a straight line, why not a spline

The class has a history that justifies its present method:

  • A local straight line (regression of first degree) does not reproduce the curvature of an accelerated motion. Its systematic error grows roughly with curvature times window width squared. According to the source comment it was larger than the shake itself in the trial, and smoothing achieved almost nothing: 1.30 against 1.38 for the raw values.
  • A natural spline through widely spaced support points overshot at the ends. A natural spline demands zero second derivative at the ends, and an accelerated camera motion does not have it. The result was worse than the raw values: 1.83 against 1.38.
  • The parabola follows acceleration and braking; the module documentation gives a deviation of 0.54 for the present smoothing with raw values of 1.38.
Convention in the framework: glaetten(werte, fenster) computes σ=max⁡(1,F/2)\sigma = \max(1, F/2) and R=⌈2.5σ⌉R = \lceil 2.5\sigma \rceil; windows 1 and 2 thus both give σ=1\sigma = 1. With fewer than three values or F<1F < 1 a copy of the input is returned. The linear system is solved by Gauss-Jordan elimination with partial pivoting; if a pivot is smaller than 10−1210^{-12}, the raw value remains at that position. The input is not modified. Since the restructuring, a natural spline exists only in framework-math (curves and splines).

Pseudocode – Glaettung.glaetten(werte, F)

n ← length(values)
if n < 3 or F < 1: return copy of values
σ ← max(1, F/2);  R ← ⌈2.5·σ⌉
for i ← 0 to n−1:
    M ← 3×4 zero matrix                         augmented matrix of the normal equations
    for j ← max(0, i−R) to min(n−1, i+R):
        d ← j − i;  g ← exp(−d²/(2σ²))
        p ← (1, d, d²)
        for a ← 0..2:
            for b ← 0..2:  M[a][b] ← M[a][b] + g·p[a]·p[b]
            M[a][3] ← M[a][3] + g·p[a]·values[j]
    a0 ← solve(M)                                only the first unknown
    out[i] ← if a0 undefined then values[i] else a0
return out

function solve(M):                              Gauss-Jordan with partial pivoting
    for s ← 0..2:
        swap row s with the row ≥ s with largest |M[·][s]|
        if |M[s][s]| < 1e−12: return undefined
        for each row z ≠ s:  row z ← row z − (M[z][s]/M[s][s]) · row s
    return M[0][3] / M[0][0]

Source: envisionz-framework-image/src/envisionz/image/Glaettung.java. Cost: per position 2R+12R+1 contributions to a 3×4 matrix and one elimination; negligible for a sequence of a few thousand frames.

Worked example

A pan over 40 pixels in 150 frames that starts and brakes gently:

xintended(t)=401+e−(t−75)/12x_{\text{intended}}(t) = \frac{40}{1 + e^{-(t - 75)/12}}

Shake is added as a normally distributed deviation with standard deviation 1.5 pixels (fixed random seed). The root-mean-square distance to the intended path is measured:

MethodDistance to the intended path
raw values1.523
glaetten(…, 5)0.571
glaetten(…, 9)0.411
glaetten(…, 15)0.306
glaetten(…, 25)0.277
glaetten(…, 41)0.568
for comparison: local straight line with the same weights, F=15F = 150.389

A window that is too small lets shake through, one that is too large rounds off start and braking – at F=41F = 41 the result is clearly worse again. The straight line for comparison (computed outside the module with the same weights) lies about a quarter above the parabola at the same window, because it flattens the start and braking. The correction per frame is then the difference path[i] − sum[i].

How it is tested

Filterprobe, section smoothing, with target values from the construction:

  • Degree of reproduction: the parabola 3+0.5i−0.25i23 + 0.5\,i - 0.25\,i^2 over 25 values is reproduced exactly with F=4F = 4, also at the border (to 10−910^{-9}); a constant likewise (to 10−1210^{-12}).
  • Linearity: smooth(aa) + smooth(bb) = smooth(a+ba + b) for a sine sequence and a straight line (to 10−910^{-9}).
  • Damping: a point-to-point shake (+1,−1,+1,…+1, -1, +1, \dots), the highest representable frequency, must be damped: the largest deflection afterwards is below one.
  • Short sequences (two values) are passed through unchanged, as a new array and without touching the input; with sufficient length, too, the input stays unchanged.

The tests of the former natural spline have been with CubicSpline in framework-math since the restructuring.

Limits

  • Choice of window. No window suits every recording; the transition between shake and intended motion lies where one puts it. A sudden, intended stop is always rounded off somewhat.
  • Ends. At the beginning and end of the sequence the window is one-sided. The kernel at the first value gives that value itself the weight 0.46 (at F=15F = 15) – there much less smoothing takes place than in the middle.
  • Outliers. The regression is not robust: a single wrongly estimated shift pulls the path along over the whole window width. Wrong estimates should be detected and replaced beforehand.
  • Scene cuts are jumps in the path, not camera motion; smoothing across a cut smears the jump. The sequence must be split at cuts.
  • One quantity per call. Horizontal and vertical shift, rotation and zoom are smoothed separately; relations between them do not enter.
