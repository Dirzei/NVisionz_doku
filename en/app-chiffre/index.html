envisionz-app-chiffre

Encrypt, decrypt, analyse and break classical ciphers

Overview of the app, as of 29 September 2026. The user interface to the cipher module: choose a method, encrypt or decrypt a text, examine the ciphertext statistically, have it broken, or load a historical piece from the collection.

50methods, each tested through the interface in both directions
5cards in the tool rail
5Java source files
1user page with screenshots, German and English
223checks in 5 test programs

What the app is for

Anyone working with cryptology usually has two tasks. One is to understand how a method works, from the Caesar shift and the Vigenère square to rotor machines. The other is to examine a ciphertext at hand: which method is behind it, and can it be read without the key?

The app puts both on one screen. The plaintext is at the top, the ciphertext below. On the left you pick the method, enter the key and press Encrypt or Decrypt. The same screen shows a frequency analysis of the ciphertext, starts the breakers and loads historical ciphertexts for practice. The methods themselves live in the framework module; the app contains no cryptography of its own.

What it can do

Operation follows the pattern of the other apps: settings sit as cards in a vertical tool rail on the left, not in dialogs. The page How to use walks through the steps. Icons instead of text, with the label in the tooltip.

Method card

Choice of the method and its key.

  • Filter by epoch and region, then pick from the catalogue.
  • Origin and description of the chosen method, taken from the framework.
  • Key fields that follow from the method; each field shows its default.
  • Switches for preserving the text form and for groups of five.

Analysis card

What can be read off the ciphertext.

  • A profile of the text.
  • A bar chart of letter frequencies, text against language.
  • The analysis follows your typing with a delay, so input is not slowed down.

Breaking card

Finding the key without knowing it.

  • The breaker is chosen by guess or explicitly, together with the language.
  • After a guess only the fast breakers run; the slow ones run only on explicit choice, and their duration is shown.
  • A field for a known beginning lets you supply a piece of plaintext; Hill and M-209 use it.
  • A list of candidates; Apply sets method, key and plaintext.
  • Long runs work in the background and can be cancelled.

Collection and chronicle cards

Practice material and overview.

  • Collection: historical pieces from the file sammlung.txt, loaded with or without the solution.
  • Chronicle: all methods in chronological order; a double click selects the method.

The rail also carries the actions Encrypt, Decrypt, Swap, Open and Save, and a handle for the help; it opens the docs app on the user page, like F1. The app is reached with ALT X and uses Fugue icons.

Data and formats

  • Text: plaintext and ciphertext can be opened and saved.
  • Collection: the historical pieces are kept in the file sammlung.txt.
  • Session: only the chosen method and the open card are remembered. Plaintexts, ciphertexts and keys are deliberately kept out of the session file.
  • Language: the interface is in German and English; method names and descriptions come from the framework and are in English.

What it builds on

  • framework-chiffre: catalogue, methods, breakers, analysis, collection and chronicle. The app only calls them.
  • framework-swing: tool rail with cards, background runner with cancel, icons.
  • framework-core and framework-api: registration as an app through the ServiceLoader, session state.

The app is small: five source files for the interface, including its own frequency chart and an input field for key parts.

State and limits

The app was created on 25 September 2026 and is fully wired: all 50 methods of the catalogue can be encrypted, decrypted, analysed, broken and loaded from the collection through the interface. Since 4 October 2026 the help lives in the general documentation (this overview, the user page and the framework-chiffre pages); the app’s former own help pages have been merged into it.

Testing runs headless in five programs: registration, session state, interface (every method in both directions, analysis, breaking including Apply, collection), help button (opens the docs app) and language bundles. The number of checks is 223.

Deliberately not included: saving texts and keys in the session. After a mere guess only the fast breakers run; the slow ones need an explicit choice.

A word of honesty: all methods here are historical and broken by today’s standards – even the one-time pad as soon as it is used twice. The app is for understanding, not for protecting data.
