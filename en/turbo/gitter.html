Plane blade cascades – background

The relations behind Gitter: velocity triangles, the Lieblein diffusion factor and the de Haller number as loading limits of the compressor cascade, the deviation after Carter, the Zweifel coefficient of the turbine and the profile loss after Soderberg – with worked examples from the probe.

What it is about

A plane blade cascade is the unrolled section through a blade row at one radius. It turns the relative flow from the inlet angle β1\beta_1 to the outlet angle β2\beta_2 (angles against the axis). Design asks: how strongly may a compressor cascade decelerate before the boundary layer separates? By how much does the flow lag behind the metal angle? How closely must turbine blades be spaced so that the load per blade remains bearable? And what loss is to be expected?

Lieblein diffusion factor, de Haller number

The deceleration on the suction side cannot be measured directly; Lieblein approximated it by quantities of the velocity triangles:

D=1−W2W1+|Wu1−Wu2|2σW1,σ=bs.D = 1 - \frac{W_2}{W_1} + \frac{|W_{u1} - W_{u2}|}{2\,\sigma\,W_1}, \qquad \sigma = \frac{b}{s}.

Where the form comes from. What is meant is the deceleration on the suction side, D≈(Wmax⁡−W2)/W1D \approx (W_{\max} - W_2)/W_1. The peak velocity is estimated from the circulation: by Kutta-Joukowski it is Γ=s|Wu1−Wu2|\Gamma = s\,|W_{u1} - W_{u2}| per blade; spreading it evenly over the chord, the suction side lies on average Γ/(2b)\Gamma/(2b) above W1W_1. Substituted:

Wmax⁡−W2W1≈W1+Γ/(2b)−W2W1=1−W2W1+|Wu1−Wu2|2σW1.\frac{W_{\max} - W_2}{W_1} \approx \frac{W_1 + \Gamma/(2b) - W_2}{W_1} = 1 - \frac{W_2}{W_1} + \frac{|W_{u1} - W_{u2}|}{2\,\sigma\,W_1}.

The estimate is crude – the real peak lies in the front third –, but Lieblein, Schwenk and Broderick showed that the momentum deficit of the suction-side boundary layer in cascade tests correlates well with DD: flat up to about 0.6, steep beyond. The limit rests on that, not on the derivation.

Values up to about 0.45 to 0.6 are considered acceptable. The de Haller number W2/W1W_2/W_1 should not fall below about 0.72. lieblein(W₁, β₁, β₂, σ, W_{x2}/W_{x1}) computes both; the ratio of axial velocities accounts for a contracting duct.

Worked example (from the probe). β1=50°\beta_1 = 50°, β2=20°\beta_2 = 20°, σ=1.5\sigma = 1.5, equal axial velocity: W2/W1=cos⁡50°/cos⁡20°=0.684W_2/W_1 = \cos 50°/\cos 20° = 0.684, D=1−0.684+cos⁡50°(tan⁡50°−tan⁡20°)/3=0.4933D = 1 - 0.684 + \cos 50°\,(\tan 50° - \tan 20°)/3 = 0.4933.

Deviation after Carter

The flow does not leave the cascade exactly at the metal angle α2'\alpha_2' but lags behind by the deviation δ\delta. After Carter (with Howell's coefficient)

δ=mθσ,m=0.23(2ac)2+α2'500\delta = \frac{m\,\theta}{\sqrt{\sigma}}, \qquad m = 0.23\,\Big(\frac{2a}{c}\Big)^2 + \frac{\alpha_2'}{500}

The rule is empirical (compressor cascades at design incidence): δ\delta grows with camber because the boundary layer on the more strongly curved suction side holds the exit flow back, and falls with solidity because more blades guide the flow longer. The exponent 1/21/2 applies to compressor cascades; for inlet guide vanes the literature gives 1.

with the camber θ\theta, the position of maximum camber a/ca/c and α2'\alpha_2' in degrees. For a circular-arc profile a/c=0.5a/c = 0.5 and m=0.23+α2'/500m = 0.23 + \alpha_2'/500. The probe also checks the dependence on σ\sigma: at four times the solidity δ\delta halves.

Zweifel coefficient of the turbine

Zweifel (1945; presented in Dixon and Hall) relates the actual tangential force of a turbine blade to an ideal pressure distribution (total pressure on the pressure side, exit pressure on the suction side):

Z=2sbxcos2⁡α2(tan⁡α1+tan⁡α2)Z = 2\,\frac{s}{b_x}\,\cos^2\alpha_2\,(\tan\alpha_1 + \tan\alpha_2)

Derivation. The actual tangential force per blade and unit span follows from the momentum balance over one pitch, Fu=ρscx(cu1+cu2)=ρscx2(tan⁡α1+tan⁡α2)F_u = \rho\,s\,c_x\,(c_{u1} + c_{u2}) = \rho\,s\,c_x^2\,(\tan\alpha_1 + \tan\alpha_2) at equal axial velocity. The ideal force is the largest pressure difference over the axial chord, (pt1−p2)bx=12ρc22bx(p_{t1} - p_2)\,b_x = \tfrac12\rho c_2^2\,b_x (loss-free, incompressible). With cx=c2cos⁡α2c_x = c_2\cos\alpha_2 the ratio becomes

Z=Fu12ρc22bx=2sbxcos2⁡α2(tan⁡α1+tan⁡α2).Z = \frac{F_u}{\tfrac12\rho c_2^2 b_x} = 2\,\frac{s}{b_x}\,\cos^2\alpha_2\,(\tan\alpha_1 + \tan\alpha_2).

Zweifel's value 0.8 is again experience: smaller means more blades and more friction surface, larger means a suction side with strong deceleration behind the throat.

(angles counted so that the turning is α1+α2\alpha_1 + \alpha_2). Good values lie around 0.8. teilungNachZweifel(Z, α₁, α₂) solves for the pitch ratio; without turning there is no pitch, and the input is rejected.

Worked example. Nozzle row with axial inflow to α2=65°\alpha_2 = 65°, Z=0.8Z = 0.8: s/b=0.8/(2cos2⁡65°tan⁡65°)=0.8/sin⁡130°=1.0443s/b = 0.8/(2\cos^2 65° \tan 65°) = 0.8/\sin 130° = 1.0443.

Profile loss after Soderberg

For preliminary design the simple correlation often suffices:

ζ*=0.04+0.06(ε100)2\zeta^* = 0.04 + 0.06\,\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{100}\Big)^2

This too is a correlation (Soderberg, as given by Dixon and Hall): the basic loss holds for a blade aspect ratio of 3, a Reynolds number around 10510^5 and a thickness around 20 % of the chord; the class contains no corrections for these.

with the turning ε\varepsilon in degrees – the same form as in the stator model of the NFML example (ZA0 + ZA1 (EPSA/100)^2).

How it is checked

Without turning and at equal axial velocity D=0D = 0 (1e-14); the example with 50° to 20° is recomputed by hand (D=0.49332D = 0.49332, de Haller 0.684040.68404 to 1e-5). Carter on the circular-arc profile exactly, Zweifel there and back to 1e-14, Soderberg at two points. The error cases: solidity 0, negative velocity, negative solidity for Carter, Zweifel without turning, ZZ = NaN.

Limits

Loss correlations based on maps (Ainley-Mathieson, Dunham-Came, Kacker-Okapuu) are not included: their curves exist only as diagrams and could not be taken over as checkable data.
