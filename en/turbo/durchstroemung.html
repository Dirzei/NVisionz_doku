Meridional throughflow – background

The computation behind Durchstroemung.rechne: radial equilibrium on quasi-orthogonals, the mass-flow condition, the position of the streamlines, slope and curvature from three stations and the under-relaxation without which the method oscillates – with derivation, closed-form solutions, pseudocode after the source, checks and limits.

What it is about

In the preliminary design of a compressor or turbine the flow is averaged over the circumference; what remains is the meridional plane (x,r)(x, r). Sought are the stream surfaces that divide the mass flow into equal parts, and on them the meridional velocity cmc_m. The blades act only through the swirl rcur c_u, prescribed per station – as a free vortex, a solid-body vortex or from a design.

The streamline curvature method computes on fixed lines across the duct, the quasi-orthogonals (here the planes x=const.x = \text{const.}). On each line the balance of forces across the flow holds; the mass flow fixes the level of the velocity profile. Because curvature and slope of the streamlines enter the balance, but the streamlines only follow from the result, the method iterates.

Radial equilibrium

With total enthalpy hth_t and entropy ss fixed per streamline (adiabatic, loss-free), the momentum balance across the flow gives for the square of the meridional velocity

dcm2dr=−1r2d(rcu)2dr+2cm2cos⁡φκ+2cmsin⁡φdcmdm\frac{d\,c_m^2}{dr} = -\frac{1}{r^2}\,\frac{d\,(r c_u)^2}{dr} \;+\; 2\,c_m^2 \cos\varphi\;\kappa \;+\; 2\,c_m \sin\varphi\,\frac{d c_m}{dm}(1)

with the slope φ\varphi of the streamline against the axis, its curvature κ=r''/(1+r'2)3/2\kappa = r''/(1 + r'^2)^{3/2} and the derivative along the meridional direction mm. The first term is simple radial equilibrium: a free vortex (rcur c_u constant) gives uniform cmc_m, a solid-body vortex one falling outwards. The second term is the centrifugal force of the curved meridional motion: above a bump on the hub (κ<0\kappa < 0) the pressure rises outwards and the flow near the hub becomes faster. The third term accounts for acceleration along an inclined streamline.

(1) is integrated on each quasi-orthogonal from the hub outwards with Heun's method (predictor and corrector), on 101 equally spaced points (adjustable). Slope, curvature and dcm/dmdc_m/dm come from the streamlines of the previous pass and are transferred linearly to the fine grid.

Derivation

The starting point is two equations along the quasi-orthogonal (radial here). The Gibbs relation with the total enthalpy ht=h+(cm2+cu2)/2h_t = h + (c_m^2 + c_u^2)/2 gives

dhtdr=Tdsdr+1ρdpdr+cmdcmdr+cudcudr,\frac{dh_t}{dr} = T\,\frac{ds}{dr} + \frac{1}{\rho}\frac{dp}{dr} + c_m\frac{dc_m}{dr} + c_u\frac{dc_u}{dr},

and the momentum balance across the flow (Euler, inviscid, without blade force) balances the pressure rise against the centrifugal forces of the swirl and of the curved meridional path, and the acceleration along an inclined streamline:

1ρdpdr=cu2r−cm2cos⁡φκ−cmsin⁡φdcmdm.\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{dp}{dr} = \frac{c_u^2}{r} - c_m^2\cos\varphi\,\kappa - c_m\sin\varphi\,\frac{dc_m}{dm}.

Substituting and combining the swirl with cudcu/dr+cu2/r=12r2d(rcu)2/drc_u\,dc_u/dr + c_u^2/r = \tfrac{1}{2r^2}\,d(r c_u)^2/dr gives the general form (Smith 1966)

ddrcm22=dhtdr−Tdsdr−12r2d(rcu)2dr+cm2cos⁡φκ+cmsin⁡φdcmdm.\frac{d}{dr}\frac{c_m^2}{2} = \frac{dh_t}{dr} - T\frac{ds}{dr} - \frac{1}{2r^2}\frac{d(r c_u)^2}{dr} + c_m^2\cos\varphi\,\kappa + c_m\sin\varphi\,\frac{dc_m}{dm}.

With dht/dr=0dh_t/dr = 0 and ds/dr=0ds/dr = 0 – equal total enthalpy and entropy on all streamlines – and times 2 this is (1). The signs follow the convention of Durchstroemung: κ\kappa positive when the streamline is concave seen from the axis (r''>0r'' > 0).

Three closed-form solutions against which the method is checked (recomputed):

  • Free vortex rcu=Kr c_u = K in a cylindrical duct: all terms vanish, cmc_m is uniform. Without swirl the streamlines then divide the annulus area evenly: rj2=rH2+jn−1(rC2−rH2)r_j^2 = r_H^2 + \tfrac{j}{n-1}(r_C^2 - r_H^2).
  • Solid-body vortex cu=ωrc_u = \omega r: dcm2/dr=−4ω2rd c_m^2/dr = -4\omega^2 r, hence cm2=C−2ω2r2c_m^2 = C - 2\omega^2 r^2; CC follows in closed form from the mass flow.
  • Curved duct, at the crest φ=0\varphi = 0: dcm2/dr=2cm2κd c_m^2/dr = 2 c_m^2\kappa, hence cm=cm,Hexp⁡∫κdrc_m = c_{m,H}\exp\int\kappa\,dr. And in a conical annulus (hub and casing rays from one point on the axis) the source flow c=K/ρ2c = K/\rho^2 with the distance ρ\rho from the apex: straight streamlines, κ=0\kappa = 0, the profile on x=const.x = \text{const.} is carried by the slope term alone, cC/cH=cos2⁡φC/cos2⁡φHc_C/c_H = \cos^2\varphi_C/\cos^2\varphi_H.

The mass-flow condition

(1) fixes the profile only up to the initial value cm,hubc_{m,\text{hub}}. The mass flow determines it:

m˙=2π∫rHrCρcmcos⁡φrdr,ρ=pRT,T=Tt−cm2+cu22cp,p=pt(TTt)γ/(γ−1).\dot m = 2\pi \int_{r_H}^{r_C} \rho\, c_m \cos\varphi\; r\, dr, \qquad \rho = \frac{p}{R\,T},\quad T = T_t - \frac{c_m^2 + c_u^2}{2 c_p},\quad p = p_t \Big(\frac{T}{T_t}\Big)^{\gamma/(\gamma - 1)}.

In the compressible case m˙(cm,hub)\dot m(c_{m,\text{hub}}) rises only up to the choking limit and falls afterwards – there is a subsonic and a supersonic branch. During development a secant from the starting value ran onto the supersonic branch. The computation is therefore: the bracket is extended upwards from the last value by a factor of 1.5 while the mass flow still rises; if that does not suffice, the station is choked and the computation ends with a message. Inside the bracket the Illinois method (regula falsi with halving of the fixed end) runs; it stays on the subsonic branch.

Why there is a maximum. For a stream tube ρc\rho c at fixed total state is at most the critical mass flux at Ma⁡=1\mathrm{Ma} = 1; beyond it ρ\rho falls faster than cc rises. Integrated over the cross-section the same holds for m˙(cm,H)\dot m(c_{m,H}): asking for more than the station passes at sonic speed has no solution – that is the message “choked”, not a numerical weakness.

Position, slope and curvature of the streamlines

From the cumulative mass flow (trapezoidal rule) the radii are found where the fraction j/(n−1)j/(n-1) is reached; hub and casing are the first and last streamline. The initial position divides the annulus area evenly.

Full pipe. A hub of radius 0 is allowed. On the axis cu=(rcu)/rc_u = (r c_u)/r is finite only if rcur c_u vanishes there; then cu=0c_u = 0. A swirl rcu≠0r c_u \ne 0 on the axis is rejected.

Slope and curvature of a streamline at station ii come from the parabola through three neighbouring stations (at the ends the first or last three):

r'(x)=d01+c2(2x−x0−x1),r''=2c2,d01=r1−r0x1−x0,c2=d12−d01x2−x0.r'(x) = d_{01} + c_2\,(2x - x_0 - x_1), \quad r'' = 2 c_2, \qquad d_{01} = \frac{r_1 - r_0}{x_1 - x_0},\ c_2 = \frac{d_{12} - d_{01}}{x_2 - x_0}.

For a parabola both derivatives are exact; otherwise the error of the curvature is of order Δx\Delta x (at the ends) or Δx2\Delta x^2 (inside). Likewise dcm/dm=(dcm/dx)cos⁡φdc_m/dm = (dc_m/dx)\cos\varphi comes from the parabola through cmc_m at the same three stations; in the conical duct of the probe (station spacing 0.1) the velocity ratio is thereby 1.5e-3 off the source flow.

Without damping the method oscillates as soon as the duct height hh is large compared with the station spacing Δx\Delta x: a small shift of a streamline changes its curvature like 1/Δx21/\Delta x^2. The new position is therefore only partly taken over, with Wilkinson's factor

rnew=r+ω(requilibrium−r),ω=11+2(h/Δxmin⁡)2.r^{\text{new}} = r + \omega\,(r^{\text{equilibrium}} - r), \qquad \omega = \frac{1}{1 + 2\,(h/\Delta x_{\min})^2}.

Without curvature terms (or with fewer than three stations) ω=0.5\omega = 0.5.

Why (h/Δx)2(h/\Delta x)^2. Shifting a streamline at one station by δr\delta r changes its curvature there by about 2δr/Δx22\,\delta r/\Delta x^2. Through the curvature term the equilibrium of the next pass shifts the streamline by an amount of order (h/Δx)2δr(h/\Delta x)^2\,\delta r – with close stations more than it was shifted, and with alternating sign: the iteration oscillates. Under-relaxation makes the amplification factor smaller than one; the form 1/(1+A(h/Δx)2)1/(1 + A (h/\Delta x)^2) comes from Wilkinson's stability analysis, the constant A=2A = 2 is this code's (in the literature it depends on the Mach number).

Cost. One pass costs one Illinois search per station (each trial one integration over the points of the quasi-orthogonal), i.e. roughly stations × search steps × points evaluations. The number of passes grows like 1/ω1/\omega: in the conical duct of the probe (ω≈0.027\omega \approx 0.027) it is 854 until the change falls below 10−10ω10^{-10}\,\omega of the duct height.

Pseudocode

initial position: r[i][j] ← √(r_H² + j/(n−1) · (r_C² − r_H²))         equal areas
ω ← 1/(1 + 2 (h_max/Δx_min)²)  (with curvature, ≥ 3 stations), else 0.5
repeat up to 20 000 times, until largest change ≤ 1e−10 · ω:
    for each streamline j, station i:
        (r', r'') ← parabola through three stations;  φ ← atan r';  κ ← r''/(1 + r'²)^1.5
        dc/dm ← slope of the parabola through c_m · cos φ   (0 in the first pass)
    for each station i:
        profile(c₀): Heun over 101 points from the hub outwards, eq. (1); ṁ cumulative (trapezoid)
        extend bracket [a, b] for c₀ while ṁ(b) < ṁ_target and ṁ rises   otherwise “choked”
        c₀ ← Illinois in [a, b] until |ṁ − ṁ_target| ≤ 1e−14 ṁ_target
        new radii of equal mass-flow fractions;  r ← r + ω (r_new − r)
c_u ← (r c_u)(i, r)/r at the final positions

Limits

  • Adiabatic and loss-free; no entropy rise along the streamlines, no blade blockage.
  • Quasi-orthogonals perpendicular to the axis; strongly radial ducts (centrifugal compressors) would need inclined lines.
  • Ideal gas with fixed cpc_p and γ\gamma.
  • One total state for the whole duct: swirl that changes between stations is computed without work exchange (as behind a stator). Behind a rotor hth_t would rise by Δ(Ucu)\Delta(U c_u) according to Euler – the class does not model that.
  • The result reports passes, last change and, since 2026-10-19, konvergiert() with meldung() (maximum reached, position not finite); the maximum is 20 000, selectable via rechne(…, hoechst). Before, the caller had to read from the residual whether the tolerance was reached, and a NaN ended the loop silently.
