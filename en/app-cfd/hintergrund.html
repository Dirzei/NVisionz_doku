Convergence, comparison and preview – background

What the app computes by itself although it only calls the solver: when a run counts as converged, how the reference values of the case collection come about, how the preview estimates the size of a mesh and places the boundaries in the cut, and how a stored result notices that its case has changed. The methods of the solver itself are on the pages of framework-cfd.

What this is about

A flow solver delivers a residual per step and a field at the end. Whether that is good has to be decided by someone: has the run got far enough? Does the field agree with a known solution? The app answers both with small, open computations that are listed on this page – so that a green header or a deviation of 0.1 % can be traced.

When a run converges

Residuals differ greatly in size from case to case: around one when non-dimensional, around 10510^{5} in SI units. The app therefore measures relatively. The reference is the largest residual of the first ten steps since set-up or since continuing:

Rref⁡=maxk≤10⁡Rk,rn=max⁡(RnRref⁡,RnTRref⁡T,RnSRref⁡S)R_{\mathrm{ref}} = \max_{k \le 10} R_k ,\qquad r_n = \max\!\left(\frac{R_n}{R_{\mathrm{ref}}},\ \frac{R^{T}_n}{R^{T}_{\mathrm{ref}}},\ \frac{R^{S}_n}{R^{S}_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)

RR is the residual of density, RTR^{T} that of the turbulence quantities and RSR^{S} that of the species, each with its own reference; the last two only count when they are computed. The run counts as converged as soon as rn<10−mr_n \lt 10^{-m} with mm from “drop in orders of magnitude” – or when the residual is exactly zero. The red target line in the residual history is Rref⁡⋅10−mR_{\mathrm{ref}} \cdot 10^{-m}.

The maximum over ten steps instead of the first step has a reason: with many methods the residual still rises in the first steps before it falls. Continuing keeps the reference, so that three plus three steps give the same as six in one go. The downside is under limits.

Oblique shock

For a wedge with angle θ\theta in a supersonic flow with Mach number M1M_1 the shock relations give the shock angle β\beta, the pressure ratio p2/p1p_2/p_1 and the Mach number M2M_2 behind it (weak solution, framework-cfd). The computed field has no jump but a rise smeared over a few cells. The app therefore selects the cells

pc>p1+12(p2p1−1)p1p_c \gt p_1 + \tfrac12\left(\frac{p_2}{p_1} - 1\right) p_1

and takes the median of pressure and Mach number over them. The median is insensitive to the few cells inside the shock that lie just above the threshold, and to the region at the corner where the shock starts.

Stagnation point

When gas flows against a body at subsonic speed, it comes to rest at the stagnation point. Without losses the pressure there is the isentropic total pressure of the free stream:

p0=p∞(1+γ−12M∞2)γ/(γ−1)p_0 = p_\infty \left(1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M_\infty^2\right)^{\gamma/(\gamma - 1)}

The largest pressure in the field is compared with p0p_0. This holds for any body as long as the free stream stays subsonic; in supersonic flow a shock stands in front of the body and loses total pressure, and the app rejects the comparison with a reason.

Flat plate

Laminar, the boundary layer grows self-similarly after Blasius: the velocity profile depends on one coordinate only,

η=yUνx,uU=f'(η),cf=0.664Rex⁡\eta = y \sqrt{\frac{U}{\nu x}} ,\qquad \frac{u}{U} = f'(\eta) ,\qquad c_f = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}_x}}

with Rex⁡=Ux/ν\mathrm{Re}_x = U x / \nu. At the station xx (entry ref.vergleich.x) the app looks for the column of cells with this centre and compares each cell up to η=8\eta = 8 with f'(η)f'(\eta) from framework-cfd; the largest deviation is reported. The wall shear stress comes from the gradient at the wall, approximated with the first cell: τw≈μu1/y1\tau_w \approx \mu\, u_1 / y_1, hence cf=τw/(12ρU2)c_f = \tau_w / (\tfrac12 \rho U^2). The viscosity follows from the Reynolds number of the case, μ=ρUL/Re⁡\mu = \rho U L / \mathrm{Re}.

Turbulent, there is no closed solution. The app compares with two correlations, that of Coles and Fernholz and the 1/5 law,

cf=0.455ln2⁡(0.06Rex⁡),cf=0.0592Rex−1/5⁡c_f = \frac{0.455}{\ln^2(0.06\,\mathrm{Re}_x)} ,\qquad c_f = 0.0592\,\mathrm{Re}_x^{-1/5}

and optionally with a third reference value from the case file (ref.vergleich.cf), for instance from a database of computed reference solutions. The wall gradient from the first cell is a good approximation only when that cell lies in the viscous sublayer. The app therefore reports its wall distance in wall units:

y+=y1uτν,uτ=Ucf/2y^+ = \frac{y_1\, u_\tau}{\nu} ,\qquad u_\tau = U \sqrt{c_f / 2}

Above one the comparison should be read with care.

Mass balance and free limits

In steady through-flow as much mass must leave as enters. The solver knows the mass flow through every boundary; the app divides the magnitude of the outflow by that of the inflow and expects one. Which boundaries are meant is given by ref.vergleich.ein and ref.vergleich.aus (default einlass and auslass, several separated by commas).

|m˙aus⁡||m˙ein⁡|=1\frac{|\dot m_{\mathrm{aus}}|}{|\dot m_{\mathrm{ein}}|} = 1

For all other cases there are free limits: ref.FIELD.min, .max or .mittel against a value. Minimum and maximum run over all cells, the mean is weighted by cell volume, f=∑fcVc/∑Vc\bar f = \sum f_c V_c / \sum V_c; non-finite values do not count.

Each row of the comparison table shows computed value, reference and the deviation in percent of the reference; for a reference of zero, as for the deviation from the Blasius profile, the absolute deviation is shown.

Which edge belongs to which boundary

The mesh picture is a plane cut through the mesh: one polygon per cut cell. An edge of this polygon lies on a boundary if its midpoint m\mathbf m lies in a boundary face of the same cell. With face centre cf\mathbf c_f and face vector Af\mathbf A_f (magnitude equal to the area) the app checks

|(m−cf)⋅Af||Af|≤10−3|Af|,|m−cf|2≤2.25|Af|\frac{|(\mathbf m - \mathbf c_f) \cdot \mathbf A_f|}{|\mathbf A_f|} \le 10^{-3} \sqrt{|\mathbf A_f|} ,\qquad |\mathbf m - \mathbf c_f|^2 \le 2.25\, |\mathbf A_f|

– the midpoint lies in the plane of the face and no farther from its centre than one and a half times its edge length. Faces almost parallel to the cut plane (|Af⋅n|>0.98|Af||\mathbf A_f \cdot \mathbf n| \gt 0.98\,|\mathbf A_f|) do not count: the whole polygon lies in the top face of a layer mesh, otherwise every edge would be a boundary. The probe checks this: for the wedge the length of each boundary line equals the boundary area divided by the layer thickness, for the box exactly the four sides lie in the cut, bottom and top do not.

Stored results

A stored result belongs to exactly the case it was computed with. To show whether the case file has changed since, ergebnis.properties carries a checksum: SHA-256 over the lines of the case file without surrounding blanks, without empty lines and without comments. Comments are left out because the case collection writes the folder into the header – a moved case would otherwise remain “changed” for ever.

If the cell count of the result no longer matches the mesh of the case, it is transferred to the new mesh via the cell centres, with up to twelve source cells per cell, as for a restart (framework-cfd). The header of the tab then says that the case has changed.

Limits

  • The convergence reference comes from the run itself. A restart on an already converged solution has a very small reference, and further orders of magnitude are then hardly reachable.
  • cfc_f from the first cell is a first-order approximation; on coarse meshes at the wall it comes out too low.
  • The comparison at the oblique shock assumes a free stream without angle of attack; the stagnation point assumes loss-free deceleration.
  • The estimate of the mesh size only knows the shapes of the table and an average measure for files; the limit is a matter of waiting time, not of correctness.
