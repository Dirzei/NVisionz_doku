Mixing operators – selection by weight and hit rate

The method behind MixedProposer: how an operator is chosen per child, how feedback reaches the right operator, what the hit rate measures and how adaptive weighting follows it sluggishly – with pseudocode following the source, a worked weight trajectory, the checks and the limits.

What this is about

Every operator has a landscape in which it is good and one in which it fails. Gaussian mutation is usable everywhere and fast nowhere; differential evolution needs a minimum population; CMA-ES needs a run-up; an operator for structural genes does not touch numbers at all. Often the timing is decisive as well. The module documentation records a measurement on a search over filter orders, with an earlier, since removed operator that justified its proposals with a language model:

Operatorafter 15 evaluationsafter 60 evaluations
reasoned proposals25.54–
random mutation23.5428.54

Measurements from the class comment of MixedProposer.

The two profiles run in opposite directions: the reasoned proposals are ahead with a small budget, because their prior knowledge suggests good moves; with a larger budget they lose, because the same prior knowledge restricts them – the winning chain put a stage at the end that the proposer considered ruled out and never tried. Chance has no such blinkers but needs attempts. Hence the idea of the mixer: several operators in the same cycle, over the same archive, and measuring which of them actually delivers improvements.

Selection per child

Every entry ii has a weight wi>0w_i > 0; the weights need not sum to 1. For each of the nn requested children an operator is drawn by the roulette-wheel principle,

P(i)=wi∑jwj,P(i) = \frac{w_i}{\sum_j w_j},

and this operator is asked for one child with the same parents and the same context. A batch of 16 children can thus be mixed from three operators; which operator supplied which child the mixer remembers under the child's identifier.

Pseudocode – MixedProposer.propose(parents, context, n)

if no entries: return empty list
if adaptive: rebalance()                           once per call, i.e. per generation
W ← Σ w_i
for k ← 1 to n:
    r ← U(0,1) · W;  acc ← 0;  chosen ← last entry
    for each entry e in registration order:
        acc ← acc + e.w
        if r ≤ acc: chosen ← e;  break
    children ← chosen.operator.propose(parents, context, 1)
    for each child in children:
        origin[child.id] ← chosen
        append child

Source: envisionz-framework-evolve/src/envisionz/evolve/MixedProposer.java (propose, add). add rejects a weight ≤0\le 0 with an exception.

Feedback and hit rate

The engine reports every evaluated child with tell(child, evaluation, accepted). The mixer looks up which entry supplied the child, updates its statistics and passes the report on to exactly this operator – so CMA-ES and the surrogate model also receive their observations within the mix. The hit rate of an entry is

hi={ai/pi,pi>00.5,pi=0,h_i = \begin{cases} a_i / p_i, & p_i > 0 \\ 0.5, & p_i = 0, \end{cases}

with pip_i = children reported back (proposed()) and aia_i = those of them admitted to the archive (accepted()). It measures how often an operator occupies a niche for the first time or improves it. The ratio is the most informative figure of a mixed run: if it falls towards zero for an operator, that operator brings nothing in this landscape – and one sees it without starting a second run.

Pseudocode – tell and feedback

// tell(child, evaluation, accepted) – from the engine
e ← origin[child.id]                              read before feedback removes it
feedback(child, accepted)
if e ≠ none: e.operator.tell(child, evaluation, accepted)

// feedback(child, accepted) – also for drivers of one's own
e ← remove origin[child.id];  if e = none: done
e.p ← e.p + 1
if accepted: e.a ← e.a + 1
Convention in the framework: proposed() is incremented in feedback, not when proposing. Whoever has proposals made and reports nothing back sees zero, even after a hundred proposals. For the hit rate that is right: it measures accepted children against evaluated ones, not against those whose fate is still unknown. In 2026 the probe found that the comment "how many proposals it has made" said the opposite; the sentence was corrected, not the counting. Invalid children count as proposed and not accepted.

Adaptive weighting

With adaptive = true the weights are moved towards the hit rates at the start of every propose. With a floor of 0.05 per rate

h˜i=max⁡(0.05,hi),ti=h˜i∑jh˜j,wi←0.9wi+0.1ti.\tilde h_i = \max(0.05,\ h_i), \qquad t_i = \frac{\tilde h_i}{\sum_j \tilde h_j}, \qquad w_i \leftarrow 0.9\, w_i + 0.1\, t_i.

This is exponential smoothing towards the target tit_i, the entry's share of the hit rates. Three consequences:

  • Inertia. With fixed hit rates each weight approaches its target as wi(g)=ti+0.9g(wi(0)−ti)w_i^{(g)} = t_i + 0.9^{\,g}\,(w_i^{(0)} - t_i); the distance halves every ln⁡0.5/ln⁡0.9≈6.6\ln 0.5 / \ln 0.9 \approx 6.6 generations. An operator does not fall silent after a few failures – intentionally, because the profiles take effect at different speeds.
  • Normalisation. The targets sum to 1. Initial weights of any size shrink or grow to this scale over time; the ratio of the initial weights is lost.
  • Floor. Even an operator without a single hit keeps a target of at least 0.05/∑jh˜j0.05/\sum_j \tilde h_j – with two operators and a partner at hit rate 1 that is 4.8 %.

Pseudocode – rebalance()

S ← Σ_i max(0.05, h_i)
for each entry i:
    t_i ← max(0.05, h_i) / S
    w_i ← 0.9 · w_i + 0.1 · t_i

How it is checked

Evolveprobe, section "the mixer", with two Gaussian operators on one gene:

  • Two registrations give two entries; requesting 200 children yields 200.
  • Before feedback proposed() is zero for all; after 200 reports the sum is exactly 200 – each child counted exactly once –, and both routes got their turn.
  • 200 rejected reports give zero acceptances; ten accepted ones afterwards give ten.
  • The hit rate then lies between zero and one.

Adaptive weighting itself is not the subject of a check, and according to the module documentation it has not yet been measured on the real problem.

Limits

  • Cumulative hit rate. hih_i counts over the whole run, without forgetting. If the situation changes late – an operator becomes useful only towards the end –, the rate follows only slowly.
  • Hit rates fall during the run. The fuller and better the archive, the more rarely any child improves a niche. An operator chosen mostly early looks better than one chosen only later.
  • Children without feedback remain in the origin table; a driver of one's own that never reports back lets it grow.
  • Empty sub-proposals. If the chosen operator delivers nothing (differential evolution below four individuals), the child is missing from the batch; no replacement is drawn.
  • One child per call. Each sub-operator is called individually per child. Differential evolution rebuilds its population every time; with large genomes that costs time.
