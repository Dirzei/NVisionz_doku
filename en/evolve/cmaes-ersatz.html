Numeric genes – CMA-ES and surrogate model

The computation behind CmaEsProposer and SurrogateProposer, the two learning operators for numeric genes: the evolution strategy with covariance matrix adaptation with all constants as they stand in the code, and the surrogate model built from distance-weighted averaging with an uncertainty bonus – with pseudocode following the source, recomputed examples, the checks and the limits. The genome ↔ vector mapping, Gaussian mutation and differential evolution are on the page Gauss and DE.

What this is about

Gaussian mutation and differential evolution are stateless: on every call they see only what is currently in the archive. Two situations call for more.

  • Coupled parameters. If an improvement succeeds only when two parameters are changed together in a particular ratio – an oblique, narrow valley of the objective –, a mutation with independent genes rarely hits the valley. CMA-ES learns from the evaluations so far a covariance matrix whose principal axes align with such valleys, and a step size that regulates itself.
  • Expensive evaluations. If an evaluation takes seconds to minutes, it pays to think before the next attempt. The surrogate model estimates the fitness from all previous evaluations in fractions of a second, is queried thousands of times and proposes where a high estimate and high uncertainty coincide.

Both learn from the engine's feedback (tell) and therefore have a state that grows over the generations.

CMA-ES: the distribution

CMA-ES maintains a multivariate normal distribution N(m,σ2C)\mathcal{N}(m, \sigma^2 C) with mean mm, step size σ\sigma and covariance matrix CC. With the eigendecomposition C=BD2BTC = B D^2 B^{\mathsf T} – BB the eigenvectors as columns, DD the square roots of the eigenvalues – a child is drawn as

x=m+σBDz,z∼N(0,I).x = m + \sigma\, B D z, \qquad z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I).

Each generation produces λ\lambda children. After evaluation they are sorted by descending fitness, x1:λx_{1:\lambda} being the best. The μ=⌊λ/2⌋\mu = \lfloor \lambda/2 \rfloor best determine the new distribution with logarithmically decreasing weights:

wi=ln⁡(μ+12)−ln⁡i∑j=1μ(ln⁡(μ+12)−ln⁡j),μeff⁡=1∑i=1μwi2,m'=∑i=1μwixi:λ.w_i = \frac{\ln(\mu + \tfrac12) - \ln i}{\sum_{j=1}^{\mu} \left(\ln(\mu + \tfrac12) - \ln j\right)}, \qquad \mu_{\mathrm{eff}} = \frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{\mu} w_i^2}, \qquad m' = \sum_{i=1}^{\mu} w_i\, x_{i:\lambda}.

μeff⁡\mu_{\mathrm{eff}} is the "effective number" of parents: with equal weights it would be μ\mu; with the decreasing weights it is smaller.

Evolution paths, covariance, step size

With the normalised step of the mean y=(m'−m)/σy = (m' - m)/\sigma, two smoothed paths are updated. The step-size path pσp_\sigma measures the steps in the metric of CC, i.e. as if the distribution were spherical:

pσ←(1−cσ)pσ+cσ(2−cσ)μeff⁡C−1/2y,C−1/2=BD−1BT.p_\sigma \leftarrow (1 - c_\sigma)\, p_\sigma + \sqrt{c_\sigma (2 - c_\sigma)\, \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}}\; C^{-1/2} y, \qquad C^{-1/2} = B D^{-1} B^{\mathsf T}.

If ‖pσ‖\lVert p_\sigma \rVert is larger than expected for random steps – the steps keep pointing in the same direction –, σ\sigma is too small; if it is smaller – the steps cancel –, σ\sigma is too large. The expected length of an nn-dimensional standard normal vector is approximated by

χn=n(1−14n+121n2),σ←σexp⁡(cσdσ(‖pσ‖χn−1)).\chi_n = \sqrt{n}\left(1 - \frac{1}{4n} + \frac{1}{21 n^2}\right), \qquad \sigma \leftarrow \sigma \exp\!\left(\frac{c_\sigma}{d_\sigma}\left(\frac{\lVert p_\sigma\rVert}{\chi_n} - 1\right)\right).

The covariance path pcp_c accumulates the steps unrotated; it carries the direction in which things went uphill repeatedly of late. As long as ‖pσ‖\lVert p_\sigma\rVert is not unusually large it is updated (hσ=1h_\sigma = 1), otherwise stalled (hσ=0h_\sigma = 0):

hσ=[‖pσ‖1−(1−cσ)2(g+1)χn<1.4+2n+1],pc←(1−cc)pc+hσcc(2−cc)μeff⁡y.h_\sigma = \left[\frac{\lVert p_\sigma\rVert}{\sqrt{1 - (1 - c_\sigma)^{2(g+1)}}\; \chi_n} < 1.4 + \frac{2}{n+1}\right], \qquad p_c \leftarrow (1 - c_c)\, p_c + h_\sigma \sqrt{c_c (2 - c_c)\, \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}}\; y.

The covariance combines a rank-one update from pcp_c with a rank-μ\mu update from the selected steps yi=(xi:λ−m)/σy_i = (x_{i:\lambda} - m)/\sigma, measured from the old mean:

C←(1−c1−cμ+(1−hσ)c1cc(2−cc))C+c1pcpcT+cμ∑i=1μwiyiyiT.C \leftarrow \left(1 - c_1 - c_\mu + (1-h_\sigma)\, c_1 c_c (2 - c_c)\right) C + c_1\, p_c p_c^{\mathsf T} + c_\mu \sum_{i=1}^{\mu} w_i\, y_i y_i^{\mathsf T}.

The constants

All learning rates depend only on the dimension nn and μeff⁡\mu_{\mathrm{eff}} – there is nothing to set except σ0\sigma_0 and, if desired, λ\lambda:

λ=4+⌊3ln⁡n⌋,cc=4+μeff⁡/nn+4+2μeff⁡/n,cσ=μeff⁡+2n+μeff⁡+5,c1=2(n+1.3)2+μeff⁡,\lambda = 4 + \lfloor 3 \ln n \rfloor, \quad c_c = \frac{4 + \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}/n}{n + 4 + 2\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}/n}, \quad c_\sigma = \frac{\mu_{\mathrm{eff}} + 2}{n + \mu_{\mathrm{eff}} + 5}, \quad c_1 = \frac{2}{(n+1.3)^2 + \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}},

cμ=min⁡(1−c1,2(μeff⁡−2+1/μeff⁡)(n+2)2+μeff⁡),dσ=1+2max⁡(0,μeff⁡−1n+1−1)+cσ.c_\mu = \min\!\left(1 - c_1,\; \frac{2\,(\mu_{\mathrm{eff}} - 2 + 1/\mu_{\mathrm{eff}})}{(n+2)^2 + \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}}\right), \qquad d_\sigma = 1 + 2 \max\!\left(0,\; \sqrt{\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{eff}} - 1}{n+1}} - 1\right) + c_\sigma.

Conventions

Convention in the framework: One step size for all genes, in absolute units. σ0\sigma_0 is multiplied by the mean span of all genes. If the genes have very different spans – a radius in pixels next to a weight between 0 and 1 –, the distribution starts far too wide for one and far too narrow for the other; CC has to learn that first.
Convention in the framework: CMA-ES follows its own mean, not the archive. The children are drawn around mm, not around the drawn parents. The parent only supplies the identifier for the lineage and – on the very first call, fixed afterwards – the non-numeric genes. The archive collects the results but does not steer the distribution.
Convention in the framework: Invalid children count as −∞-\infty. They belong to the generation and end up at the bottom of the ranking. As long as at most λ−μ\lambda - \mu children of a generation are invalid, they do not shape mean and covariance. If there are more, clipped or invalid points move into the μ\mu selected ones.
Convention in the framework: After λ\lambda reports the distribution is updated and the waiting list is cleared. If the engine delivers batches with more than λ\lambda children – default 16; with twelve genes λ=11\lambda = 11 –, the reports of the remaining five go nowhere. Batches smaller than λ\lambda accumulate over several generations. The clipped point is stored, so the update computes with what was actually evaluated.

Surrogate model

The surrogate model learns the mapping "genome → fitness" from all pairs (xi,fi)(x_i, f_i) seen so far. It computes in normalised coordinates z=normalize⁡(x)∈[0,1]nz = \mathrm{normalize}(x) \in [0,1]^n, so that every gene counts equally, with the fixed bandwidth h=0.25h = 0.25.

Estimate. A distance-weighted mean of the known fitness values with Gaussian weights:

f^(x)=∑iwifi∑iwi,wi=exp⁡(−‖z−zi‖2h2).\hat f(x) = \frac{\sum_i w_i f_i}{\sum_i w_i}, \qquad w_i = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\lVert z - z_i \rVert^2}{h^2}\right).

If ∑wi<10−12\sum w_i < 10^{-12} – the point lies far from everything –, the mean of all fitness values applies. The class comment calls the model "radial basis functions"; mathematically it is a kernel-weighted mean after Nadaraya and Watson. The difference matters: the estimate does not pass through the observed points, it smooths.

Uncertainty. The distance dmin⁡d_{\min} to the nearest evaluated point, scaled by the spread sfs_f of the known fitness values (sample standard deviation, 1 with fewer than two values):

u(x)=sf(1−exp⁡(−dmin⁡2h2)).u(x) = s_f \left(1 - \exp\!\left(-\frac{d_{\min}^2}{h^2}\right)\right).

At an evaluated point u=0u = 0; far away it approaches sfs_f.

Selection. What is proposed is not the point with the best estimate but the one with the largest expected gain

a(x)=f^(x)+βu(x).a(x) = \hat f(x) + \beta\, u(x).

β=0\beta = 0 is purely greedy and gets stuck in the known; β=1\beta = 1 explores strongly. The maximum is sought not analytically but among probes random points: every third one (index 0, 3, 6, …) is uniform in the whole box, the others scatter normally with 15 % of the span around the first parent.

Pseudocode – SurrogateProposer

// propose(parents, context, count)
if parents empty: return empty list
if no observation yet: observe all context.topExamples (genome, fitness)
if fewer than 3 observations: return empty list
p ← parents[0];  centre ← toVector(p.genes)
for k ← 1 to count:
    best ← none;  a* ← −∞
    for s ← 0 to probes − 1:
        for each gene i:
            x_i ← (s mod 3 = 0) ? l_i + U(0,1)·span_i               free in the box
                                : centre_i + N(0,1)·0.15·span_i        near the parent
        x ← clamp(x)
        a ← f̂(x) + β · u(x)
        if a > a*: a* ← a;  best ← x
    child ← p.child(toGenome(best, p.genes), MUTATION);  remember best under child.id

// tell(child, evaluation, accepted)
if evaluation usable and fitness finite:
    x ← remembered point for child.id, otherwise toVector(child.genes)
    observe (x, fitness)
else: forget child.id                                   −∞ would poison the model

In the measured comparison of the module documentation (twelve parameters, 200 evaluations), CMA-ES was still at the starting value after 25 evaluations, reached −0.8906 after 50 and stayed there; the surrogate model had −0.8593 already after 25 evaluations and did not improve afterwards; differential evolution reached −0.8462 (table on the page Gauss and DE).

How it is checked

Evolveprobe checks both operators against their promises, not against a search result:

  • CMA-ES: the generation starts at zero; without a parent an empty list comes back; with a parent λ=8\lambda = 8 children come, all within the bounds [−10,10]2[-10, 10]^2; after λ\lambda reports with fitness −(x2+y2)-(x^2+y^2) the generation advances – otherwise the method would learn nothing and be an expensive random search; the same seed gives the same children.
  • Surrogate model: zero observations without observations; eleven observations of −(x−7)2-(x-7)^2 arrive; the prediction at 7 is better than at 0; the uncertainty at 7 is not larger than at the edge at 10 (tolerance 10−910^{-9}); all proposals lie within the bounds.

Neither the convergence of CMA-ES nor the location of the maximum of f^+βu\hat f + \beta u is checked; the worked examples on this page are our own recomputations.

Limits

  • CMA-ES without feedback stands still: the distribution is never updated, and the operator is then a Gaussian cloud around the starting point. The engine reports back; a driver of one's own must do so itself.
  • Lost reports with batches larger than λ\lambda (see conventions). Whoever runs CMA-ES alone sets batchSize to λ\lambda or a multiple.
  • Jacobi eigendecomposition costs O(n3)O(n^3) per sweep and according to the comment suffices up to about n=50n = 50; for more genes the code makes no promise.
  • One step size for all genes with very different spans – normalise beforehand or choose σ0\sigma_0 small.
  • CMA-ES and MAP-Elites do not pull in the same direction. CMA-ES converges on one optimum; diversity across the niches arises only incidentally.
  • Surrogate: smoothing instead of interpolation – the estimate does not even hit evaluated points. The bandwidth is fixed at 0.25 and adapts neither to the dimension nor to the number of observations; in many dimensions the points lie far apart in normalised units; the estimate is then dominated by the respective nearest point and falls back to the mean of all fitness values as soon as the weight sum drops below 10−1210^{-12}.
  • Surrogate: cost grows linearly with the number of observations per query, i.e. with observations × probes × children per generation. Old observations are never forgotten.
  • Surrogate: at least three observations. Before that it delivers nothing; used alone, the same applies as for differential evolution (Search run).
  • Every learned model has blind spots, and a search against it finds them. The uncertainty bonus counteracts this but does not replace an evaluation.
