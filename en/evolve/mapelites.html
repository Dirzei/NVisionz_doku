MAP-Elites – niches, archive and parent selection

The computation behind Candidate, Evaluation, Evaluator.Axis and MapElitesArchive: what a niche is, how a candidate gets into its cell, by which rule it stays there, how parents are drawn and what the key figures say – with pseudocode following the source, a recomputed run, the checks and the limits. The loop around the archive is described on the page Search run.

What this is about

A classical optimisation method looks for one point: the parameters at which an objective is largest. It keeps the best candidate so far and discards everything worse. That is consistent as long as the objective is smooth and has exactly one peak. In rugged landscapes it almost always ends on the nearest secondary peak: the way to the real optimum leads over intermediate stages that are worse on their own than what one already has – and exactly these stages have been thrown away.

MAP-Elites (Multi-dimensional Archive of Phenotypic Elites) turns the question around. One chooses one or two features by which solutions differ in their behaviour – for a compressor design, say, pressure ratio and mass flow; for curve tracing, say, smoothness and coverage. These features span a grid. Every cell of the grid, a niche, holds exactly one candidate: the best that ever fell into this cell. A candidate therefore only competes with its own kind. A design with a low pressure ratio is not discarded merely because a better one with a high pressure ratio exists elsewhere.

The result is not a single solution but a map: for every region of behaviour the best known solution. Such methods are called quality-diversity methods – they seek quality and diversity at once. MAP-Elites often also finds the better single solution, although it does not search for it exclusively: the mediocre candidates in neighbouring niches are the stepping stones from which the search reaches better regions.

Convention in the framework: The module knows no application. What a candidate means, how good it is and which niche it falls into is decided solely by the application's Evaluator. The archive sees only numbers: a fitness and one descriptor value per axis.

Candidate, evaluation, status

A candidate (Candidate) carries a genome: an ordered map of named strings, for instance {"radius": "2.5", "stufen": "4", "glatt": "true"}. In addition it has a short identifier (the first eight characters of a random UUID), the identifier of its parent, its origin and its generation (seeds 0, every child one more than its parent).

The evaluation (Evaluation) consists of a scalar fitness ff, a vector of descriptors d=(d1,…,dk)d = (d_1,\dots,d_k) – one per archive axis, in the order of the axes –, free additional metrics for reports and a status:

StatusMeaningFitness
OKcandidate evaluatedthe measured value
INVALIDthe candidate is unusable: parse error, NaN, range violation, no convergence−∞-\infty
ERRORthe evaluation itself failed – a fault in the evaluator, not in the candidate−∞-\infty

An evaluation is usable exactly when the status is OK and the fitness is finite. An evaluator that inadvertently reports ok(NaN, …) is therefore not admitted.

Convention in the framework: Fitness is always "larger is better". Error measures (an RMS deviation, a residual) are negated by the evaluator itself, e.g. f=−RMS⁡f = -\mathrm{RMS}. Invalid and failed are both unusable but remain distinguishable: an evaluator that crashes on certain inputs would otherwise look like a region full of invalid solutions – the search would avoid it instead of someone fixing the fault. If an evaluator throws an exception, the engine turns it into an ERROR with class name and message.

Axes and niches

An axis (Evaluator.Axis) has a name, a unit, a range [dmin⁡,dmax⁡][d_{\min}, d_{\max}] and a number of cells mm. The constructor requires m≥1m \ge 1 and dmax⁡>dmin⁡d_{\max} > d_{\min}. A descriptor value dd is mapped linearly onto the range and rounded down:

i(d)=min⁡(m−1,max⁡(0,⌊m⋅d−dmin⁡dmax⁡−dmin⁡⌋))i(d) = \min\left(m-1,\; \max\left(0,\; \left\lfloor m \cdot \frac{d - d_{\min}}{d_{\max} - d_{\min}} \right\rfloor\right)\right)

The cells are half-open intervals of equal width Δ=(dmax⁡−dmin⁡)/m\Delta = (d_{\max}-d_{\min})/m. Values below the range fall into the first cell, values above into the last; the boundary value dmax⁡d_{\max} itself belongs to the last cell. With kk axes the niche is the tuple (i1,…,ik)(i_1,\dots,i_k), and the grid has

C=∏j=1kmjC = \prod_{j=1}^{k} m_j

cells (capacity()). Recomputed for an axis "pressure ratio" from 1 to 5 with four cells (Δ=1\Delta = 1):

Convention in the framework: A NaN descriptor lands in cell 0 – not outside the grid and not as an error. The archive stores only occupied cells, in a concurrent hash table keyed by "i1:i2:…:". A grid with three axes of 20 cells each has 8000 cells and is practically never full; memory is spent only on what is occupied.

Choosing the axes

Choosing the axes is the real work – the method is only as good as they are. Two conditions must hold:

  • The descriptors describe the behaviour of the solution and come from the same computation as the fitness. If they are derived afterwards from the design variables, the grid describes where a candidate came from, not what it does – it becomes a re-sorting of the search space instead of a map of the solution space.
  • The descriptors are not coupled to the fitness. If fitness rises with a descriptor, all good solutions lie in the cells at one end; the grid turns into a ranking and diversity is lost.

The number of cells is a compromise: cells that are too coarse make different solutions compete with each other and lose stepping stones; cells that are too fine spread the evaluation budget over so many niches that little is improved in each.

Insertion: the best per niche

The entire selection of MAP-Elites lies in one rule. If E(c)E(c) is the elite of cell cc and a candidate xx with a usable evaluation and fitness f(x)f(x) comes into this cell, then

E(c)←{x,if c is empty or f(x)>f(E(c))E(c),otherwise.E(c) \leftarrow \begin{cases} x, & \text{if } c \text{ is empty or } f(x) > f(E(c)) \\ E(c), & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}

A candidate is thus accepted if it occupies its niche for the first time or strictly beats the elite there. Whether it is better or worse than elites of other niches does not matter.

Pseudocode – MapElitesArchive.insert(candidate, evaluation)

attempts ← attempts + 1                          atomic counter
if not evaluation.usable:                        status OK and fitness finite
    rejected ← rejected + 1
    return false

cell ← (bin_1(d_1), …, bin_k(d_k))               descriptor length ≠ number of axes: exception
key ← "i1:i2:…:"
new ← Elite(candidate, evaluation, cell)

// in one step, without a race between threads:
winner ← grid.merge(key, new,
            (old, new) ↦ if new.fitness > old.fitness then new else old)

if winner = new:  accepted ← accepted + 1;  return true
else:            rejected ← rejected + 1;  return false

Source: envisionz-framework-evolve/src/envisionz/evolve/MapElitesArchive.java (insert, cellOf) and Evaluator.java (Axis.bin). The merge is ConcurrentHashMap.merge: comparison and replacement happen atomically, which is why insert may be called from parallel threads.

Convention in the framework: The comparison is strict (>>). At equal fitness the older elite stays; a candidate that merely repeats the same fitness counts as rejected. Invalid and failed evaluations count as an attempt and as a rejection, but never occupy a cell.

Parent selection

The archive does not only store diversity, it feeds it back: the parents of the next generation are drawn from the archive. sampleParents(n, eliteBias, rnd) mixes two strategies. Let MM be the number of occupied cells and K=max⁡(1,⌊M/4⌋)K = \max(1, \lfloor M/4 \rfloor) the size of the top quarter by fitness. Each of the nn draws is independent:

  • with probability bb (eliteBias) uniformly from the KK best elites – exploitation;
  • otherwise uniformly from all MM elites – exploration, the classical behaviour of MAP-Elites.

For an elite in the top quarter the probability per draw is therefore

Ptop=bK+1−bM,Pother=1−bM.P_{\text{top}} = \frac{b}{K} + \frac{1-b}{M}, \qquad P_{\text{other}} = \frac{1-b}{M}.

Worked example. Twelve occupied cells, b=0.35b = 0.35 (default of Config.defaults()): K=3K = 3; the best elite is drawn per draw with 0.35/3+0.65/12=0.17080.35/3 + 0.65/12 = 0.1708, one from the lower part with 0.65/12=0.05420.65/12 = 0.0542. The top quarter together receives 0.35+0.65⋅3/12=0.51250.35 + 0.65 \cdot 3/12 = 0.5125 of all draws. Counted over 200 000 draws with the module's archive: 0.1713 and 0.5127.

Pseudocode – MapElitesArchive.sampleParents(n, eliteBias, rnd)

all ← list of elites                         empty → return empty list
rank ← all, sorted by fitness, descending
K ← max(1, ⌊|all| / 4⌋)
for z ← 1 to n:
    if rnd.uniform() < eliteBias:
        take rank[rnd.int(K)]                 one of the K best
    else:
        take all[rnd.int(|all|)]              any one

Source: envisionz-framework-evolve/src/envisionz/evolve/MapElitesArchive.java (sampleParents).

Convention in the framework: Drawing is with replacement; the same elite may appear several times in one call. eliteBias = 0 is pure MAP-Elites, eliteBias = 1 draws only from the top quarter and gives away part of the diversity. The default 0.35 lies in between.

The QD score rises when an elite is improved and – for positive fitness – also when a new niche is occupied. That is why it measures both.

Convention in the framework: qdScore() sums the fitness without an offset. If the fitness is negative – a negated error, for instance –, the QD score drops as soon as a new niche is occupied: recomputed, it falls from −1 to −3 when a second elite with −2 in another cell joins an elite with fitness −1. Whoever uses the QD score as a measure of progress shifts the fitness in the evaluator by a fixed bound into the positive. In the literature the fitness is usually shifted to non-negative values for the QD score in the same way.

How it is checked

EvolveTest (40 checks) checks the bookkeeping, not individual run results:

  • Niche mapping: with the axes pressure ratio 1…5 in four cells and mass flow 0…100 in five cells, the lower corner lies in (0,0)(0,0), the upper corner (5,100)(5, 100) in (3,4)(3,4), values far outside fall into edge cells, and the capacity is 20.
  • The core of MAP-Elites: a worse candidate in the same niche is rejected, a better one accepted, and a worse one in a different niche is accepted. Under a leaderboard it would be lost.
  • Status: invalid and failed are both unusable but distinguishable; an invalid evaluation is not admitted.
  • Key figures: the QD score is the sum of fitness values (tolerance 10−910^{-9}), coverage never exceeds capacity, parent selection delivers the requested number.

Evolveprobe adds, on the search run over the sphere function: the best value never gets worse over the generations – not an expectation of the search but a property of the archive –, and it never lies above the known maximum 0.

Limits

  • Cells of equal width. The axes are linear and evenly divided. Descriptors that spread over orders of magnitude are taken to the logarithm by the evaluator beforehand.
  • Curse of dimensionality. The number of cells grows as the product of the cells per axis. More than two or three axes spread the budget over so many niches that hardly any is improved.
  • Noisy evaluation. An elite stays until a candidate beats it. If the evaluation scatters, lucky outliers remain; the archive does not average and does not re-evaluate.
  • QD score with negative fitness – see Key figures.
  • Origin REPAIR. The class comment provides it for clipped proposals. The numeric operators of the module do clip to the bounds, but mark their children as MUTATION or CROSSOVER; the number of repairs therefore cannot be read from the origin.
  • Repeatability of identifiers. Candidate identifiers are random and differ from run to run; the search itself does not depend on them.
