Meshes and Surfaces in 3D – Background

The mathematics behind DelaunayTet, StructuredTets, IsoSurface3D, AxisSlice3D, HullSurface3D and ContourBand3D: how points with a field value become a tetrahedral mesh, and how iso-surfaces, cutting planes and colour bands are obtained from it.

Tetrahedra as building blocks

A map f(x,y,z)f(x,y,z) or a CFD result is given as points with a field value each. Between the points ff is to vary linearly – as in 2D triangulation on a triangle. In 3D the simplest region with a linear variation is the tetrahedron: four corners, four triangular faces, six edges. On it ff is determined uniquely by the four corner values, and all further methods on this page work tetrahedron by tetrahedron.

A TetMesh stores the corners of each tetrahedron (tets[4t … 4t+3]), positively oriented, that is orient3d⁡(V0,V1,V2,V3)>0\mathrm{orient3d}(V_0,V_1,V_2,V_3) > 0, and for each face the neighbour: adj[4t+i] belongs to the face opposite corner ii, and −1-1 stands at the boundary. The orientation is the determinant

orient3d⁡(a,b,c,d)=|ax−dxay−dyaz−dzbx−dxby−dybz−dzcx−dxcy−dycz−dz|=6V.\mathrm{orient3d}(a,b,c,d) = \left|\begin{matrix} a_x-d_x & a_y-d_y & a_z-d_z \\ b_x-d_x & b_y-d_y & b_z-d_z \\ c_x-d_x & c_y-d_y & c_z-d_z \end{matrix}\right| = 6\,V.

Delaunay in 3D

DelaunayTet carries Bowyer-Watson (see Triangulation in 2D) over to tetrahedra: instead of the empty circumcircle the empty circumsphere holds, and the cavity consists of all tetrahedra whose circumsphere contains the new point. The cavity is deleted and re-meshed in a star shape from pp. A point on a face or edge is therefore the normal case, not a special path. The points are inserted in Morton order (Z-curve) and found by walking from neighbour to neighbour. The same decisions apply as in 2D:

  • Computed per axis on [0,1], output in raw values. The duplicate tolerance applies per axis and is 10−910^{-9} of the span; coincident points are removed explicitly, there is no random jitter.
  • Exact predicates (Geom3.orient3dExakt, inSphereGrob, inSphereExakt): floating point, BigDecimal when in doubt. The orientation is computed on the raw values, the in-sphere test exactly on (x−xmin⁡)sysz(x-x_{\min})\,s_y s_z and the corresponding values of the other axes – not on rounded normalised values.
  • Ghost tetrahedra over the hull faces with one point at infinity instead of a super-tetrahedron. The super-tetrahedron lay 30 units out, and a flat boundary tetrahedron with a circumsphere around a super corner was lost – two boundary tetrahedra on 216 scattered points.
Decision in the framework: Slivers remain in 3D. Even for an isotropic distribution, 5 % of the tetrahedra at scattered points are almost flat (volume/edge³ below 0.001); Delaunay guarantees no minimum angle in 3D, because four almost cospherical points give a valid, almost flat tetrahedron. The way out for structured data is the next section. In the worst case the 3D Delaunay triangulation can produce O(n2)O(n^2) tetrahedra; for evenly distributed data it is about 6n6n.

Structure before Delaunay: six tetrahedra per cell

Delaunay lays tetrahedra over regions that do not belong to the computational domain: over a blade, into a recess, between two blocks. If the grid structure (i,j,k)(i,j,k) is known, the mesh is built constructively, with no geometric decision about connections. StructuredTets splits each hexahedral cell, following Kuhn/Freudenthal, along the main diagonal from the corner with the smallest to the corner with the largest index into six tetrahedra – one for each order in which the three index directions are traversed. With the corners as a bit mask (x=1x = 1, y=2y = 2, z=4z = 4) these are

{0,1,3,7},{0,1,5,7},{0,2,3,7},{0,2,6,7},{0,4,5,7},{0,4,6,7}.\{0,1,3,7\},\ \{0,1,5,7\},\ \{0,2,3,7\},\ \{0,2,6,7\},\ \{0,4,5,7\},\ \{0,4,6,7\}.

Each side face is thereby split along its own diagonal from the smallest to the largest index corner, and that diagonal is the same seen from both adjacent cells. The split is therefore conforming without a cell having to know its position. The minimal split into five tetrahedra would need a chessboard pattern by the parity of i+j+ki+j+k for this; an error there would give holes or double faces. For a unit cell each of the six tetrahedra has volume 1/61/6.

StructuredTets.build(x, y, z, f, iMax, jMax, kMax):
    check iMax, jMax, kMax ≥ 2  and length(x) = length(y) = length(z) = length(f) = iMax·jMax·kMax
    for k, j, i  (i fastest):  cells
        p ← i + iMax·(j + jMax·k)
        for m ← 0 to 7:  corner[m] ← p + (m&1) + iMax·( ((m>>1)&1) + jMax·((m>>2)&1) )
        for each quadruple (a,b,c,d) of the KUHN table:
            o ← orient3d(corner[a], corner[b], corner[c], corner[d])
            if o = 0:  continue  no volume: dropped
            if o > 0:  store (a, b, c, d)   else:  store (a, b, d, c)  make positive
    return TetMesh(x, y, z, f, tets, adjacency(tets))  coordinates unchanged

Source: envisionz-framework-data/src/envisionz/data/tri/StructuredTets.java. Point order as in Tecplot zones: ii fast, kk slow. merge appends blocks without connecting them.

Iso-surfaces by marching tetrahedra

The iso-surface f=cf = c is the surface on which the field has the value cc. In a tetrahedron ff is linear, so the surface is planar: the four corners are classified by whether f>cf > c ("inside"), and the edges on which the sign of f−cf - c changes are cut. On an edge from aa to bb the intersection lies at

t=c−fafb−fa,x→=x→a+t(x→b−x→a).t = \frac{c - f_a}{f_b - f_a}, \qquad \vec{x} = \vec{x}_a + t\,(\vec{x}_b - \vec{x}_a).

Depending on the number of inside corners one of three pictures results: with one or three inside corners the plane cuts three edges – a triangle –, with two inside corners four – a quadrilateral, which is split into two triangles. With zero or four inside corners there is nothing. Anyone who handles only the triangle case loses half of the cut surface, and the result looks perforated.

Pseudocode – IsoSurface3D.surface(mesh, scalar, level)

for each tetrahedron t:
    p[0..3] ← corners with position and scalar value
    if any quantity is NaN:  continue
    inside ← { c | scalar[c] > level };  outside ← the rest  a value exactly on the level counts as outside
    if |inside| = 0 or 4:  continue
    dir ← centroid(inside) − centroid(outside)  direction towards the higher value
    if |inside| = 1:  triangle from the cuts of the edges (i, a) for the three outside corners a
    if |inside| = 3:  triangle from the cuts of the edges (o, a) for the three inside corners a
    if |inside| = 2:  quadrilateral ik, il, jl, jk → triangles (ik, il, jl) and (ik, jl, jk)
    turn each triangle so that its normal points along dir  one-sided lighting
    append if |cross product|² > 1e-24 · (longest edge)⁴  otherwise degenerate: discard

Source: envisionz-framework-data/src/envisionz/data/tri/IsoSurface3D.java. A triangle is output as [x0,y0,z0, x1,y1,z1, x2,y2,z2]; levels(min, max, n) returns nn equally spaced levels in the open interval: ck=fmin⁡+(k+1)fmax⁡−fmin⁡n+1c_k = f_{\min} + (k+1)\,\dfrac{f_{\max}-f_{\min}}{n+1}, for fmin⁡=0f_{\min}=0, fmax⁡=1f_{\max}=1, n=3n=3 that is 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75.

Decision in the framework: Inside means strictly greater than the level; a corner exactly on it counts as outside. Without that, a level exactly at the minimum produces triangles whose three corners coincide at the minimum point – such surfaces without area are discarded, the bound being relative to the longest edge so that rounding in a + (b − a) does not count. The intersection points are limited to t∈[0,1]t \in [0,1]; for fb=faf_b = f_a (absolute difference below 10−30010^{-300}) t=0.5t = 0.5 holds.

Cutting planes, hull and colour bands

  • AxisSlice3D cuts the mesh at xaxis=wx_{\text{axis}} = w by the same pattern, only here the scalar is the coordinate itself: position and ff are interpolated linearly on the edges, and the output carries ff per corner so that the cut surface can go straight through ContourBand3D. If the plane lies exactly on the largest coordinate, a corner on it counts as inside, so that both boundary planes yield a cut (otherwise the lower would give the full cross-section and the upper nothing). slice2D projects the cut into the plane.
  • HullSurface3D delivers the boundary faces: a tetrahedron face is a boundary face if adj = −1, that is, there is no real neighbour. Each face has 12 values: three corners with ff.
  • ContourBand3D splits surface triangles along given levels into filled bands: ff runs linearly over the triangle; for each band the triangle is clipped to lo≤f≤hilo \le f \le hi with two threshold cuts (Sutherland-Hodgman in ff-space) and the result is triangulated as a fan. This gives sharply bounded colour bands with true iso-lines instead of a soft gradient.
  • GridSurface3D splits a regular height surface z=f(x,y)z = f(x,y) into two triangles per cell; cells with missing corner values (NaN) are dropped, and the height doubles as the colour value.

How it is checked

  • Tetraederprobe (81 checks): point count, raw values in the output, volume equals hull (box exactly, scatter cloud with an independently computed hull), positive orientation, adjacency, empty circumsphere in the normalised space, the same tetrahedra and the same sliver rate at 1:1:1 to 50000:1.5:0.001, duplicates per axis, 300 integer grids.
  • Strukturtetraederprobe (38 checks): conformity over the boundary faces of the index box, exact volume on sheared integer grids, points, wedge and ring segment against Delaunay. It also shows that the five-tetrahedron split is not conforming without the chessboard pattern.
  • Geometrieprobe: the tetrahedra of a cube grid fill the cube exactly, the hull has its surface area and Euler characteristic 2; signs of the predicates against integer and BigDecimal computation; band surfaces recomputed by hand.

Limits

  • Delaunay in 3D guarantees no minimum angle; 5 % almost flat tetrahedra at scattered points are normal. A tetrahedron of the Kuhn split, by contrast, always has the shape of the cell.
  • A cell grid without known indices is not read off in 3D; Delaunay remains, and with it the convex hull.
  • The iso-surface is piecewise planar and is only as accurate as the linear interpolation in the tetrahedron; a strongly curved field needs a fine mesh.
  • Cells with NaN corner values are missing from the surface.
