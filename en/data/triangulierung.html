Triangulation in 2D, Grids and Boundary – Background

The mathematics behind BowyerWatson, GuibasStolfi, SweepHull, Gitterfinder, AlphaShape and ChiShape: how a point cloud becomes a triangle mesh, when it is better to bypass the Delaunay computation, and how the concave boundary comes about.

Delaunay: the empty circumcircle

A contour plot from scattered measurement points needs a mesh of triangles on which the quantity varies linearly. Many meshes are possible; the good ones have as few sharp triangles as possible. The Delaunay triangulation chooses the mesh in which the circumcircle of no triangle contains another point. It maximises the smallest angle of all triangles and – except for cases with four points on a circle – it is unique.

Two sign tests are enough, both as determinants. The orientation says whether cc lies to the left of a→ba \to b (result greater than zero, counter-clockwise); the in-circle test says whether dd lies inside the circumcircle of the counter-clockwise triangle (a,b,c)(a,b,c):

orient⁡(a,b,c)=(bx−ax)(cy−ay)−(by−ay)(cx−ax)\mathrm{orient}(a,b,c) = (b_x-a_x)(c_y-a_y) - (b_y-a_y)(c_x-a_x)

inCircle⁡(a,b,c,d)=|ax−dxay−dy(ax−dx)2+(ay−dy)2bx−dxby−dy(bx−dx)2+(by−dy)2cx−dxcy−dy(cx−dx)2+(cy−dy)2|\mathrm{inCircle}(a,b,c,d) = \left|\begin{matrix} a_x-d_x & a_y-d_y & (a_x-d_x)^2+(a_y-d_y)^2 \\ b_x-d_x & b_y-d_y & (b_x-d_x)^2+(b_y-d_y)^2 \\ c_x-d_x & c_y-d_y & (c_x-d_x)^2+(c_y-d_y)^2 \end{matrix}\right|

If inCircle is greater than zero, dd lies inside the circle; at zero it lies on it. If a point violates the circumcircle, the shared edge of the two triangles is wrong and is replaced by the other diagonal of the quadrilateral (edge flip). With nn points, hh of them on the convex hull, every Delaunay triangulation has exactly

T=2n−h−2T = 2n - h - 2

triangles (Euler's formula); the tests use this as an independent count check.

Computed per axis on [0,1]

The circumcircle is not invariant under unequal stretching of the axes: a map with speed on one axis and pressure ratio on the other has spans that differ by orders of magnitude. On raw values the Delaunay triangulation, with an x-span of 2.7 against a y-span of 0.1, looked in the plot like half slivers, and at 50 000 against 1.5 triangles were missing altogether. But the plot draws both axes at full size – so the triangles have to be good in that space.

All methods therefore stretch each axis on its own to the interval [0,1]:

x'=x−xmin⁡xmax⁡−xmin⁡x' = \frac{x - x_{\min}}{x_{\max} - x_{\min}}

An axis without span is only shifted. The Delaunay property thus holds in the per-axis normalised space, not in the raw one.

Decision in the framework: The computation is normalised, but the output is always the original coordinates, bit-identical as a copy. Nothing is converted back – the caller assigns the corners to its table rows via the unchanged values. AlphaShape, ChiShape and DelaunayTet also measure in this space, so their thresholds are unit-free and the same for the same point picture at any aspect ratio.

Bowyer-Watson with ghost triangles

The default method (BOWYER_WATSON) inserts the points one at a time. For each new point pp all triangles whose circumcircle contains pp are found; they form the cavity. They are deleted, and pp is connected to all corners of the cavity boundary. Because every boundary edge has on its far side a triangle without pp in its circumcircle, the result is again Delaunay.

It is usual to put a huge auxiliary triangle around all points. That has a flaw: a flat boundary triangle has an arbitrarily large circumcircle, and if a corner of the auxiliary triangle lies in it, the triangle is lost. With a fixed distance factor of 20 this happened in 21 of 40 random sets of 200 points; factors of 1000 and 10610^6 also failed on individual sets. No distance is always enough – so there is none. Instead of auxiliary corners, ghost triangles over each hull edge carry a single symbolic point GG at infinity. The "circumcircle" of a ghost triangle over the hull edge u→vu \to v is the open outer half-plane together with the open edge segment; the in-circle test turns into an orientation test there.

Pseudocode – BowyerWatson.triangulate(x, y, f)

if n < 3:  return empty mesh
X, Y ← each axis stretched to [0,1]  Geom.aufEinheit
order ← points in Hilbert-curve order  spatial locality ⇒ short walks when locating
a ← order[0];  b ← first point different from a
c ← first point with |orient(a, b, c)| > EPS  EPS = 1e-12; otherwise: no triangles
if orient(a, b, c) < 0:  swap b, c  counter-clockwise
start mesh ← triangle (a,b,c) and three ghost triangles over its edges
t ← start triangle
for each point p in order, except a, b, c:
    t ← locate(p, from t)  walk from neighbour to neighbour; beyond a hull edge ⇒ its ghost triangle
    if t not found:  t ← search through all triangles
    if a corner of t is closer than EPS to p:  continue  skip duplicate
    cavity ← t, then step by step every neighbour with inCircle(neighbour, p) > 0
    delete all triangles of the cavity
    for each boundary edge (va, vb) of the cavity:
        new triangle (va, vb, p);  set adjacency outwards and to the sisters
    t ← one of the new triangles
return all triangles without G, with the original coordinates  = triangulation of the convex hull

Source: envisionz-framework-data/src/envisionz/data/tri/BowyerWatson.java. Storage is index-based with a free list, adjacency is updated during insertion, with no pass over all triangles afterwards. Expected running time O(nlog⁡n)O(n \log n).

Other methods behind the registry

Triangulierverfahren is the registry: identifier (stored), text key for the user interface and factory. A new method is entered only there; selection fields, creation and the Verfahrensprobe pick it up by themselves. With an unknown identifier erzeuge throws UnbekanntesVerfahren instead of silently falling back to the default.

  • GUIBAS_STOLFI – divide and conquer (Guibas and Stolfi, 1985) on a quad-edge structure: sort the points lexicographically and remove duplicates, halve, triangulate each half on its own (two and three points are the base cases), stitch the halves together from bottom to top along the common lower tangent. On each side the next candidate is searched; edges whose circumcircle contains it are dropped, and the candidate whose circumcircle does not contain the other is joined to the base line. O(nlog⁡n)O(n \log n) guaranteed, not only on average; about four times slower than Bowyer-Watson.
  • SWEEP_HULL – sort the points along a rotated axis, connect each point to the hull edges visible from it, and finish with a pass of Lawson flips. With a collinear start it falls back to Bowyer-Watson.
  • ConstrainedDelaunay – forces given segments (boundaries, holes, break lines) into the mesh: first the ordinary triangulation, then each segment is inserted by flipping away crossing edges (convex ones first, non-convex ones postponed), and finally a Lawson pass that leaves the fixed edges alone.
Decision in the framework: The predicates are exact. The floating-point value counts only if it lies outside its error bound, otherwise the result is recomputed with BigDecimal – stitching is a sequence of yes/no decisions about the same edges, and one that hinges on rounding can make the structure contradictory. The orientation is determined on the raw values, the in-circle test on the ideally normalised values (x−xmin⁡)⋅sy(x-x_{\min})\cdot s_y and (y−ymin⁡)⋅sx(y-y_{\min})\cdot s_x with spans ss. Not on the rounded normalised values: three points on a diagonal are no longer collinear after that, and the method returned a triangle of area zero (11 of 1956 grid sections). The same rule holds for the flips in SweepHull: with the threshold EPS instead of the exact test, 4 of 300 clouds of about 2000 points deviated from the exact result; with the exact test 0 of 300.

Structure before Delaunay: the grid finder

Delaunay covers the convex hull. On a computational mesh with concave regions – around a blade, in a recess – it fills the gaps: on a CFD mesh it produced 102 % too much area. If the grid indices (i,j)(i,j) are known, the grid is therefore split directly: StructuredMesh.ausZellen forms two triangles per complete cell (i,j),(i+1,j),(i,j+1),(i+1,j+1)(i,j),(i{+}1,j),(i,j{+}1),(i{+}1,j{+}1), each placed counter-clockwise on its own, with holes, and keeps the point order of the input.

If the indices are missing, Gitterfinder reads them from the position of the points. Because Delaunay often does not contain the grid edges of skewed, stretched cells (on a grid of 201 × 51 points, 12 624 were missing), the candidates are the Delaunay neighbours and their neighbours.

Pseudocode – Gitterfinder.untersuche(x, y)

if n < 4 or a point is not finite or a point is duplicated:  return finding without grid
D ← GuibasStolfi.triangulate(x, y)
candidates[p] ← Delaunay neighbours of p and their neighbours
for each point p:  slot[p] ← up to four neighbours in the directions +a, −a, +b, −b
        a: nearest neighbour; b: nearest one with a small share along a
        ratio of mesh widths to the opposite direction between 0.55 and 1.8, lateral offset at most 0.35 mesh widths
keep neighbours that name each other mutually  run twice

for a ← 1 to 4:  four attempts from different start points
    for each still free start point:  new block b; start ← (i, j) = (0, 0)
        while there are open points p:
            for each direction r ∈ {(±1,0), (0,±1)}:
                v ← prediction of the neighbour position  parallelogram P + U − S from a neighbouring line,
                                                           otherwise linear with the stretching ratio of the last steps
                q ← candidate of p nearest to v
                if |q − v| > 0.3 · step:  continue
                if q is already placed, but elsewhere:  count a contradiction
                else place q at (i + r_i, j + r_j)
        if the block has fewer than 4 points:  release it
    chosen: the contradiction-free attempt with the most cells

Finding without grid if: contradictions > 0, or break edges > 0, or readable only sheared,
    or fewer than 90 % of the points placed, or fewer cells than n/2
else:  shift each block to (0, 0);  return Gitter(i, j, block, blocks, cells)

Source: envisionz-framework-data/src/envisionz/data/tri/Gitterfinder.java, StructuredMesh.java. Separate connected components become separate blocks, each with its own (i,j)(i,j); two blocks side by side are not joined.

Decision in the framework: Better a known detour than a wrong mesh – in doubt the result is null and the caller triangulates. A break edge is a short Delaunay edge between two blocks (a broken grid, not two blocks). Sheared means that the row starts wander by one almost everywhere in index space: the periodic grid has then been read with a diagonal as grid direction, the interior without gaps, but two sides lose a staircase of boundary triangles. The same rejection applies to scattered points, duplicate points and single grid lines.

The boundary: alpha shape and chi shape

Even without structure the true boundary of a measurement cloud is often concave. Delaunay bridges gaps that had no measurement; a boundary method then removes triangles. The registry Randverfahren knows KEINES (none), ALPHA (default) and CHI; anwende(mesh, setting) is the one place where the computation happens.

AlphaShape

A triangle is discarded if its measure exceeds α\alpha. Selectable are the circumradius R=abc4AR = \dfrac{abc}{4A} (the classical criterion; a degenerate triangle with area below 10−1510^{-15} gets ∞\infty and falls) or the longest edge, which is more robust against sliver triangles. For the triangle (0|0),(4|0),(2|3)(0|0),(4|0),(2|3), R=4⋅13/(4⋅6)=13/6R = 4\cdot 13/(4\cdot 6) = 13/6 and the longest edge is 4. The automatic threshold is Tukey's rule

α=Q3+k(Q3−Q1)\alpha = Q_3 + k\,(Q_3 - Q_1)

from the quartiles of all criterion values; a smaller kk cuts more (guide value 1.5). Measurement is in the normalised space, the value list for manual choice is dimensionless. Weakness: alpha measures the size of a triangle, not its position. On a test grid it removed eight triangles from the middle of the mesh, and four points dropped out entirely (alpha = 0.1339).

ChiShape

The method of Duckham, Kulik, Worboys and Galton (2008) removes triangles only from the boundary inwards. The threshold is a multiple λ\lambda of the median edge length (median over all distinct edges, in the normalised space); large means little is removed, at infinity the convex hull remains.

ChiShape.filter(mesh, λ):
    xs, ys ← each axis on [0,1];  median ← median of all edge lengths;  threshold ← λ · median
    q ← queue of all boundary edges, longest first  end points count as boundary points
    while q is not empty:
        r ← take the longest boundary edge
        if length(r) ≤ threshold:  stop  all the others are shorter
        if r is no longer a boundary edge:  continue
        t ← the triangle at r;  g ← its opposite corner
        if g already lies on the boundary:  count as rejected;  continue  regularity condition
        remove t;  g becomes a boundary point
        insert the two other edges of t into q, as far as they are now boundary edges
    return mesh without the removed triangles, adjacency rebuilt

Source: envisionz-framework-data/src/envisionz/data/tri/ChiShape.java. Edges of equal length are ordered by point numbers so that the result does not depend on the order of a hash table.

Because no boundary point may reach the boundary a second time, the boundary stays a simple polygon, the mesh does not pinch off, and no point drops out. That is the structural difference from alpha. Default: λ=2.0\lambda = 2.0 (VORGABE_LAMBDA).

Manual touch-up

A gap and a boundary look the same in a point cloud; on the C-shaped test grid (41 × 9 points, 679 triangles, 39 of which do not belong) there is no λ\lambda for which chi shape is exactly right: at λ=2.0\lambda = 2.0 two triangles of the recess remain, at λ=1.0\lambda = 1.0 the last one falls at the price of 46 wrongly removed. Nachbesserung therefore applies a list of exceptions, with only two safe kinds: BEHALTEN (keep) brings back a discarded triangle – but only one that exists in the base triangulation, nothing is invented –, VERWERFEN (discard) removes a kept one. Exceptions are identified by the table rows of the three corners, not by point or triangle numbers, which change with every rebuild. Each exception is then ANGEWANDT (applied), UEBERFLUESSIG (superfluous: the mesh is like that anyway) or VERFALLEN (lapsed: rows are missing from the slice or the triangle does not exist in the base); lapsed ones are counted and reported.

Worked example

The four points of the figure give two triangles: n=4n = 4, the hull is the quadrilateral A,P,B,CA, P, B, C, so h=4h = 4 and T=2⋅4−4−2=2T = 2\cdot 4 - 4 - 2 = 2. To check that PP violates the circumcircle of ABCABC, the determinant is enough: with d=P=(2|−1)d = P = (2|-1) the rows are (−2,1,5)(-2, 1, 5), (2,1,5)(2, 1, 5), (0,4,16)(0, 4, 16), and the expansion gives −2(16−20)−1(32−0)+5(8−0)=8−32+40=16-2\,(16-20) - 1\,(32-0) + 5\,(8-0) = 8 - 32 + 40 = 16. The orientation (4)(3)−0=12(4)(3) - 0 = 12 is positive, so ABCABC is counter-clockwise, and 16>016 > 0 means: inside the circle. After the flip the test for (A,P,C)(A, P, C) and the point BB gives −16-16.

How it is checked

  • Skalenprobe (160 checks, 204 via the registry): the same set of triangles at stretchings of 1:1, 27:1, 33333:1 and 1:1000 for all three methods and for constraint edges; quality in image space (at most 10 % slivers, mean smallest angle above 20°, every point in a triangle); output coordinates bit-identical; Euler's formula T=2n−h−2T = 2n-h-2 and hull area over 40 random sets each with 200 and 1000 points. Reference values are computed, not stored. The test grid has exact rows and a shifted x per point – on an exactly regular grid the fault does not show.
  • Verfahrensprobe (71 checks, only via the registry): Euler with its own exact hull, empty circumcircle (tolerance Geom.EPS), scale invariance, coverage, degenerate positions. Sweepabgleichprobe: 25 clouds, the same triangles as Bowyer-Watson.
  • Gitterfinderprobe (44 checks): 12 × 6, anisotropic 1:20 with stretching, rotated circular ring, noisy, hole, C shape without bridge, two blocks; scattered points, duplicate points, strong shear and 45 % noise are read faithfully or rejected. On 77 blocks of the example grids of a CFD solver (known indices, points passed without them) 56 were recognised exactly, 21 rejected, none wrong; rejected were O-grids around circles, nozzles and blocks with duplicate points. Running time: 321 × 81 points in about 0.6 s (four attempts).
  • Randverfahrensprobe (31 checks) on three set-ups – a test grid, 300 Franke scatter points and a C-shaped region. No point is orphaned for λ=1.2\lambda = 1.2 to 3.03.0; the boundary stays simple, adjacency symmetric. Triangles removed: grid 0, scatter 13/28/41/63, C region 35/37/46/58. Nachbesserungsprobe (32): chi with λ=2.0\lambda = 2.0 keeps 642 triangles on the C arc, 2 of them wrong; two VERWERFEN give 640, none too many, none too few.
  • Every test comes with counter-experiments: the old fault is built back in and must turn red.

Limits

  • Delaunay guarantees the largest minimum angle in 2D, but only in the normalised space; a different axis scale in the plot can change the picture.
  • Duplicate points are skipped; if all points lie on a line, no triangles result.
  • The boundary methods cannot tell a gap from a true boundary – hence the manual touch-up.
  • Gitterfinder rejects what it cannot read with certainty (O-grids, nozzles, duplicate points); Delaunay with a boundary method remains.
  • Tetrahedralisation in 3D and iso-surfaces are on a page of their own.
